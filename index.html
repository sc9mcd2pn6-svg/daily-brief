⚓Iran War — Talks Ongoing

Trump pauses Iran strikes again; Oman-brokered Hormuz deal still unsigned as Gulf mediation intensifies

The 2026 Iran war — triggered February 28 by coordinated US-Israeli strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities — entered a new diplomatic phase this week. On August 3, Trump paused a planned renewed military strike following urgent appeals from Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Qatar. Oman and Qatar are now running parallel mediation tracks toward a formal Strait of Hormuz reopening agreement; Secretary of State Rubio acknowledges “progress but not finality.” Brent crude sits near $79/bbl — elevated but well off the $100+ peak of March — and will swing sharply on any deal breakthrough or collapse. A ceasefire agreed in April was extended indefinitely on April 21, but the US naval blockade was reimposed in July after it collapsed.

CNN · Al Jazeera · The National · Bloomberg
Why it matters to you

An unsigned Hormuz agreement keeps your Lebanon and Nigeria import logistics exposed to freight and fuel-cost volatility. If talks close this weekend, procurement costs ease; if they stall again, budget for $85+ Brent before year-end. Any open shipping or fuel-linked contract is worth locking in today if you can.

🕊️Lebanon — Ceasefire Fragile

IED kills two Israeli soldiers in south Lebanon; IDF resumes strikes as Rome ceasefire talks stall

Two Israeli reservists were killed and four seriously wounded on August 5 by an explosive device in Majdal Zoun, southern Lebanon — the most serious ceasefire violation in weeks. The IDF responded with strikes on Hezbollah targets. The incident came just as Lebanon-Israel talks in Rome (begun August 4) hit a wall over the Ali al-Taher Ridge: Israel is demanding full removal of Hezbollah infrastructure there as a precondition for further withdrawals, while Hezbollah has rejected Lebanon’s proposal to transfer the tunnel complex to the Lebanese army. The ceasefire is formally intact but its path to a permanent arrangement is now less clear.

Times of Israel · Terrorism Info Institute · Wikipedia
Why it matters to you

Each violation that triggers IDF strikes chips away at client and diaspora confidence in Lebanon’s stability — the variable that most directly drives your Lebanon revenue. The Rome talks are the mechanism for restoring that confidence; their stall is a risk signal worth tracking daily this week.

🌍 Africa & Nigeria
🏗️Lagos · Data Centres

Digital Realty opens LKK2 in Lekki — third Lagos data centre taps 2Africa cable, GDP boost projected at $37bn

Digital Realty has launched LKK2 at its Lekki campus, Lagos — its third Nigerian facility — delivering 2MW of IT capacity across 13,000 sq ft. The facility interconnects with LKK1, the landing station for the 2Africa subsea cable, giving tenants low-latency access to 46+ landing points across 33 countries spanning Africa, Europe, the Middle East and Asia. The company engineered the site from scratch: 4.2km of access road, a 33kV power line, and a flood-defence wall engineered to protect the campus for 275 years. The 2Africa cable connection is projected to contribute over $37bn to Africa’s GDP by fuelling connectivity and digital growth across the continent.

📊Nigeria · Economy

World Bank: Nigeria on track for 4%+ growth in 2026, but Iran war is pushing inflation back up

The World Bank’s latest Nigeria assessment confirms the economy is on a positive trajectory — GDP grew 3.87% in 2025 and is projected at 4.1–4.7% for 2026, driven by services, oil and gas, and improved FX conditions. However, the bank cautions that the Iran war is feeding imported inflation via higher fuel and freight costs, threatening the sharp decline in headline inflation (from 34.8% at end-2024 to 15.15% at end-2025). External buffers have strengthened but the supply-side price pressure is real. Nigeria could become the world’s fifth-largest economy within 50 years, per ECOWAS projections.

CNBC Africa · World Bank · News Agency of Nigeria · CBN via allAfrica
Why it matters to you

Higher fuel and input costs from Hormuz-related oil prices will compress Quasar Nigeria’s project margins. Build a cost buffer into any new contracts signed this quarter, and confirm whether key suppliers have hedged their naira/dollar exposure against further oil-price swings.

🇱🇧 Lebanon
🏦Economy — Contracting

Lebanon GDP to contract ~12% in 2026 as war losses mount and structural reforms stall

The Institute of International Finance projects Lebanon’s real GDP will contract roughly 12% this year, driven by the collapse of tourism and trade following the resumed regional conflict in early 2026, absent reconstruction inflows, and the continued fragility of the ceasefire. War-related economic losses were estimated at $3bn by April; the total is significantly higher with conflict ongoing. International financial support remains conditional on banking and public-sector reforms that have not materialised. The Iran war has worsened Lebanon’s access to regional trade financing. Real estate transactions fell 29.3% year-on-year in Q1 2026 as foreign investment dried up.

Al Jazeera · IIF · Barchart · Coface · RM Real Estate Consultant
Why it matters to you

A 12% GDP contraction means client budgets are under severe pressure and cash collection will remain difficult through year-end. Prioritise clients with fresh-dollar revenue streams, factor 60–90 day payment extensions into your forecasts, and avoid any new Lebanon commitments without upfront-payment terms.

Data centres: $87bn market, 13% CAGR — power delivery now beats connectivity as the decisive site-selection factor

The global data centre real estate market is valued at $87.17B in 2026 and is projected to reach $143B by 2030 at a 13.2% CAGR, according to CBRE, Research and Markets, and Data Centre Frontier. 🏙️Prime Beirut

Prime Beirut yields hold at 5–7% in fresh dollars, but Q1 transactions fell 29% as conflict uncertainty bites

Rental yields in prime Beirut neighbourhoods — Achrafieh and the Metn suburbs — remain competitive at 5–7% annually in fresh dollars, on par with Dubai, Cyprus and Athens. However, first-quarter 2026 data shows a 29.3% year-on-year decline in transaction count and an 18.2% drop in total value, reflecting the chilling effect of regional conflict and political uncertainty on foreign investment. Capital values remain around 20% below pre-2019 levels. The market has effectively become a cash-driven, fresh-dollar safe-haven operating independently of the formal banking system.
