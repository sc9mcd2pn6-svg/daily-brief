World · Climate

Update: COP31 collapses in Antalya — EU rejects text silent on fossil fuels; first UN climate summit to end without a cover deal

The COP31 climate summit in Antalya closed on Sunday, 22 November, without adopting a cover decision — the first in the summit’s 31-year history to do so. The European Union, United Kingdom, Norway, Panama and a bloc of island states rejected the final draft text, which made no mention of fossil fuels or any recall of the Dubai (COP28) phaseout language. The talks ran two days beyond their scheduled 20 November close, the longest UN climate conference on record. The only outcome formally adopted was a narrower Climate Finance Work Programme. UN Secretary-General Guterres expressed deep disappointment. Turkey’s presidency said the impasse was “the product of geopolitics, not science.”

Russia, Saudi Arabia and their allies successfully stripped any fossil-fuel reference from the text. The EU argued this represented a step backward from Dubai and walked out rather than adopt it. The failure leaves global climate governance without an agreed framework for the first time, setting up a contentious road to COP32.

Saudi Arabia’s $1 trillion PIF in identity crisis — Neom writedowns, new-deal freeze, Iran war strains sovereign finances

The Economist profiles Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund as an institution in crisis of purpose: the fund has written down $8 billion in megaprojects including Neom, is exploring securitisations and stake sales to generate cash, and has reportedly paused new acquisitions. Its assets under management shrank by $10 billion last year to around $900 billion. The Iran war, now in its ninth month, has placed new fiscal pressure across Gulf sovereigns, diverting resources from Vision 2030 to defence and regional security spending.

Reuters reported on 10 November that PIF is in early-stage talks about fresh fundraising options without deploying more of its own equity. The Economist frames this as an identity crisis rather than a collapse: the fund is recalibrating toward domestic private-sector leadership, but the ambition of becoming a global sovereign wealth giant has been substantially scaled back.

Sources: The Economist (Nov 22, 2026); Reuters (Nov 10); Semafor (Aug 2026); Khaleej Times

Spain election Nov 29: last polling day today — PP+Vox pointing to right-wing majority; Sánchez makes final push

Today is the final day on which new polls can legally be published before Spain’s snap election on 29 November (a five-day blackout applies from midnight). All 15 private polls published as of 12 November gave the People’s Party (PP) and far-right Vox a combined absolute majority, though the state pollster put the Socialists fractionally ahead. The official campaign ran 13–27 November. Main voter issues include housing, immigration and the fallout from the Urda rail disaster in October, which cost Transport Minister Puente his seat.

A PP-Vox majority would be the first far-right-backed government in Spain since Franco. It would shift European politics further right, complicating EU consensus on climate, migration and Ukraine. The Economist puts Sánchez’s rent-control proposals as his strongest card; his party has been gaining but remains behind in most polls.
