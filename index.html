✈️ World · US / Iran War

Trump’s Retreat: From the Gulf to Britain, American Forces Pull Back — Iran War Delivers Unintended Consequences

The NYT leads its Thursday edition with a striking reversal: the Iran war, launched to demonstrate American reach, has produced the opposite result. The US military has quietly evacuated hundreds of troops from key bases in Qatar and Bahrain, and pulled all twelve B-1 bombers out of RAF Fairford in the UK after intelligence revealed an imminent Iranian-directed plot — the seventh arrest in the Fairford conspiracy — targeting American aircraft and troops. Trump called the Fairford withdrawal moving the planes “around a little bit” but former RAF commander Greg Bagwell called it “very damaging” to perceptions of UK base security. US-UK communication over the move was described by officials as minimal.

The broader strategic picture: the Iran war was supposed to enforce American dominance of the Gulf energy corridor, but with Houthis now controlling the entire Red Sea coast, Gulf crude flows recovering slowly at 13.5M bpd (still below pre-war baseline), and refined-fuel supply at 81% below pre-war levels, the conflict has reshuffled military dispositions without delivering the decisive outcome Washington planned. Secretary of State Rubio, speaking in Athens, urged Europe to “awaken from slumber” — somewhat awkwardly timed given Washington’s own pullback. Whether the troop movements are tactical repositioning or the start of a broader drawdown is the defining question in defence circles.

✈️ World · Houthis / Saudi Arabia

Houthis Kill 3, Wound Dozens in Attacks on Saudi Airports — Riyadh Struck for First Time Since 2022

Yemeni Houthi forces attacked multiple Saudi airports on Wednesday, killing three people — two women and a Sudanese man — and wounding dozens more. Saudi authorities confirmed that Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airport was among the targets, the first strike on the capital’s airport since 2022, when the ceasefire first held. The Houthi military spokesman confirmed the attacks as deliberate strikes, framing them as retaliation for continued Saudi involvement in the Yemen conflict. This follows the group’s confirmation earlier this week of full control over Yemen’s Red Sea coast, including Perim Island at the mouth of Bab-el-Mandeb — the strategic chokepoint that has effectively closed the Suez route for commercial shipping.

The attack represents a significant escalation: striking the Saudi capital’s main international airport — a civilian facility handling millions of passengers — is qualitatively different from the ongoing port and military-facility strikes. Saudi Arabia scrambled fighter jets and activated air-defence systems. Insurance premiums for Gulf commercial aviation are expected to rise sharply. The L’Orient Today notes that these strikes are being tracked closely in Beirut, given the interconnected nature of the Houthi-Hezbollah-Iran axis and Lebanon’s own fragile security arrangements.

🏠 World · Spain / Housing / Politics

Spain Calls Snap Election for November 29 — Maricarmen Abascal Dies at 87; Housing Crisis Forces Sánchez to the Polls Early

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez called a snap general election for November 29 on Monday after parliament rejected his government’s emergency housing package. The decisive votes against came from the People’s Party, Vox, and crucially Junts per Catalunya — the Catalan separatist party whose seven votes destroyed his coalition’s working majority. The immediate trigger was the eviction of Maricarmen Abascal, 87, from her Madrid flat of 70 years by investment fund Urbagestión, which tripled her rent beyond her pension. Maricarmen returned home after a legal deal but has now died, the NYT reported Thursday, making her a posthumous symbol of Spain’s housing crisis. Over 500,000 demonstrators had marched in Madrid at the protest peak; more than 70,000 turned out in the last organised march.

Polls consistently show the conservative People’s Party (PP) well ahead, with a potential coalition with far-right Vox — the first time Vox would enter central government since Spain’s return to democracy in 1978. Sánchez frames the election as “mobilising a progressive majority,” but his party is trailing badly. The election comes at a moment of acute European political fragility: France is in social crisis, Germany is approaching its own elections, and Britain’s Burnham government is being tested. openDemocracy notes this as one of several simultaneous stress-tests on European centre-left governance.

🏴 World · Italy / Democracy

Italy Weighs Controversial Electoral Law — Bonus Seats for Election Winners Stoke Stability vs. Democracy Debate

Italy’s parliament is debating a contentious electoral reform that would award substantial bonus seats to whichever coalition or party wins a general election, regardless of vote share. Supporters — led by PM Giorgia Meloni’s Brothers of Italy — argue the measure would end Italy’s notorious government instability: the country has had 67 governments since 1946, and no administration has served a full five-year term since Berlusconi in 2001–06. Critics, including centre-left opposition, constitutional scholars and the Quirinale (Presidential Palace), argue the law would allow a party with 35–40% of the vote to control an overwhelming parliamentary supermajority, fundamentally undermining proportional representation and opening a path to what critics call “elected authoritarianism.” The NYT reports the debate is being watched closely across the EU as a potential template or cautionary tale.

Italy’s political and business environment matters directly for European commercial and financial markets: Italy is the eurozone’s third-largest economy, and constitutional uncertainty adds to the already elevated BTP-Bund spread (Italian sovereign risk premium) at a moment of broader bond-market stress. The vote is expected in November, with outcome uncertain — Meloni may lack the two-thirds majority needed for constitutional amendment without a referendum.

🏭 World · US Economy / Rates

Fed Minutes: Rates at 3.75–4%, Majority See More Hikes — But October Odds Collapse to 20% After Weak Jobs Data

The Federal Reserve released minutes from its September 15–16 meeting on Wednesday, confirming a unanimous rate hike to the 3.75–4.00% target range — the fourth increase this year. Sixteen of eighteen policymakers projected at least one further quarter-point hike by year-end, citing stubborn inflation driven by tariffs, the Iran-war energy shock, and AI-driven capital spending pushing core PCE to 3.0% in August. The September meeting was a clear hawkish signal. However, markets have immediately reassessed the October 28 decision: the September jobs report showed only 29,000 new payrolls — the third consecutive monthly disappointment — and futures-implied odds of an October hike have fallen sharply to approximately 20%. The December meeting is now the live battleground.

The practical impact: 10-year US Treasury yields remain near 5.3%, mortgage rates above 7.3%, and the dollar is at a 17-month high against the euro. For Lebanon, the dollar strength compresses the import capacity of Lebanese firms paying in weak pounds but contracts in dollars; for Nigerian operations, higher US rates attract capital away from frontier markets and keep the naira under pressure. The Fed is in an uncomfortable position: inflation is structural (tariffs, energy) not demand-driven, yet rate hikes are the only tool available.

Update: French Student Protests Enter Week 2 — Universities Blockaded, Unions Join, 2027 Presidential Race Now Live

France’s school and university crisis is deepening into its second week. Approximately 440 schools and 30+ universities remain blocked or disrupted. Major union federations — CGT, CFDT, and the teachers’ FSU — have now joined the students, transforming a school walkout into a broader social challenge to PM Lecornu’s budget-driven austerity. Total arrests exceed 2,000. Lecornu convened an emergency cabinet session but has offered no concrete concessions, demanding instead that France Insoumise’s Mélenchon stop “encouraging” the protests — a framing Mélenchon rejected publicly.

The political consequence has shifted the story entirely: polls this week show Marine Le Pen winning the first round of a hypothetical presidential election against any rival scenario. The April 2027 presidential election — Macron’s term expires in spring — is effectively already underway. Le Pen’s National Rally is positioning as the party of order and stability against both the government’s austerity and the street chaos. Lecornu faces an impossible equation: bond markets expect fiscal discipline (France’s debt is 119% of GDP); the streets demand new spending. Neither can be satisfied without damaging the other.

🏭 Lebanon · Economy / Banking Reform

IMF Arrives in Beirut in 5 Days — Financial Gap Law Has No Parliamentary Vote Date; BDL Resistance Ongoing

Lebanon’s most consequential economic moment of the year is five days away: the IMF technical mission arrives October 13 and runs through October 18. The Financial Gap Law — which allocates the ~$72–80 billion in banking-sector losses across the state, BDL, commercial banks and depositors — cleared cabinet on October 6 (13–9) but parliament has set no vote date. The BDL and the Association of Banks of Lebanon continue to lobby against elements that would expose them to restructuring. LBCI political sources indicate extensive amendments are likely before any vote, risking a diluted version that the IMF considers insufficient. Finance Minister Jaber (who voted against his own Amal bloc) is the critical swing figure.

The stakes are the starkest they have been in years: the IMF has identified the Financial Gap Law as the last remaining legislative prerequisite for a formal programme. Lebanon’s economy is contracting 6.4% in 2026 (World Bank), Eurobonds remain stuck near 25 cents, and inflation runs at 17.5%. A vote before October 18 that the IMF accepts = real reform credibility and a path to Eurobond recovery. A delay or a heavily amended version = another failed mission, continued 25-cent Eurobonds, and tight dollar liquidity through 2027.
