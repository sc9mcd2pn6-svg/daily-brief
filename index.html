USS Abraham Lincoln heads home after 272 days — Middle East war hits stalemate, Trump turns to economic threats

The USS Abraham Lincoln, carrying roughly 5,000 sailors, began its month-long, 13,000-mile journey home on Thursday after 272 days at sea — the longest wartime carrier deployment in decades. The ship, the primary US strike platform against Iran, leaves as the conflict reaches an uneasy stalemate: neither side is negotiating nor escalating militarily. The Japan-based USS George Washington has taken the Lincoln’s place. Conditions aboard the Lincoln had deteriorated after the US Navy logistics hub in Bahrain was largely destroyed, prompting family complaints. Trump has now pivoted to economic pressure, promising steep penalties on any nation doing business with Iran, and Treasury Secretary Bessent intervened separately in currency markets last week to prop up the yen — both moves designed partly to keep US interest rates from rising further.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced he would double US government debt buybacks from $2bn to $4bn per operation — the most interventionist Treasury move in decades — and the plan worked for exactly one day. Driving the rise: Iran war inflation, persistent US fiscal deficits, and uncertainty about Fed policy. Bessent’s moves have also included intervening in the yen — making him, in Bloomberg’s words, “the most interventionist Treasury secretary in decades.”

China’s LandSpace lands reusable rocket booster — joins SpaceX and Blue Origin, closing the gap

Chinese private launch startup LandSpace successfully recovered the first stage of its Zhuque-3 rocket after completing an orbital mission, becoming the first Chinese private company ever to land an orbital-class booster on land. The milestone, achieved on its second attempt, was enabled by technical improvements including simplified landing burn management and enhanced autonomous safety control. LandSpace is seeking a 7.5bn yuan ($1.1bn) IPO and aims to be profitable by 2029. The achievement accelerates the global commercial launch market’s race and puts additional competitive pressure on European and US non-SpaceX providers.

⚖️ Nigeria · Politics

US court’s August 21 deadline hits today: FBI and DEA must release drug-trafficking records on President Tinubu

Today is the court-ordered deadline for the US FBI and Drug Enforcement Administration to release files related to a 1993 Chicago heroin investigation in which President Bola Tinubu forfeited $460,000 — though he was never charged. US District Judge Beryl Howell rejected the Trump DOJ’s request for a 10-day extension and granted only four additional days, setting today as the firm deadline. The case was filed by journalist David Hundeyin and activist Aaron Greenspan under the Freedom of Information Act; it has been pending three years. The Nigerian Presidency has dismissed the order, but Atiku Abubakar’s camp has backed its enforcement. If the records are released and prove substantive, they could trigger a constitutional crisis that disrupts Nigeria’s reform momentum and market stability.

The IMF has officially welcomed the Lebanese parliament’s passage of the Financial Stabilisation and Depositor Recovery (FSDR) law as a “major step,” but its Lebanon representative Federico Lima emphasised that “effective implementation is critical” and that discussions continue on aligning the law with international principles. UNIFIL hands over Khardali military position to Lebanese army — December drawdown accelerates

UNIFIL formally transferred its military position in Khardali, southern Lebanon, to the Lebanese Armed Forces (LAF) — the first in a series of planned handovers ahead of the December 31, 2026 mandate expiry.
