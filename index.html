🚫 World · Brazil

Brazil Votes Today — Lula vs. Flávio Bolsonaro in a Statistical Tie, Runoff on 25 October Almost Certain

Brazil is holding its first-round presidential election today, Sunday October 4, with President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva facing Senator Flávio Bolsonaro — eldest son of former president Jair Bolsonaro — in the tightest Brazilian vote since 1989. Final polls put Lula at 45% of valid votes and Flávio at 40%, with the race essentially tied in runoff polling at 48% to 45%. Because neither candidate is expected to exceed 50% tonight, a second round is virtually certain for October 25. Brazilians are also electing 27 governors, 54 senators and all 513 members of the Chamber of Deputies.

The Economist’s pre-election assessment was stark: neither candidate is capable of delivering the structural reforms Brazil needs — a verdict that frames the election less as an ideological contest and more as a choice of which faction manages national stagnation. The race has been marked by disinformation campaigns, judicial interventions and deep polarisation. The outcome will shape Brazil’s trade policy, climate commitments (Lula) and alignment with Trump’s Washington (Flávio).

📺 World · US Politics

Trump Directs Over $1.5 Million in Taxpayer Funds to Self-Promotional TV Ads — Bipartisan Senate Alarm

The New York Times and Axios reported this week that the Trump administration is running at least three government-funded television advertisements that glorify the president personally and echo his 2024 campaign material — at a cost already exceeding $1.5 million from the public purse. The ads began airing September 23, weeks ahead of November’s midterm elections. One ad is virtually identical to a 2024 Trump campaign spot, showing him walking down a hallway warning of a “final battle” against “globalists and warmongers.” A second features Mount Rushmore with Trump’s words overlaid.

Multiple Republican senators, including Senate Majority Leader John Thune, publicly stated the spending “shouldn’t be paid for with taxpayer dollars.” Legal scholars are questioning whether the ads violate the Antideficiency Act and federal restrictions on using appropriated funds for partisan political purposes. The White House has simultaneously filed a brief arguing the president has the authority to bar unfriendly news outlets (CNN, MSNBC, Politico) from the press pool — a dual assault on press and fiscal norms that is drawing international attention.

🚨 World · UK Security

Man Who Killed MP Ann Widdecombe Now Charged With Planning Terrorist Acts — Including Plot Against Farage

Joshua Kerry, the 28-year-old British man accused of killing former government minister Ann Widdecombe at her home in southwest England, has been formally charged with preparing acts of terrorism, including a specific plot against Reform UK leader Nigel Farage. The charges represent a significant escalation: Kerry now faces both murder and terrorism counts. UK Home Secretary and Prime Minister Keir Starmer have both noted it is “chilling” that three sitting or former MPs have been murdered in Britain over the past eleven years, reflecting a pattern of targeted political violence the security services are struggling to contain.

The charge sheet adds a foreign-actor dimension: US Secretary of State Rubio earlier stated that a foreign actor may be involved in separate activity near RAF Fairford, and UK intelligence services are examining whether Kerry had any overseas contact. The case has prompted renewed calls for a comprehensive review of MP security protocols and has rattled the political establishment on the eve of the midterm election season, when political tensions are at their peak. Five UK nationals were separately bailed near RAF Fairford in connection with a related investigation.

⚖️ World · Iran

Iran Jails Hard-Line Lawmaker Who Led Opposition to US Deal — Deepest Factional Rift in Years

Iranian authorities have convicted and jailed Hamid Rasaei, one of the parliament’s most vocal opponents of any diplomatic settlement with the United States, on charges of “publishing false information with intent to harm the parliament speaker.” The New York Times reports that the conviction represents an extraordinary move by the Islamic Republic: silencing one of its own hardliners to clear the way for fragile US-Iran negotiations. Rasaei had publicly accused the parliament speaker of “treason” for even engaging with Washington, and was seen as the loudest domestic voice against the preliminary Hormuz deal.

The jailing signals that Supreme Leader Khamenei has decided the diplomatic track must proceed unimpeded domestically — at least for now. But it also exposes how deep the factional rift inside the Islamic Republic has become, with the IRGC hardliners aligned with Rasaei now watching warily. Any breakdown in US-Iran negotiations, or a return to Hormuz strikes, could rapidly rehabilitate the hardline position. Analysts see this as a deliberate signal to Washington that Iran is serious about talks while managing its own internal opposition.

⛽ World · Energy

G7 and US Release 100 Million Barrels of Diesel Reserves as Fuel Prices Soar — SPR Near 1982 Lows

The Group of Seven — the US, UK, France, Germany, Japan, Italy and Canada — announced a coordinated release of 100 million barrels of diesel and oil reserves over four months, effective immediately, to contain fuel prices surging in the wake of Hormuz disruptions and Gulf energy infrastructure damage. The New York Times reports the announcement was timed to coincide with a US Energy Department bid offering 40 million barrels from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve, with bids due October 6. The US SPR will fall to its lowest level since 1982 once this drawdown is complete.

Economists and energy analysts note a structural problem: nearly half of the US crude released so far has left the country, indicating that global supply tightness is simply absorbing the reserves without sustainably capping prices. Brent held above $91 on Saturday, up from Friday’s close, as markets weighed limited reserve effectiveness against persistent Hormuz risk. The G7 move buys time but does not resolve the underlying supply gap; if Iran escalates its Hormuz harassment this autumn, pressure will be back on within weeks.

🏛️ Africa · North Africa / Morocco

Morocco Appoints Fatima Ezzahra El Mansouri as First Woman Prime Minister — Only Second in Arab World

King Mohammed VI appointed Fatima Ezzahra El Mansouri as Morocco’s prime minister on September 29, making her the country’s first woman to hold the office and only the second female head of government in the Arab world after Tunisia’s Najla Bouden. El Mansouri, a 50-year-old lawyer and former mayor of Marrakesh, leads the Authenticity and Modernity Party, which won 97 of 395 parliamentary seats in the September 23 election. Her appointment represents a landmark for the Maghreb’s political landscape and a deliberate signal from the palace about Morocco’s reform direction.

Morocco under El Mansouri will continue the country’s pivot toward Western investment and its hosting of the 2030 World Cup co-tournament with Spain and Portugal. For West Africa regional context, Morocco is also deepening trade and infrastructure links with sub-Saharan partners — including Nigeria — via its Atlantic-coast gas pipeline project. As Morocco’s profile rises, it will compete with Casablanca against Dubai and Riyadh as a regional business hub for multinationals covering the African continent.

✊ Africa · Nigeria

Update: Nigeria Public-Sector Strike Suspended for 30 Days After Government Talks

The public-sector unions behind the three-day warning strike that began October 2 have agreed to suspend industrial action for 30 days after meetings with senior government officials produced commitments on fuel pricing relief and minimum-wage escalation timelines. The strike had threatened to shut federal government agencies, customs and port operations across Nigeria. Premium Times and Africa.com report that the suspension was accepted after overnight talks in Abuja produced a written undertaking from the finance ministry. The government’s 30-day window is tight: workers expect measurable progress on petrol-price caps and arrears before early November.

The oil workers’ union Nupeng also separately suspended a week-long pay strike in the downstream petroleum sector. However, Lagos State university staff — a separate action — remain on indefinite strike, meaning academic disruptions in the city continue. The net result for this week’s business environment: federal agencies re-open, but Lagos public education and some state-government functions remain disrupted. Quasar Nigeria should expect some customs and federal permits to resume normal processing this week.

Lebanese Army Reports Progress: Phase 1 of Hezbollah Disarmament South of Litani Declared Complete

The Lebanese Army announced this week that it has completed Phase 1 of its plan to disarm non-state armed groups — principally Hezbollah — in the area south of the Litani River, as required under the Washington-brokered ceasefire agreement. L’Orient Today reports the army has been conducting active disarmament raids, including the demolition of 35 Hezbollah positions in Mansouri this week, with three individuals wounded in Nabatieh during a standoff. Phase 2 of the plan, covering the 40-kilometre stretch between the Litani and the Awali River, is now under cabinet discussion.

Israel described Phase 1 completion as “encouraging but far from sufficient,” noting that Hezbollah retains dozens of compounds north of the Litani. Hezbollah has formally cooperated with army disarmament in the south but continues to refuse discussion of its broader arsenal while Israeli forces remain on Lebanese soil. The army’s progress is nonetheless the most substantial Lebanese sovereignty assertion since the ceasefire and has been praised by France, the US and Saudi Arabia — all of which are funding military equipment transfers to the Lebanese Armed Forces.

📅 Lebanon · Politics

Lebanon Parliament Votes to Extend Its Own Term to 2028 — Planned May Elections Cancelled

The Lebanese parliament voted to extend its own mandate by two years, postponing the parliamentary elections that had been scheduled for May 2026 to 2028. The stated rationale was the ongoing security situation following the 2024–26 conflict with Israel and the unresolved south Lebanon occupation. However, political analysts including Daraj Media note the extension benefits incumbents across all factions — including Hezbollah-aligned MPs who face a challenging electoral environment given the movement’s military weakening. Lebanon’s Interior Minister had as recently as last month insisted elections would be held on time; the extension reverses that position.

The postponement carries two serious collateral consequences. First, it further undermines Lebanon’s IMF reform credibility: the Fund has pressed for electoral accountability as a precondition for a deal, and a self-granted mandate extension signals the political class is insulating itself from voter pressure precisely as the economy contracts 6.4%. Second, it removes the calendar pressure on reform — governments facing elections move; governments with two years to spare do not. Lebanon’s Eurobonds trade at ~25 cents, and this news will not help.

L’Orient Today (independent) · Global Security · Reuters

America’s Nine-Figure Listing Boom — 2026 Is a Record Year for $100 Million Homes

The New York Times reports that 2026 has become a record year for US residential listings priced at $100 million or more, driven by America’s expanding billionaire class. Properties in Palm Beach, Miami, New York and Los Angeles have been listed at $100M–$295M, with some achieving close to asking prices in all-cash deals. Despite mortgage rates at 7.28% — the highest since 2007 — the ultra-high-net-worth tier of the market operates entirely without financing and has been insulated from rate pressure. Wealth concentration has accelerated through the AI boom, and the very top of the US market is decoupling from the rest.

The same dynamic is visible in London, where the Financial Times reported prime transactions at ±14% above last year, and in Dubai, where Gulf sovereign wealth is anchoring a new ultra-prime tier. The trend has specific implications for prime Beirut: dollar-cash property in a recovering security environment at 15% below 2019 prices represents the same value logic that is driving global ultra-prime interest, and Beirut’s floor-value positioning — Achrafieh at $2,800–4,000/sqm vs. Paris at $14,000–18,000/sqm — is increasingly well-understood by informed regional buyers.
