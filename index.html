US launches “Operation Economic Outcast” on Iran — but the Economist says Tehran can shrug it off

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced on Monday what he called “economic D-Day” for Iran: a sweeping sanctions campaign targeting every Iranian revenue stream — oil, gold, crypto, aviation and shipping — with secondary sanctions threatened against China, India, Germany and any other country that continues trading with Tehran. Bessent said Trump was personally calling world leaders with “specific requests.” The announcement was billed as the “endgame” for Washington’s Iran strategy.

The Economist’s Monday lead pushes back sharply: Iran has built alternative financial rails, non-dollar payment systems, and a shadow tanker fleet over years of sanctions pressure, and China — the largest buyer of Iranian oil — has a financial system largely insulated from US threats. Brent crude fell $2 to $92.34 as traders read Bessent’s announcement as a warning shot lacking immediate enforcement bite. Iran’s leaders responded by saying they would not bow to pressure. The detail that will determine whether this moves markets: whether Washington actually designates Chinese financial institutions under secondary sanctions.

Supreme Court clears Trump’s mail-voting restrictions to proceed — “chaos and uncertainty” ahead of November midterms

In an emergency order Monday afternoon, the US Supreme Court allowed President Trump to begin implementing his executive order directing the Department of Homeland Security to build state-by-state citizen lists to monitor voter rolls, and instructing the Postal Service to use state-provided lists to restrict who can vote by mail. With eight weeks until Election Day — and early voting beginning in many states within weeks — it is unclear whether the full apparatus will be operational in time, or how many voters it will affect.

The three liberal justices dissented. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson warned the majority “needlessly injects chaos and uncertainty into the upcoming midterm elections.” The court cautioned that its order is not a final ruling on the legality of the executive order, meaning the legal battle is likely to return before November. The ruling adds to the fog already flagged by the NYT’s polling-credibility reporting: November could produce sharp surprises in either direction.

The Economist named this its “Falling fast fashion” story of the week, with the sting in the tail: Shein’s disruptor model — ultra-cheap, algorithm-driven, Chinese-made clothing sold at Western prices — is being squeezed from below by Temu and from above by de minimis tariff changes. The story is a wider signal that the era of Chinese consumer-tech unicorns listing at stratospheric multiples is over.

Indonesia’s wildfires surge 3× under El Niño — 1,612 hotspots in first 13 days of August, worst since 2023

El Niño has made Indonesia’s 2026 dry season hotter, drier and longer than usual, driving a threefold spike in forest fires: the Forestry Ministry’s SiPongi platform recorded 1,612 hotspots in the first 13 days of August alone, already the highest August reading in three years, with peak season still a month away. Close to 50,000 firefighting and disaster personnel, backed by dozens of helicopters, are deployed across multiple provinces.

The NYT’s Monday morning brief led with the Indonesia story, drawing a direct line to the El Niño pattern also driving Indonesia’s peatlands — a particularly dangerous fuel source because peat fires burn underground and are nearly impossible to extinguish fully. Peak fire season is forecast for September–October. The pattern mirrors France’s worst wildfire season (covered in this brief two weeks ago) and signals a broader 2026 climate inflection year.
