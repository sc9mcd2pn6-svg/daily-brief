☢️US & Iran

Trump calls off “biggest strike since WWII” — Hormuz deadlock persists as 60-day deadline lapses

President Trump posted on Truth Social that the US was “locked and loaded” for a strike he described as potentially “the biggest attack since World War II,” then stood down hours later after Iran and regional partners requested a pause. The June 19 Geneva MOU’s 60-day window has now expired with the core deadlock unchanged: Iran’s 440 kg highly-enriched uranium stockpile, IAEA inspection sequencing, and $24 billion in frozen assets. Iran continues to block and tax commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz; a vessel was struck on Monday. Markets rallied sharply on the stand-down — Brent pulled back, US equities rose — but analysts warn a deal collapse reverses both moves quickly.

🇺🇦Ukraine

Russia kills 9 in Kyiv ballistic barrage — Trump walks back Patriot missile-manufacturing promise

Russia launched its second major attack on Kyiv in 48 hours on August 1, killing at least 9 people and wounding 23 across five districts with 35 ballistic missiles and 185 attack drones. In the same period, Trump reversed a previously endorsed deal to license Ukraine to manufacture its own Patriot air-defence missiles, telling his Cabinet “we have not agreed to that.” Russia suffered 42,860 casualties in July — its bloodiest single month of the war — yet its air campaign is intensifying. Diplomatic sources say peace talks have “little chance of success.”

💴Currencies

US and Japan confirm rare joint yen intervention — dollar drops 1.4% to 155–156 yen

In a historically unusual move, Japan and the United States jointly confirmed a coordinated currency intervention last week to reverse the yen’s slide to levels not seen since 1986. The US sold euros rather than dollars to buy yen — a departure from standard mechanics — triggering an immediate 1–1.4% dollar decline. Washington’s motivation is partly self-interested: a persistently weak yen widens the US trade deficit, counter to the Trump administration’s trade agenda. Analysts say US backing makes the intervention more durable than Japan acting alone.

Japan Times · Al Jazeera · CNBC · NPR

🏙️Prime Beirut

Beirut transactions fall 29% in Q1 as conflict returns — diaspora cash buyers still see a generational entry

Property transactions in Lebanon fell 29.3% year-on-year in Q1 2026 and total transaction value dropped 18.2%, as renewed hostilities that flared on March 2 crushed activity and Beirut airport arrivals fell below 50,000 in March. Yet diaspora buyers from the UAE, USA, Europe, and Australia continue to pursue cash purchases in Achrafieh and Metn, citing prices still a fraction of 2018 valuations and rental yields of 5–7% annually in fresh dollars. New-permit supply remains constrained, supporting prices for existing prime inventory.

The Beiruter · Executive Magazine · Now Lebanon · RM Real Estate Consultant
Why it matters to you

Your prime Beirut holdings are insulated by the cash-only market structure and constrained supply — but the 29% transaction drop signals declining liquidity; the current diaspora buying wave remains your most credible exit window if you need to free capital.

🏦Economy & Security

IIF confirms 12% GDP contraction — reserves hold at $11.6bn, daily strikes continue, IMF preconditions mount

The Institute of International Finance maintains its forecast of a 12% real GDP contraction for Lebanon in 2026 — a sharp reversal of 5% growth in 2025 — as tourism has collapsed, reconstruction inflows have stalled, and fiscal fragility persists. Official reserves hold at roughly $11.6 billion near LBP 89,700/USD. Israel has continued daily strikes and displacement orders.
