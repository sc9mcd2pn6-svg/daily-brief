Climate

COP31 opens in Antalya today — climate finance the defining battle

The 2026 UN Climate Change Conference officially opens this morning at the Antalya Expo Center, running through November 20 with 190+ countries and an expected 30,000 delegates. Turkey holds the presidency (Environment Minister Murat Kurum as COP31 president) while Australia’s Chris Bowen leads formal negotiations — a split arrangement reflecting the geopolitics of climate diplomacy. The World Leaders’ Summit is set for November 11–12 when heads of state are expected to announce new national pledges.

The central fight is climate finance: how much the wealthy world commits to help developing countries cut emissions and adapt to damage already baked in. Following a summer of record temperatures and sporadic disasters, keeping global warming as close to 1.5°C as possible will dominate the agenda. An Antalya Ocean Declaration is also expected among the finalised outcomes. The Economist notes the summit follows US consumer inflation at 3.7% in September, adding political pressure on the Democratic Congress to show fiscal seriousness even while legislating climate action.

Source: The Economist (8 Nov); UNCCD; Global Landscapes Forum; Michael West Media

Ukraine

Russia–Ukraine–US trilateral talks open in Abu Dhabi tomorrow — first since the Iran war

US special envoy Steve Witkoff confirmed that working-level trilateral talks between Russia, Ukraine and the United States will be held in Abu Dhabi on November 10–11 — the fourth formal round since January 2026 and the first since the sessions broke down when Iran launched its February offensive that pulled the US into direct conflict. Witkoff and Jared Kushner will represent Washington. The agenda covers a potential energy and maritime ceasefire, prisoner exchanges and the return of Ukrainian children taken to Russia.

Ukraine’s President Zelenskyy said he hopes the Abu Dhabi talks will open the path to a direct meeting with Putin. The two sides remain far apart on territorial terms. European governments insist on being involved but are unlikely to be at the table. Germany’s Chancellor Merz said he does not see Russia engaging seriously. Meanwhile the NYT reports that inside Ukraine, commanders have been quietly turning Russian territorial gains in some sectors, with new details of operations kept secret for months.

Source: Khaleej Times; Al-Monitor; Al Jazeera (Nov 3–4); NYT (8 Nov)

Israel

Eisenkot formally tapped by Herzog to form Israel’s government — coalition math still treacherous

President Isaac Herzog formally named opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot as the first candidate to try to form a government after October’s inconclusive elections, in which Netanyahu’s bloc and the opposition finished at roughly 51 seats each. Eisenkot — a former IDF chief — secured backing from Liberman’s Yisrael Beytenu, Benny Gantz’s party and Yair Golan’s Greens. A majority of Knesset members are reported to have recommended him. Knesset members are sworn in today.

The path to 61 is narrow. Eisenkot has publicly ruled out a formal coalition with Arab-party lawmakers, yet he may need their support to reach a majority — a promise he would have to break. Netanyahu is meanwhile pushing for a broad unity government, arguing that any stable coalition requires his bloc. The Arab parties have backed Eisenkot conditionally, and ultra-Orthodox parties refuse to sit with him. Eisenkot has 28 days to assemble the coalition.

South Africa

South Africa ANC suffers worst local election result since taking power in 1994

Results from South Africa’s November local government elections show the ANC polling around 32%, down sharply from 45.6% in the 2021 municipal polls — its worst performance since the end of apartheid. The DA surged, sweeping through Gauteng and Cape Town, while the country is dominated by hung councils where no single party commands a majority. The IEC was due to announce final results today.

The NYT describes how the ANC has been reduced from “a once-dominant force credited with overturning apartheid” to “any other political party.” Coalition negotiations will now dominate South African politics, with the ANC seeking coalition partners to govern the municipalities where it lost its outright majority. South Africa joins a string of previously dominant parties across the continent facing voter punishment for failing to deliver growth, services and security.

Source: NYT Today’s Headlines (8 Nov); BusinessTech; EWN (6 Nov)

Science Policy

American brain drain claims another scientist — Trump cuts reshaping global research

The NYT’s Sunday magazine leads with astrophysicist Kelly Holley-Bockelmann, one of the country’s top scientists, who has left the United States after Trump’s funding cuts and politicisation of research affected every part of her work. It is part of a wider exodus: a Nature poll of more than 1,600 scientists found 75% are considering leaving the US; doctoral programme admissions at major research universities are down 15% for the fall 2026 cohort.

Destinations include Europe and Canada, which have launched targeted recruitment programmes. The AAU says the administration’s cuts to federal research funding, university crackdowns and visa restrictions have “unleashed a loss of scientific talent” with one estimate projecting a $1 trillion long-term economic cost. NASA is simultaneously recruiting its next astronaut class for missions to low-Earth orbit, an irony the NYT does not miss. The White House says it “remains committed to keeping America the world leader in scientific innovation.”

Nigeria

Update: Nigeria Ebola — 62 contacts under monitoring; Bundibugyo strain; Lagos on heightened alert

As of the NCDC’s November 2 situation report, Nigeria is monitoring 62 contacts following the confirmed Abuja Ebola case — a woman who arrived from Uganda on October 20. Contact tracing runs through November 13. The case is the rare Bundibugyo strain, for which there is currently no approved vaccine or specific treatment. No confirmed case has been reported in Lagos, but the state has activated its emergency Ebola response system and issued hygiene guidelines to hotels, clubs and public establishments.

Premium Times reports the NCDC has flagged Lagos, the FCT, Kano and Rivers among the high-risk states requiring heightened surveillance. Airport biosecurity has been reinforced across all major Nigerian airports. The outbreak originated in the DRC in May and has now spread to five countries. WHO has warned this could become the most complex Ebola outbreak in years due to the strain’s rarity and cross-border spread pattern.

Source: Sahara Reporters (2 Nov); Premium Times; WHO; SCMP

Economy

IMF mission lands in Beirut this month — banking law progress, but GDP to contract 6.4% in 2026

IMF staff are visiting Lebanon in November for the latest round of reform discussions, following parliament’s August approval of the Banking Resolution Law — welcomed by the Fund as “a significant step” in restructuring the banking sector. Despite the progress, the Financial Gap Law allocating losses between the state, banks and depositors remains unscheduled in parliament. Bank deposits are still frozen, the banking sector cannot extend credit to the economy, and the IMF and World Bank both project GDP to contract 6.4% in 2026 after a 4.2% expansion in 2025.

The IMF is urging Lebanon to strengthen the banking resolution law further and adopt a more ambitious 2026 budget. BLOMINVEST data puts the amount owed to banks and depositors at around $79 billion. The IIF’s July scenarios warned that political paralysis and security incidents could delay IMF negotiations well into 2027. The November mission is an opportunity but no guarantee of a programme.

NYT: US cleared Nvidia H200 chips to Chinese firms with “empty offices and troubling ties”

The NYT reports today that the Commerce Department has approved dozens of Chinese companies to handle Nvidia’s H200 AI chips — some of which carry ties to entities already under US national-security sanctions. Investigators found that several approved firms operated from addresses with empty offices, raising questions about whether export-control due diligence was adequate. Each approved customer is licensed to purchase up to 75,000 H200 chips, with buyers certifying no military use and accepting security controls.

Despite US approval, some deals have stalled as Beijing told Chinese customs to bar H200 imports. Nvidia’s most advanced Blackwell chips remain off limits to China. The story adds pressure on the Commerce Department at a moment when the new Democratic-controlled Congress is expected to tighten technology-export oversight. It also follows the Economist’s call this week for governments globally to “make sure defenders have better models than attackers” in the looming AI-driven cyber-security race.
