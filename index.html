🌍 World — Top Update
🛢️Hormuz — Deal in Principle

Iran–Oman Hormuz deal confirmed in principle — but a tanker reports explosions in the strait

Iran confirmed it has agreed in principle with Oman on a managed shipping corridor through the Strait of Hormuz; a joint statement is in final review. Oil is holding its three-day slide: WTI near $75/bbl, Brent near $79. Saudi Arabia simultaneously cut its official selling prices, adding to downward pressure. The deal, however, is still unsigned—and the UK Navy reported a tanker hearing two explosions while transiting the strait near Kumzar, Oman, a reminder that the route remains contested until formally implemented.

Bloomberg · Irish Times · AGBI · The National
Why it matters to you

The window for locking in equipment at lower freight and fuel costs is open, but today’s tanker incident shows it can snap shut fast. The deal is still not signed. Act on any open procurement decisions now; if the strait re-escalates, prices can retrace $10–15/bbl in a session.

🌍 Middle East
🕊️Gaza — Regional Rift

UAE joins Turkey and Saudi Arabia accusing Israel of sabotaging Trump’s Gaza plan

The UAE became the latest Gulf state to publicly accuse Israel of undermining President Trump’s proposed Gaza framework—joining Turkey and Saudi Arabia in a coordinated rebuke. Hamas has signed the US-backed disarmament plan; Israel responded that the roadmap “does not reflect Israel’s positions” and “stands in direct contrast to the current reality.” The growing Arab-Israeli diplomatic fracture adds uncertainty to the broader Middle East security picture, which Lebanon’s ceasefire still depends on.

Times of Israel · Reuters
Why it matters to you

Lebanon’s ceasefire stability is linked to whether Gaza diplomacy holds. A Gulf–Israel breakdown raises the risk of renewed escalation regionally—which directly threatens client confidence and cash collection in Lebanon. No immediate danger, but raise your risk antenna if this fracture deepens over the coming week.

🌍 Africa & Nigeria
🏗️Lagos · Data Centres

Digital Realty opens third Lagos data centre (LKK2) — direct link to 2Africa subsea cable

Digital Realty has formally launched LKK2, its third facility in Lagos, located in Lekki and adding nearly 2MW of IT capacity across 13,000 sq ft of data hall space. The facility is directly connected to LKK1, Digital Realty’s existing Lagos site which serves as the landing station for the 2Africa cable—providing access to 46 landing points across 33 countries. Separately, Open Access Data Centres has confirmed plans for a $240M, 24MW Lagos facility. Nigeria’s total data centre count stands at 28 operational or under-construction sites.

Capacity Media · Data Centre Magazine · Ecofin Agency · Technext24
Why it matters to you

Digital Realty LKK2 is in fresh fit-out in Lekki right now—fire suppression and security systems are live procurement items, not future pipeline. LKK1 is also operational and expanding. Two buildings, one operator, same campus: that’s a consolidatable conversation. Get in front of the facilities team this week while fit-out decisions are still open.

📈 Markets
📉Markets — Today

Fiserv tumbles 12% on profit-cut; Honeywell surges 7.5% — S&P 500 edges lower

Fiserv was today’s biggest loser in the US, crashing 12% after slashing its 2026 EPS guidance to $7.20–$7.40 from $8.00–$8.30 following the departure of its previous CEO. Honeywell International surged 7.5%—now up 42% year-to-date—on strong earnings, outpacing the broader market. The S&P 500 slipped 0.2%, with fewer than 200 of its 500 stocks in the green. Snap reported strong Q2 earnings, adding optimism on consumer digital spending.

TheStreet · Bloomberg · CNBC
Why it matters to you

Honeywell’s 42% YTD gain reflects strong demand for building automation and controls globally—the same category you operate in. Their pricing power is real right now; clients are spending on smart-building upgrades. That’s a tailwind for BMS pitches, but also a signal that Honeywell will be competing hard for any large integrated project. Know their current product positioning before your next major tender.

🎾 Sport
🎾Tennis · Canadian Open

Sinner, Alcaraz and Djokovic all out of Canadian Open — field reshuffled ahead of US Open

The Canadian Open’s draw has been gutted: Jannik Sinner withdrew to manage his workload ahead of Cincinnati and the US Open, Carlos Alcaraz pulled out due to the right wrist injury he suffered at the Barcelona Open in April, and Novak Djokovic is also absent. Emma Raducanu has also withdrawn on the women’s side. The tournament, normally the last major hard-court tune-up before the US Open (31 August), now begins with a weakened field. The US Open draw and seedings will look very different as a result.
