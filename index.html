⚡ World · Russia / Ukraine

Russia Planning Its Most Powerful Winter Blow Yet — Targeting Power, Heat and Water Across Ukraine’s Cities

The New York Times reports that Ukraine has shared with European partners intercepted Russian military planning documents detailing a winter infrastructure campaign far more destructive than the 2024–25 attacks. The plans call for simultaneous, coordinated strikes on power-generation stations, district-heating networks and urban water-supply systems in Kyiv, Kharkiv, Zaporizhzhia and Dnipro, timed to inflict maximum civilian damage before winter temperatures drop below freezing. US and European officials assessed the intelligence as highly credible and reflecting genuine military intent.

Ukraine is urgently requesting additional Patriot batteries and air-defence systems from Western allies before the cold snap hits in late October. Germany confirmed it would accelerate Iris-T SLM deliveries. NATO Secretary-General Rutte called an emergency session. Energy officials in Poland, Slovakia and Hungary are reviewing winter contingency plans. The threat is assessed as the single biggest risk to European energy security this winter, with potential knock-on effects on gas and electricity prices across the continent.

📈 World · Economy / Rates

US Hiring Slows for Third Straight Month; Bond Yields Hit 2002 High — Markets Under Sustained Pressure

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics released Friday’s September jobs report showing continued deceleration in hiring — the third straight weak monthly reading — as persistently high interest rates weigh on business investment and consumer spending. The 10-year Treasury yield held near 5.2%, its highest level since 2002, exerting the kind of gravitational pull on equities, mortgages and global credit that was last felt before the 2008 financial crisis. The Dow and S&P 500 fell sharply on Friday, with the S&P touching its lowest close since summer.

Congress has now left Washington ahead of the November 3 midterms, meaning fiscal relief is off the table for at least five weeks. The NYT reports that Republican candidates in competitive House and Senate races are facing voter fury over bond-driven mortgage unaffordability — the 30-year fixed mortgage is now at 7.28%, the highest since 2007. The Fed’s October meeting is live: market odds for another rate hike are priced at 70%, which would take the federal funds rate to its highest level since 2001.

The New York Times (anchor) · Bureau of Labor Statistics · Bloomberg

🚢 World · Iran / Shipping

Oil Flows Through Hormuz Recovering — but Iran Fires Drones at Shipping Every Week, Keeping Risk Premium Alive

The New York Times reports that Gulf crude transiting the Strait of Hormuz has returned to near-baseline (roughly 13.5 million bpd) as Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Kuwait reroute shipments through alternative channels, effectively backfilling Iran’s vanished exports. However, a Western security official told the NYT that Iran has been launching drones and missiles at commercial vessels in the waterway weekly — though Tehran has often failed to hit its targets. The pattern suggests a deliberate Iranian strategy: keep the harassment campaign active to maintain insurance-cost pressure and escalation leverage without crossing the line into a direct confrontation.

Lloyd’s war-risk premiums for Hormuz passage have risen over 400% since the war began. Any Iranian upgrade in targeting capability, or a successful major-tanker strike, could instantly re-spike Brent. Crude fell to ~$89 on Friday (weak US jobs data softened demand outlook) but remains structurally elevated far above pre-war norms due to the Hormuz risk premium baked into global oil pricing.

The New York Times (anchor) · Lloyd’s of London · Reuters

🌐 World · Horn of Africa

Ethiopia and Eritrea Sever Diplomatic Ties — Addis Accuses Asmara of Financing Tigray Rebels

Ethiopia formally closed its embassy in Eritrea on Friday and expelled ten Eritrean diplomats, accusing Asmara of financing and equipping the Tigray People’s Liberation Front in violation of the 2022 Pretoria peace agreement. Eritrea responded within hours by announcing it was cutting all diplomatic relations with Addis Ababa. Suspected drone strikes were also reported near Ethiopia’s national defence headquarters in Addis, though attribution remained unclear Friday evening. The rupture effectively collapses the post-2018 Abiy-Afwerki rapprochement that won the Ethiopian PM the Nobel Peace Prize.

Analysts warn the break could reignite conflict in contested border areas and destabilise the Horn of Africa region more broadly — particularly if Tigray insurgents are emboldened and Egypt, locked in its Nile dam dispute with Addis, is tempted to exploit the moment. The African Union, headquartered in Addis Abeba, has called for emergency dialogue. This is the most serious Horn of Africa diplomatic collapse since the 2020 Tigray war began.

📍 World · Middle East

Kushner’s $2.45 Billion Gaza Recovery Plan — The Economist Says the ‘Board of Peace’ Rings Hollow

Jared Kushner’s self-appointed “board of peace” unveiled a $2.45 billion recovery plan for Gaza this week, promising housing, infrastructure and economic normalisation. The Economist’s assessment is blunt: the plan is hollow. It sidesteps the fundamental question of who governs Gaza, ignores that Israel is still conducting military operations in parts of the Strip, and has no binding donor commitments — Gulf interest is “vague.” The money has not been committed; the governance vacuum has not been addressed.

Regional analysts note the plan functions primarily as a domestic political product ahead of November’s US midterms. For Lebanon specifically, any credible Gaza reconstruction blueprint eventually raises the question of Palestinian repatriation — including the roughly 500,000 Palestinian refugees in Lebanon’s camps. Beirut is watching to see whether Washington attaches repatriation conditions to any recovery framework, which would create enormous domestic political pressure in Lebanon.

🏫 Africa · Nigeria / Lagos

Lagos State University Staff Declare Indefinite Strike From Today Over Unpaid Arrears

Academic and non-academic staff unions across all six Lagos State-owned universities declared an indefinite work stoppage starting October 3, after negotiations with the state government failed to resolve months of accumulated salary arrears and broken commitments. The action covers Lagos State University (LASU) and five other institutions, shutting campuses across Africa’s largest commercial city. Union leaders warned the strike could spread to other Lagos public-sector bodies within five days if the government does not open serious talks. This action runs alongside the federal public-sector three-day warning strike that began October 2.

Guardian NG reports that the twin strike actions are deepening investor anxiety about Nigeria’s operating environment, particularly given that Tinubu’s fuel-subsidy removal is compressing real wages across both state and federal payrolls. Business continuity teams at major Lagos companies are reviewing contingency plans for potential disruptions in government responsiveness through at least October 8.

🔥 France · Politics / Labour

New Wave of French Strikes Planned for October as Budget Spending Cuts Take Effect

With Prime Minister Lecornu’s 2026 budget forced through parliament via article 49.3 on October 2, France’s major unions — the CGT, CFDT and CGE — are planning a new and larger wave of coordinated national strikes for October, targeting cuts to public services, education and transport. A Stratfor situation report identifies “a new wave of nationwide strikes and demonstrations” as the immediate consequence of the budget’s passage. October’s planned marches are expected to exceed the September 30 action, which drew hundreds of thousands into the streets and resulted in 440 arrests in Paris alone.

Politically, Lecornu has secured his budget but lost whatever residual RN goodwill remained. The far-right bloc now views October’s strike wave as an opportunity to ally with popular discontent and call for snap elections. With France’s national debt at 119% of GDP, there is almost no fiscal room for union concessions. The sustained disruption creates meaningful headwinds for French consumer demand in Q4 2026 and will weigh on France’s growth outlook into 2027.

📍 Lebanon · Security / Diplomacy

Lebanon-Israel Eighth Round Postponed Again — Israel Cited for 7,700 Violations of the June Ceasefire

The eighth round of US-brokered Lebanon-Israel negotiations has been delayed to later in October, L’Orient Today reports, after the seventh round in Rome failed to resolve three core disputes: the binding timetable for Israeli forces to withdraw from five occupied villages in south Lebanon; the fate of Lebanese prisoners held in Israel; and the monitoring mechanism for compliance. The Lebanese Army has formally logged 7,700 ceasefire violations by Israel since the June 26 agreement was signed — a figure that has severely eroded domestic public confidence in the deal and in the government’s ability to enforce Lebanese sovereignty.

President Aoun and PM Salam continue to demand a binding withdrawal schedule, which Israel has refused to commit to, citing Hezbollah rearmament concerns south of the Litani. International supporters of the Lebanese Army — France, the US and Saudi Arabia — are urging both sides to return to talks before winter. Without a clear Israeli exit date, south Lebanon’s agricultural communities and the displaced population face another winter of occupation.

L’Orient Today (independent) · Naharnet · Al Jazeera

🏭 Lebanon · Economy / IMF

No Lebanon IMF Deal at October 13–18 Meetings — Banking Law Needs 11 Amendments as BDL and Banks Push Back

With the IMF and World Bank annual meetings scheduled for Washington October 13–18, L’Orient Today reports no decisive progress toward a Lebanon staff-level agreement (SLA) is expected. The root cause: the banking restructuring law adopted by parliament in July still requires at least 11 substantial amendments to meet IMF standards. An L’Orient Today investigation found that the Banque du Liban and private commercial banks are mounting a coordinated legal and lobbying campaign to water down or delay the required changes, arguing the IMF’s conditions would wipe out bank shareholders and undermine deposit protection.

The IMF has been explicit: without the amended banking law, there is no deal and no money. The revised internal timeline now targets Q1 2027 at the earliest for a possible SLA. Lebanon’s Eurobonds hold near 25 cents on the dollar — reflecting market scepticism that restructuring happens quickly. The World Bank has revised Lebanon’s 2026 GDP growth to a contraction of 6.4%, reversing the 4.2% expansion of 2025.
