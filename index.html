🌍 World
🛢️ Iran — Hormuz & Tanker Strike

Iran missiles ADNOC tanker in the Strait — the 16th attack since the war began; Brent closes the week at $83.55 as the deal that “could come today” doesn’t

On August 8, Iran struck an Abu Dhabi National Oil Company (ADNOC) tanker with a missile as it transited the Strait of Hormuz — the 16th vessel attack since the war began. No casualties were reported; the ship is “under control.” The UAE, Qatar and the Arab bloc condemned the strike as a “flagrant violation” of UN Security Council Resolution 2817. The attack landed at the worst possible moment for diplomacy: US Treasury Secretary Bessent had told markets a Hormuz agreement could come “today or tomorrow,” driving oil down more than 7% mid-week before Friday’s missile strike reversed the move. Brent settled the week at $83.55 — elevated, volatile, and with no deal in place as of Saturday night. An interim arrangement (inbound ships via Iranian waters, outbound via Oman’s) is being negotiated but Tehran’s demands still include US and Israeli exclusion.

Al Jazeera · Gulf News · Al Arabiya · CNN · CNBC · France 24 · The National
Why it matters to you

Brent at $83–$84 with no deal signed is the working cost baseline for this week in both Lebanon and Nigeria. Any supply contract priced Monday should carry an explicit oil-escalation clause; a US counterstrike or a deal collapse could move Brent $10 in either direction in a single session. Keep Q4 cost assumptions provisional until the Hormuz situation resolves — this is the most fluid it has been in months.

☪️ Iraq · Karbala Pilgrimage

20 million Shia pilgrims — including 3 million Iranians — gather at Karbala for the first Arbaeen since Khamenei’s killing

More than 20 million Shia Muslim pilgrims, including over 3 million Iranians, gathered in Karbala, Iraq this week for the annual Arbaeen commemoration — the largest religious gathering in the world. The pilgrimage carries exceptional weight this year: Iran’s Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei was killed in US and Israeli airstrikes in the early months of the war, and his body was taken to Karbala and Najaf before burial in July. Many Iranian participants describe the journey as simultaneously religious and an act of national grief. The enormous Iranian turnout is a potent signal of post-war Shia solidarity at the very moment Hormuz diplomacy is at its most fragile.

🤖 US–China — Pre-Summit Escalation

Beijing unleashes broadest countermeasures since October truce — bans 7 US firms, tightens drone controls — as Xi’s September Washington visit builds

China deployed its broadest package of trade countermeasures since last October’s Busan truce on August 6, barring Chinese entities from transacting with seven US companies, imposing new export controls on drones and optical modules bound for America, and prohibiting Chinese firms from cooperating with US compliance and certification bodies. The package mirrors Washington’s own tech-war playbook and is timed to build leverage before President Xi Jinping’s planned September visit to Washington for a summit with President Trump. Disputes over AI models, advanced robotics, rare earth restrictions, and semiconductor access remain structurally unresolved. Markets are pricing the September summit as a pressure release valve; analysts note that each week of escalation narrows the available deal space.

US equities closed a volatile but strong week on Friday: the Nasdaq posted its best weekly performance since April, the S&P 500 held near record highs, and the Russell 2000 also advanced. 🏗️ Lagos · Data Centres

West Africa’s first hyperscale AI data centre is live: Kasi Cloud’s 100MW Lekki campus commissioned; Airtel’s 38MW build on track for end-2026; OADC $240m advancing

Kasi Cloud Datacenters commissioned West Africa’s first hyperscale-ready, AI-capable data centre campus in Lekki, Lagos in May 2026: a 100MW design on four hectares, adjacent to six subsea cable landing stations including Equiano and 2Africa, aligned with Nigeria’s National Cloud Policy 2025 mandate for in-country hosting of government and financial data. Separately, Nxtra by Airtel (part of India’s Bharti Airtel group) is on track to open its 38MW hyperscale campus in Lagos by end-2026 — which would make it Nigeria’s largest single data centre. Open Access Data Centres’ $240 million, 24MW Tier III Uptime-certified facility is also advancing. Lagos now has 28 operational or under-construction data centres, holding 29.7% of Nigeria’s national market.

TechCabal · GlobeNewswire · Data Centre Dynamics · TechNext24 · Connecting Africa

Nigeria data-centre market at $374m, 11% CAGR; broader macro recovery intact — but Hormuz oil spike is the main near-term threat to naira stability

Nigeria’s data centre market is valued at $374 million in 2026, with Lagos holding 29.7% share and the sector growing at 11.34% CAGR through 2031. 🇱🇧 Lebanon
🕊️ Security — IDF Strikes South

IDF strikes south Lebanon on Aug 5–6 citing Hezbollah ceasefire violations; one killed, twelve wounded; Rome talks deadlocked; US Embassy issues security alert

The Israeli Defence Forces struck southern Lebanon on August 5 and 6, citing “blatant ceasefire violations by Hezbollah.” Lebanese authorities confirmed one fatality and twelve injuries; the IDF issued an evacuation warning for Mansouri — the first such specific area warning in weeks. Israeli political leaders blocked IDF requests for broader escalation, opting for limited targeted strikes while Rome negotiations continue. The seventh round of US-mediated Rome talks remains deadlocked over the Ali al-Taher Ridge tunnel complex; Israel demands full Hezbollah removal while Lebanon’s army-transfer proposal has been rejected. The US Embassy Beirut issued a security alert on August 1; all non-emergency US visa services in Lebanon remain suspended, and the embassy is on ordered departure status for non-emergency personnel.

Haaretz · Times of Israel · Crisis Group · US Embassy Beirut · Security Council Report
Why it matters to you

IDF strikes and ceasefire fragility are the baseline for Lebanon through at least end Q3 — plan all Quasar Lebanon operations around that. 🏙️ Economy · Real Estate

Prime Beirut holds 5–7% fresh-dollar yields, but Q1 2026 transactions fell 29% year-on-year as security uncertainty suppresses volumes

Lebanon’s real estate sector entered 2026 under measurable pressure: Q1 2026 figures show a 29.3% year-on-year decline in transaction numbers and an 18.2% decline in total value, reflecting reduced foreign investment and security uncertainty. Despite this, prime Beirut — Achrafieh and the Metn suburbs — continues to generate 5–7% annual rental yields in fresh dollars, comparable to Dubai and Cyprus on a yield basis; transactions are conducted entirely in cash, eliminating the mortgage risk that plagued Lebanon’s pre-2019 market. Capital values remain roughly 20% below pre-2019 peaks. The reconstruction premium is deferred pending a durable ceasefire.

🏠 Real Estate
🏢 Data Centre RE · Supercycle

Data centre real estate hits $87.17bn in 2026 at 13.4% CAGR, en route to $143bn by 2030 as AI capex sustains the biggest infrastructure supercycle in decades

The global data centre real estate market is valued at $87.17 billion in 2026, up from $76.88 billion in 2025 at a 13.4% CAGR, and forecast to reach $143.32 billion by 2030. The primary driver is hyperscaler AI capex, set to exceed $600 billion globally this year. Research and Markets and GlobeNewswire describe the current period as “the beginning of one of the largest infrastructure investment supercycles in the modern era.” Power delivery — not bandwidth — is now the decisive site-selection variable in mature markets, making energy-reliable generator-backed facilities in fast-growth markets like Lagos increasingly attractive, not less. JLL’s 2026 Global Outlook identifies Africa as one of the fastest-growing regions by new supply.
