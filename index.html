Space

SpaceX catches Starship’s upper stage — a world first; orbital ship now fully recovered at the launch tower

SpaceX achieved what it called “another historic first” on Saturday: the Starship upper stage — the ship itself, not just the booster — was caught by the launch tower’s mechanical arms after returning from orbit. This is the first time an orbital-class spacecraft has been caught back at its launch site. The booster had failed its separate catch on Friday (reported then as a loss at sea), making Saturday’s ship recovery all the more striking. Both stages of the system have now individually been caught, completing the technical proof of concept for a fully reusable, rapidly re-launchable rocket.

The Economist led its weekend newsletter with the story and noted it sets the stage for SpaceX to begin point-to-point Earth transport and deep-space missions at dramatically lower cost. The NYT said the catch “marks a key milestone in SpaceX’s vision of building fully reusable rockets.” Musk had personally committed Starship to the data-centre infrastructure market earlier this month in a $74 billion investment announcement, adding a commercial backdrop to the technical achievement.

Source: NYT (15 Nov); The Economist Today (15 Nov)

Energy & Shipping

Iranian supertanker with 2 million barrels of oil sinks in Gulf — mine strike feared; Hormuz tension spikes

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard said a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels of crude oil sank in the Persian Gulf after striking what officials described as a mine. No group has claimed responsibility. The incident is the most significant maritime casualty in the Gulf since the Iran war began and has sent immediate shockwaves through oil freight markets. The tanker’s sinking adds to already elevated tension around the Strait of Hormuz, through which about 20% of globally traded oil passes.

L’Orient Today reported the development Sunday evening; the sinking has not been independently confirmed by US or European naval forces as of this morning. Iran still retains between 1,500 and 2,500 missiles according to a Wall Street Journal report published Saturday, reinforcing concerns that the conflict remains live and capable of escalation. If Hormuz shipping comes under further threat, oil prices could spike materially regardless of the Venezuela supply discussions.

Source: L’Orient Today (15 Nov); WSJ via L’Orient Today (15 Nov)

Why it matters to you

A Hormuz disruption spikes global Brent prices and tightens freight insurance. For Quasar Nigeria and QSS in Lagos, this means higher costs on any equipment shipped from Asia via the Gulf route, and potentially higher diesel prices in Nigeria that squeeze client operating budgets. It also validates the data-centre-as-critical-infrastructure pitch — clients with generator capacity become more valuable.

Security

Iran’s proxies drive anxiety across Europe — prosecutors link Tehran to security breaches in UK, Germany and the Netherlands

Prosecutors and government officials in Britain, Germany and the Netherlands are treating Iran or its proxies as responsible for a rising series of security incidents across Europe, the NYT reported from its November 15 morning newsletter. The pattern includes direct threats against individuals, infrastructure probes and criminal-network recruitment, all traceable to Iranian intelligence services. The incidents are described as part of a systematic campaign of intimidation that has “unsettled the region.”

Europol had flagged the trend as early as July 2026, warning that mounting pressure on Iran and its proxy networks could translate into more direct attacks inside the EU. France itself reported a suspected Iranian cyberattack on supermarket refrigeration and BMS systems in October. Germany’s intelligence chief said Iran relies on criminal networks to recruit operatives across Europe to plan and execute attacks. The campaign appears designed to signal Iranian reach without triggering a direct NATO Article 5 response.

Source: NYT (15 Nov); Europol report (Jul 2026); Reuters

Why it matters to you

The France BMS cyberattack precedent is directly relevant to Automation & Controls: a building management system is now proven to be a viable attack vector in the Iran conflict context. European clients in finance, retail and logistics are waking up to this risk. It is a genuine new sales angle for A&C’s cybersecure BMS and access-control offerings.

Conflict & Shipping

Yemen: Houthi forces seize key Bab al-Mandeb territory — government troops retreat in major setback for Red Sea shipping

Yemeni government forces announced Sunday they were withdrawing from the area near the Bab al-Mandeb Strait, the narrow chokepoint between the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden, in what they called a tactical retreat. Houthi forces have been pressing to consolidate control over the strait, through which about 10% of global trade passes. The withdrawal represents a significant strategic gain for the Houthis and raises the prospect of renewed interference with commercial shipping through the Red Sea corridor.

Red Sea shipping disruption first spiked in late 2023 and again in early 2026 during the Iran war. If the Houthis now fully command the strait, container freight rates between Asia and Europe — which route through the Red Sea or around the Cape of Good Hope as an alternative — could rise sharply again. West Africa-bound shipments are among the most affected when the Cape route is used, adding days and cost.

Source: L’Orient Today (15 Nov); Reuters

Why it matters to you

Equipment sourced from Asia for Quasar Nigeria and QSS projects typically routes through Suez/Red Sea or around the Cape. A renewed Houthi stranglehold at Bab al-Mandeb means Cape routing becomes mandatory again, adding 2–3 weeks and 15–25% freight costs to any Asia-sourced shipment into Lagos.

Act on this: QNG team to review outstanding purchase orders with Asian suppliers — order ahead if possible before freight rates spike.

Update: APEC Shenzhen summit opens Wednesday — Trump meets Xi (and possibly Putin) on Iran war, Taiwan, trade truce

The 33rd APEC leaders’ summit opens in Shenzhen in two days, bringing together Trump and Xi for what will be their third meeting this year. The Economist’s weekend edition flagged Taiwan, the Iran war and AI safety as the core agenda items. A possible three-way meeting with Putin is not confirmed but has been discussed. The US-China trade truce has been extended through January 10, 2027. Major trade deliverables are expected to wait for the December G20 in Miami, but Shenzhen will set the temperature.

Nvidia’s AI-chip export controls and the collapse of Jensen Huang’s personal relationships with Chinese partners add a volatile backdrop. The Economist noted that Nvidia earnings this week will be the first under interim CEO Colette Kress, with the company having promised 70% revenue growth in 2027 — targets now seen as highly uncertain. European leaders are not invited to Shenzhen, deepening frustration in Brussels.

Source: The Economist Today (15 Nov); SCMP; Reuters

Energy & Nigeria

Update: Trump claims US has “total control” of Venezuela’s oil — more cheap crude hits the market, Nigerian revenues under pressure

Trump told Fox News on the eve of the US midterms that the US has “total control” of Venezuela and more than half the world’s oil — claims that overstate the August deal but signal the administration’s intent to flood markets with discounted Venezuelan crude. Under that deal, the US secured partial control over Venezuela’s export sales, with 30–50 million barrels of sanctioned crude earmarked for US-aligned buyers. Venezuelan acting president Delcy Rodríguez pushed back publicly on Trump’s characterisation.

Nigeria is a direct loser from sustained Venezuelan crude entering global markets at discount prices: Nigerian medium crude and Venezuelan crude overlap in buyer demographics. Analysts note that if Venezuelan output grows materially, OPEC+ production discipline may be undermined and Brent could soften by $5–10 per barrel, compressing Nigeria’s fiscal revenues. Combined with the concurrent Iran supertanker incident, oil market volatility is likely to be high this week.

Source: NYT (15 Nov); Reuters; Latin Times; PBS NewsHour

Why it matters to you

Softer oil revenues tighten Nigeria’s federal budget, which delays infrastructure spending — the client pool for Quasar Smart Solutions. Projects dependent on government procurement or World Bank/AfDB co-financing become more vulnerable to slow payment when the oil price softens.

Security & Politics

US NSA Waltz lands in Beirut — presses for “a Lebanon free from Hezbollah’s influence” before UNIFIL exits December 31

US National Security Advisor Mike Waltz arrived in Beirut Sunday for high-level talks with the Lebanese government, according to L’Orient Today’s evening recap. The visit follows Waltz’s public announcement at the Israel Hayom Summit on November 11, where he described UNIFIL as “a human shield for terrorists” and said he would travel to Beirut “next week” to press for concrete changes. His central demand: the Lebanese army must visibly assert control over Hezbollah-held areas in the south before UNIFIL’s December 31 mandate expiry, or Lebanon faces serious diplomatic and potentially financial consequences.

The visit coincides with the Houthi advance at Bab al-Mandeb and the supertanker sinking, all of which feed Iran’s regional calculus. Hezbollah remains defiant; it called Lebanon’s participation in earlier US-brokered military talks a “great sin.” Waltz told Fox News earlier this month that “Trump has created the best chance in our lifetime to break Hezbollah’s grip on Lebanon.” How that pressure translates into action over the next 45 days will define Lebanon’s security trajectory into 2027.

Source: L’Orient Today (15 Nov); Fox News; Israel Hayom (11 Nov)

Why it matters to you

High-level US engagement is positive for Lebanon’s medium-term stability and reinforces the case for continued investment in Lebanese infrastructure. But the next 45 days carry binary risk: if Waltz’s pressure triggers Hezbollah escalation rather than compliance, security conditions in the south could deteriorate sharply. Watch the Lebanese army’s next move as the leading indicator.

Energy & Economy

PM Salam in Doha for Qatar electricity talks — gas and power transmission through Syria on the table

Prime Minister Nawaf Salam and Energy Minister Joe Saddi flew to Doha on Sunday for discussions with Qatari officials about proposed power-generation projects for Lebanon, L’Orient Today reported. The talks cover Qatari funding and gas supply for new generation capacity, with transmission envisaged through Syria via a route that uses existing Arab Gas Pipeline infrastructure. Damascus also reversed some visa restrictions this weekend in a sign of warming Syria-Lebanon relations that facilitates the energy corridor.

Lebanon’s chronic power crisis — averaging fewer than three hours of state electricity per day — remains the single greatest drag on economic productivity and business confidence. A Qatar deal could break the deadlock that has stalled both World Bank and EU-backed electricity proposals. The EU is also meeting Syria in Brussels this week to discuss investment and trade cooperation, suggesting a broader realignment around Syrian infrastructure is under way.

Source: L’Orient Today (15 Nov); Arab News

Why it matters to you

Reliable grid power in Lebanon is the single biggest unlock for A&C’s BMS and smart-building business: clients who currently over-specify generators and UPS because of grid unreliability will upgrade their entire building automation stack once hours of supply improve. This deal, if it closes, creates a multi-year wave of building retrofit opportunity.

Act on this: Track this deal actively — when Qatar commits, position A&C with a note to key developer and hospitality clients about the grid stability opportunity and what that means for their BMS strategy.

The $26 billion concurrent private placement by Nvidia, Google and Microsoft is a stabiliser, but retail demand will be the true test of market appetite at this valuation.

Source: The Economist Today (15 Nov); Reuters; Axios (10 Nov)
