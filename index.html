Update: Lebanon VAT Q3 deadline — Nov 30 is a MONDAY (correction to yesterday); effective cut-off is Monday 30 Nov

Yesterday’s brief stated that 30 November falls on a Sunday and suggested treating Friday 28 November as the effective VAT cut-off. This is a correction: 30 November 2026 is a Monday — a working day. Ministry of Finance Decisions 942/1 and 943/1 set the Q3 2026 VAT filing and payment deadline at 30 November 2026 inclusive, and that date is fully operational.

Banks are open on Monday 30 November. The next Wednesday bank strike is 2 December, which is after the deadline. This means: (1) there is no Sunday problem; (2) Monday 30 Nov is the real cut-off; (3) this week’s open days (Thursday 27, Friday 28) can be used for preparation, and Monday 30 is available for the actual filing and payment if needed. Acting early remains prudent — do not leave the payment to the morning of the 30th in case of system issues.

Direct impact · Lebanon · Import Costs

Finance Minister proposes exempting industrialists from environmental waste fee — draft covers raw materials (code 322) and industrial equipment (code 522)

Finance Minister Yassine Jaber has sent Parliament a draft decree that would exempt imports under customs codes 322 (raw materials already exempt from customs duties) and 522 (industrial equipment already exempt from customs duties) from the environmental waste fee. The fee — ranging from 1% to 3% — was introduced by cabinet decree in August 2026 and applies on a list of imported goods at customs clearance. Business associations strongly objected because the fee applied to industrial inputs, raising production costs for Lebanese manufacturers and importers.

The Association of Industrialists confirmed that Finance Minister Jaber consulted the sector before drafting the exemption. The measure is not yet law; Parliament must approve the decree. If enacted, importers of qualifying raw materials and industrial equipment would no longer pay the waste fee at customs. No implementation date has been announced. The existing Law 38/2026 (the waste-fee framework) remains in force pending any parliamentary amendment.

Sources: L’Orient Today; LBC Lebanon; Lexis Middle East (Aug 2026); Forvis Mazars

Why it matters to you

Quasar, Automation & Controls and FGC import fire alarm panels, suppression equipment, BMS hardware and low-current systems. If those goods are classified under customs codes 322 or 522, the pending exemption would remove the 1–3% waste fee from future import invoices. Not yet law, but worth monitoring closely.

Act on this: Ask Joelle and the customs broker to check whether Group imports are classified under codes 322 or 522. If yes, flag the pending parliamentary vote and monitor for enactment.

Direct impact · Nigeria · Tax

Nigeria NRS e-invoicing enforcement begins January 2027 for medium taxpayers — QSS must verify compliance now; six weeks to deadline

The Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS) published its phased e-invoicing and Electronic Fiscal System (EFS) implementation schedule in February 2026. Medium taxpayers — annual turnover between ₦1 billion and ₦5 billion — went live on the NRS Merchant Buyer Solution (MBS) platform in July 2026 and face enforcement from January 2027. The post-go-live review window is October–November 2026 (the current period); penalty notices are expected from January 2027 onwards. Companies in this bracket that are not registered on MBS and not issuing electronic invoices by January 2027 risk fines.

Large taxpayers (above ₦5 billion turnover) are already in the enforcement phase since Q2 2026. Smaller taxpayers (below ₦1 billion) have a later schedule: stakeholder engagement Q1 2027, go-live July 2027, enforcement from January 2028. Registration requires a valid Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) on the NRS portal. Sources: NRS Public Notice (Feb 17, 2026); KPMG Nigeria; Forvis Mazars Nigeria; Nairametrics.

Sources: NRS (Feb 2026); KPMG Nigeria (Feb 2026); Forvis Mazars Nigeria; Nairametrics

World · US · Thanksgiving

Macy’s Thanksgiving parade postponed in first 100-year change — 58 mph gusts ground the balloons; moves to Saturday

For the first time in its 100-year history, the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade has been postponed to another day. The announcement was made yesterday afternoon as the parade’s 700-pound helium balloons were being inflated, citing extreme weather: New York City is under a rare high-wind warning today with gusts expected at 58 mph and sustained winds of 40 mph. City regulations prohibit the giant balloons from flying if sustained winds exceed 23 mph. The procession — the most-watched US television entertainment program on Thanksgiving — will now take place on Saturday 28 November.

Much of the US East Coast faces the same storm: high-wind warnings extend across New Jersey, Maryland, Delaware and eastern Pennsylvania. The last time wind grounded the balloons was 2013, when they were forced to fly so low they scraped the pavement; the last major change to the parade format was in 1971. It was cancelled entirely from 1942 to 1945 due to a wartime helium shortage. This year was the centennial parade, for which organisers had been preparing for five years.

Sources: NYT The Evening (Nov 25, 2026); NYT Today’s Headlines (Nov 25)

Trump unveils sweeping new tariffs on China, Japan, EU — latest attempt after Supreme Court struck down earlier round

The Trump administration published a new round of proposed tariff schedules on Wednesday targeting imports from dozens of countries including China, Japan and all EU member states. The administration framed the action as a response to “unfair trade practices.” The proposals will go through a public feedback period before taking effect. Specific rates were not finalised in the feedback-period publication. The EU had already indicated it would respond symmetrically to any new US tariff action. For companies importing US goods or selling into the US market, the feedback window is the last opportunity to influence the final rate schedule.

Sources: NYT The Evening (Nov 25, 2026); Reuters; AP

World · China Politics

Shanghai party boss Chen Jining purged from Xi Jinping’s top team — sudden, high-profile removal

The Economist’s Wednesday newsletter reports that Chen Jining, the Communist Party secretary of Shanghai and Politburo member, has been removed from his posts. The newsletter describes it as one of the most sudden and high-profile purges of a senior official in years. Chen’s removal follows Xi Jinping’s established pattern of dismissing officials seen as building independent political networks. Shanghai is China’s most important commercial city and its party secretary is among the most powerful posts below the Politburo Standing Committee’s top seven.

Xi has purged multiple senior officials across 2026, including PLA generals and financial regulators. Western analysts note that the rapid pace of purges is slowing institutional decision-making as officials become risk-averse about proposing anything that could be construed as disloyalty. The removal of a Shanghai chief is especially significant given the city’s role in China’s outward financial and commercial links.

Sources: The Economist Today (Nov 25, 2026); SCMP; Semafor (Jul 2026)

Why it matters to you

Continued senior purges in China signal a risk-averse bureaucracy and slower project approvals, which affects global infrastructure supply chains that run through Shanghai. World · Technology · Legal

Huawei convicted of stealing trade secrets in US federal trial — major DoJ victory in long-running case

A federal jury in New York found Huawei Technologies guilty of stealing trade secrets from US companies in one of the most significant corporate espionage verdicts in recent years. Federal prosecutors described the theft as “brazen” and said it formed part of a systematic effort to acquire US technology without authorisation. The case had been in progress for several years; the verdict represents both a symbolic and legal landmark given Huawei’s size and its central role in global 5G and telecommunications infrastructure.

The verdict is likely to deepen existing US export control restrictions on Huawei components and may influence other governments’ decisions on Huawei’s role in national telecommunications networks. It also signals continued DoJ appetite for high-profile technology-theft prosecutions against Chinese firms.

Sources: NYT Today’s Headlines (Nov 25, 2026); Bloomberg Law
