Middle East – Security

Iran in “advanced stages” of planning attacks on Americans; US orders Saudi Arabia diplomatic evacuation

The US government has issued a rare order for diplomats and other federal employees to leave Saudi Arabia immediately, citing intelligence that Iran is in “advanced stages” of planning attacks against Americans in the region. US staff are also being drawn down at other missions across the Middle East, including in Jordan and Kuwait. The warning follows a sharp escalation in Houthi attacks on Saudi infrastructure and growing Iranian signalling ahead of the Israeli election on October 27.

The evacuation is among the most significant US diplomatic retreats from the Arabian Peninsula in years. Analysts note the timing—just days before the Israeli vote—as potentially coordinated to deter an Israeli strike on Iran or, alternatively, to reduce exposure ahead of one. Oil markets, already at $116 after Thursday’s Houthi strikes, will be sensitive to any further escalation signal.

US Politics

DOJ sues to pre-emptively block Alaska Senate result on Trump’s orders

Acting on President Trump’s instructions, the Justice Department filed an unprecedented pre-election lawsuit seeking to prevent certification of Alaska’s Senate race if the Republican incumbent narrowly loses. The department argued that confusion over two candidates with similar names could taint the result. Legal experts say there is no established legal authority for such pre-emptive intervention, and the move is drawing wide condemnation as a direct assault on election administration.

The same day, the DOJ dispatched letters to California counties demanding they help identify non-citizen voters or “risk significant federal scrutiny.” The two actions together signal an acceleration of the administration’s willingness to use federal law enforcement to shape election outcomes ahead of Tuesday’s midterms.

Brazil – Update

Update: Brazil presidential runoff today — Bolsonaro vs Lula in the decisive vote

Brazilians are voting today in the presidential runoff between Flávio Bolsonaro (47.0% in the October 4 first round) and former President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva (45.2%). Since the first round, right-wing consolidation has moved Lula into underdog territory. Polls show the race within the margin of error, with Bolsonaro carrying a narrow structural advantage among evangelical and rural voters. Results are expected Sunday evening Brasília time.

The outcome shapes Latin America’s political direction for four years: a Bolsonaro win would align Brazil with Trump’s worldview on trade, climate and security; a Lula win would re-engage with multilateral institutions and likely push back on tariffs. Either way, Brazil’s massive commodity export machine continues regardless of the winner.

Source: Reuters; The Economist (24 Oct 2026)

Africa – Humanitarian

Sudan’s last doctor: keeping a hospital alive as a genocidal militia closes in on el-Fasher

The Economist profiles the last doctor fighting to maintain a functioning hospital in el-Fasher—the sole remaining major city in Darfur not under control of the Rapid Support Forces militia. El-Fasher’s fall would complete the RSF’s dominance of an entire region and, aid workers warn, trigger mass atrocities on a scale not yet seen in this conflict. International access has been severed; the UN and multiple NGOs have described conditions meeting the threshold definition of genocide.

Sudan’s civil war is now well into its second year with no ceasefire in sight. The story is a marker of how completely the international community’s attention has been displaced by other crises: the war barely features in Western media despite its scale.

Source: The Economist (24 Oct 2026)

Israel – Update

Update: Israel votes Tuesday — Arab bloc poised to determine the next prime minister

Israel’s election is two days away and no bloc is near the 61-seat majority needed to form a government. Eisenkot’s centrist Yashar party leads at around 22 projected seats; Netanyahu’s Likud trails at roughly 21. A Supreme Court ruling in early October overturned the disqualification of Arab party candidates, meaning Arab parties will almost certainly hold kingmaker status for the first time in Israeli political history. No government will be formed without their support or abstention.

The election outcome will directly shape whether Washington-mediated Lebanon-Israel ceasefire talks continue, and whether any US-backed military action against Iran is authorised. A Netanyahu government would likely move more quickly on Iran; an Eisenkot government would be more cautious on both fronts.

Source: Al-Monitor; Al-Jazeera; Reuters

Why it matters to you

Lebanon’s reconstruction environment depends entirely on who forms the next Israeli government and whether the ceasefire holds. An Eisenkot government would likely continue talks; a Netanyahu return would introduce fresh uncertainty. Your Lebanese clients will be watching Tuesday night closely.

France – Economy

Moody’s spares France’s credit rating — but issues a sharp debt warning

Moody’s held France’s sovereign credit rating steady on Friday but delivered an unusually blunt warning: rising borrowing costs, political paralysis and an expanding deficit mean a future downgrade is plausible unless structural reforms are delivered. The agency cited the Lecornu government’s inability to pass a credible budget and the coming November 5 general strike as compounding factors. French 10-year spreads over German bunds have widened significantly this month.

The decision to hold was described by analysts as a reprieve rather than a clean bill of health. With CGT, FO and CFDT all confirmed for the November 5 strike and major rail services set to be paralysed, the political environment that Moody’s cited as a risk shows no signs of improving before the next review.

Source: The New York Times (24 Oct 2026)

Iran’s planned attacks on Americans put Lebanon ceasefire talks at acute risk

The US evacuation of diplomatic personnel from Saudi Arabia and warnings of advanced Iranian attack planning arrive at the most fragile moment for Lebanon’s ceasefire. Lebanon itself would face a renewed security premium on all investment.
