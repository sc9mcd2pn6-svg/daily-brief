🌍 World
⚓Iran — Hormuz Strikes

Iran fires on “hostile targets” in the Strait; Brent climbs to $82 as US strike risk sharpens

Iran attacked what it described as “hostile targets” in the Strait of Hormuz over the weekend, pushing Brent crude from $78 to $82 a barrel as markets priced in renewed escalation risk. The strikes came as the Iran–Oman framework talks remain deadlocked: Tehran insists US and Israeli vessels be excluded and “hostile nations” pay transit fees, while Washington demands unconditional reopening. Trump renewed his threat of a decisive military response if the strait is not fully reopened on US terms. Bloomberg and Seeking Alpha report WTI rose above $78 and Brent settled near $82, with analysts flagging continued high sensitivity to any further escalation — a US counterstrike could spike prices to $95+.

Bloomberg · Al Jazeera · CNBC · Seeking Alpha
Why it matters to you

Oil at $82 and rising makes yesterday’s $78 assumptions obsolete for any contract priced this week — both Lebanon and Nigeria import nearly all fire suppression and security equipment, so a $10 Brent move lands directly on your project costs. Add an explicit oil-escalation clause to any Q4 deliverable priced today, and if a US counterstrike follows, $95+ is a 72-hour scenario that makes the Q4 cost base even harder to defend.

🤖US–China — Pre-Summit Escalation

Beijing bars seven US firms and tightens drone export controls as Xi’s September Washington summit looms

China unveiled its broadest countermeasures package since last October on August 6, barring seven American companies from doing business with Chinese entities, tightening drone and optical-module export controls, and prohibiting certain cross-border tech transactions. The moves respond directly to FCC restrictions on Chinese drone models and power routers added to its “Covered List.” CNBC reports Beijing is replicating Washington’s tech-war playbook to build leverage before Xi Jinping’s expected September Washington visit. CSIS analysts say the May summit stabilised relations but left AI, robotics and advanced manufacturing fundamentally contested. Markets are pricing the September summit as a release valve — but each week that passes adds more pressure to release.

CNBC · South China Morning Post · CSIS

🌍 Africa & Nigeria
🏗️Lagos · Data Centres

Digital Realty opens third Lagos data centre LKK2 in Lekki, tapping the 2Africa subsea cable; OADC $240m Tier III facility advancing

Digital Realty has opened LKK2, its third Lagos data centre, in Lekki — delivering nearly 2MW of installed IT capacity across 13,000 sq ft of data hall space and interconnected with its existing LKK1 2Africa subsea cable landing station on Victoria Island. The facility adds a key node on West Africa’s international connectivity backbone, interconnecting with LOS1 and LOS2 on Victoria Island. Separately, Open Access Data Centres’ $240 million, 24MW Tier III facility — OADC Lagos — is advancing as the most client-centric data centre in West Africa and the first pan-African facility certified by the Uptime Institute. Nigeria now has 28 operational or under-construction data centres, with Lagos holding nearly 30% of national market share.

Data Centre Dynamics · Ecofin Agency · Data Centre Magazine · TechNext24
Why it matters to you

Two major Lagos projects — Digital Realty LKK2 in active commissioning and OADC’s $240m build in fit-out — are at the procurement window for fire suppression, security and CCTV right now. 📊Nigeria · Economy

Nigeria back to Africa’s third-largest economy at $334bn; inflation halved to 15.4%, CBN cuts rates — but Hormuz oil spike threatens the recovery

Nigeria’s GDP is projected to grow 4.3% in 2026, supported by oil production gains and a recovering services sector. Inflation has more than halved from 34% at end-2024 to 15.38%, enabling the Central Bank of Nigeria to cut its Monetary Policy Rate from 27.5% to 26.5% in May — the most significant macro improvement in two years. The IMF confirms Nigeria has returned to Africa’s third-largest economy position at $334bn GDP. The key downside risk is this weekend’s Iranian Hormuz strikes: any sustained oil price above $85 will rekindle import inflation through fuel and logistics costs, potentially reversing the CBN’s hard-won naira stability of the past 18 months.

IMF · AfDB · PwC Nigeria · CNBC Africa
Why it matters to you

The macro backdrop for Quasar Nigeria is the best it has been in two years — easing costs, a rate cut, and a recovering client base. The Hormuz spike is the main near-term threat: if Brent stays above $85, watch for naira pressure and renewed import-cost inflation on your equipment. Price any large Q4 Nigeria project in dollars, not naira, and check your key suppliers’ FX hedging status now.

🕊️Security — IDF Strikes South

IDF strikes Zawtar al-Sharqiyah and Tyre district this weekend; Rome talks deadlocked; ceasefire fraying on the ground

Israeli forces struck Zawtar al-Sharqiyah in southern Lebanon and shelled the Mashaa al-Mansouri area in the Tyre district over the weekend, with Al Mayadeen reporting explosions in several towns despite the April 2026 ceasefire framework. The attacks follow last week’s IED incident in Majdal Zoun that killed two Israeli reservists. The seventh round of US-mediated Rome talks remains deadlocked over the Ali al-Taher Ridge tunnel complex: Israel demands full Hezbollah removal; Lebanon’s army-transfer proposal has been rejected. Global Security sources confirm IDF raids on Lebanon are now a near-daily pattern, keeping the ceasefire formally intact but operationally hollow.

Global Security · Al Jazeera · Security Council Report · Al Mayadeen
