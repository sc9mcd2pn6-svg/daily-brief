Geopolitics

Update: Trump meets Xi and Putin in Shenzhen — first-ever trilateral; Economist says the summit will shrink America’s standing

The APEC leaders’ summit opened in Shenzhen today (Wednesday), with the historic first Trump–Xi–Putin three-way meeting happening on the sidelines. Washington says the agenda centres on a new nuclear non-proliferation framework; Iran war de-escalation and AI chips are also on the table. A senior US official told NBC “no agreement is anticipated” — this is described as an “entry point.” Beijing called it a meeting of “groundbreaking significance.”

The Economist’s lead Telegram column argues the optics of Trump sharing a stage with Putin in China will confirm to allies that US leadership is in structural retreat. CNN analysis adds that the meeting “may ultimately yield few tangible outcomes” while giving Xi a home-court diplomatic win. Inside the US delegation, Lutnick and Bessent are openly at odds over chip-export enforcement — a split that China is reading closely.

Sources: Al Jazeera (14 Nov); NBC News; CNN; The Economist (17 Nov)

Public Health

US stripped of measles elimination status after 26 years — 7,700 cases, 10 deaths; RFK Jr.’s response judged inadequate

The Pan American Health Organization officially revoked the United States’ measles elimination status on Tuesday — the first time the designation has been lost since it was awarded in 2000. The US has recorded more than 7,700 measles cases since the resurgence began in Texas in early 2025, with 10 confirmed deaths. Vaccination rates now sit well below the 95% herd-immunity threshold, having declined throughout the COVID-19 pandemic and been left unaddressed under Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.

Experts say the federal response was too slow to suppress outbreaks when they were small. Canada has also recently lost its elimination status. The NYT Evening described it as “a stunning step back” to an era several decades ago when measles infected nearly every child in the US. The CDC noted that some pregnant women and newborns are among the most severely affected.

Sources: NYT Evening (17 Nov); Al Jazeera (14 Nov); WUNC/NPR (14 Nov)

Ukraine War

Ukraine significantly damages Kerch Bridge in overnight strike — large section collapses; Russia’s Crimea supply route hit

Ukraine launched an overnight strike on the Kerch Strait Bridge — the 12-mile crossing linking mainland Russia to occupied Crimea — causing significant structural damage confirmed by independent video analysis. President Zelensky said the attack required “several weeks of systematically destroying nearby air defenses” first. The bridge is among the most heavily protected pieces of infrastructure in the conflict; it has been a prime Ukrainian target since Russia used Crimea as a forward base in its 2022 invasion.

The strike comes as Trump is heading to Shenzhen and signalling he wants to bring the war to a close by mid-2027, but Russian drones kept firing during the latest round of Abu Dhabi talks, and Moscow this week recirculated its hard-line 2022 demands for Ukrainian neutrality and territorial concessions. A large bridge section collapse would meaningfully disrupt Russian military logistics to Crimea.

Sources: NYT Evening (17 Nov); Denver7/AP; Euromaidanpress

Tech Business

Update: Nvidia posts first-ever $100B quarter — board rules out Jensen Huang return; daughter still at firm, unsettling interim CEO

Nvidia reported quarterly revenue topping $100 billion for the first time ever on Tuesday — more than double the year-ago period — in what may prove the final testament to outgoing CEO Jensen Huang’s era. The board chair said publicly there are no plans to bring Huang back, even after Huang told reporters he would return if asked. Meanwhile, Huang’s daughter, installed as his de facto chief of staff, remains in a senior role and has alienated multiple top executives, posing a governance test for interim CEO Colette Kress.

Markets initially rallied on the earnings beat in after-hours trading. The AI infrastructure buildout Huang orchestrated continues to generate staggering demand — and the scandal has not dented it. California is separately suing OpenAI over AI safety concerns, adding to the regulatory pressure on the broader sector.

Sources: NYT Evening (17 Nov); NYT Today’s Headlines (17 Nov)

Cybersecurity

AI discovers critical Java zero-day — as NSA network, Chase Bank, and air traffic control all fail in the same week

Researchers at a California start-up disclosed on Tuesday that they used an OpenAI model to identify a critical vulnerability in Java — a language one expert called “the beating heart of the modern economy” — that could have allowed hackers to gain remote access to sensitive internal systems at scale. The discovery was made as a remarkable run of infrastructure failures struck the US: the NSA’s classified network went offline (explanation pending), Chase Bank suffered a software bug disrupting customer transactions, United Airlines grounded planes following a nationwide FAA ground-stop last week, and Microsoft’s M365/Azure suffered a 2-day global outage affecting 850 million users.

Officials say each of the recent outages appears to have a benign technical explanation rather than a coordinated attack. But the NYT noted the confluence is feeding “a sense of doom” about the systems underpinning daily life — a concern that security and intelligence services take seriously regardless of attribution.

Sources: NYT Evening (17 Nov); NYT Today’s Headlines (17 Nov); Upstox

Nigeria Health

Update: WHO formally rejects “Ebola-free” claims — December 7 is the earliest Nigeria can be officially declared clear

The World Health Organization issued a public statement calling media reports of Nigeria being declared Ebola-free “inaccurate and misleading.” WHO confirmed it has not made any such declaration, and that the official 42-day countdown from the last confirmed case has not been completed. Health Minister Muhammad Ali Pate has said December 7 is the target date if no further cases emerge. As of November 9, Nigeria had one confirmed imported Bundibugyo Virus Disease case — the patient evacuated to Switzerland is stable — with no secondary transmission.

Contact tracing was completed on November 13, and the NCDC raised importation-risk alerts for travellers from high-risk countries. The outbreak remains classified as open; the December 7 Ebola-free declaration requires WHO sign-off under international protocols. Premium Times and WHO tracked this closely.

Sources: WHO statement; Sahara Reporters (28 Oct); Premium Times; Africanews/Reuters (13 Nov)

Why it matters to you

The Lagos outbreak remains formally open until December 7 at the earliest. Business travel and site visits to Nigeria carry a residual health protocol requirement — keep the QNG team briefed and check whether any planned visits need to account for entry screening procedures.

Africa Tech

Africa’s solar revolution is powering its data-centre boom — The Economist highlights a vast and fast buildout reshaping the continent

The Economist’s November 17 edition spotlights Africa’s accelerating solar energy revolution, calling it “vast and speedy” and noting that in Zimbabwe and across much of Africa and Asia, the signs of an energy revolution are everywhere even if the full scale is hard to assess. The solar buildout is increasingly tied directly to data-centre demand: South Africa’s Teraco began construction of a 120 MW utility-scale solar plant on November 12 to power its data centres, while Algeria commissioned 1.48 GW of solar specifically to power a planned data-centre cluster — with first PPAs expected H2 2026.

In Lagos, Africa Data Centres approved a $240 million investment to expand its facility to 24 MW by 2027; Airtel Nigeria’s 38 MW Eko Atlantic hyperscale data centre remains under construction. Lagos State is targeting 250 MW of data-centre capacity by 2030 and has flagged 146 MW in the pipeline. The solar-data-centre nexus is the defining infrastructure story in sub-Saharan Africa right now.

Sources: The Economist (17 Nov); Africa Sustainability Matters (12 Nov); ITWeb Africa; Nairametrics; IT Edge News

Why it matters to you

Every new data centre in Lagos — powered by on-site or dedicated solar — is a potential contract for Quasar Nigeria: fire suppression, gas detection, BMS, CCTV and access control. The Airtel/Eko Atlantic site and Africa Data Centres’ expansion are the two most concrete near-term targets.

Lebanon Economy

IMF mission: more banking reform work needed — Financial Gap Law still stalled; banks ask for cooperation as next steps stay unclear

Following the November 11–13 IMF staff visit to Beirut, the fund concluded that “further work is needed to reach an agreement on a strategy for an effective and durable restructuring” of Lebanon’s banking sector. The mission chief, Ernesto Ramirez Rigo, cited progress on the banking restructuring strategy but stopped short of announcing a staff-level agreement or new programme. The World Bank has the economy contracting 6.4% in 2026; IMF programme access — unlocking financing — remains tied to passing the Financial Gap Law, which Parliament deadlocked over for the third time before the mission arrived.

The Association of Banks in Lebanon has called for “cooperation between all parties” to address the crisis. The Constitutional Council challenge to parts of the existing bank resolution law adds another layer of legal uncertainty. L’Orient Today reported Lebanon’s Eurobond prices are “holding firm despite security concerns and lack of reforms” — a sign of some market patience, but that patience is not limitless.

Sources: The National (12–13 Nov); Asharq Al-Awsat; LBC Group; L’Orient Today

No IMF deal means no fresh dollar inflows into the banking system this quarter. For the Fakhry Group, this sustains the client-payment squeeze — expect continued pressure on USD-denominated contracts and slow collections from clients who rely on bank credit. Dollar-pricing discipline is essential.

Lebanon Security

Update: UNIFIL 43 days from mandate expiry — Hezbollah disarmament unresolved; EU security mission planning underway

The December 31 expiry of UNIFIL’s mandate is 43 days away. UNIFIL peacekeepers were fired upon in south Lebanon on November 11 (they returned fire and the incident ended without casualties). US NSA Mike Waltz visited Beirut on November 16, pressing Lebanese officials to ensure Hezbollah disarmament before UNIFIL withdraws. G7 foreign ministers have pledged no security void, and the EU Security Mission for Lebanon (EUSML) has been activated as the planned successor force.

The Lebanese Army remains overstretched and under-equipped for a south Lebanon deployment at scale. The ceasefire framework signed with Israel in June 2026 requires Hezbollah to withdraw north of the Litani River, but the timetable is under severe strain. Israel is reportedly “weighing options” on the border geometry if the LAF cannot fill the vacuum by year-end. The Jakarta Post (single-sourced) reported the November 11 patrol attack; al-Monitor confirms the broader tension pattern.

Sources: Jakarta Post (11 Nov); al-Monitor; Reuters; G7 foreign ministers’ communiqué

Film

Robert Pattinson confirmed as next James Bond — a franchise worth $23 billion picks a specialist in “petulant losers”

EON Productions confirmed Tuesday that Robert Pattinson will play James Bond, ending years of speculation following Daniel Craig’s five-film run. The NYT’s arts desk called it a surprising choice: Pattinson is known for playing “damaged, petulant losers” in films like The Batman, The Lighthouse and Good Time — a far cry from the suave archetype. The Economist ran a full piece titled “Good evening, Mr Bond. We’ve been expecting you,” noting the choice is a deliberately counter-intuitive one that may revitalize a franchise whose last entry underperformed. The 25 Bond films have grossed $23 billion adjusted for inflation.

No production start date or plot details have been announced. Pattinson, 40, is currently finishing post-production on a Christopher Nolan film.

Sources: NYT Today’s Headlines (17 Nov); The Economist (17 Nov)
