⚓ World · Oil / Red Sea (Update)

Update: Houthis Seize Mocha Port Near Bab el-Mandeb; Oil Surges Above $108 — Red Sea Now Exposed

Houthi forces captured the port city of Mocha in Yemen on Thursday, tightening their strategic hold over the Bab el-Mandeb strait — one of the world’s most critical oil and goods chokepoints. Brent crude surged above $108 per barrel, the highest since the Iran war began in February, as markets absorbed the news alongside IRGC naval attacks on two US Navy vessels and multiple oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. Saudi Arabia’s oil exports have fallen to a 13-year low as the kingdom struggles to route shipments safely. US gas prices averaged $4.22 per gallon on Thursday, their highest since early June, with the biggest single-day pump-price increase since May according to AAA.

The White House has not publicly acknowledged the maritime escalation. The NYT reports Pentagon officials are privately alarmed by the widening front at sea; alongside the munitions crisis revealed in the Vance story below, the military picture is deteriorating on multiple vectors simultaneously.

The National News · Washington Times · NYT (anchor) · Reuters

Oil at $108 with both Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb under pressure means Lebanon’s generator-diesel bill and Nigeria’s industrial energy costs stay at crisis levels. If the Levant land-trade corridor strengthens further as sea routes close, regional logistics infrastructure — including smart buildings and security systems along those routes — becomes strategically more valuable.

🔒 World · Iran War

Vance Secretly Polled Generals on Iran War; Their Assessment Was Sobering — Munitions at Record Lows

Vice President JD Vance spent spring and summer directly calling US military commanders in the Middle East, Europe and Asia — an unusual step that bypassed the normal chain of reporting — seeking unfiltered views of the Iran war. The New York Times reports the commanders told Vance that US stocks of Patriot interceptors and THAAD missiles are at the lowest levels commanders have ever seen: roughly 80% of the pre-war THAAD inventory has been expended, and one theater was down to fewer than 100 Patriot interceptors by late spring. Iran, they said, appears willing to endure “an extraordinary amount of pain” to survive.

Vance, who opposed the war from the outset, incorporated those concerns into private talks with Trump. The commanders’ assessment helps explain why the US has not escalated to a broader air campaign despite Iranian provocations: the munitions stockpile may not support it. The gap between the official narrative and battlefield reality is now, via Vance’s back-channel, part of the White House’s internal reckoning.

💸 World · US Economy / Politics

Trump’s $5,000 “Trump Dividend” Pledge Triggers Bond Market Revolt — 30-Year Yield Hits 30-Year High

At the Republican midterm convention in Dallas on Wednesday, President Trump promised every American adult a $5,000 cash payment if his party keeps control of Congress in November — a plan costing roughly $1.3 trillion. The bond market responded immediately: the 30-year Treasury yield rose to 5.35%, a fresh 30-year high, and the 10-year yield jumped 9 basis points to 4.92%, its highest level in three years. Some Republican fiscal hawks publicly called it a “socialist-style giveaway.” The Economist’s Lexington column argues the convention reveals how completely Trump has hollowed out the GOP — leaving Republicans little choice but to run as an extension of him despite his personal unpopularity. VP Vance headlines the convention’s final night on Thursday ahead of an expected 2028 presidential run.

Separately, the NYT disclosed that Trump’s special envoy Steve Witkoff earned at least $257 million last year, including from a holding company invested in the Trump family’s crypto business. The Treasury’s simultaneous $6 billion debt-buyback plan was rebuffed by bond investors, with the 10-year yield rising rather than falling on the announcement.

🤝 World · Geopolitics

Update: BRICS Summit Opens New Delhi Today — Xi, Putin, Modi Together as Iran War Tests Bloc Unity

The 18th BRICS Leaders’ Summit opened Friday at Bharat Mandapam in New Delhi, bringing together the leaders of Brazil, China, Egypt, Ethiopia, India, Indonesia, Iran, Russia, South Africa and the UAE. Modi is holding bilaterals with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin. The bloc faces unprecedented internal strain: Iran and UAE are both members, yet are on opposite sides of the regional conflict, and Saudi Arabia — a BRICS candidate partner — is now actively striking Iranian militias in Iraq. India is seeking consensus on West Asia and pushing its chairship motto of “Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation, Sustainability,” but the gap between bloc members’ interests is wider than at any prior summit.

30,000 security personnel are deployed across New Delhi, with 35 roads under regulation. The summit’s most-watched outcome will be whether the joint communiqué can produce a coherent statement on the Iran war — or whether the bloc fractures publicly along pro-Tehran and pro-Gulf lines.

🧬 World · AI / Bio-Risk

Anthropic Reveals It Blocked Five Scientists From Using AI to Develop Biological Weapons

AI company Anthropic disclosed Thursday that between December 2025 and August 2026 it blocked five separate cases where scientists used its Claude models in ways that could have assisted biological weapons research. Cases involved gain-of-function research on chikungunya and avian influenza, orthopoxvirus studies (including smallpox relatives), and venom toxin analysis. Anthropic said it could not always determine whether intent was malicious or legitimate, but erred on the side of caution given the catastrophic downside risk. The disclosure is the first by any major AI lab of intercepting specifically bioweapons-adjacent queries at scale.

The announcement coincides with a separate NYT report that a group of more than 800 former EPA officials warned data-centre power consumption is adding billions in air-pollution-related public health costs. The Economist’s newsletter this week also flags growing alarm among Anthropic researchers about the pace of AI acceleration — echoing similar warnings from other labs.

⛽ Africa · Nigeria Energy

Nigeria Diesel Tops ₦2,000/Litre in Industrial Zones — Manufacturers Losing Half Their Revenue to Energy Costs

Diesel prices in Nigerian industrial areas have crossed ₦2,000 per litre as global oil above $108 feeds through to domestic prices, according to Guardian Nigeria and Premium Times. The Manufacturers Association of Nigeria warns that energy costs now exceed 50% of operating expenses at dozens of factories in Lagos and Kano — a level that makes many facilities economically unviable. Several manufacturers have indicated they may reduce or halt production before year-end if the situation does not improve. The underlying problem is structural: an unreliable national grid forces business to self-generate on diesel, turning every global oil spike into a local existential cost.

Opposition leader Atiku Abubakar simultaneously challenged President Tinubu to justify new external borrowing given recent revenue gains, as the government pursues a Vienna-listed Eurobond. The oil-cost squeeze is hitting precisely the sectors — manufacturing, tech, data — meant to drive the next growth phase.

Guardian Nigeria · Premium Times (independent) · BusinessDay NG

🏛️ Lebanon · Banking / IMF

Lebanon’s Deposit Recovery Law Still Stalled — Last Key Blocker for IMF Deal as Pressure Mounts

Lebanon’s Parliament has yet to pass the Deposit Recovery Law (the Gap Law), which together with the bank restructuring law enacted in August is the final legislative prerequisite for a formal IMF programme and Eurobond restructuring, according to L’Orient Today and BLOMINVEST. Banks are objecting to how losses would be allocated among shareholders, the state, and depositors — a politically explosive question given the $80-billion-plus hole in the financial system. Without the law, Lebanon cannot unlock IMF credit lines, cannot restructure its Eurobond pile, and cannot formally advance the Alvarez & Marsal BDL forensic audit to its next phase.

Goldman Sachs reiterated this week that Lebanese Eurobonds remain overvalued at current BBI levels (~29.7 cents on the dollar) given this legislative gap. Oil at $108 compounds the pressure by enlarging Lebanon’s energy import bill. With the UNIFIL transition creating a security vacuum in the south and the Israeli strikes continuing, the window for an orderly reform sequence is narrowing every week.

L’Orient Today (independent) · BLOMINVEST · Reuters
Why it matters to you

Until the Gap Law passes, Lebanese banks remain paralysed for project finance — meaning large capital projects that local clients want to fund via the banking system will not happen. Clients who are “waiting for things to normalise” before signing a major fire-suppression or BMS contract are waiting for something still many months away. Price that delay into your Lebanon pipeline assumptions for 2026-27.

📉 France · Economy

INSEE Cuts France 2026 Growth Forecast to 0.4% — Oil Shock, Heatwaves and Fiscal Drag Converge

France’s national statistics institute INSEE revised its 2026 GDP growth forecast sharply downward to 0.4%, from a prior 0.7%, citing a compounding set of pressures: an oil-price shock squeezing energy-intensive sectors, repeated September heatwaves disrupting construction and outdoor work, a slowdown in public-works spending tied to the municipal election cycle, and softer consumer confidence. The labour market is expected to weaken through Q4. INSEE stops short of forecasting recession but the margin for error is thin. France’s growth trajectory is now one of the weakest among major eurozone economies.

The political backdrop: France holds the UN Security Council presidency this September with Lebanon and the Iran war atop the agenda, and the government is managing simultaneous pressure from hospital workers and firefighters who mobilised in strikes last week. Paris Design Week (Sept 10–19) brings international cultural attention to the capital.
