Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles land in SE Poland — NATO calls ‘grave violation,’ Rutte flies to Warsaw

Two Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles and a suspected drone came down in south-east Poland near Nowa Deba overnight on 21 November, during a period when European military drills were taking place in the region. Poland’s prime minister described the incident as a likely deliberate provocation. NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte flew immediately to Warsaw and called it a “grave violation” of Polish airspace. No injuries or significant damage were reported, and both missiles have been confirmed as Russian-made.

Poland has formally requested NATO emergency consultations and additional air-defence assets. Russia has not commented. NATO has not invoked Article 5, though Article 4 consultations are underway. The incident echoes November 2022, when debris fell in Poland (later attributed to Ukrainian air-defence). Unlike that event, these missiles have been confirmed as Russian military hardware. A UN Security Council emergency session has been called. This is the most direct incursion into NATO territory since the 2022 conflict began.

Sources: AP (via PBS); Manila Times; Latin Times (21 Nov 2026)

Climate / Global

COP31 Antalya climate talks limp into overtime — draft drops all fossil-fuel exit language; 23 nations push back

The COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, ran past its Friday deadline without agreement after a revised draft text, released on 19 November, eliminated all commitments to transition away from fossil fuels, replacing phaseout language with carbon capture and storage (CCUS) as the central strategy. Saudi Arabia and India backed the CCUS framework. A bloc of 23 nations including Ireland, Norway and the European Union demanded the fossil-fuel language be restored, calling the draft “a fossil summit.” The United States is absent for the second year running.

COP31 president Murat Kurum defended the shift, arguing that the COP28 Global Stocktake already embedded fossil-fuel transition commitments. Turkey said it has agreed bilateral deals worth over $26 billion on the sidelines. ABC News quoted observers calling this potentially the “worst COP ever” if no strengthening occurs. Talks resumed Saturday; no final outcome was available at the time of publication. The $120 billion adaptation finance target for developing countries was also dropped from the draft.

Sources: Al Jazeera; ABC News; Carbon Herald; UNFCCC (20–21 Nov 2026)

Lebanon IMF deal shifts to Q1 2027 — Jaber confirms deadline; World Bank projects −6.4% GDP; banking stalemate persists

Finance Minister Yassine Jaber confirmed this week that Lebanon’s target is to sign a formal IMF programme in the first quarter of 2027, following the breakdown of November talks. The IMF concluded its Beirut mission saying “further work is needed to reach agreement on a strategy for effective and durable restructuring” of the banking sector — specifically that the August banking resolution law “needs further refinement” and that the Financial Gap Law, which addresses how banking-sector losses are distributed across depositors, banks and the state, has not been voted on by parliament.

The economic backdrop is worsening: the World Bank’s November Lebanon Economic Monitor projects GDP will contract 6.4% in 2026, the third consecutive year of contraction. Lebanese Eurobonds have held firm despite the stalemate, per L’Orient Today, sustained by speculative positioning on a future restructuring rather than any fundamental improvement. The UNIFIL security vacuum from January adds another layer of risk to Lebanon’s fragile recovery timeline.

Sources: Arab News; The National; L’Orient Today; World Bank Lebanon Economic Monitor (Nov 2026)
