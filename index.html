🌋 Breaking — Colombia Quake

Magnitude 7.4 earthquake strikes Colombia’s Pacific coast — at least 111 killed, 87 injured; tremor felt in all 32 departments and in Venezuela

A 7.4-magnitude earthquake struck Colombia’s Chocó province at 7:34 local time (12:34 GMT) on August 10, killing at least 111 people and injuring 87 more. The highest single-city toll was in Pereira (18 deaths); Quibdó, Buenaventura, and Manizales also suffered casualties and building collapses. The tremor was felt in all 32 Colombian departments and in Venezuela. President Petro declared a national emergency. The US tsunami warning system confirmed no tsunami threat. This is one of the deadliest natural disasters in South America in 2026.

Update: Iran publishes its full demands list before the Strait of Hormuz reopens — US naval withdrawal, war reparations, sanctions lifted, frozen assets released — while nearing a lane deal with Oman

Iran’s Supreme National Security Council published its formal non-negotiable conditions for reopening the Strait on August 10: the US must lift its naval blockade and all sanctions, withdraw military forces from the region, pay war reparations, and release frozen Iranian assets. Lebanese government counters Solidere’s 25-year extension request with hard conditions: independent audit and binding project timelines — PM Salam draws a line; SOLA at $76.35, 30% below its 52-week high

Solidere requested a 25-year mandate extension (its original mandate expires in 2029); the Lebanese government countered with a conditional 8-year offer (plus a possible further 6 years), attaching two binding conditions: an independent forensic audit of all Solidere accounts, and legally enforceable timelines for incomplete key projects including the Corniche and Martyrs Square. 🏦 Lebanon · Banking Reform

Lebanon’s parliament finance committee approves 11 new amendments to the Banking Restructuring Law this week — reviving legislation struck down by the Constitutional Council last October

Lebanon’s parliament finance and budget committee approved 11 new articles this week revamping the Banking Restructuring Law, which was partially struck down by the Constitutional Council in October 2025. More amendments go to vote next week. The broader FSDR (Financial Stabilization and Depositor Recovery) framework addresses the $80–83 billion gap between deposits owed and available bank assets; depositors under $100,000 would be repaid in full, while larger depositors would receive BdL-backed securities. The IMF continues to press Lebanon to move faster, calling the 2026 budget insufficiently ambitious on fiscal reform.

LBCI · Enterprise MENA+ · IMF · Carnegie Endowment
Why it matters to you

Every step in banking reform reduces the depositor-uncertainty overhang that has suppressed consumer and business confidence since 2019. Faster reform progress means clients and counterparties gradually regain access to deposits — a positive demand signal for the next 12–18 months. If the revamped law clears parliament, it will be the single biggest confidence catalyst for the Lebanese private sector since the ceasefire.
