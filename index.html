🔒 World · Cybersecurity / FBI

ShinyHunters Breach FBI Jobs Portal — Thousands of Agents’ Home Addresses, Intelligence Assignments Stolen

The criminal hacking group ShinyHunters claims to have stolen the personal records of thousands of FBI employees and applicants from the bureau’s online recruitment portal (apply.fbijobs.gov), briefly defacing it with a fake “SEIZED BY SHINYHUNTERS” banner on Monday night. The stolen data reportedly includes home addresses, spousal information, and details of agents’ sensitive assignments — covering operations against Chinese intelligence, Russian services and drug cartels. Cybersecurity experts examining the sample data say the breach appears authentic.

The FBI confirmed “unauthorised activity” affecting its recruitment infrastructure. ShinyHunters framed the attack as retaliation for an FBI advisory about the group’s extortion tactics, and issued a demand that the bureau retract the document within one week. Security researchers warn that residential addresses and family details of intelligence operatives could be weaponised for doxxing, blackmail, or foreign targeting campaigns.

🚨 World · Iran / UNGA

Iran at UNGA: “We Will Never Bow Our Head” — Pezeshkian Rejects Nuclear Limits, US Diplomat Walks Out

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian delivered a defiant speech to the UN General Assembly on Wednesday, declaring Tehran will never accept limits on its civilian nuclear programme and vowing to defend the country “to its last breath.” He accused the US of flouting international law by targeting civilian infrastructure. In a pointed gesture, the lone American diplomat in the hall rose and left mid-speech — the first time in the UN’s 81-year history that a head of state at war with the US addressed the Assembly. Pezeshkian also left a narrow opening for diplomacy, calling for negotiations “with dignity.”

Behind the scenes, US and Iranian officials held a two-hour direct meeting in New York without reaching a breakthrough. Trump has indicated he expects no deal before the November midterms. Zelensky also addressed the Assembly, calling Russia’s “ridiculous and never-ending war” a threat to all of Europe and seeking tighter economic pressure on Moscow. Police reinforced security around UN headquarters after three people emerged from a maintenance manhole near the perimeter.

Update: Trump–Xi Summit Day 2 — AI Safety Notification Deal and Rare-Earth Détente the Realistic Wins

The Trump–Xi White House summit — Xi arrived Tuesday evening — enters its key bilateral session today. 🏠 World · Finance / Data Centres

Data Centres Are Straining the Global Insurance Market — Premiums Juicy but Stakes “Hard to Digest”

The Economist’s markets coverage today flags a growing stress point in the data centre boom: the global insurance industry is struggling to underwrite the concentration of risk that mega-scale data centre campuses represent. Facilities now valued at $2–5 billion sit on single sites; a single catastrophic fire, flood or power event would create claims that would dwarf historical technology-sector losses. Insurers are increasing premiums sharply — making the economics attractive to write — but are simultaneously worried about their own exposure concentrations if multiple campuses fail simultaneously.

This is a structural development for the sector: data centre developers in Africa and the Gulf, where fire suppression, BMS and physical-security standards are less mature than in the US or Europe, are likely to face increasing pressure from international insurers to prove compliance with higher standards as a condition of coverage. The Economist notes that the financial stakes “are hard to digest,” and reinsurers are already pulling back from the largest single-campus risks.

Digital Parks Africa Enters Lagos Race with Nigeria Data Centre 1 — Carrier-Neutral, West Africa’s Newest Operator

South Africa’s Digital Parks Africa has announced plans to build Nigeria Data Centre 1 (NDC1) in Lagos — its first facility outside South Africa — in partnership with Master Power Technologies Group. The facility will be carrier-neutral, giving banks, cloud providers and connectivity firms maximum flexibility in how they connect. The announcement followed Digital Parks’ debut at the ITW Africa conference in Kenya on September 8, with the Lagos project confirmed publicly on September 11. The exact location, capacity and cost have not yet been disclosed.

NDC1 adds a sixth major operator to a Lagos market that already includes Equinix, Rack Centre, MainOne (now part of Equinix), KASI and MTN’s planned 150MW campus. Premium Times and Ripples Nigeria report strong local interest from financial services firms seeking to meet the Central Bank of Nigeria’s data-localisation mandate. Digital Parks specifically targets financial institutions, banks and cloud providers as its anchor tenants.

Update: French Senate Electoral College Votes Today — Far-Right Gains Expected to Be Confirmed by Sunday

France’s senatorial electoral college — made up 95% of town councillors and local delegates — meets today (Thursday, September 24) to elect 170 senators across 42 mainland and overseas departments. The process concludes Sunday, September 27, when the remaining seats are filled. Marine Le Pen’s Rassemblement National is expected to gain Senate seats for the first time, driven by strong showings in this year’s municipal elections in departments where senatorial voting is taking place.

The stakes go beyond symbolism: a larger RN Senate presence would constrain the government’s ability to legislate and shift the dynamics heading into the 2027 presidential cycle. Connexion France notes that far-right council wins have changed the composition of the electoral college in several key departments, giving the RN a structural advantage it has not had before. Watch Sunday evening for the results.

📈 Lebanon · Economy / IMF

Update: IMF Mission Concludes Beirut Visit — Bank Reform Progress Noted, But Economy Contracting “Significantly” in 2026

The IMF mission led by Ernesto Ramírez Rigo completed its Beirut visit (September 22–25), covering banking sector restructuring, the 2026 state budget, and Lebanon’s reform trajectory. The mission welcomed amendments to the Bank Resolution Law adopted in August as “aligned with international standards,” calling it a meaningful step toward the IMF arrangement that Lebanon has sought since 2022. However, the IMF said economic activity is expected to contract “significantly” in 2026, with inflation in double digits, a widening current-account deficit, and continued infrastructure damage from the ongoing conflict.

The key remaining prerequisite for an IMF arrangement — the Financial Stability and Deposit Recovery Law — has not yet passed. Bank associations have resisted it for years over fears of massive write-downs; without it, any IMF programme remains out of reach. The Daily Beirut reports that the IMF explicitly named deposit-protection reform and banking sector consolidation as its top priorities for the coming months.
