🤝 World · Russia / US Geopolitics

Trump Weighs Russia Business Deals Before Any Ukraine Ceasefire — A Strategic Break From the West

The Trump administration is considering concluding economic agreements with Moscow before a Ukraine peace deal is reached — a significant shift from the Western position that business ties with Russia must follow a settlement. Two US officials told the New York Times that such deals could “show the United States is serious about a reset relationship with Russia.” Special envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner recently travelled to Moscow, where Kremlin adviser Yuri Ushakov confirmed discussions touched on “mutually beneficial Russian-American projects.”

The move risks fracturing Western sanctions unity: European allies have predicated trade normalisation on Russian withdrawal from Ukraine. Meanwhile Russia’s three-day Duma elections (Sep 18–20) are wrapping this weekend; the NYT Evening on Friday reported the vote is “stage-managed,” with no credible opposition allowed and Moscow cafes buzzing with rumours of renewed military mobilisation after a United Russia supermajority result is confirmed.

🏴 World · Finance / Central Banks

Bank of Japan Hikes to 1.25% — 31-Year High — in a Decision Tainted by US Treasury Pressure

The Bank of Japan voted 7–2 on September 18 to raise its policy rate from 1.0% to 1.25% — the highest level since 1995 — citing persistent inflation driven by Middle East energy tensions and a weak yen. The widely expected hike came amid sustained public pressure from US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent for tighter Japanese monetary policy, which the NYT flagged as an unusual and unprecedented campaign against a foreign central bank’s independence. The yen fell sharply after the announcement, surpassing 157 per dollar, and the Nikkei rose 1.9%.

Analysts anticipate a further hike to 1.5% in December, with rates potentially reaching 2.0% by mid-2027. Higher Japanese rates strengthen global monetary tightening pressure and compress the carry-trade dynamics that have underpinned EM equity risk appetite — a headwind for frontier markets including Nigeria.

✈️ World · Arms / Gulf

Trump Clears F-35 Sale to Saudi Arabia Over CIA Warning That China Could Steal the Technology

The Trump administration has approved the sale of F-35 stealth fighters to Saudi Arabia, overriding classified intelligence assessments from US agencies warning that Beijing could acquire or steal advanced US warplane technology through Saudi–China defence cooperation or espionage channels. The sale is part of Trump’s Gulf economic engagement strategy, but congressional defence hawks from both parties have objected; the NSA reportedly told the White House the risk is “not theoretical.”

Saudi Arabia maintains deep defence and economic ties with China, and US intelligence has documented Chinese efforts to embed in Gulf military supply chains. The deal underlines the tension between Trump’s transactional Gulf policy and long-run US technological security — a tension being watched closely by European allies and by Israel.

📈 Africa · Nigeria / Capital Markets

Dangote Refinery Launches Africa’s Largest-Ever IPO — $2.1 B “People’s” Share Sale on NGX

Aliko Dangote opened the Nigerian Exchange’s biggest-ever initial public offering on September 14, offering 4.1 billion shares in his oil refinery and petrochemicals complex at ₦525 per share to raise up to ₦2.15 trillion (~$2.1 B). Africa’s richest man is marketing it as a “people’s IPO” — designed to draw Nigeria’s growing retail-investor class into one of the continent’s most important industrial assets. Nigeria’s trade minister said a fully subscribed offering could add $60 B to the NGX’s capitalisation. The offer closes October 13.

The Economist flagged this week that the Dangote IPO could become a turning point for African capital markets, testing whether Nigeria can convert millions of savers into shareholders and re-attract foreign institutional attention to the NGX — which foreign investors have largely exited since 2019. TikTok creators are already targeting younger Nigerian investors with “buy in” messaging.

Digital Parks Africa Announces Carrier-Neutral Lagos Data Centre — NDC1

Digital Parks Africa (DPA) announced on September 11 the development of NDC1, a new carrier-neutral data centre in Lagos as the company expands its West Africa footprint. Carrier-neutral facilities allow international operators to connect with multiple providers without lock-in — the preferred model for hyperscalers and large cloud operators entering new markets. Nigeria now has 28 data centres operational or under construction, with Lagos holding approximately 30% of national capacity. DPA joins Equinix, Digital Realty, Nxtra by Airtel, and Kasi Cloud in an accelerating build-out of the Lagos digital infrastructure cluster.

The broader Nigerian data centre market is valued at $374 M in 2026 and growing at 15.9% CAGR toward $783 M by 2031. Power availability remains the binding constraint, creating a premium for sites with on-site generation — a differentiator your fire-suppression and BMS work already addresses for multiple clients.

🏛 France · Diplomacy & Labour

France Hosts Lebanon Security Summit; Nationwide Public-Sector Strike Called for September 29

France convened a trilateral summit with Lebanon and Jordan on September 17 in Paris to discuss the future of southern Lebanon’s security framework as UNIFIL’s mandate approaches its December 31 expiry. President Aoun attended and told the gathering that “the opportunity may not come again” for an international peacekeeping arrangement. France holds the rotating UN Security Council presidency for September 2026, giving Paris unusual leverage to push a resolution before year-end. No concrete replacement force has been agreed.

Domestically, France’s eight largest public-sector unions have called a nationwide strike for September 29 covering central government, hospitals, local authorities and SNCF rail, over real-wage erosion and staff shortages. Airlines including easyJet and French Bee have already issued contingency notices. The action is the largest coordinated strike call since the 2023 pension reform protests.

🇱🇧 Lebanon
📉 Lebanon · Economy / IMF

IMF Mission Concludes Four Days in Beirut — Financial Gap Law Is Now the Last Key Hurdle

An IMF staff mission completed intensive talks in Beirut from September 15–18 with Finance Ministry, Banque du Liban, and parliamentary contacts, focusing on advancing a Staff-Level Agreement. The mission identified the Financial Gap Law — which determines how losses from Lebanon’s banking sector collapse are distributed across shareholders, depositors, and the state — as the single remaining critical step. The IMF welcomed earlier parliamentary amendments to the Bank Resolution Law but made clear that without the Financial Gap Law, unlocking international financial support is impossible.

Economic conditions remain severe: activity is projected to contract significantly in 2026, inflation remains in double digits, and the current account deficit is widening on higher energy costs. The BLOM Bond Index stands at 29.57 — Lebanon’s sovereign debt still trading around 30 cents on the dollar. The parliamentary calendar for the Financial Gap Law vote has not yet been set.

IMF · DailyBeirut.com · BLOMINVEST
Why it matters to you

The Financial Gap Law is the lever that unlocks debt restructuring and institutional capital inflows frozen since 2020. If parliament acts before year-end, client cash-flow and banking conditions in Lebanon could begin to normalise within 12 months — materially reducing the credit risk on domestic project receivables. If it stalls, economic contraction continues and dollar-invoiced clients face increasing strain.

Act on this: Flag to Sami — major Lebanese clients holding frozen bank deposits should be briefed on the Financial Gap Law timeline as it will shape whether those balances move within a year.
🚫 Lebanon · Security

Israel Seizes Deir Mimas Military Post in Direct Standoff With Lebanese Army

Israeli forces seized the Deir Mimas military post in south Lebanon on September 18 in a direct confrontation with the Lebanese Armed Forces — the most overt Israeli-LAF standoff since the ceasefire. L’Orient Today reports President Aoun condemned the move as a violation of UN Resolution 1701 and demanded immediate Israeli withdrawal. The Paris summit the same day (see France above) was partly a response to the mounting security vacuum ahead of UNIFIL’s December expiry.

In a separate security development, Lebanon charged a former Syrian officer and Hezbollah member on September 18 with planning attacks on Lebanese soil — a selective prosecution the government is using to demonstrate security competence to Western partners ahead of any UNIFIL successor discussion. The combination of Israeli provocations and Hezbollah judicial moves signals a tense and unstable south-Lebanon situation heading into autumn.

L’Orient Today (independent) · Reuters
Why it matters to you

Deir Mimas marks a step up: Israeli forces are now directly engaging Lebanese state infrastructure, not just Hezbollah. Any broadening of Israeli operations to target LAF positions would trigger a regional escalation and freeze every infrastructure project in Lebanon. The risk of a full south-Lebanon security collapse heading into December — when UNIFIL expires — is higher this week than any point since the ceasefire.

🏛 Lebanon · Real Estate

Prime Beirut Stabilises in Cash Market — Achrafieh Window Widening for Diaspora Buyers

Real estate consultancy Ramco’s latest assessment, reported by L’Orient Today, describes a two-speed Beirut market: land and commercial leasing are showing real momentum in prime districts, while apartment sales and office leasing remain subdued. Achrafieh and Metn are the clear bright spots — properties available at 30–40% of 2018 peak prices in fresh dollars, all-cash transactions only, with rising diaspora buyer interest from the UAE, US, and Europe. Ramco warns the window may be temporary: security uncertainty and IMF reform delays are structural dampeners, but the scarcity of fresh-dollar inventory acts as a floor under prices in the best micro-markets.

The all-cash character of today’s market eliminates the bank-seizure and mortgage risk that plagued Lebanon’s pre-2019 property market. A successful Financial Gap Law vote — see L1 above — could begin unlocking depositor balances and reignite broader transaction volumes in Q1 2027.
