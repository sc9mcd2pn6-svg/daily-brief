US Midterms

Update: Democrats complete the sweep — Senate confirmed, biggest congressional landslide in 50 years

CBS News and ABC News confirmed late Wednesday that Democrats won control of the Senate, adding to their already-confirmed House majority. Senate Minority Leader Schumer called it “a vindication for Democrats.” The final tally appears to give Democrats around 54 Senate seats to Republicans’ 44, with Mississippi still counting in a nail-biter. Democrats flipped seats in North Carolina (Roy Cooper over Michael Whatley), South Carolina, Texas, Kansas, Iowa and other battlegrounds. The Economist calls it the biggest congressional landslide in 50 years. Every statewide office in Texas reportedly flipped blue, and a Democrat could win the Mississippi Senate race — which is not a misprint, as The Economist noted.

VP Vance issued a stinging critique on the morning after, blaming the Iran war for Republican losses — with what the NYT describes as Trump’s “apparent approval.” Trump retains the presidency and all executive powers until January 2029, but a Dem Congress can now block legislation, launch oversight investigations, and reshape military aid and trade conditions. The Economist’s Lexington column argues Republicans now face a brutal 2028 presidential primary, with Vance as the early frontrunner but scarred by his Iran-war association.

Source: NYT Today’s Headlines (5 Nov); Economist Today (5 Nov); CBS News; ABC News

Markets

Markets sell off sharply on blue-wave shock — S&P −4.4%, Nasdaq −5.5%, Brent oil −10%

US equity markets fell hard on Thursday as investors digested the full scale of the Democratic sweep. The S&P 500 shed 4.36% to close at 7,353.73 — reversing most of its midterm-night rally — while the Nasdaq entered correction territory with a 5.52% drop. The Dow Jones lost more than 1,800 points (3.44%). The rout was led by AI and chip stocks: Nvidia fell roughly 9.3%, dragging the semiconductor sector lower. CNBC and Fox Business attribute the AI selloff to investor concern that a Dem-controlled Congress will impose tighter export controls on advanced chips and may push harder on AI regulation.

Brent crude fell roughly 10% from Wednesday’s level to around $99 per barrel, as markets began pricing in the possibility of a less hawkish Iran policy under a divided government — potentially allowing a diplomatic path that would eventually lift the Iran oil embargo and add supply. Saudi Aramco’s quarterly profit of $31 billion (reported by The Economist) illustrates how much the Iran-war premium has boosted Gulf oil revenues; a thaw would erode that premium. The oil selloff has a direct positive for Lebanon’s import bill and for dollar-exposed Fakhry Group clients.

Ukraine

Ukraine’s demographic collapse deepens — 3.55 deaths for every birth in H1 2026; Russia celebrating

The Economist this week runs a major feature headlined “No babies, boom” on Ukraine’s population crisis. Registry data shows 73,292 births against 259,853 deaths in the first half of 2026 — a death-to-birth ratio of 3.55:1. Ukraine’s pre-war population of about 44 million has already shrunk to an estimated 22–25 million in government-controlled territory after wartime deaths, displacement and refugee flight. IntelliNews reports that more than 5.6 million Ukrainians remain as refugees abroad, with projections suggesting 20% or more will not return even if the war ends. The collapse has accelerated since Russia shifted to mass deep strikes on cities and energy infrastructure.

The Economist notes Russia is calculating that demographic attrition — not territorial conquest — may be its most decisive weapon. A newly Dem-controlled US Congress is more likely to restore military and humanitarian aid to Ukraine than the previous Republican House, which repeatedly blocked it. Whether that aid can reverse the battlefield stalemate and reduce civilian losses is the open question.

US Supreme Court

Justice Alito will not retire immediately despite Democratic sweep — calculates Trump retains appointment power

The NYT reports that Supreme Court Justice Samuel Alito told associates he has no intention of retiring in the near term despite Wednesday’s Democratic midterm sweep. His reasoning: Trump retains the presidency until January 2029 and thus retains the power to appoint his successor, regardless of Senate composition. Democrats cannot constitutionally force a Supreme Court appointment; they can only confirm or block nominees the president sends up. Alito, 76, has faced calls from Democrats to step down and allow a liberal replacement under a potential future Democratic president.

The court’s 6–3 conservative majority therefore appears set to remain intact through at least the 2027 term. Justice Thomas, also the subject of retirement speculation, has given no signals of an imminent departure. The court’s composition will be a mobilising issue for both parties heading into 2028.

Source: NYT Today’s Headlines (5 Nov); NYT The Evening (5 Nov)

France Strike

Update: France general strike — up to 1.6 million march, tear gas in Paris & Lyon, CGT vows to continue

Yesterday’s general strike was massive by any measure. The Interior Ministry counted 575,000 demonstrators nationwide (60,000 in Paris); the CGT union federation claimed nearly 1.6 million, with 350,000 in Paris alone. France 24 and CNN both called it the largest day of action in years. French riot police fired tear gas during scuffles in Paris and Lyon; clashes also broke out in Nantes and Rennes. SNCF rail services were heavily disrupted across the country and schools remained closed in many departments.

CGT chief Sophie Binet declared that the turnout “marked a new stage” in the mobilisation and that the union would “continue its fight for as long as necessary.” She demands an immediate 10% minimum wage hike — the government’s November 1 increase of roughly €15 a month was dismissed as far too small. The Yellow Vests called follow-up protests for today (Friday) and Saturday. The Macron government has not yet responded substantively; Prime Minister Lecornu faces a conference call with union leaders on Monday. If turnout holds through the weekend, Macron faces a political crisis ahead of a critical 2027 budget vote.

Source: France 24 (5 Nov); CNN (5 Nov); Sortiraparis; Democrata.es; manifs.win

Lebanon / Economy

BdL raises deposit withdrawal limits again; currency board debate reignites; Finance Minister warns of up to −10% GDP

L’Orient Today’s economy section reports that the Banque du Liban has again revised upward the limits on withdrawals from frozen deposits under circulars 158 and 166, in a move banks say raises questions about the pace and structure of depositor repayments ahead of the Financial Gap Law’s passage. A parallel debate has erupted over Lebanon’s exchange-rate framework: economists and senior BdL officials are split on whether a currency-board model (fully fixed to the dollar) or a managed-float arrangement better serves a post-crisis recovery. Neither side has prevailed and the IMF has not yet weighed in publicly on the choice.

Finance Minister Yassine Jaber separately warned that Lebanon’s economy could contract by up to 10% in 2026 due to continuing conflict and institutional paralysis — somewhat bleaker than the World Bank’s 6.4% contraction projection. Reconstruction is estimated to require at least $25 billion, with donor pledges well short of that figure. The IMF staff mission that concluded in late September is still finalising conditions for a formal programme, with banking-sector capitalisation and the Financial Gap Law as the two outstanding sticking points.

Source: L’Orient Today (economy section); World Bank Lebanon update; Arabian Business; Intellinews
