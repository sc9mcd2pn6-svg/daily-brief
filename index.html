Direct impact · Lebanon · Banking

Lebanon commercial banks announce weekly Wednesday work stoppage — next is 25 November; MoF extends Eurobond provisions tax deadline to 15 December

The Association of Banks in Lebanon announced a weekly Wednesday general closure, in protest at provisions of the Financial Gap Law that the banking sector considers punitive. The first stoppage took place on 18 November. The next is Wednesday 25 November, followed by 2 December. All commercial bank branches and operations are closed on those days. Separately, the Ministry of Finance has extended the declaration and payment deadline for the tax on banks’ Eurobond provisions (the levy on banks that took advantage of the state-debt restructuring framework) to 15 December 2026.

The weekly strikes reflect the unresolved standoff between the banking sector and parliament over the Financial Gap Law, which Parliament has not yet passed. The IMF has told Beirut that the law is a prerequisite for any formal programme. The MoF Eurobond-provisions tax extension gives Lebanese banks two additional weeks to file; it does not affect client accounts or the VAT deadline of 30 November (reported yesterday).

Sources: L’Orient Today; Association of Banks in Lebanon; MoF communiqué (Nov 2026)

Direct impact · Lebanon · Banking

BDL raises Circular 158 monthly dollar withdrawal limit to $1,200 — effective 3 November 2026

Banque du Liban raised the monthly dollar withdrawal ceiling under Circular 158 from $1,000 to $1,200 per account, effective 3 November 2026. The new allocation breaks down as $800 in cash (ATM and teller) and $400 on debit/payment card. The change applies to all depositors holding pre-crisis (lollar) accounts at Lebanese commercial banks. Circular 158, first issued in 2020, governs the conditions under which depositors can access US dollar balances trapped in the banking system since the 2019 financial crisis.

The increase is modest — a $200/month improvement — and reflects incremental BDL policy easing rather than a structural breakthrough. It does not affect fresh-dollar accounts or company operating accounts. For businesses with lollar deposits at Lebanese banks, this increases the monthly liquidity available for small cash expenditures.

Sources: BDL Circular 158 amendment; L’Orient Today; NNA (Nov 2026) — verify against official BDL circular register

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards destroy 2 US Patriot radars in Bahrain — Trump floats ‘permanent deal’ as Gulf states go to highest alert

Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps struck and destroyed two US-operated Patriot radar installations in Bahrain on Saturday, its fourth attack on Bahrain in a single week. The strikes significantly degrade the US-led missile defence network covering the Fifth Fleet’s headquarters and the Gulf Cooperation Council states. No US personnel fatalities were confirmed immediately, though the IRGC claimed casualties in its communiqué. Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Kuwait raised their military readiness to the highest peacetime level.

Hours after the strike, President Trump posted on Truth Social that he was “open to a permanent and honourable deal” with Iran, calling for direct talks “within days.” Iran’s foreign minister publicly dismissed the offer as “a media stunt” and said Iran would not negotiate under military pressure. CBS News and Anadolu Agency both reported the radar destruction; The National (Abu Dhabi) confirmed GCC security consultations were convened by Saturday afternoon. The pattern — attack then offer — echoes earlier Trump pressure plays in the first term.

Sources: CBS News; Anadolu Agency; The National (Abu Dhabi); Al Jazeera (22 Nov 2026)

Update: COP31 president says “close to final deal” — fossil-fuel fight persists; vote expected today

COP31 summit president Murat Kurum told negotiators late Saturday that the talks were “close to a final agreement” and that he was confident of resolving outstanding gaps before Sunday evening. The revised text circulated overnight includes a compromise on fossil-fuel language: rather than the full phaseout language of COP28, it references “accelerated transition away from unabated fossil fuels” — weaker than the EU’s demand but stronger than the previous week’s CCUS-only draft. Saudi Arabia and India have not publicly accepted the compromise. The 23-nation bloc led by the EU said Saturday night that their “core concerns have been addressed” in the latest text, though language on the $120 billion adaptation finance target for developing nations remains unresolved.

UN News, RTE and Business Standard all reported the overnight developments. If a deal is reached today, Antalya will be the first COP in three years to produce a formal text — though environmental groups have already described the likely outcome as “a fig leaf, not a framework.” The absence of the United States from the talks for the second year running limits the agreement’s practical reach.

Sources: UN News; RTE; Business Standard; Al Jazeera (22 Nov 2026)

Lebanon / Economy

Lebanon parliament approves $200M World Bank ‘Aman’ social safety net, Turkey military pact and Ivory Coast air deal

In its 12 November plenary session, Lebanon’s parliament approved three distinct measures. First, the $200 million World Bank “Aman” programme — a social protection scheme channelling direct cash transfers to Lebanon’s most vulnerable households via a new national registry. Second, a military cooperation agreement with Turkey, covering joint training, equipment and intelligence sharing — the first such bilateral security pact Lebanon has signed with Ankara and seen as partly offsetting the UNIFIL withdrawal vacuum. Third, an air services agreement with Côte d’Ivoire, adding a new direct route into the West African market.

L’Orient Today and Credit Libanais both noted that the Aman programme is significant: it creates a formal social registry that future IMF discussions may rely on as a fiscal targeting tool. The Turkey pact is diplomatically sensitive — it was passed despite Hezbollah abstentions and represents Lebanon’s most significant external security commitment since the ceasefire.

Sources: L’Orient Today; Credit Libanais research note; NNA (12–13 Nov 2026)

Why it matters to you

The Ivory Coast air services agreement and Turkey military pact both signal Lebanon trying to build bilateral bridges amid the UNIFIL vacuum. For the group, the Ivory Coast route is a minor positive for QSS travel connectivity to West Africa.
