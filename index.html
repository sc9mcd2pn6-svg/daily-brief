AI & Markets

Gigantic AI debts are making investors nervous

The Economist leads today with a warning that investors across stock markets are increasingly worried that AI companies will be unable to service their vast debts. The IMF’s October 2026 Global Financial Stability Report identifies $650 billion in circular AI debt — hyperscalers investing in financially weaker AI firms whose revenue then flows back to buy hyperscaler cloud services — and counts $300 billion in related equity deals since 2023. SK Hynix fell 10% and Samsung 8% in Seoul last week; CoreWeave shares plunged 15% as bond yields spiked near 15%.

The S&P 500 lost nearly 2% last week — its biggest weekly drop since late June — before a partial tech-led recovery on Monday (S&P +0.41% to 7,691). AI bond issuance is on pace to exceed $570 billion in 2026, four times the 2025 rate, and investor demand per dollar of bonds has fallen from 5x in February to below 2x. The Economist asks: who picks up the bill if confidence cracks?

Source: The Economist Today (19 Oct 2026); IMF GFSR Oct 2026; CNBC, Yahoo Finance (19 Oct 2026)

Why it matters to you

If AI capex spending slows sharply, demand for new data-centre builds cools with it — which could ease the Lagos power crunch and construction pipeline. But the near-term risk is a tighter credit market that raises financing costs for your clients’ projects and slows deals across the board.

Canada

Alberta holds independence referendum — result expected today

Alberta voted yesterday (Oct 19) on a non-binding question: whether to remain in Canada or take legal steps toward a binding independence referendum. Premier Danielle Smith, emboldened by Trump’s pressure on Ottawa, called the vote after polling showed about 33% of Albertans willing to pursue independence. The “remain” side was expected to win comfortably — pre-vote polling showed 70% intending to vote “stay” — but even a decisive pro-Canada result leaves a sizeable separatist minority that will not disappear.

PM Mark Carney campaigned on unity and promised “a better Canada” while the Canadian Chamber of Commerce warned that prolonged constitutional uncertainty damages investor confidence. The Economist’s figure of the day: Canada’s GDP per person grew just 4% in the ten years to 2025 versus 23% in the US — a gap that Carney must close to make the unity case stick.

Source: The Economist Today (19 Oct 2026); CP24, CTV News, WCAX (Oct 2026)

Lebanon · Israel · US

Lebanon-Israel direct talks resume in Washington — today

The latest round of Lebanon-Israel ceasefire talks is taking place in Washington today (Tuesday, Oct 20), following an Oct 19 Israeli strike near Ali Taher that raised tensions in the south. The talks, direct and US-mediated, aim to establish a verification mechanism for Hezbollah disarmament and cement the conditional ceasefire agreed in June after a 45-day extension from May. Hezbollah has consistently opposed the direct-talks format, and a June agreement has held precariously, with Israeli strikes continuing to kill people during the truce period.

L’Orient Today and regional reporting confirm Lebanon’s negotiating team is engaged, but Hezbollah’s refusal to be represented creates structural fragility. The talks are the eighth-plus round; earlier sessions in April and May established the framework but left disarmament timelines unresolved. Progress today would be significant; a breakdown could reignite hostilities as the Israeli election on Oct 27 approaches.

Source: L’Orient Today, France 24, PBS NewsHour (Apr–Jun 2026 rounds); NYT Evening (19 Oct 2026)

Why it matters to you

A durable ceasefire is the single biggest determinant of Beirut real-estate transaction flow and client confidence in Lebanon. If today’s talks collapse, expect Gulf capital to pause and prime Beirut prices to soften further from their already-elevated “out of sync” levels (RAMCO).

Act on this: Monitor today’s Washington talks outcome — if breakdown, flag to the Beirut team and consider timing of any real-estate decisions tied to Lebanese recovery assumptions.

Tech · Connectivity

SpaceX Starlink’s global expansion: a bold robotics bet

The Economist covers SpaceX’s accelerating Starlink rollout as a strategic gamble premised on a specific thesis: that robotics will dramatically increase demand for satellite connectivity over the next decade, justifying the enormous capital expenditure. Elon Musk is betting that autonomous machines — from agricultural robots to factory lines to autonomous vehicles — will each require persistent low-latency uplinks, turning Starlink into essential industrial infrastructure rather than a consumer broadband play.

The investment case is unproven, but the rollout is real: Starlink already serves military-grade users (Ukraine’s Starshield contract, until recently), maritime, and aviation. In markets with poor terrestrial fibre — including West Africa — it is increasingly the only viable high-bandwidth option for remote industrial sites.

Source: The Economist Today (19 Oct 2026)

Why it matters to you

For Quasar Nigeria and Lagos data centre clients, Starlink low-earth-orbit connectivity is already a practical backup link option — and could become the primary WAN for remote security and BMS installations across West Africa where fibre is absent.

Act on this: Ask the Lagos team whether any current or prospective clients are discussing Starlink as part of their connectivity design — if so, Quasar could position as the integration partner for the BMS/CCTV layer.

France · Curfew

France imposes curfews after worst riots in decades — 3,000 businesses looted

Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu on Monday invoked emergency powers to impose overnight curfews (8 pm–6 am) in Lille and three other northern cities, and an 11 pm–6 am curfew in several Paris neighbourhoods, after the most destructive weekend of rioting France has seen in a generation. More than 3,000 businesses were looted or vandalised across France on Saturday — from Paris and Lyon to the normally quiet city of Limoges, whose city hall was set on fire. Marseille banned all public gatherings on Monday. At least two people were killed and a 14-year-old was hit in the face by a tear-gas grenade, breaking his jaw.

The riots erupted from mostly peaceful protests over school conditions and the cost of living, but spun into generalised looting and arson. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez acknowledged that police had been underprepared. President Macron declined to accept Nuñez’s resignation and has said the government will use “whatever tools are at its disposal.” More than 2,000 people were arrested over the weekend. The Economist warns that the core problems — stagnating wages, inadequate schools, housing costs — are not being addressed by Lecornu’s budget talks, meaning further violence is probable before the November 5 general strike.

Source: NYT The Evening (19 Oct 2026); Euronews, Al Jazeera, PBS NewsHour, CNN (19 Oct 2026); The Economist Today (19 Oct 2026)

Global data-centre vacancy hits record low — Lagos positioned but power-constrained

CBRE’s Global Data Center Trends report (Q1 2026) confirms the tightest market on record: global vacancy has fallen to 6.7% across the 16 largest markets, down from 8.3% a year ago, even as supply grew 25% year-on-year to 16 GW. JLL reports North American demand doubled in H1 2026, rental rates are up 70% since 2020, and 95% of the new 66-GW pipeline is already pre-committed. Knight Frank values the stabilised global data-centre market at $1.95 trillion, up 39% on 2024. European demand rose 90% against Q1 2025.

For Lagos, the tightening global market reinforces the strategic case for West Africa’s first hyperscale-grade campuses — but the power constraint identified last week (Tetracore/Huawei’s 100 MW off-grid gas plant; Dolphin Telecoms’ solar deal for the ACE cable landing station) remains the binding limit. The Lagos government’s target is to triple data-centre capacity by 2030, yet without reliable grid or large-scale off-grid power, that target is aspirational.

Source: CBRE Global Data Center Trends Q1 2026; JLL H1 2026; Knight Frank; DatacenterDynamics; TechCabal (May 2026)

Lebanon · Economy

IMF staff-level deal on hold — November now last credible window before year-end

The IMF’s October mission to Beirut ended last week without a staff-level agreement, with the Financial Gap Law — the final condition — still unvoted by parliament. The fund and the central bank remain deadlocked on the hierarchy-of-claims language: the IMF insists deposits be protected in priority order; BdL wants carve-outs and for the state to absorb more of the recapitalization cost. Governor Souaid expressed confidence in a “breakthrough by year-end” and the November mission is confirmed, but the parliamentary vote has no date.

Without the law, no staff-level deal can be signed, no Extended Fund Facility can be activated, and the $2.5B+ in bilateral and multilateral lending behind the IMF programme remains locked. Eurobonds continue to trade around 25 cents on the dollar (L’Orient Today), reflecting market scepticism that 2026 will produce a deal but holding on the option value of eventual reform. A Janus Henderson analysis warns that markets may be pricing in too optimistic a reform trajectory.

Source: Enterprise/MENAplus (Sep–Oct 2026); L’Orient Today; Janus Henderson (2025)

Why it matters to you

For Lebanese project clients, the absence of a banking reform deal keeps deposit access constrained and USD liquidity scarce. Any project requiring a significant letter of credit or bank-backed payment from a Lebanese counterparty remains high-risk until the law passes. Quasar should continue pricing Lebanese contracts in cash or fully secured terms.

Real Estate · Global

Data centres become global real estate’s most sought-after asset class

The data-centre real-estate wave is accelerating well beyond technology into mainstream property investment. Knight Frank values the stabilised global market at $1.95 trillion — a 39% increase on 2024 — and JLL reports rental rates up 70% since 2020 with no near-term correction in sight given that 95% of the global pipeline is pre-committed. In Europe, tightening supply has driven Frankfurt and London vacancy below 1%. Investors — from sovereign wealth funds to pension funds — are rushing to acquire operational data centres as bond-like yield vehicles.

For prime Beirut, the contrast is sharp: RAMCO’s October report called asking prices “out of sync with reality” and transactions remain sporadic. Gulf capital that was flowing in after the June ceasefire framework has paused ahead of the Washington talks outcome. The structural tailwind for Beirut prime real estate remains tied entirely to political and security clarity — neither of which exists today.

Source: Knight Frank Data Centre Atlas 2026; JLL H1 2026; CBRE Q1 2026; L’Orient Today / RAMCO (Oct 2026)

Why it matters to you

Your prime Beirut holdings benefit from the Gulf capital narrative but are hostage to the ceasefire story. A breakdown in Washington today would likely stall any near-term sale or valuation uplift. Conversely, a meaningful deal could re-open Gulf interest quickly — worth monitoring closely this week.
