🛢️ Hormuz — No Deal, Brent $84

Brent at $84.18 this morning as the Hormuz standoff holds — no deal reached over the weekend despite US pressure; Iran still demands US & Israeli exclusion

Brent crude rose to $84.18/bbl on Monday morning, up 0.75% from Friday’s close, as the weekend produced no Hormuz agreement. The ADNOC tanker attack on August 8 — the 16th vessel struck since the Iran war began — reversed the mid-week optimism that had briefly pushed Brent below $80 when Treasury Secretary Bessent signalled a deal was imminent. Iranian negotiators continue to demand that US and Israeli-flagged or chartered vessels be excluded from any transit arrangement; Washington has rejected this. An interim Oman-routing scheme remains under negotiation. Brent is now up 26% year-on-year.

🤖 US–China — Robot & Inverter Bans

Washington bans imports of foreign-made humanoid robots and data-centre power inverters; Beijing retaliates with its broadest tech countermeasures since October’s truce

The US has banned imports of foreign-manufactured humanoid robots and blocked power inverters used in data centres and solar energy systems, escalating the bilateral tech war weeks before President Xi Jinping’s scheduled September summit in Washington. China responded by curbing the flow of Chinese technology to America in what CNBC describes as Beijing “replicating Washington’s playbook.” Al Jazeera notes that America’s ability to slow China’s robotics and AI rise remains an open strategic question. Both sides are building maximum leverage ahead of the summit; analysts say the available deal space narrows with each escalatory week.

🌡️ France · 5th Heatwave

Paris enters its fifth heatwave of the summer from today — 33–35°C in Île-de-France, 35–38°C in the South — Meteo-France flags possible orange or red alert

A strengthening high-pressure system over Europe is funnelling very hot air from the Iberian Peninsula and North Africa into France, pushing temperatures in Paris and Île-de-France to a projected 33–35°C from Monday August 10. Southern France faces 35–38°C. Meteo-France has not yet issued an official alert but a renewed orange — possibly red — canicule warning cannot be ruled out. Wildfire and air-quality risks are elevated alongside. This is France’s fifth distinct heat episode of summer 2026; the main wave of Parisians returning from the August break is expected in the final week of the month.

MTN Nigeria officially launches Phase 1 of its $235m Lagos data centre — 780 racks, 4.5MW IT load — entering the densest simultaneous data-centre build wave West Africa has seen

⚡ Nigeria · Power & Macro

“Lagos can’t power the cloud” — grid reliability is Nigeria’s data-centre Achilles heel as hyperscale demand outpaces grid capacity; macro window remains the best in two years

As the Lagos data-centre boom accelerates, Nigeria’s structural power challenge is becoming the sector’s defining constraint. Analysis of the $250m hyperscale build wave notes that grid reliability — not land or fibre — is the bottleneck: all Tier III/IV facilities rely on multi-redundant diesel and gas-fired generator systems for 99.982%+ uptime guarantees, making generator-backed power infrastructure a non-negotiable fit-out requirement. On the macro side, Nigeria’s GDP growth of 4.3%, inflation declining to 15.38%, and the CBN rate cut from 27.5% to 26.5% represent the best macro window in two years — though Brent holding at $84 keeps naira stability under watch.
