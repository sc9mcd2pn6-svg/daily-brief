The Iran war unexpectedly drove down global CO2 emissions — first drop since the pandemic

The NYT and Carbon Brief report that global fossil-fuel CO2 emissions are on track for their first annual decline since 2020, driven not by climate policy but by the disruption to oil and gas supply caused by the Iran war. The finding arrives as COP31 is mid-session in Antalya (Nov 9–20), where climate-finance and fossil-fuel phase-out remain the central battles. The United States is absent after leaving the Paris Agreement. The irony — that a Middle East war achieved what a decade of climate summits could not — is not lost on delegates.

Cybersecurity

France accuses Iran of cyberattack on supermarket refrigeration systems — OT security now a state-level battleground

French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot formally accused Iran of orchestrating the October cyberattack that disrupted refrigeration management systems at approximately 700 supermarkets across France, forcing stores to defrost refrigerators overnight and discard perishables worth tens of millions of euros. The Iranian ambassador was summoned to the Quai d’Orsay; Iran denied involvement. The attack targeted Danfoss remote-control systems used in commercial refrigeration — a category of operational technology (OT) or building management system (BMS) that falls squarely within the scope of building automation infrastructure.

France’s cybersecurity agency (ANSSI) warned the attack may still be ongoing and urged all operators to update firmware, change default credentials, and air-gap remote connections. The incident marks the first formal government attribution of a state-sponsored cyberattack specifically targeting building-automation and OT refrigeration systems at scale.

Source: NYT Today’s Headlines (10 Nov); Al Jazeera (13 Oct); Daily Sabah; INCIBE; Manufacturing.net
