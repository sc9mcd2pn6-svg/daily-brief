Europe stands alone as America abandons Ukraine

The Economist leads this week with a blunt assessment: as Trump’s estrangement from Zelensky deepens, European governments are being forced to assume the role of Ukraine’s sole serious Western backer. EU military aid in 2026 is running 67% above the 2022–24 average, with $90 billion financed via loans backed by frozen Russian state funds — while the US has zeroed Ukraine aid in its FY2027 defence budget. But European solidarity is fraying from within: far-right parties in Germany, France and Italy are tying cost-of-living pressures to the cost of the war, with a leading French far-right figure declaring that “France can no longer afford it.”

Update: Trump offers Putin economic relief — banking on Russia easing oil before midterms

The NYT reveals the full White House calculation behind Saturday’s Russia sanctions waiver: the administration is explicitly hoping that giving Moscow economic breathing room will lead Putin to ease global oil supply — pushing US pump prices below $6/gallon before November’s midterms. In effect, Trump is betting on Putin to bail out Republican candidates. The Lindsey Graham Sanctions Act, which mandated 100% tariffs on the five largest buyers of Russian energy and required Treasury designations by Oct 18, has been neutered by executive order on its very deadline day.

A bipartisan Senate caucus is exploring emergency legislation to curtail the waiver authority. If Putin does not ease production, Trump secures neither the political benefit nor the diplomatic return — and will have handed Moscow a free pass at a moment when Ukraine is under maximum pressure.

Latin America

Brazil presidential runoff this Sunday — Bolsonaro ahead of Lula in watershed vote

Flávio Bolsonaro, senator and Trump-aligned inheritor of his father’s movement, took 47% of valid votes in Brazil’s October 4 first round to President Lula’s 45%, defying polls that had put the left-wing incumbent slightly ahead. The runoff is this coming Sunday, October 25. The Economist notes that since Brazil returned to democracy in 1985, no candidate who lost the first round has won the runoff — making Bolsonaro the clear favourite.

A Bolsonaro victory would swing Latin America’s largest economy firmly into the Trump-MAGA orbit, with implications for Amazon deforestation policy, trade, and democratic governance across the region. Lula’s campaign is concentrating on urban working-class districts in São Paulo, but momentum is against him. Jair Bolsonaro, the father, is serving a 27-year jail sentence for plotting a coup; the son has made his own distance from that episode part of his pitch.

Source: The Economist Today (18 Oct 2026); Reuters / Euronews (4–5 Oct 2026)

Jio Platforms — Reliance Industries’ telecoms arm and the world’s second-largest mobile operator by users — opens anchor-investor bidding today for what will be India’s largest IPO in history. Jio’s 5G rollout is now India’s primary connectivity infrastructure and the company is the second-biggest mobile operator in the world by user count.

France

Update: Student movement turns national — mass marches and riots across France on Saturday

What began five weeks ago as high-school students blocking school gates over dilapidated facilities and teacher shortages has expanded into a national movement. On Saturday October 18, tens of thousands of people joined peaceful marches across multiple French cities while riots broke out in several locations simultaneously — the most significant escalation yet. The NYT reports it as a turning point: adult workers and the main trade union federations formally joined the student core for the first time, giving the movement both breadth and organised labour backing.

Prime Minister Lecornu has begun budget talks with union leaders, offered concessions on school conditions, and confirmed he will not seek to ban the November 5 general strike jointly called by the CGT, FO and CFDT. Cumulative arrests since the protests began exceed 6,600. With adult workers now in the streets and a national strike two weeks away, the government faces its most serious political test since Lecornu’s appointment.

Source: The New York Times (18 Oct 2026); Ynetnews, CTV (earlier Oct 2026)

Lebanon Economy

Financial Gap Law limbo: October IMF mission ends without deal — November is the last window

Lebanon’s October IMF mission concluded without a staff-level agreement, chiefly because parliament has still not set a vote date for the Financial Gap Law — the legislation establishing a hierarchy of claims among depositors, banks and the state. Finance Minister Yassine Jaber met IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva in Bangkok on the sidelines of the annual meetings, reaffirming Beirut’s commitment to reforms. A November mission has been confirmed, which multiple sources describe as the final practical window before year-end.

Central Bank Governor Souaid has said publicly that he expects a breakthrough before year-end. The IIF projects Lebanon’s real GDP will contract roughly 12% in 2026. Lebanon’s Eurobonds remain at approximately 25 cents on the dollar on the secondary market — cautious market optimism that a deal is still possible, but far from certain.

Source: L’Orient Today; Blominvest; Reuters / QNA (13 Oct 2026)
