A sharp global bond sell-off has driven sovereign yields to multi-decade highs. Brent crude at $108 this morning adds inflationary pressure that keeps central banks from cutting. Banque du Liban Rewrites Exchange Institution Rules — Four-Tier Licensing System Takes Shape

Banque du Liban’s Basic Decision No. 13837 is a sweeping overhaul of the rules governing Lebanon’s exchange institutions — the sector that became a critical financial lifeline after the 2019 banking crisis locked most depositors out of the formal system. The regulation establishes four distinct licence categories with minimum capital requirements ranging from 10 billion to 100 billion Lebanese pounds depending on permitted activity scope. It is the most significant formalisation of the parallel currency market since the crisis began.

The move aligns with IMF demands that Lebanon structurally reform its financial intermediation infrastructure ahead of any Staff-Level Agreement. Coming days after the IMF mission departed with the Financial Gap Law as the final outstanding legislative hurdle, this BdL decision signals the central bank is acting on technical reform even as parliament has not yet delivered the legislative piece. The combined pressure — IMF conditions plus BdL regulation — suggests a narrowing window for the SLA to close before end-of-year.
