🏭 World · Industry

Volkswagen Cuts 50,000 Jobs and Halves Vehicle Lineup in the Biggest Overhaul in Its History

Volkswagen’s supervisory board unanimously approved “Future Plan 2030” on Thursday — a 12-part restructuring that the company calls the most extensive transformation in its 89-year history. The plan targets 50,000 job cuts across the group over roughly three years, representing about 8% of VW’s global workforce. Four German manufacturing plants will phase out vehicle production between 2031 and 2034. By 2035, VW aims to slim its vehicle lineup by as much as 50%, abandoning low-margin models that cannot compete on price with Chinese rivals such as BYD and SAIC. The cuts build on an earlier 35,000-job reduction agreed in late 2024.

The driver is brutal: Chinese electric-vehicle makers now undercut VW on price by 20–40% in European and African markets, while surging energy costs have made German manufacturing globally uncompetitive. Bloomberg notes VW CEO Oliver Blume received unanimous board backing, signalling that German labour, which holds half the supervisory board seats, has accepted the scale of the crisis. European auto-sector shares rose modestly on the clarity but analysts warn the restructuring does not address the fundamental technology gap with Chinese rivals.

🏫 World · US Policy

Trump IRS Move Threatens Tax-Exempt Status of 18,000 Schools With Minority Support Programs

The Treasury Department and IRS published proposed regulations on Wednesday that would strip federal tax-exempt status from any college or private school that offers targeted support to minority students — including scholarships, mentoring programmes, or admission preferences based on race. The rules could affect up to 18,000 schools if finalised; they are set to take effect no sooner than May 2027, giving schools time to challenge them in court. Education groups immediately indicated legal challenges. Universities have long relied on race-targeted programmes to address historical discrimination, and tax exemptions are especially valuable as they make donations deductible.

The NYT reports the plan thrusts the IRS into the centre of Trump’s war on diversity, equity and inclusion in education — going further than earlier executive orders by using tax law as the enforcement lever. Race-neutral criteria such as family income or academic hardship would still be permitted. Vice-President Vance meanwhile said he does not know whether the Iran war will be over before November’s midterm elections, a rare public admission of uncertainty amid growing Congressional unease.

⚔️ World · Russia-Ukraine

Putin Orders All of Donbas by Dec 31 and Signals Ballistic Strikes on Kyiv City Centre

Bloomberg and the Critical Threats Project report that Russia’s political leadership on August 29 issued a formal order to its forces to reach the full administrative borders of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts by December 31, 2026. Simultaneously, three sources close to the Kremlin told Bloomberg that Russia is weighing an intensification of ballistic missile attacks on the Ukrainian capital, including — for the first time — on the centre of Kyiv, not just suburban infrastructure. Putin has concluded that peace negotiations have reached a dead end.

The military context: Russian forces suffered 42,860 casualties in July alone, yet the Kremlin is doubling down. Putin separately warned Britain of “consequences” following confirmed British-made drone strikes inside Russia. Ukraine’s interceptor shortage — flagged by The Economist last week — is a known vulnerability, and Western analysts fear the combination of US attention fixed on Iran and a Putin facing domestic difficulties creates a dangerous escalation window this autumn.

💥 World · Iran War

Update: Iran War “Increasingly Bogged Down” as Axis of Resistance Goes Its Own Way

The NYT’s morning edition Thursday leads with a stark assessment: six months into the conflict, the United States appears increasingly bogged down, despite Trump’s declarations of military dominance. The latest exchange — strikes that Iran says killed four civilians at a wedding in Sirik County, followed by Iranian IRGC counter-strikes on US bases in Jordan, UAE, and Kuwait — illustrates the cycle of escalation and retaliation that Washington has been unable to break. Vance told CBS he is “extremely skeptical” the wedding party was targeted deliberately, but stopped short of a denial.

The Economist adds a geopolitical dimension: Iran’s proxies — Hezbollah, the Houthis, and Iraqi militia groups — are going their own way. Khamenei’s death severed the command relationship; the Houthis are now manufacturing their own drones in Yemen; and Hezbollah, its Syrian supply route destroyed, is focused on survival rather than Iranian strategy. The “axis of resistance” that once gave Iran strategic depth has effectively dissolved as a co-ordinated force — which is significant, but does not mean the US has a path out of the current conflict.

🕵️ World · Ukraine

Ukraine’s Two Intelligence Services Engaged in Armed Shootout on the Streets of Kyiv

The NYT’s evening edition on Thursday carries an extraordinary report: Ukraine’s two foremost intelligence agencies — the SBU (domestic intelligence) and the HUR (military intelligence) — engaged in a live-fire confrontation on the streets of Kyiv. The incident, which the agencies have not publicly confirmed or explained, has prompted urgent investigations into what Ukrainian officials described as an “interagency rivalry” that has burst into the open at the worst possible moment. Details remain limited but the episode points to dangerous internal fractures in Ukraine’s security apparatus at a time of peak military pressure.

The episode has alarmed Western partners, who rely on both agencies for intelligence sharing and covert operations inside Russia. It also follows reports that a senior SBU official was arrested last month on suspicion of collaboration with Russian intelligence. Analysts warn the shootout — however it is eventually explained — is a gift to Kremlin information operations and could undermine trust in Ukrainian state cohesion ahead of a difficult winter.

✊ World · Obituary

Gloria Steinem, Who Gave Feminism Its Mass-Market Voice, Dies at 92

Gloria Steinem — the writer, activist, and co-founder of Ms. magazine who helped shape second-wave feminism into a mass movement — died on September 2 at 92. The NYT and The Economist both led with her death Thursday morning. She co-founded New York magazine in 1968, launched Ms. in 1972 (all 300,000 copies of the first issue sold out in eight days), and spent five decades building feminist institutions and extending the movement to women of colour, lesbians, and economically marginalised women. The Economist noted she gave feminism “mass-market appeal” — deliberately using her celebrity and glamour to broaden the movement rather than limit it.

Steinem’s passing marks the end of an era for the American women’s movement. She remained active as an organiser into her late eighties. The NYT published a previously unseen interview in which she reflected on her career; tributes from politicians, writers, and activists span the ideological spectrum. She is widely credited with making gender equality a mainstream political concern rather than a fringe cause.

US Withdraws 200 Troops From Nigeria — Military Says Security Partnership Intact

Nigeria’s Defence Headquarters confirmed this week that the United States is withdrawing 200 troops that had been deployed as part of a joint security operation against ISWAP and Boko Haram launched in May 2026. The Nigerian military was careful to state the withdrawal does not represent a breakdown in bilateral security cooperation, framing it as a planned drawdown after the operation met its initial objectives. US officials have not publicly explained the decision, which comes as Washington’s military attention — and budget — is heavily focused on Iran and Ukraine.

Premium Times reports the wider context: Nigeria continues to face around 10,000 armed-conflict deaths per year in its northeast and northwest, and the Nigeria Football Federation separately exposed a fake FIFA letter circulating in political circles — a sign of the disinformation pressures surrounding Nigerian institutions. The US troop withdrawal, even if routine, shifts a degree of security responsibility back to Nigerian forces at a moment when their capacity is stretched and US interest in the Sahel region is diminishing across the board.

EU Moves to Launch Security Mission in Lebanon as UNIFIL’s Mandate Expires in December

With the UN Security Council having voted to end UNIFIL’s mandate by December 2026 — requiring the withdrawal of 10,800 military and civilian personnel — the European Union is moving to fill the gap with its own security mission in southern Lebanon. The EU initiative, reported by BLiTZ and confirmed by Al Jazeera, would be a separate and smaller force than UNIFIL but would maintain a European security presence south of the Litani. Italy, France and Spain had already signalled willingness to contribute to any successor force during the Rome talks in September.

L’Orient Today reports that US Ambassador Michel Issa stated bluntly this week that “we are constantly asking Israel to stop or reduce its strikes, but, in return, Hezbollah is not making any steps” in the direction of disarmament — the key precondition under Security Council Resolution 1701. Israel says it will not leave south Lebanon until full disarmament occurs. The December UNIFIL deadline is now a hard geopolitical constraint: whatever comes after it must be agreed in the next 90 days.

📉 Lebanon · Economy

World Bank Pencils In −6.4% GDP for Lebanon in 2026; IMF Calls Banking Law “Major Step” But Flags Gaps

The World Bank’s August Lebanon Economic Monitor — now circulating widely — projects a 6.4% real GDP contraction for 2026 as the March conflict escalation reversed the 4.2% expansion recorded in 2025. Inflation is expected to reach 17.5% this year, driven by supply disruptions, higher shipping costs, and rising oil prices. Tourism has collapsed, consumption has weakened, and foreign investment is in limbo. Lebanon entered 2026 on the strongest economic footing since 2019; the conflict reset that progress entirely.

On the reform side, the IMF has welcomed parliament’s passage of amendments to the bank resolution law as a “major step,” but IMF representative Federico Lima warned that “effective implementation is critical” and that further alignment with the FSDR law and international principles is still needed. Banking-sector governance remains contested. Without a functioning resolution framework for the 50+ insolvent banks, depositor recovery — and any IMF deal — remains a distant prospect.
