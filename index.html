⚖️ World · Syria

Syria sentences Assad and Maher to death in absentia — first convictions against the regime since it fell in December 2024

A Damascus court convicted former President Bashar al-Assad, his brother Maher al-Assad and their maternal cousin Atef Najib of war crimes, crimes against humanity, murder and the torture and detention of children, handing down death sentences on August 11. It is the first judicial ruling against any member of the Assad inner circle since the family’s 50-year grip on power ended with their flight to Moscow. Najib — who commanded the Daraa crackdown that sparked the 2011 uprising and the civil war that followed — was the sole defendant physically present, appearing in a cage. Syria’s transitional government has formally requested Russia extradite the brothers; Moscow has not responded. The sentences are symbolic in the immediate term but carry significant weight for international justice accountability mechanisms.

🌊 World · Iran — Hormuz Update

Update — IEA warns global oil stockpiles at lowest since 2025; 4% supply deficit deepens; oil slick spreads off Qeshm Island as Hormuz talks falter again

The IEA’s August 12 monthly report found global oil inventories fell by 2.2 million barrels per day in July, dropping total world stocks below 7.9 billion barrels for the first time since early 2025 and nearly doubling the projected H2 supply deficit to 1.8 million barrels per day. The IEA now expects global supply to fall 4.3% in 2026 due to the Hormuz disruption. Separately, satellite imagery and geolocated video from August 10 show large oil slicks extending more than one kilometre off Iran’s Qeshm and Hengam islands — polluting protected mangrove habitats in a potential environmental liability issue that Iran has attributed to US Navy operations. Omani-mediated talks have again stalled over new Iranian conditions; Brent settled at $87.18 on Friday as traders priced in an extended supply gap through Q3.

🕯️ World · Iran — Human Rights

Iran executes 23-year-old Marzieh Nieri for wounding a man who tried to assault her — 14th woman put to death in Iran in 2026

Marzieh Nieri, 23, was executed on August 14 at Qazvin Central Prison after a court rejected her account that she had stabbed a man in self-defence when he attempted to sexually assault her. Amnesty International and Iranian human rights groups immediately condemned the execution, noting a pattern of Iranian courts refusing to recognise women’s self-defence claims in assault cases. Rights monitors report at least 14 women have been executed in Iran so far in 2026 — the highest annual rate in at least a decade — occurring against the backdrop of the Hormuz standoff, which has so far not generated meaningful pressure on Tehran’s domestic repression. The case has drawn renewed international attention to the conditions that sparked the 2022 Mahsa Amini uprising.

📈 World · US Markets & Inflation

US July CPI: inflation 3.4% annual, core 2.5% — both softer than expected; S&P 500 closes at a record 7,799 as Fed rate-hike pressure eases

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on August 12 that US consumer prices rose 3.4% annually in July — down from 3.5% in June — with the monthly gain just 0.1%, softer than consensus. Core CPI fell to 2.5% annually, its lowest since May. The print reduced pressure on the Federal Reserve ahead of its September meeting, where markets now broadly expect rates to remain unchanged. The S&P 500 responded by closing at a new all-time record of 7,799, with the Dow at 53,840. Analysts note that the Hormuz-driven energy shock, which had complicated the Fed’s 2026 calculus, appears to be cresting at the consumer level, with oil price pass-through slower than feared in spring models.

🌱 Africa · Nigeria Economy

Nigeria reform dividend arriving: inflation down from 33% to 23%, naira stable, West Africa GDP growth at 4.7% — the most settled macro in two years

PwC’s 2026 West Africa Economic Outlook and new African Development Bank data confirm that Nigeria’s economic reform programme — launched under President Tinubu in 2023 — is delivering its first sustained macro gains. Consumer inflation has eased from a peak of 33.2% to approximately 23%, driven by tighter monetary policy, exchange-rate stability and lower food prices. The naira appreciated roughly 6% against the dollar in 2025 and has held those gains through 2026, supported by higher oil export revenues and FX reforms. Nigeria’s GDP is projected to grow 4.1% in 2026, within a West Africa regional average of 4.7%. The country’s debt service-to-revenue ratio remains elevated at 45% — the primary fiscal vulnerability — but the overall picture represents the best operating environment for Nigeria-based businesses since 2024.

🇱🇧 Lebanon
📜 Lebanon · Landmark Reform

Lebanon abolishes the death penalty — first Arab country to do so; parliament vote on August 11 ends a 22-year de-facto moratorium

Lebanon’s parliament voted on August 11 to formally abolish capital punishment, replacing it with life imprisonment with aggravated hard labour as the maximum sentence. The country becomes the first in the Arab world to take this step — ending a de-facto moratorium that had been in place since the last execution in January 2004, during which 85 people remained on death row. The legislation had cleared the Parliamentary Human Rights, Justice and Joint committees in successive votes since February before passing the full chamber. Amnesty International described it as “a landmark victory” and called on other Arab states to follow Lebanon’s example. PM Nawaf Salam’s government, which has framed constitutional and legal reform as a signature commitment of its first year, welcomed the vote as evidence that the new political order can deliver substantive change.

Amnesty International · Al Jazeera · The National · US News · OHCHR
🛡️ Lebanon · Security Update

Update — Rome Round 7 concluded August 6 without breakthrough; LAF formally complains of continued IDF violations; next talks not before September

Lebanon’s seventh round of US-mediated talks with Israel in Rome ended on August 6 without agreement on the two core sticking points: Israel’s withdrawal timeline from five retained positions in the south, and Lebanon’s demand for full sovereignty restoration before any Hezbollah disarmament concessions. The Lebanese Armed Forces issued a formal complaint this week that Israeli occupation forces continue attacks and demolitions in southern border villages agreed for pilot-zone hand-off — compromising the LAF’s ability to deploy. Israel’s defence minister publicly stated the IDF will maintain operations in Lebanon indefinitely. US interlocutors described both sides as “not yet aligned” and no date has been set for a next round before early September, with further delays likely as the Israeli political calendar tightens.
