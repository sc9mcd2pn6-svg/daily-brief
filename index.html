🧮 World · AI & Science

OpenAI Claims to Have Solved 372 Major Unsolved Maths Problems — Scientists Alarmed

OpenAI published 722 manuscripts on GitHub last week covering 372 families of mathematical results across 17 fields, from number theory to operator algebras. Claimed breakthroughs include a proof of the Unique Games Conjecture — one of the deepest open problems in computational complexity — the four-dimensional Kakeya conjecture, and claimed progress toward the Riemann hypothesis. All results come from an internal model OpenAI has not publicly released or named. The Economist calls it the “end of mathematics” and asks what happens when AI solves things faster than humans can verify. Mathematicians describe themselves as “outraged”: prompts, model identity and per-problem compute are all withheld, making independent verification impossible.

The Economist warns other disciplines — drug discovery, materials science, engineering simulation — face the same impending verification crisis. None of the 722 papers has cleared peer review; OpenAI acknowledges “verification levels vary.” But the sheer volume and the claimed depth of the results, if even partly confirmed, mark a turning point in how AI intersects with knowledge-creation. The paper release follows OpenAI’s earlier disputed claim to have solved the Navier-Stokes equations, which is still contested.

NYT Investigation: America’s Elected Officials Are Living in Fear

A major New York Times investigation published Monday finds that violent threats against American public officials — school board members, local judges, state legislators, federal representatives — have become a “new normal,” reshaping private lives and pushing experienced people out of office. The phenomenon cuts across party lines. Update: France Protests Enter Week 4 — Lecornu Offers Concessions; 6,629 Arrests Total

France’s lycée student protest movement, now in its fourth week, shows no sign of collapse as universities and high schools across the country remain blockaded. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu has shifted from confrontation to partial accommodation: he has announced a suspension of classes for a half-day of “internal democracy” consultations at each school, launched a ministry platform that has received over 40,000 student contributions, and announced measures to reduce fuel costs for young commuters. The total arrest count since the movement began in mid-September stands at 6,629. Lecornu defended law and order before the National Assembly but explicitly rejected any charge of police repression.

President Macron has convened a crisis meeting at the Élysée with senior ministers. Both Marine Le Pen and Jean-Luc Mélenchon are openly exploiting the crisis for 2027 presidential positioning. Students cite chronically overcrowded classes and a decade of under-investment in national education; teachers’ unions have mobilised in solidarity. With Macron unable to call early elections and Lecornu unlikely to survive a motion of censure, the government’s room for manoeuvre is narrow.

🏭️ Lebanon · Economy

IMF October Mission: Bank Law Passes Muster, but Financial Gap Law Remains the Last Hurdle

The IMF has confirmed it views Lebanon’s banking restructuring law — passed by parliament in August 2026 — as broadly consistent with international standards, a significant acknowledgement. However the Fund is still monitoring President Aoun’s constitutional challenge to one article of the law; if the constitutional council strikes it, the bank law reverts to parliament and reform progress unravels. The Financial Gap Law — the $72 billion loss-allocation measure — remains without a scheduled parliamentary vote and is the last major gate before a staff-level IMF agreement. The Fund also requires a VAT increase from 11% to 12%, a compliant deposit recovery bill, and a medium-term fiscal framework.

Finance Minister Jaber maintains that Lebanon aims to sign a staff-level deal; the IMF’s own public language is considerably more guarded. Eurobonds continue to hold at roughly 25 cents on the dollar — the market is pricing in optionality, not certainty. Every additional month of delay compounds the pressure on Lebanese banks holding government paper and narrows the window for diaspora investors weighing re-entry.
