Anthropic files for IPO at potential $2 trillion valuation — $16.3B in revenue, $807B in compute commitments

Anthropic, the five-year-old San Francisco AI company behind Claude, published its S-1 prospectus Monday, revealing $16.3 billion in revenue in the first half of 2026 alone — a 14-fold increase year-on-year. AI – Economist

China copies American AI — but model distillation may not be the threat it looks

The Economist devotes a major piece to China’s strategy of training its large language models by distilling from America’s frontier ones — using outputs of models like Claude and GPT-4 to train cheaper Chinese alternatives. Anthropic and OpenAI want to cut this “rope”, arguing it hands China an unfair shortcut. The analogy used: America is a speedboat, China the water-skier benefiting from its wake.

The Economist’s analysis cuts against the alarm: it argues distillation may not be as good for China as it appears, because copied models inherit the limitations of the model they copy, not just its strengths. China’s firms must still train on the outputs they can access, not on the weights themselves, which limits how far they can go. Still, export controls and model licensing are tightening fast, and this race will shape the AI-infrastructure investment landscape for years.

Source: The Economist (Oct 26 2026)

France – Update

Update: Nine days to France’s general strike — SNCF service maps published, rail paralysis confirmed

Sortiraparis and Connexion France confirm that all four SNCF rail unions (CGT-Cheminots, UNSA-Ferroviaire, SUD-Rail, CFDT-Cheminots) have now filed their November 5 strike notices, and SNCF has published its preliminary service-disruption maps. High-speed TGV services will be heavily curtailed; suburban RER lines in Paris face minimum service schedules. Schools, post offices and many municipal services will also be affected. The government of Prime Minister Lecornu has said it will require a minimum service declaration from all transport operators.

The five-union coalition is demanding a 10% minimum-wage rise and price freezes on fuel and essential goods. Talks broke down last week with no new government offer. The CGT has signalled it may call a follow-on indefinite strike if the one-day action fails to move the government — a sharp escalation risk for business continuity across France.
