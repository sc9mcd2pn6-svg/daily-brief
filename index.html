France Rolls Back Pension Reform Today — Retirement Age Drops for 1964–68 Birth Years

Effective today, September 1, the suspension of France’s controversial 2023 pension reform takes force: workers born between 1964 and 1968 are now entitled to retire three to six months earlier than the Macron-era changes required, restoring their qualifying trimester counts to pre-reform levels. The measure, passed as a parliamentary compromise, is a partial rollback of the reform that triggered widespread strikes and street protests across France in 2023. It does not affect those born after 1968, leaving the broader retirement-age debate unresolved.

Separately, France’s firefighters and public-sector unions have called a joint national day of mobilisation for September 29 to demand resources in the wake of the country’s record summer drought (92 departments under water restrictions). The combination of pension relief and restive unions signals that France’s government faces a politically turbulent autumn alongside its ongoing budget consolidation effort. No direct impact on business conditions in Lebanon, but noteworthy for a French-born CEO who retains ties to the French business community.

NYC’s Office-to-Residential Conversion Wave “Quickens Sharply”
