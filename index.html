Sweden’s Centre-Left Edges to Razor-Thin Win; Far Right Loses Ground

Sweden held general elections Sunday, September 13, and with nearly 90% of votes counted the centre-left opposition bloc led by Social Democrat Magdalena Andersson held a one-seat majority in the 349-seat Riksdag over incumbent PM Ulf Kristersson’s right-wing alliance. The anti-immigration Sweden Democrats, once the kingmakers, saw their vote share slip — a notable reversal after years of rising far-right support across Europe. Both Andersson and Kristersson claimed readiness to govern, and final certified results are expected today; the margin is too tight to call a decisive mandate for either bloc.

The NYT notes that with various crises — from the pandemic to energy shocks — easing somewhat, centre-left politics appear back in fashion across Scandinavia, though the far right cannot be discounted once governing difficulties emerge. Sweden is also the first Scandinavian country to vote after the Iran-driven energy shock reshaped European energy politics, and the result will be closely watched for what it signals about voters’ priorities as the EU heads into its own budget negotiations.

🏭 Lebanon · Economy / Finance

Lebanon Banking Reform Advances; IMF Welcomes Depositor-Access Law as ‘Major Step’

The IMF has welcomed Lebanese parliament’s amendments to the bank resolution law as a “major step,” describing it as a key requirement for Lebanon to access international funding and bring government debt out of default. Under the framework, losses from Lebanon’s 2019–2021 financial collapse are to be shared between the state, the Central Bank, commercial banks, and depositors — with depositors able to retrieve up to $100,000 over four years. L’Orient Today reports the cabinet has also adopted the 2026 budget bill, framed as a revenue-collection rather than a tax-raising measure, in the context of an ongoing fiscal crisis.

The reform trajectory is cautiously positive: a banking law is on the books, a budget has been adopted, and the IMF’s engagement signals growing confidence that Lebanon’s new government is serious about reform. However, execution risk remains very high — Lebanon has announced reform programs before without following through — and the Lebanese Eurobond BLOM Bond Index is down 0.3% to 29.57 points this week, reflecting continued investor caution about fiscal strains and the ongoing security situation.
