Germany Votes Today: AfD Polling at 41% in Saxony-Anhalt — Far-Right Could Make Historic Breakthrough

Saxony-Anhalt holds its state election today, Sunday September 6, with the Alternative for Germany (AfD) leading polls at 41% — its strongest projected result in any German state since the war. The CDU trails at 22.5%, with Die Linke at 12%, SPD at 7.9% and the Greens at 5.5%. The AfD is projected to win around 39 of 83 Landtag seats, making it by far the largest party. The NYT calls this “a threat to postwar Germany’s taboo on extremists in power.” Results are expected from around 8 pm local time.

All mainstream parties have pledged not to govern with the AfD — Germany’s so-called “firewall.” If that holds, CDU must cobble together a minority government with left-leaning parties, an extraordinary arrangement. If any mainstream partner breaks ranks, far-right governance of a German state becomes a reality for the first time since 1945. CSIS analysts note that a breakthrough here would accelerate pressure on the firewall at the federal level ahead of Germany’s scheduled 2028 election.

Update: Iran Emerges More Confident After Six Months — New Intelligence Finds Tehran Has Mapped US Power Limits

New US intelligence assessments, leading the NYT today, conclude that Iran now has “a far better understanding of its capabilities and the limits of US power” after six months of war. Tehran has calculated that prolonging the conflict inflicts maximum political damage on Trump before November’s midterms, making a deal very hard to reach. Meanwhile, US CENTCOM confirmed it permanently disabled two Iranian oil tankers and destroyed a third at Kharg Island’s anchorage on September 5 — hitting Iran’s ability to fund the war through oil exports. The Strait of Hormuz remains contested; Washington has ruled out talks until ship attacks stop.

Six months in, the conflict has hardened into an attrition dynamic. Iran’s IRGC continues to attack US bases in Jordan and the UAE; the US is striking Iranian oil infrastructure in retaliation. Brent crude at $96.28 reflects a risk premium that shows no sign of easing before the US midterms in November.

Pentagon Gives Polygraph Tests to ~50 Joint Staff Members in Unprecedented Leak Hunt

The US Defence Department has administered polygraph tests to approximately 50 members of the Joint Staff in an unusually broad internal investigation, the NYT reports. The leak hunt is directly tied to news reports on the Iran war — specifically reporting on US munitions shortages, classified strike plans and the real state of US readiness. About 50 people were tested, a scope the NYT calls “unparalleled.” The investigation follows Pete Hegseth’s purge of 80+ flag officers and reflects a Pentagon in deep internal crisis: leaking to the press to expose what they believe is dangerous mismanagement.

The leak investigations come as Army Secretary Driscoll’s resignation last month left the service leaderless during active wartime. Pentagon sources describe a culture of fear, with officers uncertain whether candid internal assessments will result in firing or prosecution. Democratic senators have called for a congressional investigation into the Hegseth purges and their impact on combat readiness.

Trump: “Stop Trading” With Deficit Nations Unless Fed Cuts Rates — Markets Fell; September Decision Now Critical

President Trump posted an explicit ultimatum on Truth Social Friday after August’s strong jobs report (162,000 added, 4.1% unemployment): the US would “STOP TRADING WITH COUNTRIES WITH WHICH WE HAVE A DEFICIT” if the Federal Reserve does not cut rates. He called on Fed Chair Kevin Warsh to “BE PATRIOTS for a change.” Markets reacted immediately: the Dow fell 260+ points, the Nasdaq and S&P 500 also slipped, closing Friday at their lowest levels since mid-August. The dollar strengthened sharply, pushing EUR/USD down to 1.1610 and gold down to $4,380.

An unusually broad pressure campaign hit the Fed last week: Trump, Vance, Treasury Secretary Bessent and a senior White House economic counsellor all publicly urged Warsh not to raise rates. This is the most explicit attempt to tie US trade policy to monetary policy in modern US history. The Fed’s September meeting will now be watched globally for signs of capitulation or independence.

NYT (anchor, 5 Sep) · CNBC · Scotsman Guide · Philadelphia Inquirer
Why it matters to you

If the Fed bends and cuts rates, a weaker dollar follows — which would reduce Lebanon’s dollar liquidity premium but help dollar-priced import bills. If Warsh holds or hikes, the dollar stays strong and US equities risk a sharper sell-off that reduces global risk appetite for emerging-market business. Either way, September’s Fed decision is a macro hinge point for dollar-exposed businesses like yours.

Nigeria Cholera Crisis Surpasses 65,000 Cases in 2026; NCDC Issues Urgent Warning as Water Infrastructure Fails

Nigeria’s National Centre for Disease Control has issued a fresh alert as confirmed cholera cases in 2026 surpassed 65,000, with fatalities reported across multiple states including Lagos. The outbreak is directly linked to widespread water infrastructure failure: collapsed treatment plants, ageing pipelines and the inability of state governments to provide consistent clean water supply have created conditions for endemic disease spread. The crisis echoes warnings raised earlier in the year when the World Health Organisation flagged Nigeria’s water system as critically under-maintained.

For Lagos specifically — Nigeria’s commercial capital and home to Quasar Nigeria’s operations — the outbreak compounds an already difficult environment of flooding, power outages and road infrastructure decay. Businesses operating in the city are increasingly forced to maintain independent water supplies and on-site health provisions for their workforce. NCDC is urging employers and building operators to accelerate water safety protocols.

UNIFIL December Exit Confirmed: Troop Count Could Fall From 7,000 to Under 1,000 — EU Mission Race Against Time

UN Security Council Resolution 2790 has confirmed UNIFIL’s December 31 mandate expiry as final — no further renewal. The force, which peaked at 15,000 personnel, has already drawn down to approximately 7,000. UN Secretary-General Guterres has presented three post-expiry options to the Security Council: a residual force of 900, 2,000 or 3,000 troops. None of these approaches the current level; Lebanon faces 11 months of intensive diplomacy to secure a credible replacement framework before December. LBCI reports that the security vacuum is already visible in south Lebanon, where UNIFIL patrol frequency has declined sharply.

The EU is advancing a parallel security mission proposal, but its mandate, rules of engagement, contributing nations and funding remain completely undefined. France, Italy and Spain are in principle supportive, but formal commitments have not been made. The 8th round of Israel-Lebanon Rome talks this month is now critical: without a security framework agreed between Beirut and Tel Aviv, even a residual force has no operational basis.

Security Council Report · DIIS · Washington Institute · Al Jazeera · LBCI
Why it matters to you

A security vacuum in south Lebanon as UNIFIL exits — compounded by ongoing IDF operations and undefined EU mission parameters — is the single largest near-term risk to Beirut real-estate confidence, client project timelines and insurance premiums on commercial properties. Watch the Rome talks this month as the clearest signal of where this goes.

Act on this: Flag to Sami: the December UNIFIL exit window is now four months away. Begin scenario planning for how a security escalation would affect your client base in south Beirut and the Bekaa — and review whether project contracts in exposed areas have appropriate force-majeure clauses.
📈 Lebanon · Economy

IMF Resumes Technical Meetings in Beirut; Goldman Sachs Says Eurobond Valuations Are Stretched

The IMF has confirmed it will resume technical meetings in Beirut next month to evaluate further structural policy measures, following parliament’s passage of the amended bank restructuring law in August. The IMF has described the amendment as “a major step,” but stressed that significant gaps remain in governance and shareholder burden-sharing. The meetings will focus on the 2027 government budget and banking-sector capital adequacy. This signals cautious progress on the road to a formal IMF program — still a multi-year journey.

On the market side, the BLOM Bond Index (BBI) is trading near its highest level since March 2026, around 29.7. However, Goldman Sachs has issued a note warning that Lebanese Eurobond valuations “look overly optimistic” given continuing macro deterioration: the World Bank forecasts -6.4% GDP contraction in 2026 and inflation at 17.5%. Goldman notes that yields around 61% already price in a highly optimistic restructuring scenario that the political environment does not support.

IMF statement · BLOMINVEST · World Bank · Goldman Sachs (via Reuters) · L’Orient Today
Why it matters to you

The IMF resuming technical talks is a genuine positive signal for Lebanon’s reform path — but Goldman’s caution is worth keeping in mind: bond prices may be ahead of the underlying fiscal reality. For Ralph’s prime Beirut real estate, the direction of travel is constructive (recovery narrative), but the pace is slow and risks remain tied to the UNIFIL/security situation and the banking sector’s actual resolution.
