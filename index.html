Iowa Army detonates 9,700 lbs of lead azide TODAY — toxic cloud could affect 350 farms and 148 sq miles; residents evacuated

The US Army is carrying out a planned controlled detonation this morning of a cache of lead azide explosive stored at a military site in Iowa. The cache — approximately 9,700 lbs of deteriorating World War II-era primary explosive — was deemed too unstable to transport and must be destroyed in place. Lead azide is acutely toxic: when detonated, it generates a lead-bearing dust cloud that can settle across a wide area and contaminate soil, water and crops.

Authorities have warned that the toxic cloud could potentially affect up to 350 farms and 148 square miles downwind, depending on weather conditions. Residents within a 1-mile blast radius were evacuated before the detonation. The NYT’s evening newsletter on 24 November flagged the event as happening “tomorrow morning” and Today’s Headlines confirmed it for Wednesday 25 November.
