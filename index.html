US – Israel – Iran

US and Israel quietly draw up plans for renewed Iran strikes after elections

The New York Times reports that the US and Israel have jointly developed contingency plans for a new military campaign against Iran, targeting nuclear facilities, energy infrastructure and other strategic sites. The Pentagon has told US Central Command to be ready to resume military operations. Prime Minister Netanyahu briefed President Trump on the rationale — Iran’s alleged efforts to rebuild nuclear capacity — though Trump has not made a final decision and has publicly said he would refrain from striking before the Israeli vote on October 27.

Timing is complicated by two electoral calendars: Israel votes next Tuesday, and the US midterms follow on November 3. One Israeli military source warned that renewed fighting could force a postponement of the Knesset election. Anonymous US sources confirm options have been drawn up for action before or after the midterms, with nuclear, infrastructure and energy sites among the targets. The situation is fluid, with sources disagreeing on whether planning has been paused or remains live.

Source: The New York Times Today’s Headlines (22 Oct 2026); Axios; i24 News

Your Lebanon business and Quasar Nigeria both operate with dollar-priced inputs. A second closure would spike your cost base and freeze Gulf investment flows into Beirut and Lagos simultaneously.

US Midterms

Democrats lead by 11 points — but the Economist says history warns the wave will shrink

A new NYT/Siena poll finds Democratic congressional candidates leading Republicans by 11 percentage points nationally, with President Trump’s approval at a new low two weeks before the midterms. Reliably red Iowa is now in play: Trump flew there Thursday hoping to stop farmers from defecting to Democratic candidates, after the Iowa Farmers Union — one of the state’s oldest farm organisations — backed the entire Democratic congressional slate. The Economist is less bullish. Its number-crunching warns that predictable polling errors repeatedly overestimate Democratic margins, and the actual midterm result may fall well short of the blue wave the polls currently suggest. The magazine also notes that student protests have mushroomed across Europe, exposing “bleak views of the future” among youth — a pattern that may resonate with younger American voters in a different direction.

Gulf – Sudan – Yemen

Economist cover: the Saudi-UAE “petty feud” is fuelling catastrophic wars in Sudan and Yemen

The Economist’s cover package this week exposes the Emirati-Saudi rivalry as a key driver of the world’s most devastating conflicts. Riyadh, coordinating with Egypt, backs Sudan’s national army; Abu Dhabi backs the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces. The same dynamic plays out in Yemen. The Economist calls it “a petty feud with horrific consequences” and says it is “prolonging and deepening deadly wars.” The rift has already led to Saudi airstrikes on Emirati-backed forces in Yemen and the evacuation of all UAE personnel from the country.

Separately, Thursday the Iran-backed Houthi militia struck a major Saudi airport, killing at least two people and wounding others. The US ordered some of its diplomatic staff to leave the Kingdom in response. This is the sharpest Houthi-on-Saudi escalation in months and coincides with rising US-Iran tensions over the nuclear file.

Source: The Economist This Week (22 Oct 2026); The New York Times Evening (22 Oct 2026); South China Morning Post

Why it matters to you

Gulf capital has been one of the main drivers of Beirut real estate recovery and West African infrastructure investment. Pakistan – Afghanistan

Pakistan-Afghanistan “open war” has killed nearly 500 civilians — UN confirms devastating toll

The United Nations Assistance Mission in Afghanistan (UNAMA) reports that nearly 500 Afghan civilians have been killed and over 1,200 wounded in Pakistani airstrikes and shelling since October 2025. The deadliest single incident was a March 2026 Pakistani strike on a Kabul drug treatment centre, which Amnesty International is investigating as a possible war crime. Pakistan declared it was in “open war” with Afghanistan in February following an Afghan cross-border raid. The NYT featured this conflict today as a growing “volatile element” in global stability.

The latest strikes on October 1 killed at least 10 civilians, mostly children, in Helmand and Kunar provinces. Pakistan claims it is targeting militant hideouts. With both countries now engaged in proxy group warfare and neither willing to de-escalate, the UNAMA calls the humanitarian situation critical. The conflict adds a sixth active war front to a world already managing Gaza, Ukraine, Sudan, Ethiopia-Eritrea and Yemen.

Source: The New York Times Today’s Headlines (22 Oct 2026); Arab News; UNAMA report (Oct 2026)

Brazil

Update: Brazil presidential runoff Sunday — Lula now the underdog as right-wing votes consolidate

Brazil votes Sunday, October 25 in a presidential runoff between incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and Flávio Bolsonaro (son of Jair Bolsonaro, who is serving 27 years for plotting a coup). The first round gave Bolsonaro roughly 47% and Lula 45%. The Economist now characterises Lula as “the underdog”: the third through fifth-place candidates were all right-wing and their voters are expected to consolidate behind Bolsonaro. The magazine also flags that Brazil’s economy “needs serious reform” whoever wins, noting that capitalists briefly excited about Bolsonaro may find his economic record disappointing.

The result will matter for West Africa: Brazil has deep economic ties with Lusophone African nations and its political direction influences trade, investment and diplomatic positioning across the Atlantic. A Bolsonaro presidency would likely tilt Brazil toward the Trump alignment, affecting multilateral trade frameworks.

Source: The Economist Today (22 Oct 2026); WUNC / NPR (4 Oct 2026)

Health Emergency

Ebola reaches Kenya: Congo’s record-breaking epidemic now crosses into a third country

Congo’s Bundibugyo-strain Ebola outbreak — now the second-largest in history with more than 9,400 confirmed cases and over 4,500 deaths — has spread beyond Uganda and into Kenya, where at least eight cases have been confirmed. Kenya’s first case was a man who had travelled from Congo to Nairobi; subsequent cases include a patient attendant at his facility. The outbreak has now affected eight provinces within DRC and crossed into two neighbouring countries, making this the widest geographic footprint of any Ebola outbreak on record, the NYT reports.

The outbreak began in May 2026 and has been exceptionally difficult to contain given the conflict zones in eastern Congo. In the past 24 hours to October 16, Congo recorded 67 new cases and 26 deaths. The cross-border spread to Kenya — a major transport and business hub for East Africa — has alarmed health officials. West Africa, including Lagos, has not yet been affected, but WHO has activated enhanced surveillance at all major West African ports and airports.

Source: The New York Times Evening (22 Oct 2026); Daily Maverick (19 Oct 2026); PBS NewsHour / polity.org.za (16 Oct 2026)

Why it matters to you

Quasar Nigeria operates in Lagos, a West African transport hub with daily connections to Nairobi. While Lagos is currently unaffected, a further westward spread would immediately affect staff safety protocols and potentially disrupt project site work. The early Kenya cases emerged from a Nairobi hospital, not a rural village — this is urban spread, which travels faster.

Act on this: Ask Quasar Nigeria management to confirm they have a staff health-risk protocol and are monitoring WHO alerts; make sure the Lagos team has emergency evacuation contacts up to date.

France & Europe

Update: Economist cover “The Agony of Liberal Europe” — protests spread to 7+ countries; Le Pen path to presidency opens

The Economist devotes its cover this week to the collapse of Europe’s political centre, with France as the epicentre. What began as student protests in Seine-Saint-Denis has now reached at least seven European countries: Albania (students burning the Education Ministry in Tirana), Italy (two consecutive nights of looting in Milan), Portugal (hundreds of thousands march over housing costs), Belgium, and others. The NYT confirms young protesters across Europe share “bleak views of the future” rooted in high prices, poor job prospects and dilapidated public services.

The Economist argues that Europe’s centrist governments — France, Germany, Spain, Italy — have “fallen dangerously short” of what voters need, and the alternative is populist parties that “promise goodies from the magic money tree” without viable economic plans. In France specifically, Marine Le Pen now has a clear path to the 2027 presidency; the Economist calls her victory “plausible.” Meanwhile PM Lecornu’s government cannot meet protesters’ demands due to France’s already-high public debt. The November 5 general strike remains confirmed.

Source: The Economist This Week (22 Oct 2026); The New York Times Today’s Headlines (22 Oct 2026); The Star Malaysia / AFP

AI & Finance

AI compute becomes the new oil: CME launching GPU futures, financial firms race to trade processing power

The Economist this week reports that AI computing power is becoming a tradeable commodity, with major financial firms racing to create derivative instruments to price and trade GPU processing capacity. CME Group has planned the launch of H100 and B200 GPU futures contracts — the first of their kind — while Kalshi has already been running prediction markets on GPU rental prices since March 2026. The Economist frames this as “AI compute is the new oil”: projected 2026 AI capital spending of ~$765 billion now surpasses estimated global oil and gas capex.

The market faces a fundamental pricing problem — the same Nvidia H100 rents for anywhere from $1.79 to $10 per hour across different providers, with no widely accepted benchmark — but the financialisation of compute is now underway regardless. For anyone supplying infrastructure to data centres (power, cooling, physical security, BMS), the emergence of AI compute as a financial asset class signals that data-centre demand is structural, not cyclical.

Source: The Economist Today (22 Oct 2026); Kalshi research whitepaper (Oct 2026); CME Group (Oct 2026)

Why it matters to you

If AI compute is being financialised like oil, the data centres that house it are the new refineries — long-duration, high-security assets that institutional capital will compete to supply. This strengthens the investment thesis for your Lagos data-centre push (Quasar Nigeria) and for Quasar’s positioning as the fire suppression, security and BMS provider of choice to those facilities.

Act on this: Review your Lagos data-centre prospect list and prioritise the hyperscale and carrier-neutral facilities where you have no relationship yet; the institutional money coming into this space makes them more creditworthy counterparties than they were 12 months ago.

Lebanon – Economy

Lebanese Eurobonds hold firm near 25 cents despite reform delays — IMF November window still alive

Lebanese Eurobonds have held broadly firm at approximately 25 cents on the dollar despite the October IMF mission ending without a staff-level deal and the Financial Gap Law remaining unvoted in parliament. L’Orient Today reports that bond prices held “despite security concerns and the lack of reforms” — the market appears to be pricing in some probability that the November parliamentary session, confirmed as the last credible window for a 2026 IMF deal, will deliver a vote on the law. If parliament meets the November deadline, a sharp upward re-rating of Eurobonds is possible.

The IMF’s key outstanding demand remains a revised Financial Gap Law with a clear hierarchy of losses — determining how losses are distributed among the state, the central bank, commercial banks and depositors. Prime Minister Salam has acknowledged the IMF wants “clearer provisions.” The central bank governor expects a breakthrough by year-end; the Finance Minister is currently abroad. A slip to 2027 would test market patience and risk a reversal of the recent Eurobond stability.

Source: L’Orient Today (Oct 2026); Blominvest Bank research (Oct 2026); Arab News

Why it matters to you

The Eurobond market holding near 25 cents is one of the few positive signals for Lebanon’s investment climate. If the Financial Gap Law passes in November, credit conditions will ease, Gulf capital will return faster, and your FGC contracts tied to commercial reconstruction could benefit significantly in Q1 2027. Keep your ear to the ground on the parliamentary calendar.

Act on this: Track the Lebanese parliamentary session schedule for November; a Financial Gap Law vote would be a meaningful positive catalyst for Beirut real estate and construction activity.
