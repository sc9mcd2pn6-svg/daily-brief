UN Officially Abandons 1.5°C Target: Planet Tracking 2.6°C; Strategy Shifts to “Navigate the Overshoot”

The UN Environment Programme published an assessment on Wednesday that is the first by a UN agency to unambiguously state that the 1.5°C Paris target will be breached — and that the breach is “unavoidable” with some effects “irreversible.” Based on current national policies, the planet is tracking 2.6°C warming by 2100. Rather than preventing the overshoot, UNEP says strategy must now shift to “navigating it” — limiting the extent and duration of the overshoot, then reversing it through large-scale atmospheric carbon removal technologies that are still largely unproven at scale. Climate-vulnerable nations, led by small-island states, reacted with dismay.

The NYT reported simultaneously that scientists believe the Nepal glacial flood that killed 469+ in August was most likely caused by bedrock failure — weakened by warming-driven permafrost thaw and meltwater — releasing more than seven billion cubic feet of rock and ice. Geomorphologists warn such events will become more frequent as temperatures rise.
