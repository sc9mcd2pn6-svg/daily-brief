Energy – Middle East

Oil hits $116 a barrel as Houthis strike Saudi Aramco HQ and Riyadh airport

Global oil prices surged to $116 a barrel on Friday, the highest level since April, after Houthi forces launched strikes on the Saudi Aramco headquarters in Riyadh and the city’s international airport. The attacks, the most audacious on Saudi infrastructure since 2019, rattled global supply expectations and added a sharp premium to Brent crude already elevated by tensions over a possible US-Israel strike on Iran. US oil prices also topped $100 a barrel during the session.

The price spike rippled through global equity markets, where it compounded AI financing anxieties to send the Dow Jones down 652 points on Friday. Saudi authorities confirmed the attacks and said defences intercepted some projectiles; casualties were reported. The episode signals Houthi confidence and an escalating proxy war between Iran-backed forces and Riyadh.

Source: The New York Times Evening (23 Oct 2026); L’Orient Today (23 Oct 2026)

Why it matters to you

Brent at $116 drives up diesel costs for every generator-dependent client in Lebanon and Nigeria. If the Strait of Hormuz scenario resurfaces (as it did in April), your dollar-priced project budgets tighten on both sides of the business simultaneously.

Act on this: Review any fixed-price supply or installation contracts with diesel clauses; flag to Sami as a potential repricing trigger for open proposals in both markets.

Israel – West Bank

Israel launches West Bank offensive hours before tomorrow’s election

Palestinian gunmen killed two Israeli soldiers in Hebron on Friday, and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu immediately vowed to carry out “a major operation” in the neighbourhood the attackers came from, invoking the same aggressive military model used in the Jenin and Tulkarm refugee camps. The announcement comes less than 24 hours before Israeli voters go to the polls on Saturday, October 25, with Netanyahu facing former military chief Gadi Eisenkot who is leading in most polls.

Eisenkot, described by The New York Times as almost the polar opposite of Netanyahu in temperament, calls the prime minister an “anti-leader.” The West Bank offensive announcement is widely read as an election-eve move. Violence in the occupied territory has been escalating for weeks; this year’s Palestinian olive harvest has been the most violent in recent history, with armed settlers launching hundreds of attacks. Arab parties may hold kingmaker status if no bloc reaches the 61-seat threshold.

Markets – AI

Wall Street’s worst week in months: Dow −652 points, AI debt fears combine with oil shock

US stocks closed sharply lower on Friday, capping a punishing week. The Dow fell 652 points (−1.23%) to 52,215; the S&P 500 slipped to 7,624 (−0.52%). The selloff was concentrated on Thursday, when the S&P fell 1.4% and the Nasdaq slumped 2.5% — its biggest single-day decline since June — as worries over the enormous financing costs required to sustain AI investment spending spooked the market. The Friday session was then hit again by the Aramco attack and oil surge, with energy and airline stocks leading declines.

Congress is also beginning to legislate around the possibility that AI could put large numbers of Americans out of work, adding to long-term uncertainty. Markets are looking to the Federal Reserve’s policy meeting next week for direction. On the week, the S&P 500 shed 0.5% and the Nasdaq lost 0.7%.

Source: The New York Times Today’s Headlines (23 Oct 2026); TheStreet; Yahoo Finance (23 Oct 2026)

US Politics – Military

Military boards defy Hegseth: 9 of 12 review boards vote to retain transgender troops

Despite Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth’s explicit and often crude orders to remove transgender service members (“No fatties. No trannies. No beardos. No weirdos,” he said last month), uniformed members of Navy and Marine Corps review boards have voted to retain transgender troops in nine of twelve cases. The pattern has not yet emerged in the Army or Air Force. The votes represent a striking institutional rebuke from a culture where following orders is fundamental.

The outcomes underline a growing civil-military tension in Washington over the Pentagon’s culture-war directives. Hegseth has not commented directly on the board decisions. Legal challenges from discharged service members are expected to reach federal courts in the coming weeks.

Source: The New York Times Evening (23 Oct 2026)

Science – Health

Merck & Moderna: cancer vaccine cuts melanoma recurrence risk by 42%

In a landmark announcement, Merck and Moderna reported detailed results showing that patients with melanoma who received their experimental mRNA-based vaccine were 42% less likely to experience a cancer recurrence within two years compared to those who did not receive it. The results provide significant new hope after years of effort to develop a vaccine that trains a patient’s immune system to attack their own cancer cells.

Researchers are now investigating whether the same approach could work against glioblastoma (a severe brain cancer) and pancreatic cancer. The vaccine is personalised — manufactured from each patient’s tumour — meaning it is not yet a mass-market product, but the results are seen as a breakthrough proof of concept for mRNA oncology.

Source: The New York Times Today’s Headlines and Evening (23 Oct 2026)

Finance – Crypto

Sam Bankman-Fried speaks from prison: “I have paid my debts” — eyes Trump pardon

From a low-security federal prison in Los Angeles, FTX founder Sam Bankman-Fried gave a rare interview to The Economist, claiming he has paid his debts and that the crypto exchange’s creditors have been made whole. He said he hopes either the Supreme Court might free him or the Trump administration might issue a pardon. Bankman-Fried was the most prominent figure in the effective altruism movement before FTX collapsed in 2022 and he was convicted of fraud.

The interview is the first time he has spoken publicly at length since his sentencing. He offered no apologies and maintained that his actions were motivated by a desire to do good. The Economist published a companion story and podcast episode.

Source: The Economist Today (23 Oct 2026)

Lebanon – Security

Israel threatens Beirut’s southern suburbs as ceasefire grows more fragile

Israel issued fresh warnings of strikes against Hezbollah positions in Beirut’s southern suburbs (Dahieh) on Thursday, while Houthi forces — coordinating with Iran’s axis of resistance — struck Aramco’s headquarters in Riyadh and the Saudi capital’s airport. The fragility of the ceasefire and the proximity of the Israeli election (Oct 25) mean any miscalculation could trigger a rapid escalation. Lebanese officials are urging continued US engagement to hold the line.

Lebanon – Politics

Hezbollah claims smuggled explosives cache; army disarmament process under scrutiny

Hezbollah publicly claimed ownership this Friday of a quantity of explosives intercepted by the Lebanese Army in southern Lebanon, insisting the state should return them. The claim is an open challenge to the army’s disarmament mandate under the ceasefire terms and comes as both the US and France push Beirut to show measurable progress on the security framework before the next round of talks.

The episode illustrates the limits of the army’s leverage: it can intercept materiel but lacks the political authority to destroy or retain items Hezbollah declares its own. The Beirut port blast indictment is also reportedly near a decision, adding to political tensions within the government.

Nigeria – Data Centres

Digital Parks Africa announces carrier-neutral data centre NDC1 in Lagos

Digital Parks Africa, linked to South Africa’s Master Power Technologies Group, has announced a new carrier-neutral facility — Nigeria Data Centre 1 (NDC1) — in Lagos, unveiled at the ITW Africa conference in Nairobi. The facility targets enterprises needing reliable local infrastructure and is positioned as an alternative to the hyperscale campuses being built by Equinix and Airtel’s Nxtra. Lagos now has at least four major data centre projects at various stages, with operators reporting that energy costs have surged 64% since January 2026.

The power constraint remains the defining challenge: NDC1’s success depends on how Digital Parks Africa resolves its energy sourcing. Earlier projects, including Tetracore’s 100 MW off-grid gas plant, have gone fully off-grid to sidestep the unreliable national grid. The carrier-neutral model, however, creates a natural market for low-current systems integrators to add fire safety, access control and BMS services.

Source: TechAfrica News; w.media (Sept–Oct 2026)

Every new data centre in Lagos is a potential Quasar Nigeria contract — fire suppression, FM200 systems, CCTV, BMS. NDC1 at the carrier-neutral tier is exactly the size of project where your team can be competitive on fire safety and low-current systems without going up against the largest hyperscale integrators.

France – Labour

Update: Nov 5 general strike on track — rail, schools and transport to be paralysed

France’s November 5 general strike is confirmed after a government meeting with union leaders on October 21 failed to produce any concessions. Five unions — CFDT, CGT, FO, Unsa and FSU — are calling a national mobilisation on the date, which coincides with the US midterm elections. Rail unions (CGT-Cheminots, UNSA-Ferroviaire, SUD-Rail and CFDT-Cheminots) have filed strike notices for TGV, Intercités and regional services, while Paris RER and Transilien status remains to be confirmed.

Prime Minister Lecornu, who invoked emergency powers last week after France’s worst riots in decades, has said security conditions must be met before the march. The unions reject preconditions. Art Basel Paris, which opened this week at the Grand Palais, has drawn international buyers despite the unrest, but the Nov 5 disruption is expected to be far broader than last week’s violence.
