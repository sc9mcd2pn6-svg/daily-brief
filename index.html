Turkey has set three priorities: climate finance for developing countries, the food-water-climate nexus, and youth education — though observers note the presidency’s credibility is under scrutiny given Turkey’s own record on fossil-fuel use. COP31 runs alongside the first full week of the new Democratic-controlled lame-duck Congress, whose members are themselves divided on how aggressive to be on climate legislation before January.

Economy

Financial Gap Law deadlocked as IMF mission lands in Beirut — GDP to contract 6.4% in 2026

IMF staff are in Beirut this month for a critical round of technical discussions, but the precondition for any formal programme — passage of the Financial Gap Law allocating losses between the state, the central bank, commercial banks and depositors — remains stuck in parliamentary limbo. The Finance and Budget Committee has met repeatedly without advancing the draft: the Banque du Liban is pushing for carve-outs to the hierarchy-of-claims provisions that the IMF has explicitly said it will not accept. EnterpriseAM and L’Orient Today report the IMF has made an appropriate Gap Law “the last obstacle” to a full programme. Without it, the Banking Resolution Law passed in August — welcomed by the IMF as a “major step” — remains unenforceable.

The World Bank and IMF both project GDP to contract 6.4% in 2026 after a 4.2% expansion in 2025, driven by the continuing banking-sector freeze, low public investment and the lingering impact of the regional conflict. BLOMINVEST puts total losses owed to banks and depositors at around $79 billion. With no programme, Lebanon cannot access the external financing that would allow the state to invest in public infrastructure, unblock credit to businesses and stabilise the pound.
