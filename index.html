🌍 World
📉 World · US Economy

US national debt crosses $40 trillion for the first time — doubling in a decade as Iran war spending and tax cuts accelerate the overshoot

The US Treasury announced Wednesday that America’s national debt has surpassed $40 trillion for the first time in history, landing at exactly $40,047,425,768,420.22 — having doubled in under a decade. The milestone arrived months earlier than forecasters expected, driven by two compounding forces: the billions in new military and logistical spending on the Iran war, and the package of Republican tax cuts enacted last year. The 30-year US Treasury bond yield hit its highest level since 2007 this week, pushing bond prices down and raising borrowing costs across the economy. For the first time in modern history, the US government now spends more on servicing its debt than on national defence or Medicare. The Peterson Foundation estimates the debt could hit $50 trillion within six years absent reform. The New York Times noted that interest payments alone already exceed the size of the US defence budget, framing this less as a political argument and more as a fiscal arithmetic problem that compounds regardless of who governs.

🚀 World · Iran & Gulf — Escalation

Iran fires two ballistic missiles at UAE; UAE halts all trade and financial transactions with Iran — Gulf conflict spreads beyond Hormuz

Iran launched two ballistic missiles toward the UAE on Tuesday in the Islamic Republic’s first known attack on a Gulf state since May, with UAE air defences intercepting the threat. Hours later, the UAE announced a complete halt to all trade and financial transactions with Iran — an extraordinary economic rupture that, if sustained, closes one of the last remaining Arab diplomatic and commercial channels with Tehran. The escalation marks a dangerous new phase: the conflict, which began as a Hormuz shipping dispute, has now spilled into a direct Iran-Gulf state confrontation. Iran denied having targeted the UAE, but the UAE stated its air defences detected and responded to two incoming missiles. Brent extended its gains on the news. Trump posted an image describing the Strait of Hormuz as “New US Territory” and confirmed there are “no talks, no conversations ongoing or scheduled” with Iran.

⚓ World · Hormuz — Revealed

Revealed: US Navy has secretly escorted more than 1,000 tankers through Hormuz since May — five million barrels a day moving with transponders off

The New York Times reported Wednesday that since May, US military forces have been covertly shepherding commercial oil tankers through the southern part of the Strait of Hormuz, assisting more than 1,000 ships and enabling approximately five million barrels of crude oil per day to flow out of the Persian Gulf — down from 15 million barrels before the war, but enough to prevent a total supply collapse. Ships moved under US protection have sailed with their Automatic Identification System transponders switched off to avoid targeting. The operation has never been publicly confirmed by the Pentagon. Officials said the effort has helped keep oil prices from climbing even further than the current Brent level above $91. The disclosure is significant: it means global oil supply at current levels is entirely dependent on a sustained and secret US military commitment — one that any escalation, political decision or asset loss could terminate overnight.

🕊️ World · Gaza

Israel strikes Gaza café, killing civilians a day after Kushner visit — peace plan doubts deepen as Hamas holds firm on withdrawal demand

One day after Trump envoy Jared Kushner visited Israel to advance the stalled Gaza disarmament roadmap, Israeli forces struck a café in Gaza, killing civilians while the military said it had targeted Hamas commanders. The strike deepened doubts about whether any peace process is viable: Hamas continues to insist it will not proceed with any disarmament steps until Israeli forces withdraw to pre-October 2025 ceasefire lines, while Israel’s government has said categorically it will not withdraw before full Hamas disarmament. The sequencing impasse, which has existed since the Board of Peace announced the roadmap last month, has now survived the expiry of a 14-day implementation deadline without any movement. US officials have not announced an extended deadline or any consequence for Israeli non-compliance with the withdrawal condition — a silence that Hamas and outside observers read as effective backing for Israel’s position.

🤝 World · Trade & Tariffs

Trump claims US-Canada trade deal is near; tariffs on hold as Carney says “important work remains”

President Trump said Tuesday he believes the US and Canada have come to a trade deal, citing “intense talks” and announcing a pause on tariffs against Canadian goods while negotiations continue. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney was notably less definitive, saying publicly that “important work remains” — a phrase that reads as a polite rebuff of Trump’s characterisation. Key sticking points reportedly include dairy access, softwood lumber, the auto sector and energy pricing. A genuine deal would significantly de-risk North American supply chains and reduce the broader tariff-escalation cycle that has added to global inflationary pressure. Trump also separately hailed progress on trade talks with several other countries, consistent with his “deal of the week” communication style that has made each announcement harder to price into markets.

💉 World · Science & Health

Merck and Moderna’s personalised mRNA melanoma vaccine meets Phase 3 goals — 49% fewer recurrences, filing for approval within months

Moderna and Merck announced Wednesday that their personalised mRNA cancer vaccine, intismeran autogene, combined with Merck’s Keytruda immunotherapy, met both primary and secondary endpoints in a landmark Phase 3 trial of 1,137 patients with high-risk melanoma: a 49% reduction in the risk of recurrence or death, and a 59% reduction in the risk of distant metastasis, both compared to Keytruda alone. The vaccine is built from a sample of each patient’s own tumour, targeting mutations specific to that individual using the same mRNA platform that produced COVID vaccines at speed. No new safety signals emerged. The companies said they plan to file for regulatory approval within months, which could make this the first personalised mRNA cancer vaccine approved anywhere in the world. Moderna’s stock surged on the news. The Economist’s Buttonwood column this week separately cautioned that the S&P 500 and major indices no longer accurately reflect the real equity market — making Moderna’s stock surge harder to interpret through conventional benchmark lenses.

Nigeria: Adeleke wins Osun governorship re-election by 66,000 votes — APC loses its second South-West contest of 2026

The Independent National Electoral Commission declared Ademola Adeleke of the Accord Party the winner of Saturday’s Osun State governorship election, with 511,067 votes to the APC challenger Bola Oyebamiji’s 444,815 — a margin of 66,252 votes. 🇱🇧 Lebanon
⚖️ Lebanon · Banking Reform — Landmark

Lebanon parliament passes FSDR banking reform law — most consequential financial legislation since the 2020 collapse opens IMF path

The Lebanese parliament voted to pass the amended Financial Stabilization and Depositors Recovery (FSDR) law with little resistance, completing a process that began with the Constitutional Council’s annulment of earlier provisions in October 2025 and the finance committee’s revised articles approved this week. The law addresses the approximately $82 billion gap between deposit liabilities and actual bank assets, assigning shared responsibility to the state, the Banque du Liban and commercial banks — the fundamental accounting exercise that the IMF has insisted Lebanon complete before unlocking any Extended Fund Facility. With the law now on the books, Lebanon moves from a reform-intent country to a reform-on-paper country: the next test is whether the IMF considers the passed text sufficiently un-diluted to activate formal programme negotiations. The BLOM Bond Index closed the week of August 6 at 28.72 (yield 61.12%), up 19.26% year-to-date, suggesting bond markets had been pricing in exactly this kind of legislative progress.

L’Orient Today (independent) · The New Arab · BLOMINVEST · Enterprise AM
Why it matters to you

A clean FSDR passage is the single most important precondition for Lebanon’s economic stabilisation. If the IMF validates it and moves toward a formal programme, you should expect: (a) gradual return of some dollar liquidity to Lebanese banks; (b) renewed confidence in the pound; (c) reopening of larger corporate credit lines that would make clients able to finance bigger system contracts. The timeline is 12–24 months if the process moves cleanly, but the passage itself ends years of deadlock.

Act on this: Flag this to your Lebanese management team. If the IMF responds positively within 4–6 weeks, revise your Lebanon revenue projections for 2027 upward and begin preparing proposal templates for contracts that clients previously could not finance.
🛡️ Lebanon · Security — Update

Update — UNIFIL: 137 projectiles fired per day and 1,700 airspace violations in south Lebanon — civilian toll mounts as December mandate exit looms

UNIFIL’s latest operational data (August 5–16) documents an average of 137 projectiles fired daily across the southern Lebanon zone of operations — the highest sustained rate since the June ceasefire — and more than 1,700 instances of Israeli airspace violations in Lebanon between June 21 and August 11, predominantly by drones, helicopters and fighter aircraft. Al Jazeera reported that “civilians are paying the price as Israeli military activity rises.” UNIFIL itself described the pace of violations as extraordinary. With the UN mission’s mandate set to expire December 31 under last August’s Security Council resolution, no successor framework has been formally agreed: Germany’s proposed EU peacekeeping force has gained support from Lebanon and some European troop-contributing countries, but has not been adopted by the Security Council, and Israel has not endorsed any replacement arrangement.

UNIFIL · Al Jazeera · Atlantic Council · LBC Group
Why it matters to you

The December 31 date is 133 days away. Without a successor security framework, Beirut real estate values and any construction or systems contracts in the south face a sharp risk-off event in Q4. The FSDR passage helps the economy; the UNIFIL vacuum could undo confidence gains quickly if the December handover is bungled.

🖥️ Real Estate · Data Centres & Property

Data centre tax windfall: Loudoun County, Virginia will collect $1.3 billion — 40% of its entire tax base — from server farms in 2027

Loudoun County, Virginia — home to the world’s largest concentration of data centre infrastructure — is projected to receive $1.3 billion in tax revenue from data centres in 2027, representing approximately 40% of its total county tax base, the New York Times reported Wednesday. Officials and some residents say they fear the county has become perilously dependent on a single industry for its fiscal stability. The figure illustrates the extraordinary property and business tax impact of hyperscale computing clusters on surrounding real estate markets: data centres directly inflate commercial land values, industrial zoning premiums and nearby residential prices while simultaneously crowding out other uses. The pattern is instructive for Lagos, where the buildout is still in its early phases but where the same fiscal and real-estate dynamics will eventually play out — and where land near landing stations and power substations is the scarce input.
