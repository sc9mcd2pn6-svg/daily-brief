🚢 World · Red Sea / Iran War (Update)

Update: Saudi Oil Pipeline Struck by Iraqi Drones; Oil Briefly Hits $110 as Iran Tightens Grip on Both Export Routes

Iraqi militia groups launched drone attacks on a major Saudi oil pipeline, igniting a 60-mile plume of smoke visible in satellite imagery; Riyadh shut the pipeline as a precaution, briefly pushing Brent crude to $110 per barrel before it eased to ~$104 as markets absorbed news of a potential Iran-Gulf diplomatic meeting in Oman. The attack comes on top of Houthi forces having seized Perim Island at the mouth of the Bab el-Mandeb Strait and the port of Mokha, giving Iran-aligned forces effective control or threat over both primary oil-export routes from the Arabian Peninsula simultaneously — a strategic encirclement not previously achieved.

The New York Times reports that Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman now faces a stark choice: escalate a military campaign that has failed for years or accept that Iran-backed forces hold unprecedented leverage over his kingdom’s export lifeline. BRICS leaders — which include both Iran and the UAE — avoided the conflict in their joint communiqué. Oil is up 17% over the past month and 55% year-on-year; shipping war-risk insurance for Red Sea transits has reached multi-year highs as Red Sea oil loadings fall from 3.8 to 2.2 million bpd.

📈 World · Economy / Markets

US Inflation Holds at 3.4% Annually; Monthly CPI Rises 0.4%; Fed Rate Hike Expected Tuesday

The August US Consumer Price Index showed annual inflation at 3.4% — well above the Federal Reserve’s 2% target — with monthly gains of 0.4%, the strongest monthly rise in three months. The 10-year US Treasury yield touched 4.97%, near the psychologically significant 5% threshold last seen in October 2023, reinforcing market expectations of a Federal Reserve interest-rate increase at its September 15–16 meeting. Higher energy costs driven by the Middle East oil shock are a key contributor to the sticky inflation picture. The Social Security COLA for 2027 is now estimated at 3.6% — the largest since 2023 — underscoring that inflation remains politically embedded ahead of November elections.

US equity markets snapped a four-session losing streak on Friday: the Dow gained 0.98% to 52,573 and the S&P 500 rose 0.86% to 7,657, as the CPI print met headline consensus and oil retreated from its $110 spike. Gold also dipped as investors took some safe-haven profit. The broader macro picture — sticky inflation, elevated yields, approaching Fed hike — continues to tighten financial conditions globally.

NYT (anchor) · Reuters · Rio Times
Why it matters to you

A Fed rate hike tightens dollar borrowing costs globally and keeps Lebanon’s Eurobond restructuring expensive. It also reinforces the strong-dollar environment that makes dollar-denominated equipment more costly for clients paying in Lebanese lira or Nigerian naira — which can stall project sign-offs.

☢️ World · Geopolitics

Economist: Putin, Trump and Xi Are Losing Their Dread of Nuclear War — a Dangerous New Normal

The Economist’s Saturday Telegram column sounds a striking alarm: the shared terror of nuclear annihilation that led American and Soviet leaders to pursue arms-control agreements during the Cold War has broken down among today’s nuclear-armed leaders. Vladimir Putin has made repeated thinly veiled nuclear threats since 2022 without meaningful consequence; President Trump has discussed tactical nuclear options openly; and Xi Jinping is rapidly expanding China’s arsenal while refusing standard transparency protocols. Without the Cold War’s stabilising horror — which produced INF, START and the Non-Proliferation Treaty — humanity is drifting toward nuclear brinksmanship with fewer guardrails than at any point since the Cuban Missile Crisis.

The column comes on the 25th anniversary of 9/11, a reminder of how asymmetric threat perceptions can reshape global security in ways that take decades to play out. The Economist warns that the erosion of nuclear norms is happening gradually, without a single triggering moment, making it harder to build political will for course-correction before a miscalculation occurs. The preheader to the newsletter asks pointedly: are world leaders insufficiently horrified by their own firepower?

🏭 World · Trade

India Wants to Cut Chinese Imports but Keeps Needing More as Bilateral Deficit Nearly Doubles in Five Years

Trade between India and China has nearly doubled over the past five years, according to the New York Times, leaving New Delhi with a widening trade deficit now exceeding $100 billion annually — a record — and a difficult industrial dilemma. Despite repeated political pledges to reduce dependence on Chinese electronics, solar panels, pharmaceuticals and industrial machinery, Indian manufacturers cannot source alternatives at competitive cost or scale. The deficit is growing even as India builds domestic manufacturing through Production Linked Incentive schemes. The result: deep economic interdependence with a country India simultaneously treats as a border adversary and diplomatic rival.

The tension is geopolitically significant: New Delhi is trying to navigate between strategic partnership with the US and the Quad on one side, and essential trade ties with China on the other. Analysts note that India’s strategy of engaging China economically while building military and diplomatic hedges is increasingly strained as the numbers grow — and as both countries are represented in the same BRICS bloc, now also including Iran.

Digital Parks Africa Enters Lagos Data Centre Race With New Carrier-Neutral Facility NDC1

Digital Parks Africa announced on September 11 the development of Nigeria Data Centre 1 (NDC1), a new carrier-neutral facility in Lagos targeting financial institutions, cloud providers and connectivity companies requiring resilient infrastructure close to West African markets. The announcement was made at the ITW Africa conference in Kenya, underscoring Lagos’s consolidating status as the region’s primary digital infrastructure hub. While investment figures and capacity were not disclosed, NDC1 targets the same Tier III/IV institutional-grade market as Equinix ($22m LG3), Open Access Data Centres ($240m, 24MW) and MainOne — a competitive field that is growing fast as cloud demand expands.

Lagos data centre operators are drawn by Nigeria’s large domestic market, improving subsea cable connectivity, and the broader West Africa AI-infrastructure wave. Premium Times (independent) reported separately that the United States delivered military equipment via C-17 to Nigeria this week for counterterrorism use, signalling continued US strategic engagement with Nigeria’s security environment — a stability factor that data centre operators weigh when assessing Lagos-market risk.

🏢 Real Estate · Data Centres / Asia

Economist: Malaysia Is Enjoying a Massive Data Centre Boom — Could It Escape Its Middle-Income Trap?

The Economist’s Saturday edition highlights Malaysia’s extraordinary data centre construction surge as a potential economic breakthrough. Cumulative capacity could reach 8–9 gigawatts by 2030 — a RM128–162 billion addressable market for the domestic mechanical and electrical sector alone. Services sector GDP grew 5.9% in Q2 2026 driven by ICT, and construction grew 6.5%, with data centre development a primary driver. Microsoft, Google and Amazon are actively building megacampuses in Springhill, Serendah and Johor, with 3–4 new project rollouts expected in H2 2026. Malaysia began restricting non-AI data centre investments in February 2026 to preserve power and water for the most sophisticated facilities.

The magazine argues that the global AI infrastructure race is redrawing Asia’s investment map, and Malaysia — like Singapore, Indonesia and India — is competing for hyperscaler capex. The broader wave creates a real opening for West Africa: Lagos is increasingly cited as an alternative hub for workloads not requiring lowest-latency to East Asia. The M&E market opportunity the data centre boom creates in Malaysia — fire suppression, mechanical cooling, electrical infrastructure — is instructive as a template for what Lagos can become.

Why it matters to you

The global data centre boom is the biggest single demand driver for fire suppression and integrated security in commercial construction today. Malaysia’s RM162bn M&E market illustrates the scale; Lagos’s equivalent is smaller but far less competitive. This is the market Quasar Nigeria should be systematically building into now.

🇱🇧 Lebanon
⛰️ Lebanon · Security / Politics

President Aoun Makes Rare Visit to South Lebanon Amid Ongoing Israeli Military Presence

Lebanese President Joseph Aoun made a rare and symbolically significant visit to Nabatiyeh in southern Lebanon on Saturday, near a strategic hilltop recently captured by Israeli forces. The visit underscores that Israeli military activity in southern Lebanese territory continues under the contested post-ceasefire framework — a situation that has displaced hundreds of thousands of residents and remains a central sticking point in any permanent settlement. Over 4,000 people were killed in Lebanon in the latest Israel-Hezbollah conflict, with hundreds of thousands having returned to the south but facing persistent Israeli military activity in border areas.

The security situation continues to cast a shadow over Lebanon’s fragile economic recovery. Parliament’s two-year mandate extension (passed March 2026) has delayed elections and kept the reform process on a slow track. L’Orient Today documents both the human toll in the south and the political paralysis hampering reconstruction in Beirut’s southern suburbs. Any new escalation between Israel and remaining Hezbollah elements would immediately threaten the recovery trajectory.

🏭 Lebanon · Economy / Banking

Lebanese Eurobonds Slip Again; IMF Welcomes Bank Resolution Law but Lending Remains Near Zero Six Years After Collapse

Lebanese Eurobonds weakened this week, with the BLOMINVEST BLOM Bond Index (BBI) dipping 0.3% to 29.57, as fiscal strain, the Israeli occupation of southern territory and Red Sea shipping disruption weighed on investor confidence. The IMF publicly welcomed Lebanon parliament’s passage of amendments to a bank resolution law as a “major step” toward a formal programme. But L’Orient Today reports that commercial bank lending remains near zero six years after the financial collapse — housing loans have almost vanished, and SMEs face effectively blocked access to credit. The banking sector plays only a marginal role in the economy despite carrying the unresolved losses of the crisis.

The still-unpasssed Deposit Recovery Law — needed to unlock the full IMF programme — remains the critical bottleneck. Bank Audi research flagged that the 2026 economic outlook is highly fragile: 4–5% growth if the security situation holds, but potential contraction if conflict escalates or spreads. The BDL (Banque du Liban) is attempting to open credit channels pending full restructuring, but progress is incremental against a backdrop of systemic distrust.

Lebanon’s banking sector remaining functionally locked means your clients continue financing projects from personal liquidity or diaspora transfers — both constrain deal size and payment pace. 🇫🇷 France
🇦🇳 France · International / Politics

France Holds UN Security Council Presidency in September; Nationwide Public-Sector Strike Called for September 29

France assumed the rotating presidency of the United Nations Security Council for September 2026, deploying the role to push for Council action on the Iran war, the Red Sea shipping crisis and Ukraine. French diplomats are organizing ministerial-level sessions on the Middle East conflict, seeking to position France as a mediating force. The presidency coincides with one of France’s most challenging domestic economic periods: official 2026 growth has been revised to just 0.5% and the public deficit is expected to exceed 5% of GDP, putting France back under European Commission fiscal surveillance.

At home, the main public-sector unions — representing approximately six million workers including firefighters, teachers and transport staff — have called a nationwide strike for September 29 over resource cuts and the pension-reform dispute. Measures suspending France’s pension reform took effect September 1, allowing earlier retirement for those born 1964–1968. Macron’s coalition controls neither the Assemblée Nationale nor the Sénat, making any budget deal a political high-wire act as the autumn parliamentary session opens.

🎾 Sport · US Open Grand Slam

Rybakina Claims US Open Women’s Title; Zverev vs Shelton Men’s Final Today — American Drought May Finally End

Elena Rybakina won the 2026 US Open women’s singles title on Saturday, defeating defending champion Aryna Sabalenka 6–4, 5–7, 6–2 in a high-quality three-set final at Arthur Ashe Stadium. It is Rybakina’s third Grand Slam title overall and her first US Open crown, reinforcing her position as the dominant player of the season. Sabalenka fell short of a historic third consecutive title at Flushing Meadows.

The men’s final takes place today between No. 1 seed Alexander Zverev and No. 8 seed Ben Shelton, who defeated compatriot Frances Tiafoe in Friday’s all-American semifinal — the first Black man since Arthur Ashe in 1972 to reach the US Open final. A Shelton victory would end a 23-year drought for American men at Grand Slams, with Andy Roddick’s 2003 US Open title the last. Zverev, seeking his second major, enters as favourite but faces the full weight of the American crowd behind Shelton.
