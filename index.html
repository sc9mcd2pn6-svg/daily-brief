White House

Trump elevates Stephen Miller to chief of staff — Susie Wiles ousted after midterm rout

President Trump is replacing Susie Wiles with Stephen Miller, his domestic policy adviser and the driving force behind the administration’s immigration crackdown. Wiles — the first woman to hold the top White House staff role and instrumental in Trump’s 2024 comeback — is the highest-ranking official to leave in the wake of the midterm losses. Trump has reportedly blamed her for the Republicans’ rout. Miller had made known his interest in the job; his pitch to Trump, per NYT White House correspondents, was that the president needed to wind down the Iran war.

Miller is widely regarded as the most powerful policy staff member in modern White House history, deeply ideological and uncompromising on immigration. His elevation signals Trump is doubling down on his agenda rather than moderating after the electoral rebuke. Also in play: Pete Hegseth posted a defiant video as impeachment talk grows among Democrats and at least one Republican; and Elon Musk, who spent nearly half a billion dollars on the midterms, has little to show for it, per a NYT investigation.

Source: NYT The Evening (6 Nov); NYT Today’s Headlines (6 Nov)

Israel

Israel: President Herzog announces Sunday who gets first crack at forming a coalition

With last month’s parliamentary elections producing no clear winner — Netanyahu’s bloc and the opposition each tied at roughly 51 seats — President Isaac Herzog will announce on Sunday which politician gets the mandate to form a government. Opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot, endorsed by the most party leaders including Liberman, Bennett and Golan, is the likeliest candidate, but his path is far from clear. He could try to form a coalition with Arab-led parties or seek a unity deal with Netanyahu, but either move would break campaign promises made during the election.

Arab parties have backed Eisenkot but set conditions, and Eisenkot has publicly ruled out a formal coalition with Arab lawmakers — leaving him potentially short of a majority. Netanyahu’s associates argue a unity government is the only stable outcome. Knesset members are sworn in on November 10 regardless. The deadlock reflects a deeply fractured Israeli electorate after two years of the Iran war and ongoing conflict.

Source: NYT The Evening (6 Nov); Al Jazeera (2–3 Nov)

Geopolitics

Lame-duck Trump turns abroad — Economist warns of an embattled president with vast foreign powers

American foreign policy, already radical and hyperactive, could become even more so in Trump’s post-midterm lame-duck phase, The Economist warns. Constrained at home by a Democratic Congress that can now block legislation and investigate his administration, Trump retains enormous discretion over foreign affairs — troop deployments, diplomatic deals, sanctions, tariffs — none of which require Congressional approval. The Economist’s analysis says the world should be worried: a president seeking a legacy, free from re-election pressure, may move faster and less predictably on Iran, Ukraine and China.

Atlantic Council analysts flag that any strike on Iran would most likely come during this lame-duck window, before Democrats are seated in January. Meanwhile Stephen Miller’s stated pitch — that Trump should wind down the Iran war — could either restrain or accelerate action depending on Trump’s mood. For US allies and business partners across the Middle East and Africa, the next 14 months may be the most volatile of the Trump presidency.

Source: Economist Today (6 Nov); Atlantic Council; Roll Call (Sep 2026)

Why it matters to you

A sudden US move on Iran — military or diplomatic — would immediately hit Lebanon’s ceasefire fragility, Hezbollah’s calculus, and your clients’ confidence in Beirut. Watch closely. If Trump accelerates Iran pressure, any ceasefire extension could collapse fast.

Economy

Surprisingly strong jobs report: 165,000 added, unemployment drops to 4.1%

US employers added 165,000 jobs in October — well above the weak September figure of 29,000 and enough to drop the unemployment rate to 4.1%, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Friday. The NYT calls it “a surprisingly good jobs report” that reaffirms the general picture of the labour market as healthy. Wage growth, however, remained tepid, and job seekers — especially recent college graduates — still face limited entry-level opportunities.

Notably, NYT reports signs that AI advances are depressing hiring: Microsoft recently offered buyouts to 60,000 workers (more than a quarter of its entire workforce), and employees say the offer landed “amid mounting expectations to use AI.” The job market is therefore healthy in aggregate but hollowing out at the base. The report landed the same day markets staged a partial rebound, suggesting the economy can still absorb political turbulence.

Source: NYT The Evening (6 Nov); Bureau of Labor Statistics (6 Nov)

UK & Law

Russell Brand found guilty of rape and sexual assault in London — “significant custodial time” ahead

A London jury found Russell Brand, the comedic actor and conspiracist YouTuber, guilty of rape and sexual assault at Southwark Crown Court. The judge stated Brand would receive “significant custodial time,” with sentencing expected shortly. Brand had pleaded not guilty to seven counts including three counts of rape, three of sexual assault and one of attempted indecent assault. The jury had deliberated since November 4.

The verdict closes a years-long saga of allegations first published in 2023 by the Sunday Times and Channel 4. Brand’s conspiratorial online persona — built on a following of millions — continued to draw both defenders and critics through the trial. Under UK sentencing guidelines, rape carries a range of four to 19 years; Brand’s sentence is expected to be at the higher end given the multiple counts.

Source: NYT Today’s Headlines (6 Nov)

Health & Risk

Ebola reaches Nigeria as outbreak spreads — WHO warns this could be the worst ever

Nigeria has confirmed its first Bundibugyo Ebola case, a traveller from Uganda who was hospitalised in Abuja and later medically evacuated to Switzerland. Two additional suspected cases in northeast Nigeria were under investigation as of late October, results pending. Kenya has confirmed nine cases including two deaths, with 135 contacts traced. The source outbreak in the DRC has now recorded more than 10,500 confirmed cases and 5,100 deaths — the Economist calls it potentially the worst Ebola outbreak in history. New cases in Kenya and Nigeria show it is spreading unchecked across borders.

WHO and African CDC have deployed rapid-response teams, but containment has been complicated by the DRC’s ongoing security crisis in the east. Cross-border trade routes through Uganda and Kenya are under heightened surveillance. For Lagos, the confirmed case is in Abuja (not Lagos itself), but Nigeria’s porous internal movement means the risk to the commercial capital is real and rising. The NCDC is urging heightened screening at airports and land borders.

Source: Economist Today (6 Nov); Al Jazeera (20–31 Oct); EWN; ECDC

Quasar Smart Solutions operates in Lagos — this is a staff-safety and business-continuity issue. If the outbreak reaches Lagos, project site work, client meetings and equipment delivery could all be disrupted by travel restrictions or government-mandated protocols. Brief the Lagos team now and confirm their emergency protocols are current.

Act on this: Flag to the QSS Lagos team today. Ask them to confirm health protocols are in place and to monitor NCDC daily bulletins. If a Lagos case is confirmed, review whether any November site visits should be postponed.

Cybersecurity

AI is turbocharging cyberattacks — firms are racing to defend, most are behind

The Economist this week covers a devastating wave of AI-powered cyberattacks, noting that hackers have weaponised generative AI to lower the cost of attacks dramatically. CrowdStrike found that 88% of publicly disclosed vulnerabilities were exploited within 48 hours during the first half of 2026 — a pace that outstrips most corporate patching cycles. The Economist’s analysis says the balance has tipped toward offence: BCG surveys found 60% of executives reported facing AI-enhanced attacks, but only 7% had deployed defensive AI at scale. Spending on corporate cybersecurity is forecast to rise 25% from 2024 to 2026 (Gartner).

Building-automation systems, security networks and BMS platforms are explicitly flagged as under-secured entry points by multiple security firms. The same protocols that allow remote monitoring of Quasar’s fire and security systems also create attack surface. The October Binance $6.3 billion crypto heist (attributed to North Korea’s Lazarus Group) demonstrated the scale of ambition now possible with AI-assisted attacks.

Source: Economist Today (6 Nov); CS Monitor (30 Oct); CrowdStrike; Gartner

Why it matters to you

Quasar and A&C sell and maintain networked fire, security and BMS systems. If a client suffers a cyberattack through a connected building system your firm integrated, the reputational and legal exposure is real. Cyber-resilience is becoming a sales differentiator in the data-centre segment.

Act on this: Ask Sami to review whether Quasar’s standard BMS/security delivery contracts include any cyber-liability clauses, and whether a cyber-hardening add-on (network segmentation, firmware updates, remote-access controls) can be packaged as a service offering.

Economy

IMF mission lands in Lebanon this month — GDP on track to contract 6.4% in 2026

An IMF senior official confirmed that Fund staff will conduct a November visit to Lebanon to review the reform agenda — the first formal mission since the Banking Restructuring Law was passed in August, which the IMF called “a significant step.” The World Bank’s Summer 2026 Lebanon Economic Monitor projects GDP will contract 6.4% this year, after growing 4.2% in 2025 — the conflict impact is severe. The IMF’s own forecast also points to a sharp 2026 contraction. The Financial Gap Law, which must pass parliament before the IMF can unlock a full programme, has still not been scheduled for a vote.

Bank deposits remain frozen and the banking sector is unable to provide credit to the economy. BLOMINVEST data puts the amount owed to depositors at approximately $79 billion, with no restructuring haircut agreed. Banque du Liban raised withdrawal limits again (reported 6 Nov) but analysts say this is a pressure valve, not a resolution. The IMF’s November engagement is the best near-term catalyst for unblocking the reform pipeline — if parliament can be induced to schedule the Financial Gap Law vote before year-end.

Source: Asharq Al-Awsat; World Bank Lebanon Economic Monitor (Aug 2026); BLOMINVEST; L’Orient Today snippets

Why it matters to you

No IMF programme means no sustained recovery, which keeps client budgets under pressure across Lebanon. The November mission is a potential inflection point: if the Fund gives a positive signal, Eurobond prices could rally further and investor confidence tick up — which benefits prime Beirut real estate and large-contract clients. Watch for any post-mission press statement.

Los Angeles

Nithya Raman elected LA Mayor — progressive City Council member ousts Karen Bass

Nithya Raman, a 45-year-old City Council member, defeated incumbent Mayor Karen Bass in the Los Angeles mayoral race — a sweeping success for a new generation of progressive urban leaders. The NYT calls it “a vote for change” by LA’s electorate, fed up with Bass’s handling of the January wildfires and the city’s homelessness crisis. Raman ran on a housing-first, transit-forward platform and will be one of the youngest mayors in Los Angeles history.

The result is part of a wider pattern from Tuesday’s midterms: voters in major US cities swung decisively toward reformist and progressive candidates, even as the national Democratic wave was largely driven by moderate candidates in swing districts (The Economist’s analysis). Brooklyn’s Sunset Park neighbourhood also mourned six people who died in a deadly apartment fire — the worst in New York City this year; Mayor Zohran Mamdani raised concerns about electric-bike battery fires.

Source: NYT Today’s Headlines (6 Nov); Economist Today (6 Nov)
