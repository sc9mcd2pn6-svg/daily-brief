World · Technology

Economist cover: Humanoid robots are coming — capabilities doubling every two months

This week’s Economist devotes its cover to the robot revolution. AI “brains” are now being merged with general-purpose android “bodies,” and by one measure physical-world capabilities are doubling every two months — faster than Moore’s Law at its peak. The magazine argues the revolution will arrive faster than any previous technological shift: robots will first handle domestic chores, then displace human builders, electricians and factory workers. One analyst cited inside warns that “if you thought rent was bad today, just wait until it forms most of measured economic output” — robot-made goods will become dirt cheap while land and energy become vastly more valuable.

The cover leader asks humans to prepare for a future in which androids are “vastly more capable” than today. Barclays Research separately estimates robots could fill up to 60% of China’s aging-workforce gap by 2035. Bank of America forecasts 3 billion humanoid units globally by 2060.

Source: The Economist (26 Nov 2026)

Why it matters to you

If humanoids can eventually install cables, suppression systems and BMS hardware the way human technicians do, Quasar and A&C will face labour-cost competition in their core business faster than most industries. The flip side: data centres and AI infrastructure needed to run robots are exactly the facilities that need your fire and BMS systems now. The trend accelerates demand for what you sell short-term even as it reshapes who installs it long-term.

Act on this: Keep Sami focused on data-centre BD leads in Lagos — that pipeline is building on the same AI wave driving the robot story.

World · Trade

Trump imposes 10–12.5% tariffs on dozens of countries as 10% stopgap expires

The Trump administration unveiled new tariff schedules covering dozens of countries — including the EU, Japan and several Asian trading partners — citing “unfair practices.” The move came as the earlier 10% blanket levy was set to expire, following a Court of International Trade ruling that struck down an earlier round. New tariffs range from 10% to 12.5%. The incoming Democratic-led Congress has signalled it will challenge the programme legislatively, raising uncertainty over how long the rates will hold.

Import-dependent sectors, including electronics and industrial equipment, are preparing for cost increases. Several trading partners have filed fresh WTO complaints. The administration is running the tariff programme through executive-branch mechanisms while the courts continue to review its legal basis.

Sources: NYT (26 Nov 2026); PBS NewsHour; Reed Smith Tariff Tracker

Why it matters to you

Quasar and A&C import fire-safety and BMS equipment mainly from Europe and Asia. If Lebanese customs applies updated tariff-equivalent rates, or if suppliers pass through cost increases on orders sourced from EU/Asian manufacturers, your import invoices could rise. Watch for any change in the customs valuation basis used by Lebanese authorities that references US tariff schedules.

World · Middle East

Israeli opposition sets Dec 6 hard deadline to oust Netanyahu — Eisenkot allies say he is on track

Avigdor Lieberman, an Eisenkot ally, told media on November 26 that Gadi Eisenkot is “on track” to meet his December 6 deadline to form a new Israeli government without Benjamin Netanyahu. The 26th Knesset was sworn in on November 10; the seat split puts Netanyahu’s bloc at roughly 53 seats and the opposition at 48, with 19 Arab-majority party seats as the decisive bloc. Netanyahu has called for a “broad national unity government” under his leadership, which the opposition has uniformly rejected. Ultra-Orthodox coalition partners are reported to be wavering in their alliance with him.

If Eisenkot succeeds, the new government would likely take a different approach to the Lebanon ceasefire framework, UNIFIL succession talks and the Iran-Gulf conflict posture. Failure by December 6 would likely trigger a no-confidence vote or new round of deadlock in the coalition negotiations.

Sources: NYT (26 Nov 2026); Al-Monitor (Nov 2026); Al Jazeera (Nov 2026)

Why it matters to you

A change of government in Israel by Dec 6 would reshape the political dynamic along Lebanon’s southern border just as UNIFIL prepares to exit on Dec 31. A new, more centrist Israeli government might be more willing to negotiate a successor security arrangement — reducing risk for Quasar’s Lebanese operations and clients in border-adjacent areas.

World · Europe

Spain votes Sunday — PP–Vox within reach of first right-wing majority in a decade

Spain’s general election takes place Sunday 29 November, polls open 09:00–20:00. The latest opinion surveys put PP and Vox together at roughly 50–51% of the vote and between 200 and 213 of the 350 Congress seats — a majority threshold of 176. Pedro Sánchez’s Socialists have recovered slightly in late polls but remain well behind. The election was triggered in October after Sánchez failed a confidence vote over a housing budget. Alberto Fejóo (PP) would lead a coalition government with Vox’s Santiago Abascal.

A PP–Vox majority would mark a sharp political shift in a major eurozone economy and could affect the ECB succession race: Spain’s candidate Pablo Hernández de Cos is backed partly by the outgoing Socialist government, and a new right-wing cabinet may withdraw support for his candidacy.

Sources: Reuters; Idealista; Ara.cat; Majorca Daily Bulletin (Nov 2026)

World · Europe · Finance

ECB: Lagarde exits February 7, 2027 — successor race enters December crunch

Christine Lagarde confirmed on 29 October that she will step down from the ECB presidency on 7 February 2027, three months before her term ends, to allow France time to pick a president before her departure. The European Council began the selection process immediately. Leading candidates are Klaas Knot (former Netherlands central bank head) and Pablo Hernández de Cos (Spain’s BIS general manager). The December EU summit is expected to reach an informal consensus. A PP–Vox victory in Sunday’s Spanish election could complicate Hernández de Cos’s candidacy.

The choice matters: the eurozone faces inflationary 2027 headwinds — the Economist projects 2027 as the hottest year on record, with El Niño responses proving inadequate — alongside fiscal pressures in France, Italy and others. The new ECB president will set rates during a critical period.

Sources: ECB press release (29 Oct 2026); Al Jazeera; Aberdeen Investments; GFMag

Lebanon · Security

Update: UNIFIL mandate confirmed Dec 31 — 34 days to exit, no successor force agreed

UN Security Council Resolution 2790 (August 2025) extended UNIFIL’s mandate “for a final time” to 31 December 2026 — now 34 days away. The resolution required the Secretary-General to propose successor security options by 1 June 2026; the report was delivered but no replacement force has been agreed in the Security Council. The Lebanese Army is widely described as overstretched and under-resourced. The EU has warned of a “security vacuum” and 86 Lebanese lawmakers wrote to the UNSC in July requesting a mandate extension, which was not granted.

Israel’s potential change of government (Dec 6 deadline) adds further uncertainty: a new Eisenkot-led government might negotiate differently with Lebanon on border arrangements. The formal UNIFIL drawdown of roughly 10,800 personnel and equipment begins 1 January 2027. An emergency extension remains theoretically possible if political conditions shift before year-end.

Sources: UN Peacekeeping; Al Jazeera; Times of Israel (Aug–Nov 2026)

Why it matters to you

The security vacuum in south Lebanon from January could affect client confidence and project tendering in regions near the border. Beirut and the core market are operationally unaffected for now. Monitor the December Security Council session closely — an emergency extension, while unlikely, is the most positive near-term scenario for south Lebanon stability.

Lebanon · Economy

Lebanon bank–MoF standoff deepens — Eurobond tax dispute, Dec 15 revised deadline, IMF program firmly in Q1 2027

The standoff between the Ministry of Finance and Lebanese banks over the Eurobond provisions tax remains unresolved. Banks continue their weekly Wednesday strike (25 Nov last week; 2 Dec next). Finance Minister Jaber extended the deadline for banks to revise their tax declarations to 15 December 2026 and reiterated that no exceptions to the law will be made. The IMF staff visit (November 11–13) reported “progress” on the banking sector restructuring strategy but no program agreement — the first full program is now expected in Q1 2027 at the earliest, per Minister Jaber’s own public statements.

L’Orient Today reported that Lebanese Eurobond prices are holding firm despite the institutional standoff, sustained by reform expectations and offshore demand. The World Bank’s $250M reconstruction loan remains largely unused due to slow implementation. GDP is forecast to contract by 6.4% this year (World Bank, August 2026).

Sources: The National (UAE); L’Orient Today; Annahar; BLOMINVEST (Nov 2026)

Why it matters to you

The banking standoff has limited practical impact on day-to-day operations given banks are open Mon–Fri except Wednesdays. The bigger watch item is whether a Q1 2027 IMF program actually gets signed — that would unlock reforms and potentially restart real-estate and infrastructure tendering in Lebanon at meaningful scale.
