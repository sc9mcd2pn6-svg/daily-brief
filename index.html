Geopolitics

Update: APEC Day 1 — Trump, Xi and Putin set for first three-way summit on Thursday; Nvidia spy scandal looms over Shenzhen

The APEC CEO Summit opened in Shenzhen today (Tuesday), ahead of the leaders’ gathering on Wednesday–Thursday. The White House has confirmed a trilateral Trump–Xi–Putin session on the margins on Thursday, November 19 — the first time the three leaders will have met simultaneously. Washington’s stated priority is to bring both China and Russia into a new nuclear non-proliferation framework. Iran war de-escalation, Taiwan, and the US-China trade truce (currently extended through January 10, 2027) are also on the agenda. Canada’s PM Mark Carney is attending as well, courting Asia as an alternative trade partner to the US.

The shadow of the Nvidia/Jensen Huang spy affair hangs over the technology agenda. Trump told reporters he does not plan to press Xi on US intelligence findings linking a Chinese agent to Huang — a signal that the broader AI-chip relationship remains a point of maximum tension but also maximum ambiguity. Commerce Secretary Lutnick and Treasury Secretary Bessent are both travelling with Trump and are publicly at odds on chip policy. Nikkei Asia describes the CEO Summit as the “highest-stakes business gathering of the decade.”

Source: NYT Today’s Headlines (16 Nov); Nikkei Asia; Al Jazeera; NBC News

AI & Tech Policy

US moves to close AI chip loophole — new rule requires SE Asia data centres to screen all customers for China, Russia and Iran links

The Trump administration announced Monday that it is advancing a rule to stop Chinese firms remotely accessing advanced Nvidia AI chips by renting compute time from data centres in Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. The rule would require operators of those facilities to perform strict due-diligence checks on every customer and to deny access to any entity on a restricted-country list. It is the most significant chip-export enforcement action since the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule was rescinded in May 2025.

Commerce Secretary Lutnick is the architect of the new rule; Treasury Secretary Bessent is pushing back, concerned about the economic impact on US allies in Southeast Asia and on Lutnick’s own former firm, which counts affected data centres as clients. The rule was timed to arrive just before Trump left for Shenzhen, adding to the already combustible AI-and-trade atmosphere at APEC. Analysts say full enforcement could displace significant volumes of Chinese AI compute demand — potentially reshaping where new data centres are built globally.

Source: NYT Evening (16 Nov); w.media; Asia Times; BTW Media

Why it matters to you

Compliance pressure on SE Asia data centres may push Chinese operators and tenants to look at alternative hubs — including West Africa. Lagos is already positioning as a neutral data-centre jurisdiction with no China-restriction issues. This rule could accelerate conversations Ralph has been having with Lagos data-centre developers about Quasar Nigeria’s fire-safety and security systems.

Iran & Middle East

Iran is functioning without a supreme leader — six generals and bureaucrats now dominate decision-making, The Economist reports

As the Iran war grinds on, The Economist’s Monday edition reveals that the country is effectively operating without its supreme leader — a vacancy that has been filled by an informal council of six senior military and bureaucratic figures. The arrangement is not constitutionally recognised and represents a fundamental transformation of Iran’s governing structure: power has migrated from the theocratic office of the velayat-e faqih to a collective of Revolutionary Guard commanders and technocrats. The Economist describes it as an “unusual experiment in collective governance.”

The implications for the conflict are significant. A military council is likely to be more hard-line and less ideologically flexible than a supreme leader with legitimacy to make concessions. It may also be more internally fragmented, making coherent ceasefire decisions harder to reach. Western intelligence services have reportedly struggled to identify a single interlocutor for back-channel communications. At the same time, the council may be more pragmatic in ways that do not follow the theological logic that shaped Khamenei-era policy.

Source: The Economist Today (16 Nov)

Why it matters to you

A military-council Iran is less predictable on escalation. For Lebanon, where the Iran-Hezbollah command line is critical, a fragmented Iranian leadership could either accelerate the disarmament window Waltz is pushing for — or trigger an opportunistic escalation if the council feels pressure to show strength. The 45-day UNIFIL countdown is happening against this backdrop. Watch for any change in Hezbollah rhetoric as an indicator of which way Iran is leaning.

Ukraine & Russia

Update: Russia bombs Ukraine’s winter infrastructure — NYT says Trump’s concessions to Moscow “aren’t working”

Russia has launched a sustained campaign targeting the heating plants, electricity grids and water systems that keep Ukrainians alive through winter, the NYT’s evening newsletter reported from its Monday wrap-up. The timing — as temperatures drop across Ukraine — is deliberate: each infrastructure strike reduces the Ukrainian civilian population’s ability to endure the winter and ramps pressure on Kyiv to accept terms. The NYT is explicit that the Trump administration’s diplomatic concessions to Moscow, made in hopes of de-escalating Russian strikes, have so far produced no restraint.

The backdrop is the APEC trilateral: Trump will sit with Putin in Shenzhen on Thursday, and Ukraine is expected to be a central topic. Kyiv fears that the summit will produce another package of concessions to Russia — territorial, diplomatic or sanctions-related — in exchange for a ceasefire framework that falls short of its security needs. The Democrats, now controlling six new US state governments after last week’s elections, are positioning to use budget leverage in January to reinstate Ukraine military aid that the administration has quietly reduced.

Source: NYT Evening (16 Nov); NYT Today’s Headlines (16 Nov)

AI & Finance

Update: Anthropic IPO final countdown — $70–78/share, 825 million shares, targeting Wednesday Nov 19 Nasdaq debut as ANT

Anthropic’s amended S-1 filing covers 825 million Class A shares priced at $70–$78 each, with a concurrent $26 billion private placement led by Nvidia, Google and Microsoft — implying a total company valuation in the range of $1.1–$1.75 trillion. First trading under the ticker ANT is targeted for Wednesday, November 19. The Economist’s Monday edition frames the IPO as marking the moment AI labs shift from foundation-model research to “coding agents” as their core commercial product — and simultaneously warns, in a separate leader, that “you should expect an AI crash.”

The IPO arrives in the same week as the APEC summit (where AI safety is on the Trump–Xi agenda), Nvidia’s first earnings under interim CEO Colette Kress, and the new US chip-export enforcement rule. Market appetite will be tested in the most volatile possible environment. The $26 billion strategic placement from hyperscalers acts as a stabiliser, but the retail open price on Wednesday will be the real signal of whether the AI capex cycle has plateaued or still has room to run.

Source: The Economist Today (16 Nov); letsdatascience.com; Axios; Reuters

Why it matters to you

The ANT open price on Wednesday is the clearest real-money verdict on whether AI infrastructure spending sustains. If the stock opens well above the $78 ceiling, the data-centre build-out cycle continues — good for the Lagos pipeline. If it opens below the range or immediately dips, it signals that the AI crash the Economist warned about is beginning, and that could slow new data-centre commitments from hyperscalers in West Africa.

Nigeria & Health

Update: Nigeria Ebola situation essentially resolved — NCDC records no new cases after 21-day window; final contacts finishing monitoring

Nigeria’s Centre for Disease Control confirmed that no new Ebola cases have been recorded in the 21 days since the single index patient was identified on October 20. Of the 62 contacts traced, 34 have completed their monitoring period and been cleared; 28 were still finishing follow-up as of November 11, with that final group expected to be released by mid-November. The WHO is reporting the same picture at the regional level: no new confirmed cases in Nigeria or Kenya since its last update of November 3.

The DRC outbreak, which is the source of the imported case, remains active at over 10,000 confirmed cases. Lagos airports have maintained heightened screening since October but the city is now on track for an official Ebola-free declaration, likely this week. Nigeria’s rapid contact-tracing response has been held up by the WHO as a model for the continent. Premium Times Nigeria (searched; no current Nov 17 stories found — monitoring continues).

Source: AllAfrica / NCDC (11 Nov); Nairametrics (11 Nov); Sahara Reporters (2 Nov)

Economy & Politics

France in political paralysis — National Assembly rejects austerity budget, GDP contracts Q3, Lecornu clinging on 18 months from presidential vote

France’s National Assembly voted down the Lecornu government’s austerity budget in early November, triggering a nationwide strike on November 5 that drew 575,000 participants according to Interior Ministry figures. The budget rejection leaves the country without a legal spending framework for 2027, forcing the government to rely on a provisional continuing-resolution mechanism that restricts new investment. GDP contracted 0.1% in the third quarter — France’s third consecutive flat or negative quarter — and public debt has reached approximately 120% of GDP, far above the EU’s 60% ceiling.

Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu survived two no-confidence votes by razor-thin margins, but analysts describe him as politically exhausted. The presidential election is 18 months away, and the race is wide open: Marine Le Pen leads polls at 34–36%, Jean-Luc Mélenchon is building an LFI platform for a 2027 run, and centrist forces are still searching for a viable candidate. The Airbus Toulouse factory announcement (3,500 jobs for A330neo expansion) is one of the few unambiguous economic positives of recent weeks.

Source: Brussels Signal (5 Nov); Business Standard (5 Nov); KAS Wochenspiegel (6 Nov); Institut Montaigne

Security & Diplomacy

Update: UNIFIL transition enters critical phase — UN peacekeeping chief visits Beirut; force to begin drawdown after Dec 31 mandate expiry

The UN’s Under-Secretary-General for Peace Operations travelled to Beirut this week and said publicly that “a smooth transition is critical” before UNIFIL begins its withdrawal after the mandate expires on December 31. UNIFIL has confirmed it will start troop reduction after that date; the Lebanese government has asked for an extension, but no Security Council agreement has been reached. The Lebanese Armed Forces, by analysts’ estimates, would need up to 10,000 troops deployed to the south to cover the area UNIFIL currently patrols — nearly double what they currently have in the region.

L’Orient Today reported that UNIFIL soldiers have faced movement restrictions across large parts of their operating area since March 2026, limiting patrols. The IDF said last week it found Hezbollah weapons stockpiles near UN positions — a contested claim that the UN has not confirmed. G7 foreign ministers have committed to avoiding a “security void” after December 31, but the mechanism for achieving that — the EU Security Mission for Lebanon (EUSML) activated November 11 — is still standing up its command structure and will not be operational in time to seamlessly replace UNIFIL.

Source: L’Orient Today; ABC News; Al Jazeera; Long War Journal (Nov 8)

Why it matters to you

The 44 days to December 31 are a binary hinge for Beirut business confidence. If the Lebanese army demonstrably deploys south and the EUSML stands up without incident, stability holds and investor sentiment — already recovering — can build through Q1 2027. If there is a security vacuum, client projects in Beirut pause and cash flow tightens. Track Lebanese army troop numbers in the south as the leading indicator.

Act on this: Flag the Dec 31 cliff date to any clients with large project payments due in Q1 2027 — build in a contingency buffer for potential payment delays if the security picture deteriorates.

Art & Restitution

Update: Sotheby’s meets Van Gogh heirs’ lawyers today — $180M “Blossoming Chestnut Trees” sale in legal jeopardy over Jewish family restitution claim

Sotheby’s is meeting lawyers representing the heirs of the Wertheimer family — the original Jewish owners of Van Gogh’s Blossoming Chestnut Trees — in New York today (Monday), according to the NYT’s evening newsletter. The meeting follows the auction house’s decision to proceed with the sale despite well-known restitution claims on the painting; the NYT describes this as “risk-taking that left a $180 million Van Gogh in the lurch.” The heirs say the work was sold under duress during the Nazi occupation of France. The original sale is disputed; the buyer has not been publicly named.

The case fits a broader pattern: in the same week, a French court ordered Sotheby’s to cancel and return a Pissarro sold in 2024 to heirs of a Jewish banker (Salomon Flavian), and a German museum returned a Slevogt painting to the heirs of a Nazi-persecuted collector. Restitution claims are increasingly being litigated aggressively and successfully, raising the compliance burden on major auction houses for any work with a European Jewish provenance from the 1930s–40s. No Castellani news this week.

Source: NYT Evening (16 Nov); NYT Today’s Headlines (16 Nov); The Art Newspaper (Nov 2026)

Music

2027 Grammy nominations: Raye leads all artists with 11 nods — ceremony at Crypto.com Arena, Los Angeles, February 7, 2027

The Recording Academy announced the 2027 Grammy Award nominations on Monday, with British singer-songwriter Raye leading all artists at 11 nominations. Raye, known for her jazz-inflected pop and R&B, had a breakout year defined by live performances and her second studio album. Other artists atop the nominee list include Rosalía, Olivia Rodrigo, Ella Langley and Phoebe Bridgers. The NYT’s music writers noted the strong tilt toward young women across the top categories — a contrast to recent Grammy cycles that have leaned male and legacy-driven.

Notable omissions include Madonna and Noah Kahan from top categories. The NYT flagged the Canadian bass-and-drums duo Angine de Poitrine as a surprise entrant in several categories. The ceremony airs on ABC, Disney+ and Hulu on February 7, 2027, from Crypto.com Arena in Los Angeles. The eligibility window covered September 2025 to August 2026, meaning Taylor Swift’s October album will not appear until the 2028 nominations cycle.

Source: NYT Evening (16 Nov)
