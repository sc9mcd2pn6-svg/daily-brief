Cybersecurity

AI uncovers critical flaw in software powering 70% of all websites

Researchers using OpenAI’s advanced AI models have unearthed major vulnerabilities in PHP — a programming language used in an estimated 70% of all websites and applications. The US government and allied authorities are urging every affected site to immediately install patches. Security professionals are warning that the fallout could be significant, particularly given how slowly many organisations apply updates.

“AI means that more significant flaws are being discovered at an unprecedented rate. In some ways that is a good thing, because weak or faulty code is being fixed. But AI also can help malicious hackers move a lot more quickly,” the NYT’s cybersecurity reporter told readers. The episode signals that AI-accelerated vulnerability discovery is now a permanent feature of the threat landscape.

Source: The New York Times Evening (21 Oct 2026)

Saudi Arabia

Everything is going wrong for MBS — Saudi GDP falls 4.8%

The Economist’s most-read story of the week examines the unravelling of Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman’s grand project. Saudi GDP contracted 4.8% year-on-year in Q2 2026, with oil activity alone down 24.7%, as the Iran war’s Strait of Hormuz disruption and the East–West Pipeline shutdown combine to crush revenue. The IMF has cut its Saudi 2026 growth forecast and the Atlantic Council warns that while the Kingdom’s buffers can absorb the shock, they do so at considerable cost.

Structural problems compound the cyclical shock: foreign direct investment is weak, debt is rising, and the timeline for Vision 2030 diversification is slipping. Regional geopolitical risk premium, not just oil prices, is now defining the Kingdom’s economic trajectory.

Source: The Economist Today (20–21 Oct 2026); Atlantic Council (Oct 2026)

Russia’s African student pipeline: 37,000 enrolled, scholarships tripled since 2020

The Economist’s figure of the day highlights that 37,000 African students are now studying at Russian universities, with Moscow’s scholarship programme nearly tripling since 2020 to over 5,300 funded places annually. Estonia’s 2026 foreign intelligence yearbook documents that the Kremlin is building seven “Russian Houses” across Africa as soft-power centres ahead of the third Russia–Africa Summit, and that expelled European-posted Russian diplomats and intelligence officers are being redeployed to the continent.

For businesses operating across West Africa, this is a slow-moving but significant geopolitical shift.

Lebanon — IMF & Security

Financial Gap Law: bond surge cools as parliament delays; Washington talks advance security framework

Lebanon’s Economy Minister recently promised that the Financial Gap Law — the IMF’s last outstanding condition for a staff-level deal — would be presented to parliament soon. The market reacted: Lebanese Eurobonds spiked roughly 12% in mid-October. But no formal draft reached cabinet, and prices have since retreated by around 10%. Bonds remain near 25 cents on the dollar, per L’Orient Today’s most recent market coverage. November is now the last credible window for a 2026 IMF deal; a slip to 2027 would test market patience significantly.

On the security front, the Lebanon–Israel framework agreement signed in Washington on 26 June — establishing a roadmap for Hezbollah disarmament and Lebanese state sovereignty over the south — continues to be implemented. The anticipated Rome round of direct negotiations has been pushed to October; a subsequent Washington ambassadorial meeting was also planned. Lebanese President Aoun has stated publicly that Beirut wants a full security agreement with Israel.

Source: L’Orient Today (Oct 2026); Al-Monitor (Oct 2026); Janus Henderson Research (Oct 2026)

Why it matters to you

A delayed IMF deal keeps credit conditions for Lebanese businesses tight and Gulf capital cautious about Beirut real estate. Your FGC contracts tied to reconstruction or commercial development depend on this reform momentum. If the Financial Gap Law clears before year-end, expect a sharp Eurobond re-rating and a pick-up in Gulf-funded project flow — positioning now matters.

Act on this: Track the parliamentary calendar for Financial Gap Law hearings; an unexpected yes-vote could accelerate deal flow fast.
