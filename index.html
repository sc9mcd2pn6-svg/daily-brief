Economy

US inflation surges to 3.7% — gas up 34% since Iran war, zero real wage growth

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported September consumer prices rose 3.7% year-on-year, driven overwhelmingly by energy: gasoline is up 34.2% over the past year as the US-Iran war disrupts global supply chains. Beyond fuel, airline tickets, heating oil, toys, lettuce, and consumer electronics all jumped. American workers have seen zero real wage growth since Trump returned to office in 2025, with wages consistently outpaced by inflation. A new Pew study finds close to half of Americans cannot comfortably pay for basic needs — potent ammunition for Democrats as midterms draw within three weeks. Retirees will see a 3.6% Social Security bump next year to compensate.

Source: The New York Times (Evening newsletter, 14 Oct 2026); Bureau of Labor Statistics

Russia’s largest missile barrage of the war: 39 ballistic missiles, Kyiv blackout; Starshield severed

Russia launched 39 ballistic missiles overnight at Ukraine’s energy infrastructure — the largest single attack since the war began — knocking out power to 600,000 residents in Kyiv ahead of winter. China’s exports grew last month at their fastest pace in almost any month since the 2008 global financial crisis, putting its annual trade surplus on course to break the record $1.2 trillion set in 2025 — even as exports to the US have fallen 20% under Trump-era tariffs. IMF · Economy

IMF mission: critical final days — Financial Gap Law still without a vote date

The IMF’s October mission, now on day three in Beirut, has secured bank restructuring legislation but remains stuck on the Financial Gap Law — the last formal obstacle to a staff-level agreement. The Fund’s core demands include VAT raised to 12%, a depositor hierarchy-of-claims framework, and a medium-term fiscal programme; the Central Bank is pressing for carve-outs that the IMF says would hollow out the law’s purpose. Parliament has still set no vote date. Market analysts note Lebanon’s Eurobond price is holding at roughly 25 cents on the dollar, pricing in the optionality of a deal, but failure to pass the law this session risks pushing the full programme to 2027.

Source: L’Orient Today (independent); Enterprise AM / MenaPlus (Oct 2026); Janus Henderson analysis
