Oregon school shooting: father on lunch break kills teenage gunman, saves daughters’ school

A 16-year-old opened fire at Silver Rail Elementary School in Bend, Oregon on Friday, killing a teacher and three young children (ages 5, 8 and 11) before being shot dead by Kaylub Giamanco, a father of two girls attending the school who happened to be on his lunch break nearby. Giamanco entered the building, engaged the attacker — who had blasted through locked doors and still carried 44 rounds of unused ammunition — and killed him. A 7-year-old and another teacher were critically injured; a teacher who tackled the shooter is also credited by police with limiting casualties.

The shooting is the deadliest at a US elementary school since Uvalde in 2022 and will intensify the election-year debate over gun rights and armed bystanders three weeks before the midterms. The weapon was a shotgun registered to the attacker’s mother.

Source: The New York Times (Headlines & Evening, 16 Oct 2026)

US Economy

US federal deficit hits $2 trillion for fiscal 2026 — highest since the pandemic

The Treasury Department reported the US budget gap for fiscal year 2026 (ending September 30) reached $1.99 trillion, the largest since 2021, according to Congressional Budget Office data. The deficit rose $218 billion year-on-year, driven by surging net interest payments (up $115 billion, or 11%), military spending and Social Security costs — all of which have overwhelmed the cuts Trump made to federal programmes. Revenue fell partly because a Supreme Court ruling reduced expected tariff receipts.

Publicly held US debt now exceeds 100% of GDP. The figure lands three weeks before midterms, handing Democrats a fresh line of attack against the administration’s fiscal management, while bond markets are already watching US debt dynamics closely.

Source: The New York Times (Evening, 16 Oct 2026); Congressional Budget Office; Fiscal Times

Israel election Oct 27: Arab parties as kingmakers — neither bloc near a majority

IMF: Lebanon central bank chief expects breakthrough by year-end — November mission confirmed

Central Bank Governor Karim Souaid said this week that IMF talks are “progressing at a good pace” and he expects a breakthrough before the end of 2026, Bloomberg and Daily Beirut reported on October 15-16. He acknowledged parliament must pass the depositor repayment law “not later than mid-2027” to unlock the staff-level agreement. The IMF confirmed it will send a staff mission to Beirut in November; Finance Minister Yassine Jaber reaffirmed Lebanon’s reform commitments at the IMF/World Bank annual meetings in Bangkok this week. The IMF’s remaining conditions include passage of the Financial Gap Law, a 2027 budget and a medium-term fiscal framework.

Separately, President Aoun’s appeal of one article of the bank restructuring law to the Constitutional Council has introduced a new procedural uncertainty; the IMF has said it will assess any resulting changes.

Source: Bloomberg (15 Oct 2026); Daily Beirut (16 Oct 2026); Asharq Al-Awsat

Financial Gap Law: still no parliamentary vote date as IMF pressure peaks

The Financial Gap Law — which aims to address the $80-83 billion shortfall between deposits and bank assets — remains before parliament without a scheduled vote. The cabinet approved the draft in late December 2025 and sent it to parliament, but political disagreements between Hezbollah, the Lebanese Forces and reform-oriented lawmakers have blocked scheduling. The IMF has made passage of this law, alongside the bank restructuring framework, an explicit condition of any deal. With the November mission approaching, there is mounting pressure on parliament to act — but the track record of delay makes a pre-November vote unlikely.

European museum theft wave: $8 billion lost annually as gangs shift to “smash-and-grab” tactics

The Economist flags that art theft costs $8 billion globally each year, and Europe’s museums are bearing the brunt of a notable surge in 2026. The Louvre jewel heist of October 2025, valued at €88 million, appears to have inspired copycat operations.

The Economist separately notes the Frieze London week (running through Oct 18) and the ongoing Sotheby’s sales season as the legitimate market’s counterpart to this criminal activity.
