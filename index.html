Update: Digital Parks Africa Joins Lagos Data-Centre Race With Carrier-Neutral NDC1 Facility

Digital Parks Africa (data-centre arm of Master Power Technologies Group) announced at ITW Africa in Kenya its expansion into West Africa with Nigeria Data Centre 1 (NDC1) — a carrier-neutral Tier IV facility in Lagos targeting banks, fintechs, cloud providers, and connectivity operators. NDC1 joins a rapidly filling pipeline: Equinix is building LG3 ($22m), Open Access Data Centres is developing a $240m 24MW facility, and Airtel Nigeria’s Nxtra platform is constructing a $120m hyperscale campus at Eko Atlantic. Nairametrics and Premium Times Nigeria put Nigeria’s total at 28 operational or under-construction facilities, almost entirely concentrated in Lagos.

The CBN mandate — all banks, fintechs, and payment processors must store Nigerian payment data on local servers by January 1, 2027 — remains the single biggest structural driver. Nigeria’s sovereign cloud certification programme (NITDA) is also due to go live by October 2026, adding a security-compliance layer. Every new-build carrier-neutral facility entering the market must demonstrate compliant suppression, detection, and physical security from fit-out stage.

World Bank: Lebanon Economy Contracting 6.4% in 2026 as March Conflict Reversed 2025 Rebound

A World Bank assessment (August 21, 2026) confirms Lebanon entered 2026 on its strongest economic footing since the 2019 crisis — 4.2% real GDP growth in 2025, driven by diaspora tourism, reconstruction inflows, and a functioning new government — but the March 2026 escalation in conflict reversed those gains sharply. The Bank now projects a 6.4% contraction in 2026, citing damage to housing and infrastructure, displaced communities, disrupted supply chains, and a severe hit to tourism and domestic demand. The IIF is more pessimistic still: it projects a 12% contraction, flagging near-total tourism collapse and continued absence of a credible IMF-backed reform framework as the key drivers.

On a more positive note, L’Orient Today reports the Banque du Liban’s cashless push is gaining traction: electronic payments have more than doubled at restaurants and supermarkets after the BdL cut merchant interchange fees and required card terminals inside government offices. The IMF’s depositor-access law — welcomed last month as a “major step” — remains the linchpin of any recovery, but execution risk is high given the security environment and the ceasefire’s fragility.
