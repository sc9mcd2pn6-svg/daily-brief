Israel – Election Result

Update: Netanyahu denied majority — Eisenkot bloc also falls short; Arab parties hold the key

With counting near-complete, Israel’s October 27 election has denied Benjamin Netanyahu a governing majority for the third time since 2022. His right-wing bloc fell short of the 61 seats required, and so did Gadi Eisenkot’s centrist opposition. Arab parties won a record 17–18 seats, making them the arithmetic kingmakers. Eisenkot’s path to power depends on Arab party support, which he has historically rejected, while Netanyahu has reportedly floated a broad unity government — which Eisenkot has refused, calling Netanyahu unfit to lead.

The official certified result is expected Thursday. Formal coalition negotiations could take weeks and a fifth election is not ruled out. Both the NYT and The Economist (which ran “Bibi’s last stand?” as its lead story Wednesday) framed the result as a punishment of Netanyahu that the opposition cannot yet capitalise on. The Economist noted that either outcome will have to grapple with the Lebanon ceasefire and Iran threat without a stable government in place.

Source: NYT Today’s Headlines (Oct 28); NYT Evening (Oct 28); The Economist (Oct 28)

Why it matters to you

A Netanyahu-led government would harden Israel’s ceasefire demands and increase the risk of renewed hostilities in south Lebanon, directly threatening Quasar and FGC project pipelines in Beirut and the south. An Eisenkot government reopens talks and reduces near-term escalation risk. The weeks of coalition negotiations are themselves a period of uncertainty. Thursday’s certified result will clarify the trajectory; do not commit to south Lebanon project timelines until the coalition picture is clearer.

Act on this: hold any new south Lebanon proposals pending certified coalition outcome, expected by end of October.

Spain – Disaster

68 killed as high-speed trains collide south of Madrid — Spain’s deadliest rail crash in over a decade

A Renfe high-speed train rear-ended a stationary Iryo train near Urda, south of Madrid, on Wednesday morning, killing at least 68 people and injuring dozens more. Spain’s transport minister said the automated signalling system at Urda failed and six trains were stopped on the network, but operators made the “inexplicable” decision to allow one train to continue while another sat in its path. Two carriages derailed and fell down an embankment; one survivor described “a twisted tangle of metal.”

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez declared three days of national mourning and ordered an independent inquiry. Railway infrastructure body Adif is investigating. This is the second deadly high-speed rail collision in Spain in 2026 — the January crash near A­damuz, Córdoba killed 40. The NYT Evening newsletter called it “one of the deadliest train crashes in the country’s history.”

Source: CNBC (Oct 28); NPR (Oct 28); NYT Evening (Oct 28)

Markets – Fed

Fed holds rates 10–2 — December hike 85% priced as inflation refuses to fall

The Federal Reserve held the federal funds rate at 3.75–4.00% on Wednesday, but in a split 10–2 vote: board members Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack dissented in favour of an immediate hike. Chair Kevin Warsh declined to rule out a December move, calling the next few weeks “information-rich.” Markets moved immediately: traders now price about an 85% probability of a hike at the December 9 meeting. The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.044% and the 30-year to 5.36%.

Equities shrugged off the rate uncertainty — the S&P 500 rose 0.28% to a third straight gain at 7,743.98 and the Dow gained 0.19% to 52,475 — helped by Oracle surging 11% after hours on a cloud contract. The Fed’s split vote is the clearest signal yet that the tightening cycle is not finished, which has implications for USD strength and dollar-denominated contracts.

Source: CNN (Oct 28); Motley Fool (Oct 28); bbntimes (Oct 28)

Why it matters to you

A December rate hike would further strengthen the dollar, raising the cost of USD-priced equipment imports for Quasar’s Lebanon and Nigeria projects and tightening already-thin margins on dollar-denominated contracts. If you have open import orders or ongoing dollar-billed projects, locking in exchange rates now is worth discussing with the finance team.

Act on this: flag to Sami — review open USD import exposures before the December 9 Fed meeting.

US – Health Policy

Kennedy bypasses FDA — unilaterally opens access to six unapproved peptide injections

US Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. declared on Wednesday that he would allow widespread patient access to six unapproved peptide injections, bypassing the Food and Drug Administration’s standard approval process. Kennedy has publicly claimed personal benefit from peptides; FDA scientists had previously reviewed the compounds and advised against broad access. The declaration is unilateral and does not require congressional or FDA sign-off.

Legal experts quoted by the NYT questioned the constitutionality of the move and said it opens the door to liability claims if patients are harmed. The decision follows the ChatGPT epilepsy death story from earlier this week and contributes to a broader narrative of erosion in US federal science oversight. The NYT called it a pattern of “Kennedy’s unilateral declarations bypassing standard agency processes.”

Source: NYT Evening (Oct 28); NYT Today’s Headlines (Oct 28)

World – US/Russia

Trump waives sweeping Russia energy sanctions — diesel-for-peace deal as Ukraine talks stall

President Trump signed an executive order on October 17 waiving the sweeping US sanctions and tariffs on Russia’s energy revenues, in exchange for Moscow releasing large volumes of diesel onto global markets. The move, described as an economic incentive to keep the Kremlin at Ukraine peace talks, drew bipartisan criticism in Congress and a direct warning from Zelensky that easing pressure on Moscow will not stop the war. Trump separately threatened to reimpose “very significant” sanctions if talks collapse.

The NYT added a new angle Wednesday: the administration is also considering lifting sanctions on a specific fund that the US previously called “Putin’s slush fund,” as part of a broader economic concessions package. The Russia sanctions saga has driven recent Brent oil volatility — the waiver briefly pushed Brent down before Middle East tensions pushed it back above $110 — and signals that Washington’s foreign-policy tool box is being repurposed as a negotiating chip.

Source: NYT Today’s Headlines (Oct 28); ARY News / AllSides (Oct 17–19)

Brazil – Update

Update: Flávio Bolsonaro lands in Miami — Economist sees “fresh start for Brazil’s political right”

Defeated presidential candidate Flávio Bolsonaro landed in Miami on Wednesday after fleeing Brazil, where the Supreme Court had seized his passport in a money-laundering probe tied to his father’s January 6-style coup attempt. US officials confirmed they plan to issue visas to him and his family. The Supreme Court also issued a warrant after he defied a subpoena to appear before investigators.

The Economist took a wider view, arguing that Bolsonarismo without Bolsonaro may actually be more electorally effective — a more polished successor could unite the Brazilian right without the father’s legal baggage. President Lula’s surprise victory has rattled markets: the Bovespa fell 14% and the real dropped 10% on the day of his win. Lula now faces an opposition in exile that The Economist says could consolidate around a cleaner candidate for 2030.

Source: NYT Evening (Oct 28); The Economist (Oct 28)

France – Strike

T–7 to France’s general strike — Oct 31 protests confirmed, unrest spreads to Italy and Belgium

Seven days out from the November 5 nationwide strike, France’s five major unions have all confirmed their participation and SNCF strike notices have been filed, meaning rail services will be severely disrupted. Unions also announced a separate day of street protests for Saturday October 31, targeting wages and the 2027 budget. The government’s failure to reach a deal at talks with Prime Minister Lecornu earlier this month has deepened the impasse.

The unrest is no longer confined to France. Similar protests have broken out in Italy and Belgium over cost-of-living pressures and pension reform, raising the prospect of coordinated cross-border labour action in early November. The Economist’s “Agony of liberal Europe” framing from last week continues to play out. With the November 5 strike now a near-certainty, Paris logistics and travel plans should be revised.

Source: Connexion France; Euronews (Oct 21); Sortiraparis; journalarta.com (Oct 26)

Lebanon – Economy & Security

Israel election shapes Lebanon ceasefire; IMF’s Georgieva says talks will accelerate — BdL governor expects year-end breakthrough

The Israeli election’s outcome is directly linked to Lebanon’s ceasefire stability. An Eisenkot-led government, if it forms, is expected to re-engage US-brokered ceasefire talks and reduce the risk of renewed strikes; a Netanyahu government, even a unity one, keeps Lebanon’s southern border tense and subject to Israel’s harder demands. L’Orient Today’s most recent reporting shows Eurobond prices holding firm around 20–25 cents despite persistent reform delays and security concerns.

On the economic front, IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva said at the annual IMF-World Bank meetings that the Fund would “accelerate” talks with Lebanon, citing progress by PM Nawaf Salam’s government. BdL governor Karim Suaid expressed confidence that a breakthrough on the bank resolution law — the main sticking point in negotiations — can be reached before year-end. A December deal remains plausible but hinges on the Financial Gap Law reaching a parliamentary vote.

Source: L’Orient Today (eurobond); IMF/Zawya (Georgieva Oct 2026); LBCI (Suaid)

Why it matters to you

A year-end IMF deal would be the most significant positive catalyst for Lebanon’s economy since 2019 — unlocking external financing, improving BdL reserves, and likely strengthening the lira. It could accelerate the pipeline of infrastructure and building-sector projects that Quasar and FGC depend on. The Israel election result and the Financial Gap Law vote are the two near-term triggers to watch.

Act on this: monitor the Financial Gap Law parliamentary calendar — a vote before end-November is the signal that the IMF deal is on track and that Beirut project demand could revive in Q1 2027.

Nigeria – Data Centres

Airtel’s Nxtra breaks ground on 38 MW hyperscale data centre at Eko Atlantic, Lagos — $120M+ investment

Airtel Africa’s data centre subsidiary Nxtra has broken ground on a 38-megawatt hyperscale campus at Eko Atlantic City in Lagos, the company’s largest infrastructure bet in West Africa. The facility, with an estimated investment exceeding $120 million, is positioned as a carrier-neutral colocation asset targeting hyperscalers and enterprise clients. It joins Digital Parks Africa’s NDC1 announced in September as the newest entrant in Lagos’s accelerating data centre race.

The Lagos data centre market now has 146 MW in the confirmed pipeline, though energy remains the defining constraint: costs have surged 64% since January 2026 due to grid instability and FX volatility on diesel. Despite this, Eko Atlantic’s location — with a dedicated power grid separate from the LEKKI distribution area — makes it one of the more resilient sites for power-hungry tenants. The state’s target remains 250 MW of total capacity by 2030.

Source: DataCenterDynamics; TechCabal (May 2026); DataCenterDynamics energy report (2026)

Why it matters to you

A 38 MW hyperscale campus at Eko Atlantic is a qualified prospect for Quasar Smart Solutions in Lagos: fire suppression systems (FM200/CO2/inert gas), VESDA, access control, CCTV and BMS are all standard requirements for hyperscale data centres at this scale. Eko Atlantic’s separate power grid also means the site is likely to proceed on schedule, unlike some Lagos builds held back by LEKKI grid issues.

Act on this: QSS Lagos should identify the Nxtra Eko Atlantic project team and get Quasar’s M&E/systems credentials in front of their procurement before the fit-out tender is issued.

Markets – Tech Pivot

General Motors abandons NYSE for Nasdaq after 100+ years — the biggest industrial-to-tech identity shift yet

General Motors announced it will move its stock listing from the New York Stock Exchange to the Nasdaq, ending a relationship with the NYSE that stretches back over a century. The move is explicitly symbolic as well as structural: Nasdaq is home to Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia and the AI-era tech giants, and GM wants the same investor profile as it bets its future on electric vehicles and software-defined transport. The announcement follows GM’s multi-year retreat from traditional combustion products.

The NYT noted the move has precedent — several legacy industrials have made the switch as AI and tech valuations command a premium — but GM is the largest and most symbolically significant company to do so. The shift signals that Wall Street is now fully pricing companies on their technology narrative, not their industrial heritage.

Source: NYT Today’s Headlines (Oct 28)
