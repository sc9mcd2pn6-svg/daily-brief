🤝Defence Pact — Today

Turkey, Saudi Arabia & Pakistan sign “Islamic NATO” defence pact in Jeddah today

The three nations are formally signing a Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement in Jeddah today — Erdoğan arrives for a one-day working visit with MBS and Pakistani PM Shehbaz Sharif. The accord covers joint military exercises, technology transfers, defence-industrial production and intelligence sharing, combining Pakistan’s nuclear capability, Saudi financial resources, and Turkey’s advanced defence industry. India has said it will closely study implications for regional stability; analysts are already calling it an “Islamic NATO.”

Times of Israel · The Manila Times · The Week India · Chatham House

A new Gulf-Pakistan-Turkey security axis shifts the regional power balance in ways that will ripple into Lebanese security arrangements and Gulf client confidence. 🛢️Hormuz — Oil Up 3%

Iran attacks tankers near Qeshm Island overnight — WTI above $78, Oman deal still unsigned

Iran’s IRGC struck “hostile targets” in the Strait of Hormuz overnight near Qeshm Island, sending oil surging more than 3%. West Texas Intermediate rose above $78/bbl and Brent settled around $82. The partial corridor deal brokered by Oman is in its final stages but remains unsigned — the strait has de facto split into a US-controlled southern route and an Iran-controlled northern route that Tehran insists all vessels use. Kuwait separately said it intercepted drones.

Bloomberg · CNBC
Why it matters to you

Higher oil means higher freight and logistics costs for everything you import into Lebanon and Nigeria. If the Oman deal closes this week, prices could retrace; if talks collapse, expect $85+ Brent quickly. Lock in any open shipping or fuel-linked procurement today if you can.

🇱🇧 Lebanon
🏦Economy — Fragile

IIF: Lebanon GDP to contract ~12% in 2026 — ceasefire fragility and absent reform slow recovery

The Institute of International Finance projects Lebanon’s real GDP will contract roughly 12% this year, driven by the collapse in tourism, absent reconstruction inflows, and the fragility of the November 2024 Hezbollah-Israel ceasefire. Sporadic clashes continue. Lebanon’s economy minister has stated that both economic and security reforms are prerequisites for recovery; the Lebanese army has put forward a Hezbollah disarmament plan, but progress is slow and international financial support remains conditional on structural reforms that have not materialised.

Why it matters to you
