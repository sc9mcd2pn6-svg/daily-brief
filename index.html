🏔️ World · Colombia Earthquake Update

Update — Colombia earthquake toll climbs to 265 dead, 3,000+ still missing; collapse of Cali hospital paediatric wing complicates rescue

Three days after a 7.4-magnitude earthquake struck western Colombia on August 10, the confirmed death toll has risen from 111 to at least 265, with independent databases putting the missing figure above 3,000. Rescue teams from Ecuador and across Latin America are working around the clock in San José del Palmar and surrounding communities; a critical “golden 72 hours” window closed last night, significantly reducing the chances of finding survivors in the rubble. The paediatric and neonatal sections of a major Cali hospital collapsed in the disaster, deepening the humanitarian crisis. Colombia has declared a national disaster and activated international aid channels.

🗾 World · Japan Earthquake

Japan Kumamoto earthquake: 39 dead, 6,000 residents still in evacuation shelters two weeks on — aftershock risk remains high

More than two weeks after the 6.8-magnitude (MJMA 7.1) earthquake that struck Kumamoto Prefecture in southwestern Japan on July 28, recovery is proceeding slowly. The death toll stands at at least 39, with over 170 injured; more than 200 aftershocks followed the main quake and the Japan Meteorological Agency maintains alerts for future shocks at lower-5 intensity or above. Over 6,000 residents remain in evacuation centres as structural assessments of homes and infrastructure continue. The region was hit by a similarly powerful 7.0-magnitude quake in 2016 — Japan’s most seismically active prefecture and home to major chemical and semiconductor facilities.

Iran formally states “Hormuz remains blocked” — directly contradicting US claims; Oman-mediated talks stall as IRGC-aligned security chief holds harder line

Iran’s Persian Gulf Authority issued a formal statement on August 13 asserting that “the Strait of Hormuz remains blocked and will not be reopened until Iran’s conditions are accepted,” directly contradicting statements from Washington suggesting the situation was being managed. 🕌 World · Mecca Pact

Mecca Pact redraws the Gulf’s escalation map — Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey defence treaty means another major Iranian strike could widen the war to 1.5 billion people

A defence treaty signed at Mecca last week among Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey formally commits all three nations to mutual defence in the event of a major attack on Saudi territory — the most significant regional security architecture created since the Iran war began. Iran has threatened to strike Gulf energy and desalination facilities if the US launches new strikes; the Houthis and Iraqi militias have already targeted Saudi Arabia’s Jazan refinery. If Tehran triggers the Mecca Pact, Pakistan’s nuclear capacity and Turkey’s NATO membership would both become live variables in a rapidly widening conflict. Qatar, Oman and UAE are pressing Washington to resume Hormuz talks before the pact’s first test.

CNN · Defense News · Al Jazeera · Korea Times · Wikipedia

Airtel to open Nigeria’s largest data centre by end 2026; Kasi Cloud 100MW AI hyperscale campus breaks ground in Lagos — but occupancy lag emerges as the structural challenge

Airtel Africa is on track to open the first of five planned hyperscale data centres on the continent by end of 2026, which will be the largest data centre facility in Nigeria at launch. 🏭 Lebanon · Economy

‘Made in Lebanon’: crisis is rebuilding the productive economy — exports, light manufacturing and agriculture rising even as GDP faces structural headwinds

A new analysis in The Middle East Observer documents how Lebanon’s acute economic crisis has inadvertently accelerated a shift toward productive activity. With imported goods expensive and foreign capital scarce, domestic light manufacturing, agricultural exports and agri-food processing have expanded as businesses adapt to constrained conditions. Exchange-rate stability around LBP 89,700/USD — maintained since August 2023 through strict liquidity management — has provided a floor for dollar-denominated producers. The Banque du Liban’s official reserve remains relatively stable at ~$11.6 billion. The trend does not offset the overall contraction risk flagged by IIF, but it points to a resilient private-sector base that will underpin eventual recovery demand for building systems.

The Middle East Observer · BLOMINVEST · Credit Libanais Economics

🔌 Real Estate · Data-Centre Regulation

Ireland’s data-centre power crisis is the world’s cautionary tale — 23% of national electricity consumed by data centres; new rules force on-site renewable generation and reshape how hyperscalers site globally

Ireland’s Central Statistics Office reports that data centres consumed 23% of the country’s national electricity in 2025, up from 21% the prior year — a concentration that the UN has called “a cautionary tale for the rest of the world.” Ireland lifted its informal moratorium on new data centres in December 2025 under stringent new rules: facilities must provide fully flexible on-site power generation and source at least 80% of annual electricity from new, unsubsidised renewable projects within six years of opening. The Large Energy User Action Plan (LEAP) now steers hyperscalers toward “green energy parks” co-located with offshore wind. JLL forecasts the average global data-centre cost rising 6% to $11.3 million per MW in 2026, with speed-to-power replacing mere connectivity as the primary siting criterion.

Irish Times · Computing · ThinkBusiness · JLL Research · EcoGPT Policy Tracker
Why it matters to you

Ireland’s model — mandatory on-site generation + renewable sourcing — is now being referenced by Nigeria’s energy regulators as a template. If Nigeria adopts a similar framework for the incoming Lagos data-centre wave, operators will require integrated power, fire-suppression and BMS from day one. Understand this regulatory direction now; it shapes the scope of every Lagos data-centre bid Quasar Nigeria writes over the next three years.
