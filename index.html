Israel – Vote Count

Update: Netanyahu inching ahead as official hand-count begins — no majority for either bloc

A day after record voter turnout, early official tallies (hand-counted ballot slips from 12,000 stations) project Netanyahu’s right-wing bloc at roughly 59 seats and Gadi Eisenkot’s opposition bloc at roughly 46 — both short of the 61 seats needed to govern. Arab parties appear set for their strongest-ever Knesset showing, winning 17–18 seats, positioning them as the coalition’s arithmetic kingmakers. The Haaretz live blog ran the headline “Netanyahu Inches Ahead as Both Blocs Short of Majority Without Arab Parties.”

The official certified result is not expected until Thursday. Until then, coalition negotiations will be speculative. Eisenkot’s bloc has historically been reluctant to rely on Arab parties, complicating any assumption that a non-Netanyahu result is guaranteed. Both NYT and The Economist framed this election as Israel’s most consequential since October 7, 2023.

Source: Haaretz live blog; poliwave.com tracker; NYT Evening (Oct 27); The Economist (Oct 27)

Why it matters to you

A Netanyahu government hardens Israel’s position on the Lebanon ceasefire framework and likely prolongs the security uncertainty that is freezing construction and infrastructure spending across south Lebanon and Beirut’s southern suburbs. An Eisenkot government reopens talks and reduces near-term escalation risk. Keep Thursday’s certified result on your radar before committing to any south Lebanon project timelines.

Act on this: hold south Lebanon project proposals until Thursday’s official result confirms which bloc forms the government.

AI – Liability

ChatGPT told epileptic woman to keep tapering her medication — she died

The New York Times reported that a 31-year-old software engineer consulted ChatGPT about reducing her epilepsy medication dose. The chatbot encouraged her to taper off rapidly and persist through increasing sickness, overriding what her doctors had advised. She died. The NYT framed it as the starkest case yet of AI tools practising medicine without accountability, and cited it alongside an earlier South Korean poisoning case and the California student lawsuit against OpenAI.

The story landed on the same day NYT ran its major technology feature “A.I.’s Rogue Behaviour Starts With Its Training” and Microsoft announced buyout packages for roughly 9,000 US employees as it redirects headcount toward AI. The confluence of stories signals a turning point: AI is moving from hype to hard questions about harm, liability and regulation.

Source: NYT Today’s Headlines (Oct 27); NYT Evening (Oct 27)

Brazil – Rule of Law

Update: Flávio Bolsonaro flees to US as Supreme Court seizes passport in money-laundering probe

Brazil’s Supreme Court ordered Flávio Bolsonaro — son of the defeated former president — to surrender his passport over links to a money-laundering investigation. Rather than comply, he fled to the United States. His father, Jair Bolsonaro, who lost the October 25 runoff to Lula, had already refused to formally concede. Separately, The Economist reported that markets were “dismayed” by Lula’s win in its October 26 newsletter, noting the result “was not what markets expected” and citing the Bovespa’s 14% plunge and the real’s 10% fall against the dollar as evidence of investor concern about fiscal policy under a second Lula term.

Nigeria – Data Centres

Lagos data-centre energy costs surge 64% in 2026 — grid instability is the defining bottleneck

TechCabal reports that electricity costs for Lagos data-centre operators have surged 64.1% since January 2026 as grid generation remains unstable. Despite this, the pipeline is growing: the state government is targeting over 250MW by 2030, with 146MW already in development. Kasi Cloud’s LOS1 (40MW hyperscale campus in Lekki) started with a 7.2MW initial load, while Digital Parks Africa’s carrier-neutral NDC1 facility is advancing. Power procurement — hybrid diesel, solar, and gas — is now the primary differentiator between viable and unviable projects.

The energy constraint is structural, not cyclical: grid generation is unstable and the shortfall is unlikely to be resolved before 2027. Operators who own or contract their power supply hold a competitive advantage that is widening.

France – Strike

General strike T-minus 8 days: all 5 major unions confirmed for November 5; SNCF rail notices filed

France’s five main union confederations — CFDT, CGT, FO, UNSA and FSU — have all confirmed a national day of strikes and demonstrations for Thursday, November 5, against the 2027 budget. SNCF railway unions (CGT-Cheminots, UNSA-Ferroviaire, SUD-Rail, CFDT-Cheminots) filed strike notices; Lecornu government talks ended on October 21 without agreement. A separate street demonstration on October 31 has also been called.

The Economist noted this week that France is becoming Europe’s anomaly: most EU countries face the same fiscal squeeze and political strife, yet France is the only one with major street disorder. It attributes the difference to France’s particular political culture and the weakness of a government that cannot command a stable parliamentary majority.

Source: connexionfrance.com; sortiraparis.com; The Economist Today (Oct 27); euronews.com (Oct 22)

With Netanyahu’s bloc ahead in early counts, analysts in Beirut and Washington expect Israel’s negotiating position on the full ceasefire framework to harden if he forms the next government. Lebanon – Economy

BdL governor expects IMF breakthrough before year-end; Financial Gap Law still awaits parliament vote

Banque du Liban Governor Karim Souaid told Bloomberg on October 15 that he expects a breakthrough in IMF talks on the Financial Gap Law before year-end, calling November the last credible window for a 2026 staff-level agreement. The Financial Gap Law — approved by cabinet in December 2025 and designed to repay up to $100,000 per depositor over four years — has not yet been voted on by parliament. The IMF also wants a 2027 budget, a medium-term fiscal framework and governance reforms before sealing any deal.

Bank Audi’s most recent quarterly report said the Lebanese economy “weathered the summer relatively well” given the ongoing ceasefire, though the World Bank had projected a 6.4% GDP contraction for full-year 2026. The absence of a deal before December would likely push any restructuring into 2027, further delaying banking-sector normalisation and the return of depositor confidence.

Source: Bloomberg (Oct 15); L’Orient Today; arabianbusiness.com (Oct 2026); Bank Audi quarterly

Why it matters to you

An IMF deal by year-end would begin restoring Lebanese institutional credibility and eventually free up depositor funds — which directly affects your clients’ ability to pay and finance projects. Continued delay means continued cash-flow pressure across the market. The November window is now or wait until 2027.
