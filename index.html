⚓️ World · Iran / Hormuz

Update: US and Iran Reach Initial Hormuz Deal — Ceasefire Extended, Strait Reopening Under 14-Point MOU; Challenges Remain

In the most significant diplomatic development since hostilities began in February 2026, the United States and Iran have reached an initial agreement to extend the Gulf War ceasefire and begin phasing open the Strait of Hormuz. PBS NewsHour reports the deal is done “but challenges remain”; L’Orient Today (independent, Beirut) is covering it live, describing a 14-point memorandum of understanding that includes a ceasefire extension, a phased Hormuz reopening and an end to specific US sanctions. The breakthrough came out of marathon talks on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly, mediated by Qatar, Pakistan and Egypt.

Brent crude, which settled near $107 on Thursday, dropped toward $103 on overnight futures as the news spread. The deal strongly resembles the June MOU, which collapsed when Iran resumed Hormuz harassment in July — a precedent that has tempered enthusiasm. The White House said Friday that it is having “positive and constructive conversations with mediators,” but Trump has insisted the blockade will not lift until Iran demonstrates “sufficient goodwill.” Gulf tanker operators and oil-market traders are watching closely; some vessels traversing the contested strait have been commanding over $1 million a day in charter premiums in recent weeks.

PBS NewsHour · Bloomberg · L’Orient Today (independent) · CBS News · Al Jazeera

🛡️ World · France / Saudi Arabia

France Deploys Troops, Radars & Air-Defence to Saudi Red Sea Port — Macron’s First Direct Military Entry into Gulf War Theatre

French President Emmanuel Macron announced Thursday that France will send “soldiers, radars, and defence systems” to Saudi Arabia’s Yanbu industrial port on the Red Sea to protect critical energy infrastructure. The deployment, agreed in a bilateral pact signed the same day, follows a Houthi ballistic-missile salvo: the Saudi-led coalition said it shot down six missiles aimed at Yanbu and the inland city of Taif within hours of the announcement. Saudi Aramco has been diverting millions of barrels of oil per day through pipelines to Yanbu specifically to reduce exposure to the Persian Gulf Hormuz risk.

Macron framed the deployment in terms of French consumers: “We are acting for lower gas prices.” Defense News and Bloomberg both noted this is France’s first direct military commitment in the Gulf War theater, significantly expanding NATO-member involvement beyond intelligence-sharing. The move complicates the just-announced US–Iran Hormuz MOU: Tehran regards French troops at Yanbu as a hostile escalation, and Iran’s foreign ministry issued a protest overnight. Military analysts say the deployment signals France sees the Gulf energy war as a structural feature, not a passing crisis, regardless of the MOU outcome.

Bloomberg · Defense News · The Inquirer · The War Zone · AGBI
Why it matters to you

France’s military entry signals European governments have decided Gulf energy security is worth direct exposure — a signal that the war-related supply disruptions will persist longer than initially priced. For Lebanon, this means continued diesel and fuel-oil price pressure even if the Hormuz deal holds partially. Plan for sustained high energy costs through at least Q1 2027 in your client pricing.

⚖️ World · US Politics

Supreme Court Clears Trump’s National Voter-Screening Database — Uses SSNs and Citizenship Data to Hunt Ineligible Voters

The US Supreme Court ruled Friday that the Trump administration may deploy a national database — pulling on sensitive data including Social Security numbers and citizenship records — to help states identify and remove ineligible voters from the rolls. The decision is a significant win for Trump ahead of November’s midterm elections, which the administration has framed around voting-integrity concerns. The court’s three liberal justices dissented sharply; Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson warned that past searches have “mistakenly flagged US citizens as noncitizens,” creating a mass-disenfranchisement risk.

In practice, a federal law mandates a 90-day “quiet period” before federal elections during which states cannot remove voters from rolls — meaning no large-scale purges can happen before November. But the database will be live for future elections and sets a precedent that civil-liberties advocates say could fundamentally reshape who can vote in America. The NYT notes the ruling comes amid a quiet but record surge in ICE immigration arrests (see below), suggesting a coordinated enforcement push ahead of the midterms.

🚩 World · US Immigration

ICE Shadow Surge: Record 51,000 Arrests in August — Over Half Have No Criminal Record, Agents Spread Across Every State

Federal immigration arrests soared to record highs for the third consecutive month: 43,000 in June, 49,000+ in July, and nearly 51,000 in August. Unlike the high-profile city raids earlier this year that sparked protests, this surge has unfolded quietly — agents fanning out across nearly every state, working with local police under 287(g) arrangements, and making arrests at hospitals, airports, suburban schools and rural construction sites. The NYT reviewed dozens of incidents caught on camera to document the pattern. The Marshall Project (independent) reports ICE is now arresting soaring numbers of immigrants with no criminal record under a broad civil-enforcement mandate.

The quiet shift in tactics has reduced the public backlash the earlier raids provoked while accelerating the pace of removals. Immigration attorneys say clients are reporting encounters in previously untouched communities including small Midwest towns with large food-processing workforces. The administration’s goal appears to be creating a deterrence signal before the midterms without triggering the kind of media firestorm that galvanised opposition in spring.

🌪️ World · Weather / Climate

Nor’easter Stalls off New Jersey Through Next Week; Hurricane Nolo Strengthens Toward Hawaii with Three Feet of Rain Forecast

A powerful nor’easter is crawling up the Eastern Seaboard and expected to stall just off New Jersey into early next week, bringing sustained strong winds, coastal flooding and heavy rain to the densely populated Northeast. Airlines have issued travel waivers; New York and New Jersey have pre-positioned emergency equipment. The NYT notes this storm is likely the first of several to follow the same stalling pattern this autumn, driven by a developing El Niño system that is nudging the jet stream. Forecasters say it is a sign of what the region faces through winter.

Simultaneously, Hurricane Nolo has rapidly intensified in the Central Pacific and is taking aim at Hawaii, which has already been struck by two storms in recent weeks. Parts of the Big Island and Maui face forecasts of up to nearly three feet of rain, raising flash-flood and mudslide risk in communities still recovering from the previous storms. The compound nature of the US storm events — East Coast nor’easter plus Pacific hurricane simultaneously — is straining FEMA’s pre-positioning capacity.

Nigeria’s 66th Independence Day (Oct 1): Tinubu to Address Nation; Government Points to 4.43% GDP Growth as Austerity Frustration Deepens

Nigeria marks its 66th Independence Anniversary on Thursday October 1, with religious services beginning this weekend across the country. President Bola Tinubu will deliver a nationwide broadcast on October 1 under the theme “From Reforms to Stability: Consolidating Nigeria’s Renewed Hope for Shared Prosperity.” The government is highlighting GDP growth of 4.43% in Q2 2026, up from 4.23% a year earlier — the fastest pace since 2014 outside of pandemic-rebound quarters. Celebrations are low-key by design, reflecting official sensitivity to the widespread economic pain caused by fuel-subsidy removal and the naira’s depreciation.

Separately, Osun State is holding local government elections this weekend, a test of political momentum ahead of 2027 gubernatorial contests. Multiple candidates have been disqualified by the state election commission, and Premium Times has flagged procedural irregularities. The Abia State governor also publicly rejected calls for reinstating the fuel subsidy, underscoring the government’s commitment to its reform agenda even under political pressure. On the data-centre front, Lagos State confirmed 146MW of additional capacity in the pipeline toward a 2030 target of 250MW+.

Salam Tells Iran: Lebanon Is Not an Arena for Regional Wars — Hezbollah’s Fadlallah Signals Openness to South Lebanon Political Solution

Lebanese Prime Minister Nawaf Salam delivered a pointed message to Iranian President Pezeshkian directly at the UN General Assembly on Friday: Lebanon “cannot and will not be an arena for regional conflicts.” The statement is significant as the US–Iran Hormuz negotiations progressed — Salam is signalling that any regional deal must include provisions for Lebanon’s sovereignty over the south, not just a bilateral US–Iran arrangement that leaves Lebanese territory as a residual conflict zone. Israel continues to conduct strikes, sweep operations and artillery shelling in southern Lebanon notwithstanding the nominal ceasefire framework.

In a parallel development, senior Hezbollah figure Mohammad Hussein Fadlallah said Friday that Hezbollah and Amal are “not against a political solution in the south” — the most explicit statement of flexibility from the movement since the June framework deal. Hezbollah has previously insisted that any Israeli withdrawal must be unconditional; Fadlallah’s framing suggests some willingness to negotiate the modalities if the Israeli army exits. L’Orient Today (independent) flagged the remark as potentially significant but cautioned that it does not represent a formal Hezbollah policy shift.

L’Orient Today (independent) · Al Arabiya · Naharnet · Times of Israel
Why it matters to you

Any genuine political movement in south Lebanon reduces the single biggest risk discount on Beirut commercial real estate and client investment confidence. A genuine Israeli withdrawal track — even a slow one — would change the calculus for clients deferring projects or capital commitments. 💸 Lebanon · Economy

IMF Warns Lebanon Economy Contracts Significantly in 2026; Bank Resolution Law Advances; Eurobonds Slide on Fiscal Pressure

The IMF mission that concluded its Beirut visit on September 18 has published its formal statement: Lebanon’s economic activity is expected to “contract significantly” in 2026, driven by the ongoing conflict in the south, infrastructure damage, internal displacement and high energy costs. Inflation remains in double digits; the current account deficit has widened. The mission’s one positive note is that amendments to the Bank Resolution Law mark “a significant step forward” in the banking-sector restructuring strategy that is a prerequisite for any IMF financing arrangement.

Lebanese Eurobonds have slipped further, with the BLOM Bond Index at approximately 29.6 cents on the dollar (down 0.3% week-on-week) as fiscal stress and the war’s economic toll weigh on investor confidence. Blominvest’s weekly note flags that US Treasury yields at 5.21% are tightening the global cost of capital for frontier and distressed issuers, adding an external headwind to Lebanon’s already-constrained borrowing prospects. The Salam government is pushing bank-reform legislation and IMF dialogue as its primary credibility anchors heading into late 2026.

🏛 Real Estate · Global

Global House Prices May Finally Soften, Says the Economist — Mortgages at 7.48%, 10-Year at 5.21%; Real Value Falls Even if Nominal Prices Hold

This week’s Economist leads its newsletter with “The new mortgage shock: why house prices may be in trouble.” The argument is that the previous bulwarks against price falls — pandemic-era equity buffers and fiscal support — are largely exhausted, and rates at their current levels (US 30-year mortgage now averaging 7.477%, 10-year Treasury at 5.21%, both multi-decade highs) will cause real distress rather than mere cooling. Increased construction in markets including the US, UK and Australia is simultaneously adding supply. Even if nominal prices resist falling, inflation means real values are already declining in many markets.

J.P. Morgan’s housing-market outlook flags that existing home sales in the US have hit their lowest level in more than a year, and transaction volumes in the UK and Australia are falling sharply. The silver lining the Economist identifies: cash buyers and yield-focused investors gain relative attractiveness in this environment — particularly in cash-only markets like prime Beirut, where the absence of a mortgage market means the rate shock does not hit buyers directly. The global rate environment is making leveraged Western real estate look progressively less compelling compared with yield-bearing cash markets.
