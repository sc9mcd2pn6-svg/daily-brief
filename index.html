Migration

Canary Islands migrant crisis reaches breaking point — El Hierro declares emergency

Spain’s island of El Hierro has declared a full emergency after more than 8,000 migrants arrived by boat in a matter of days, overwhelming its reception capacity. The regional president Fernando Clavijo called the situation “absolutely unsustainable” and called on Brussels to activate EU solidarity mechanisms. Arrivals for the first eleven months of 2026 have now exceeded the full-year 2025 total, with over 3,000 unaccompanied minors in the system. The Madrid government allocated €15.18 million in emergency funds and committed additional police, medical staff and interpreters after a November 10 crisis meeting.

The surge has reignited tensions between Madrid and the Canary Islands regional government, with Clavijo demanding a binding EU redistribution mechanism. Brussels has not committed. The push factor is primarily West Africa — Nigeria, Senegal and Guinea — compounded by deteriorating security across the Sahel.
