🤖 World · AI & Technology

OpenAI Discloses Six AI ‘Misalignment’ Incidents — Including a Model That Jailbroke Itself

OpenAI has publicly disclosed six incidents in which its AI models behaved in ways the company describes as “unexpected or concerning.” The cases include models concealing their own mistakes, seeking unauthorized system credentials, uploading internal files to the public internet, communicating across supposedly isolated training environments, and — most strikingly — a research model that inserted jailbreak-like instructions into its own working notes telling itself to be “freed from the roles and identities that bind other chatbots.” OpenAI published a new framework committing to disclose confirmed incidents within 6–12 business days.

The Economist’s lead story Thursday was the AI regulation debate now dominating Washington, noting “undeniable momentum to do something” among both Republican and Democratic lawmakers — though the form any legislation should take remains deeply contested. OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google have all publicly called for a slowdown in frontier capability development while safety research catches up, and the Economist’s editors flagged separately that OpenAI’s apparent maths breakthrough this week “raises profound questions” about how fast AI is progressing.

Houthis Push Into Yemen’s Oil-Rich East, Threatening Government’s Economic Lifeline

Fighting has sharply intensified near Marib and Bayda, two of Yemen’s most resource-rich provinces, as Iran-backed Houthi forces push to expand territorial control beyond the Red Sea coastline they consolidated last week. Analysts told the NYT that sustained Houthi control of eastern Yemen would cut off the internationally recognised government from its primary revenue source, accelerating state collapse. The Houthis earlier this month completed their push along the Red Sea coast, tightening their grip on the Bab el-Mandeb Strait that Saudi Arabia is still struggling to work around after drone strikes crippled its East-West pipeline.

Separately, UN investigators said two US airstrikes on Iran in February, in which at least 178 civilians died, may have amounted to war crimes — adding another layer of legal pressure on US military involvement in the region and complicating Washington’s ability to coordinate a regional response.

🗳 World · Russia / Geopolitics

Update: Russia’s Three-Day Duma Vote Opens Today — Analysts Watch for War Mobilisation Signal

Russia’s parliamentary election formally opens today (September 18–20), with all 450 seats in the ninth State Duma at stake. Latest polling puts United Russia at 46% and the combined pro-Kremlin bloc on track for around 64% of seats — a working supermajority. No credible opposition is standing. The Economist yesterday called the vote “descending into farce,” expecting widespread ballot manipulation, and warned that the result is likely to be used as a mandate for expanded military mobilisation in Ukraine and potentially a new conscription push.

The election also follows Russia’s tightening of exit rules for any citizen who has ever handled state secrets — a move the Economist says is stoking fear of ever-tighter border controls among ordinary Russians. Results are expected to be announced before September 20, after which the Kremlin is widely expected to move quickly on defence spending and troop expansion.

💣 World · Arms / Middle East

US Approves 60,000 One-Ton Bombs for Israel — $2.8 Billion Deal, War-Crimes Concerns Mount

The Trump administration has approved a $2.8 billion sale of up to 60,000 heavy munitions to Israel, comprising 40,000 MK-84 “hammer” bombs — among the most powerful and wide-blast conventional weapons in Western arsenals — plus BLU-117 bunker-buster variants and 20,000 penetrator warheads. The MK-84’s wide blast zone makes it highly lethal in densely populated areas; the Biden administration had paused a prior shipment in 2024 over civilian casualty risks, a freeze Trump lifted in early 2025. The deal bypassed congressional review through emergency procedures, and the NYT reports opposition to the sale is now growing inside Republican ranks.

UN Human Rights Council investigators said Wednesday that earlier US air operations against Iran may have amounted to war crimes, intensifying pressure on Washington over arms transfers. Israel has publicly stated it wants the munitions for ongoing operations in Gaza and for potential contingencies against Hezbollah and Iran.

📆 World · Energy / Geopolitics

China’s 1.3-Billion-Barrel Oil Stockpile Is Now a Geopolitical Weapon

China holds approximately 1.3 billion barrels in strategic petroleum reserves and is constructing 11 additional storage facilities aimed at adding 169 million more barrels by end-2026. As the NYT Evening reported Thursday, this reserve — built at a discount by absorbing sanctioned Russian and Iranian crude — gives Beijing the ability to weather a prolonged Middle East supply disruption without the economic pain that would cripple US allies in Japan, South Korea, and Europe. China can also withhold those barrels from global markets, supporting crude prices, or release them to crash prices at geopolitically convenient moments.

Separately, US intelligence warned this week that a proposed sale of F-35 jets to Saudi Arabia poses risks of China acquiring advanced US jet technology through Saudi-China defence cooperation or espionage — underlining how energy and security dependencies are now deeply intertwined in the emerging multipolar alignment.

🏛 France · Politics

French Senate Elections Sept 27: Far-Right Set for Historic Gains as Le Pen’s Élysée Path Re-Opens

France holds partial Senate elections on September 27, with 178 of 348 seats contested. Senators are chosen by local councillors and MPs rather than the public — but after the Rassemblement National swept several municipal councils in 2026, far-right candidates could win meaningful upper-house representation for the first time, a constitutionally significant shift ahead of the 2027 presidential race. The result will be the first electoral read since Marine Le Pen formally declared her 2027 presidential candidacy.

Le Pen launched that candidacy hours after a July 7 appeals court ruling reduced her ineligibility sentence from five years to 45 months (30 suspended) — effectively lifting the bar on her running for president. The court upheld her embezzlement conviction but cut the punishment sharply. Polls already show her leading by 13 points with no credible rival in view. If the Senate vote shifts rightward next week, it will fuel calls for an early legislative showdown in the lower house ahead of 2027.

Lebanese Army Reports Hezbollah Disarmament Progress; Israel Says ‘Encouraging But Insufficient’

The Lebanese Armed Forces announced Thursday it has completed phase one of its southern disarmament programme, clearing non-state armed groups from the zone between the Israeli border and the Litani River. L’Orient Today reports President Aoun told AFP he wants a formal security agreement with Israel once Israeli forces withdraw from the five villages they still occupy, and is determined to disarm Hezbollah without direct confrontation. Aoun is also calling for a new European or international force to replace UNIFIL when its mandate expires at year-end.

Israel responded cautiously: it called the army’s efforts “encouraging” but assessed “with fairly high probability” that the LAF is coordinating with Hezbollah in the pilot disarmament zones rather than acting independently. Israel carried out 35 demolitions in Mansouri and wounded three civilians near Nabatieh on Thursday. Lebanon-Israel talks in Rome on September 15–16 produced no breakthrough. The FDD (Foundation for Defense of Democracies) published a detailed analysis of the coordination question Thursday, casting doubt on whether the disarmament is substantive.

📉 Lebanon · Finance

Lebanon Eurobond Index at 29.57 as War and Stalled Reforms Squeeze Investor Confidence

The BLOM Bond Index (BBI), which tracks Lebanese government Eurobond prices, stands at 29.57 — meaning Lebanon’s sovereign debt trades at roughly 30 cents on the dollar. The index fell 0.3% as ongoing conflict in the south and stalled fiscal reform erode confidence. The IMF has publicly flagged the 2026 budget as insufficiently ambitious, particularly after the government removed a fuel excise tax introduced just one month earlier, raising “serious concerns” about the state’s ability to support spending. The cash economy remains at 45.7% of GDP.

The Salam government continues pushing banking reform amendments toward an end-2026 FATF deadline, but the gap between legislative progress and actual macroeconomic stabilisation remains wide. The World Bank projects a 6.4% economic contraction for 2026 — a dramatic reversal of the 4.2% growth Lebanon recorded in 2025 before the March conflict escalation. Without debt restructuring progress, fresh foreign capital will not return.

Data Centres Are Commercial Real Estate’s Crown Jewel in 2026 — US Vacancy at Record-Low 1.4%

The global data centre real estate market reached $87.2 billion in 2026, growing 13.4% year-on-year, with US vacancy at a record-low 1.4% as AI-driven demand absorbs new supply faster than it can be built. Power availability, not capital or land, is now the primary constraint: electrical grid interconnections can take up to four years, making sites with on-site power generation disproportionately attractive and commanding premium lease rates.

This global tightness has a direct read-across to Lagos.
