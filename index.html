🛢️ World · Iran — Hormuz Update

Update — Brent near $90 as GasLog Shanghai LNG tanker disabled in Hormuz and Kuwait intercepts Iranian drones — maritime pressure intensifying

The Bermuda-flagged LNG carrier GasLog Shanghai, laden with Qatari LNG from Ras Laffan, was disabled by a projectile in the Strait of Hormuz overnight — the second Qatari LNG tanker struck in the strait within a month, further contracting global LNG supply. No casualties were reported but the vessel lost all power. Kuwait’s defence ministry simultaneously confirmed detecting and intercepting Iranian drones entering its airspace, the latest in a series of incursions against Gulf neighbours. Brent crude futures climbed toward $90 per barrel — up roughly 24% since the US-Israel campaign against Iran began in late February — with October delivery contracts at $89.53. Between eight and fifteen vessels are transiting the strait daily versus the pre-war average of roughly 130. Iran’s Foreign Ministry reiterated Hormuz will remain blocked until the US lifts its naval blockade, eases sanctions and pays reparations.

🏛️ World · US–Russia

US Senate passes Graham Russia sanctions bill 86–11 — authorises 100% tariffs on China and India for buying Russian oil; House vote in September

The Senate voted 86–11 on August 7 to pass the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026, named after the late Republican senator who championed the measure for years. The bill imposes sanctions on Russian officials, oligarchs, financial institutions and energy revenues, and critically authorises the President to levy tariffs of up to 100% on any country that is a “major buyer” of Russian oil and gas — a clause aimed squarely at China and India. It also tightens sanctions on Iran across its weapons and energy industries. The House does not return from summer recess until September, so a full vote is expected in early autumn; bipartisan support is broad and the bill is widely expected to pass. Once signed into law it would give the White House significant new leverage to pressure Asian buyers of Russian oil.

Al Jazeera · RFE/RL · GlobalSecurity · Congress.gov

If Asian buyers face 100% tariffs on Russian oil, demand would redirect to alternative suppliers, bidding Brent further upward. 🌳 World · Environment

Amazon deforestation falls 37% to a 13-year record low — INPE data shows just 2,874 sq km cleared in the year to July 2026, the best figure since satellite monitoring began

Brazil’s National Institute for Space Research (INPE) released data on August 7 showing the Amazon deforestation rate has fallen to its lowest level in thirteen years, with alerts covering just 2,874 square kilometres between August 2025 and July 2026 — 37% below the previous twelve months and 55.6% below the preceding decade’s average. The decline has been sustained over three consecutive years since President Lula da Silva took office in January 2023, supported by stepped-up environmental enforcement and reduced agricultural conversion pressure in protected areas. Scientists caution that despite the historic deforestation fall, wildfire incidence in the Amazon has paradoxically increased in 2026 due to extreme dry conditions — threatening forest interior even where the boundary holds. The improvement stands in striking contrast to Europe, where unprecedented heatwave-driven wildfires are this summer’s dominant story.

🗳️ World · Europe — Hungary

Hungary elects András Baka as president — first head of state not backed by Fidesz since 1995, marking a structural break from the Orbán era

Hungary’s parliament voted 140–0 on August 11 to elect András Baka, 73, former president of the Supreme Court, as the country’s new head of state; he takes office on August 19. Baka was the sole candidate, nominated by PM Péter Magyar’s governing alliance as a deliberate rebuke of Viktor Orbán — who had dismissed Baka from the Supreme Court in 2012 for denouncing a judicial purge that lowered the mandatory retirement age for judges. It is the first presidential election not won by a Fidesz-backed candidate since 1995, marking a clean break from the near-fifteen-year period of Orbán dominance. Analysts see the election as part of Magyar’s programme to reverse Hungary’s democratic backsliding, restore the country’s EU institutional standing and dismantle the judicial overhaul Orbán implemented from 2010 to 2024.

🔥 World · Canada

Canada declares wildfire emergency in British Columbia — 20,000 flee Okanagan as entire village of Summerland evacuated in Bald Range blaze

The Bald Range wildfire erupted this week in the Okanagan region of British Columbia, forcing the evacuation of approximately 20,000 residents including the entire community of Summerland — a resort, agricultural and winery town considered one of Canada’s most prized recreational regions. The provincial government declared a state of emergency, deploying hundreds of firefighters and aerial resources as the blaze spread rapidly through drought-parched forests and vineyards. The Okanagan has faced major fires in recent years as multi-year drought and record temperatures across Western North America sharpen fire risk each summer. The Canadian military has been put on standby to support civilian agencies; no fatalities have been confirmed but the evacuation order covers a large swath of agricultural and tourism infrastructure.

CTV News · Reuters · BBC
🇫🇷 France
🌲 France · Wildfires

France battles ‘monster’ wildfire on Assumption Day — 115,000 ha burned in 2026, worst season since 1949; storms expected this weekend as heatwave peaks at 40°C

France is fighting its most destructive wildfire season in more than 75 years, with 115,000 hectares burned so far in 2026 — a year the country’s meteorological service describes as its driest on record. The latest blaze erupted in the Landes region of southwest France overnight on August 14–15, forcing more than 500 people to evacuate the town of Luglon, with hundreds of firefighters and aerial tankers deployed to the area. Across France and Spain combined, more than 300,000 people have been evacuated as wildfires rage across both countries’ coastal and forested regions. Temperatures hit 40°C in western, central and southern France on Thursday ahead of today’s Assumption Day public holiday, with the RATP and SNCF networks reporting disruptions. Atlantic storm systems are forecast to push across France this Saturday and Sunday, beginning to break the heat and ease fire conditions.

🛒 Africa · E-Commerce & Investment

Jumia raises $50m from IFC and Axian Telecom — World Bank backs African e-commerce platform targeting its first profitable quarter in Q4 2026

Pan-African e-commerce platform Jumia Technologies closed a $50 million equity fundraising on August 12, co-led by the International Finance Corporation (World Bank Group), which contributed $25 million, and Axian Telecom — a Madagascar-based telecommunications conglomerate and Jumia’s largest existing shareholder. The investment funds expansion of Jumia’s integrated marketplace, its logistics network covering last-mile delivery hubs across Nigeria, Kenya, Côte d’Ivoire and Senegal, and its digital payments arm JumiaPay. The platform serves roughly 60,000 annual active sellers and has income-generating relationships with more than 100,000 independent sales agents continent-wide. Jumia expects to report its first positive EBITDA quarter in Q4 2026 — a turning point for a platform that has been loss-making since its 2012 founding. The IFC framed the deal as supporting Africa’s digital commerce infrastructure and job creation.

🇱🇧 Lebanon
🤝 Lebanon · Economy & Trade

Lebanon and Syria announce bilateral investment conference in Damascus for autumn 2026 — business council formed and four-port maritime corridor linked for joint development

Lebanon and Syria agreed on August 14 to hold a bilateral investment conference in Damascus this autumn — the most concrete signal yet that the two neighbours are moving toward a structured post-Assad economic reset. Economy Minister Amer Bisat told The National that opportunities span “everything from electricity and construction to ports”, with explicit co-operation planned between the ports of Latakia, Tartus, Tripoli and Beirut as a linked maritime corridor. A Lebanese-Syrian Business Council has been constituted with eleven founding companies (five Lebanese, six Syrian), and a separate government-to-government committee is reviewing more than 40 economic agreements signed during the Assad era. Lebanon urgently needs foreign capital, while Syria offers a next-door reconstruction pipeline the World Bank estimates at over $400 billion — representing a generational opportunity for Lebanese contractors and service providers that arrive early.

The National · Arab Weekly · SANA · Jerusalem Post
Why it matters to you

Syria’s reconstruction market is the largest single new business opportunity within a short drive of Beirut. If the Damascus conference happens this autumn, it is the entry point for Lebanese contractors and system integrators — including fire suppression, BMS, CCTV and security specialists — before the market matures and Gulf-backed competitors move in.

Act on this: Register with the Lebanese-Syrian Business Council now to be on the outreach list, and consider sending a representative to the Damascus conference this autumn — this is a first-mover window.
🕊️ Lebanon · Security

Lebanon disputes Hezbollah disarmament verification shortlist — UK, Italy, Switzerland and Indonesia named by mediators but Beirut says no agreement has been reached

Sources briefed on the Rome talks told Al-Monitor and Arab News that Israel and the US informally proposed Britain, Italy, Switzerland and Indonesia as countries that could deploy personnel to verify Hezbollah’s disarmament in southern Lebanon pilot zones. Lebanon’s government officially denied on August 13 that Beirut had agreed to any shortlist, saying the matter “remains under discussion with US officials.” The central obstacle is the scope of inspections: Israel wants any verification mechanism to cover private homes, which Lebanon considers a sovereignty red line. The absence of a verification framework continues to block the next phase of IDF withdrawal from retained positions in the south, and with no new Rome round before September, resolution before autumn is unlikely.

Al-Monitor · Arab News · US News · Aasharq Al-Awsat · Voice of Emirates
Why it matters to you

No verification framework means no IDF withdrawal, and no IDF withdrawal means south Lebanon reconstruction demand remains frozen. The home-inspection impasse is a hard political red line for Beirut; plan for the south Lebanon project pipeline to stay offline through at least Q1 2027 and focus domestic Lebanon sales on Beirut and Metn.
