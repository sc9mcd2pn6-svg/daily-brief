Fed Decision Day: 25bp Hike to 3.75–4% Expected — Energy War Forces First Rate Rise This Cycle

The Federal Open Market Committee announces its rate decision today at 2:00 PM ET, with markets pricing a 92.5% probability of a 25-basis-point hike from the current 3.5–3.75% band to 3.75–4%. This would be the first move up of the cycle: the FOMC held at its July 28–29 meeting by a 9–3 vote, but two factors shifted the calculus sharply — Chair Kevin Warsh’s hawkish Jackson Hole speech on August 28, and a strong August jobs report confirming the labour market is still too hot. Persistent energy-price shock from the Iran war — US diesel at $6 a gallon — has kept headline inflation above the Fed’s comfort zone despite slowing core goods prices.

US equities rallied ahead of the announcement as traders priced in near-certainty: the Dow up 509 points (+0.98%) and the S&P 500 up 0.86%. The 10-year yield sits at 5.01%, near a two-decade high, having already priced the move. The bigger question is Chair Warsh’s tone at the 2:30 PM press conference: if he signals further hikes ahead, bonds and equities could sell off sharply; a “hold for now” signal could rally risk assets briefly before the next energy data lands.

Nigeria Q2 GDP: Economy Grows 4.43% — Services Boom, Stronger Oil Output Signal Reforms Paying Off

Nigeria’s National Bureau of Statistics published Q2 2026 GDP data showing real year-on-year growth of 4.43%, edging above Q2 2025’s 4.23% and marking the third consecutive quarter above 4% — the longest such run since 2014. The services sector expanded 4.60%, driven by financial services, IT, and telecommunications. Agriculture grew 4.39%, helped by improved security in the north and better input access. Oil production rose to 1.72 million barrels per day (mbpd), up from 1.55 mbpd in Q1 and above the year-ago 1.68 mbpd — adding hard-currency export earnings that ease the naira’s pressure.

The caveat: industrial growth slowed sharply — 3.96% versus 7.46% in Q2 2025 — signalling that manufacturing remains constrained by energy costs and logistics. Analysts at NAN caution that sustainability is uncertain. The naira held in a narrow band through Q2, meaning import-cost pressure for businesses buying dollar-denominated equipment eased somewhat versus the brutal 2025 depreciation. Premium Times notes the GDP data provides the most credible case yet that President Tinubu’s painful subsidy reforms are beginning to show macroeconomic results.

IMF Calls Lebanon 2026 Budget “Not Ambitious Enough”; Cash Economy Still 45.7% of GDP

IMF officials visiting Beirut in September criticised Lebanon’s 2026 draft budget as insufficiently ambitious and falling short of the structural reforms required to unlock a formal IMF programme. The Fund’s key demands — banking-sector restructuring, addressing an estimated $72 billion in financial-sector losses, and rebuilding Banque du Liban reserve adequacy — remain unmet. The IMF also highlighted that Lebanon’s cash economy accounts for 45.7% of GDP, one of the highest ratios globally, making tax collection, monetary policy, and anti-money-laundering compliance almost impossible to enforce at any meaningful scale.

A small positive: the BdL’s push for electronic payments is gaining real traction. Card transactions have more than doubled at restaurants and large supermarkets since BdL cut interchange fees and mandated POS terminals inside government offices. That shift, if sustained, creates the data trail the IMF needs to assess fiscal compliance and begins reducing the informal economy’s footprint. Without an IMF programme, however, Lebanon’s Eurobond prices will stay depressed and the government’s capacity to fund infrastructure remains severely constrained.
