Jensen Huang reinstated as Nvidia CEO — Trump overrides the board; S&P 500 posts best day since April 2025

Nvidia’s board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as president, CEO and board member on Monday, 23 November, reversing a decision taken just 11 days earlier. Trump drove the reversal: he directed Vice President JD Vance to work with Huang and Nvidia’s board over the weekend, and he personally assured the company that Huang faced no criminal charges and posed no national security risk. Nvidia shares surged 31%, adding nearly $1 trillion in market value in a single session. The S&P 500 rose 4.7% — its best day since April 2025 — and the Nasdaq soared 6.5%, recovering almost all the losses suffered since Huang’s resignation on 12 November.

The Trump administration barred Alice Huang’s firm, Megaspeed, from buying advanced AI chips. Business partners are also wary: several hyperscale clients paused contracts during the leadership uncertainty.

World · Ukraine War

Russia’s war deaths top 700,000 — hacked Rosstat data gives highest single-country toll since World War II

Exiled Russian media outlets Meduza and Mediazona have significantly raised their estimate of Russian military deaths to at least 700,000 over nearly five years of fighting, using a trove of hacked data from the Russian state statistics service Rosstat. The figure, if correct, is the highest single-country combat death toll recorded since World War II. The analysis made use of mortality data from Russian regions and cross-referenced it against official casualty reports, which Russian authorities have consistently minimised.

Peace talks between Russia and Ukraine remain stalled. As of late October, the two sides had signalled willingness to resume US-brokered negotiations under a humanitarian channel led by First Lady Melania Trump, but Kyiv insists on security guarantees and opposes ceding territory. Russia has reiterated that it will not accept Western troops in Ukraine as part of any ceasefire arrangement. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas has accused Russia of using negotiations as a pretext to continue the war.

Sources: NYT Evening (Nov 23, 2026); Al Jazeera (Nov 13, 2026); Boston Globe / AP (Oct 24, 2026)

World · US Politics

Democrats clinch 258 House seats — biggest majority for either party in 30 years; both chambers blue from January

The Associated Press has called the last outstanding House races, confirming that Democrats will hold 258 seats in the next Congress — the largest majority for either party in a mid-term election for three decades, and a record for any party this century. Combined with their Senate gains, Democrats will control both chambers when the new Congress is seated in January. The rout reflected voter backlash against Trump’s second term, particularly on economic and healthcare issues.

Among the notable results: Democrat Chuck Hubbard, a retired journalist, unseated 11-term Republican Virginia Foxx in a conservative North Carolina district. The Democratic National Committee has already announced plans for 12 presidential primary debates in advance of the 2028 election. The result makes it substantially harder for Trump to pursue legislative priorities in his final two years, though executive-branch actions remain unaffected.

Sources: NYT Today’s Headlines (Nov 23, 2026); AP; NBC News

World · Gulf Economy

Economist: Oman’s fragile boom — “the unlikely winner from war in the Gulf” as diverted cargo lifts its ports

As most Gulf economies contract under the weight of the Iran war — Capital Economics forecasts the GCC to shrink by 5% in 2026 overall — Oman stands out as a beneficiary. With its ports at Sohar, Duqm and Salalah located outside the Strait of Hormuz, Oman has absorbed a significant share of diverted cargo. Output is up roughly 10% and port revenues have surged. The Economist frames the boom as “fragile”: it rests entirely on geography and could reverse quickly if the Strait reopens or if the conflict escalates to Oman’s own territory.

The UAE’s Fujairah port, also outside the Strait, is similarly benefiting. Saudi Arabia has used its East-West pipeline to reroute some oil exports. Bahrain faces the most acute fiscal risk, with its government deficit widening sharply. The broader Gulf story is one of an uneven shock: war has exposed structural vulnerabilities across the region while rewarding neutral geography. Allianz Trade estimates total war-related losses for the Gulf could reach $790 billion through 2030.

Sources: The Economist Today (Nov 23, 2026); The National (Aug 2026); Allianz Trade; Invezz (Mar 2026)

Why it matters to you

Oman’s port boom is driving construction of new logistics, warehousing and industrial facilities at Sohar and Duqm — exactly the kind of large-footprint buildings that need fire alarm, suppression and BMS systems. If Quasar has any Gulf distribution channels, Oman deserves a closer look as a secondary market this year while the opportunity window is open.

Lebanon · Banking

Lebanese banks refuse MoF Eurobond tax; Finance Ministry says “no exceptions to the law”

Lebanon’s Association of Banks has refused to comply with a Finance Ministry demand requiring banks to refile and correct their tax declarations relating to Eurobond provisions recorded before the 2020 default. The Ministry, under Finance Minister Yassine Jaber, has held its ground, declaring in a public statement that “no exceptions” will be made in applying the law, and has extended the amended-filing deadline to 15 December 2026. The refusal is the latest escalation in the standoff between the government and the banking sector, which has been using weekly Wednesday strikes throughout November to protest a broader set of regulatory and tax demands.

The Eurobond tax question is entangled with the broader Financial Gap Law that the IMF has made a precondition for a programme: the law would formally allocate losses from the banking sector’s Eurobond exposure among the state, banks and depositors. With the IMF deal now expected no earlier than Q1 2027 (per Finance Minister Jaber), the standoff between banks and government is likely to continue through December. The next bank strike is Wednesday 25 November.

Sources: Annahar (Nov 2026); L’Orient Today (Nov 2026); The National (Nov 13, 2026)

Why it matters to you

The continuing bank-MoF standoff means that every Wednesday the banking system is partially closed — a practical operating constraint for Quasar and FGC when processing USD payments, signing cheques, or releasing project funds. Plan transactions for Tuesday, Thursday or Friday this month. The Dec 15 deadline for banks will likely be followed by another round of pressure; a forced settlement could affect bank liquidity and credit availability in Q1 2027.

World Bank: Lebanon reconstruction progress “slow” — $250M loan largely unused as rubble still uncleared in south

A World Bank official acknowledged on 18 November that Lebanon’s reconstruction is making slow progress, despite a $250 million infrastructure repair and rubble-clearing loan approved in June 2025. Lebanese officials blame Israel’s ongoing military presence in the south and near-daily air strikes for delaying work in the most damaged areas. The World Bank’s Summer 2026 Lebanon Economic Monitor projects a -6.4% GDP contraction for 2026, one of the most severe in the region.

Finance Minister Jaber, speaking to the Arab Bankers Association, said Lebanon is “working to reposition itself on the region’s economic map” and stressed the country is “not asking for charity but for partnership.” However, with the IMF programme delayed to Q1 2027 and the Financial Gap Law still unresolved in parliament, international reconstruction financing remains largely on hold. The Beirut-Khalde highway and the southern coastal road remain key unstarted projects.

Sources: Al Jazeera (Nov 18, 2026); L’Orient Today; The National (Nov 13, 2026); World Bank Lebanon Economic Monitor (Aug 2026)
