☢️US & Iran

Iran nuclear talks hit the 60-day wall — US and Tehran deadlocked on inspections and frozen assets

The 60-day negotiating window opened by the June 19 Geneva Memorandum of Understanding is at or past its deadline, with the US and Iran still split on the two core issues: the pace at which Iran must surrender its 440 kg stockpile of highly enriched uranium, and whether IAEA inspection access or the unfreezing of roughly $24 billion in Iranian assets comes first. Markets rallied on August 3 after President Trump separately called off a planned military strike against Iran, with US equities rising and Brent pulling back — though analysts warn that a deal collapse could quickly reverse both moves. The Strait of Hormuz remains a live flashpoint; a vessel was struck there in recent days.

🇺🇦Ukraine

Russia’s bloodiest month of 2026 — yet August’s opening barrage is its largest strike yet

Russia suffered 42,860 killed, wounded, or captured in July — its highest single-month toll of the war — while advancing just 1.22 km² per day. The Kremlin responded with an escalating air campaign: on August 2 alone, Russian forces launched 190 guided aviation bombs and 6,595 kamikaze drones in 24 hours, forcing Ukraine’s Pivdennyi steel plant (Kryvyi Rih) and the Poltava Iron & Steel Plant to halt or partially suspend operations as of August 3. The Kremlin is simultaneously running a cognitive campaign claiming Ukrainian lines are collapsing; ISW-linked analysts have uniformly assessed this as false.

Gaza Phase 2 ceasefire “remains on paper” — 1,222 killed since October truce took effect

The Palestinian Authority stated this morning that the Phase 2 roadmap — covering governance, disarmament, an international stabilisation force, and reconstruction — “remains on paper,” as Israeli strikes have continued daily since the agreement was announced. 📊Global Economy

IMF holds 3.0% global growth but lifts inflation to 4.7% — war-energy shock dominates the outlook

The IMF’s July 2026 World Economic Outlook Update “Global Economy in Crosscurrents of War and Technology” maintains 3.0% global growth but raises the 2026 inflation forecast from 4.1% to 4.7%, driven by elevated energy prices from Strait of Hormuz disruption. The OECD calls conditions “weakened by energy shock and rising inflationary pressures,” with Europe most exposed; the ECB has raised rates in response. The IMF does identify one meaningful tailwind: AI-driven demand, increasingly relevant for economies integrated into global technology supply chains.

🌍 Africa
🌆Nigeria · Data Centres

Nigeria now has 28 data centres — Lagos cements its position as West Africa’s digital hub

Nigeria’s data centre count has reached 28 (21 in Lagos, 3 in Abuja), with the market valued at $2.22 billion in 2026 and projected to reach $4.36 billion by 2031 at a 14.5% CAGR, per Mordor Intelligence. Equinix’s LG3 at Victoria Island opened in Q1; the 100 MW Kasi Cloud AI hyperscale campus (42 hectares) has been under construction since March; and Airtel’s Nxtra — set to be Nigeria’s largest facility — is due to go live before year-end. The principal constraint is no longer capital or land: it is reliable power delivery, an acute bottleneck given persistent grid instability.

🏛️Nigeria · Economy & Security

Nigeria’s N15.5 trillion debt-service bill exceeds security, defence, education, and health combined

Nigeria’s 2026 debt-service obligation of N15.52 trillion surpasses the combined budget allocations for security, defence, education, and health — a structural squeeze that constrains public investment. Real GDP is still forecast at 4.3% for the year, the naira is steady at N1,400–N1,500/USD, and reserves sit at $45.4 billion. On the security front, Menas Associates warns the environment is expected to deteriorate through 2026, driven by IPOB dynamics, Niger Delta militant posturing, and spillover from neighbouring-country instability — a continuing drag on FDI and agricultural output.

🔥Wildfires & Economy

France deploys A400M military transports for wildfires — fuel aid extended through August

France has activated Airbus A400M Atlas military transport aircraft in its firefighting response as the country faces an exceptionally severe 2026 wildfire season, with drought conditions expected to persist through August and further water restrictions anticipated. The government separately extended a €100 fuel aid programme for lower-income households through August 31, citing rising pump prices linked to Middle East energy disruption. Several regulatory and financial changes also took effect this August, including adjustments to the Livret A savings rate and household electricity tariffs.

📵Consumer Law

France bans unsolicited cold calls from August 11

From August 11, companies are prohibited from calling consumers without explicit prior consent unless the call relates to an existing contract or involves selling periodicals. The measure marks a significant shift in French consumer protection law and comes with financial penalties for violators — a wide-ranging rule affecting sectors from insurance to telecoms and digital services.
