NBC News projects Democrats have won control of the House of Representatives with what could be their largest majority in nearly two decades. Update: France general strike today — five confederations, SNCF walkout; biggest action in years

Today is the November 5 general strike. Five major union confederations (CFDT, CGT, FO, Unsa and FSU) called a nationwide day of action over wages, pensions and the government’s austerity-inflected 2027 budget plan. All four SNCF rail unions joined separately, targeting the day before the inaugural TGV M high-speed run as a show of force. Today’s turnout figure will be the key data point: a large number will strengthen union bargaining leverage and hand Le Pen ammunition ahead of 2027; a low number will give the government grounds to dismiss the movement. Watch the Interior Ministry’s 6 p.m. count.

US Economy

US renters face a financial cliff — 54% rent-burdened, homeownership hopes at multi-year low

The Economist highlights a deepening crisis in US rental housing: 53.5% of American renters are classified as “rent-burdened” (spending more than 30% of gross income on housing), and the Philadelphia Fed’s June 2026 survey found nearly two-thirds of renters cut back on essential or discretionary spending to make rent. Perhaps most striking, only 6.4% of renters said they expect to take out a mortgage within the next six months — down from about 15% in January 2025 — as the dream of homeownership retreats. The Fed’s decision to hold rates in October, with an 85% market-implied probability of a December hike, has kept mortgage rates elevated.

The rental crisis feeds directly into the political discontent that helped drive Democratic gains in the House. It also points to sustained demand for rental housing as an asset class: for investors able to supply well-priced rental units, the structural demand is strong even as affordability deteriorates.

Source: Economist Today (3 Nov); Philadelphia Fed; Harvard Joint Center for Housing Studies
