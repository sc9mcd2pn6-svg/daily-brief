Aviation

FAA declares nationwide ground stop for 4 hours — “outdated” air traffic control equipment blamed

Federal aviation authorities issued a rare nationwide ground stop just before 4 p.m. Eastern on Friday, halting all departing US flights for over four hours. It was lifted by 8:15 p.m. ET after the FAA restored the communications network linking air traffic control towers. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed “incredibly old” air traffic control routers that became overloaded with data and gave inconsistent readings. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin ruled out a cyberattack and said the outage was not related to the ongoing Microsoft global disruption. CBS News reported that an update to the private ATC communications network caused a system reboot that cascaded into a full outage.

Thousands of departing flights were cancelled or delayed; cascading effects continued overnight. Officials could not confirm whether the outage was related to a recently deployed AI-powered air traffic tool. It was only the third US-wide ground stop in decades — the previous two were in 2023 and on September 11, 2001.

The Supreme Court announced Friday it will take up the legality of President Trump’s Day-One executive order that effectively shut down avenues for people fleeing persecution to apply for asylum at the southern border. Lower courts have consistently ruled against the administration, which has continued to deny asylum claims under a temporary order. Economy & IMF

Lebanon IMF mission ends without preliminary deal — small committee to finalize Financial Gap Law before year-end

The IMF mission that arrived in Beirut on November 10 concluded its final session on Friday without signing a staff-level agreement. Finance Minister Yassine Jaber announced the formation of a smaller technical committee to finalize outstanding gaps in the Financial Gap Law text, with a target of taking a unified draft to Parliament before the end of 2026. The core sticking point remains how losses are distributed between the state, the central bank, commercial banks, and depositors — the central bank is seeking carve-outs that the IMF has resisted. The gap to be distributed is estimated at $70–80 billion.

Jaber said the sequencing is: pass the Financial Gap Law, then banking-sector reform, then depositor repayment begins. Central Bank Governor Karim Souaid said a breakthrough is imminent “before year-end.” L’Orient Today notes the committee route is a face-saving measure, not a failure — but it pushes the earliest possible IMF programme into 2027, prolonging Lebanon’s financial limbo as GDP contracts 6.4% in 2026.

Source: Daily Beirut (13 Nov); L’Orient Today; LBC Lebanon (13 Nov)
