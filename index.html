💥 World · Middle East / Energy

Houthis Fire Ballistic Missiles at Riyadh and Yanbu Oil Facilities — Saudi Defences Intercept

Yemen’s Houthi militia fired a wave of ballistic missiles and drones at the Saudi capital Riyadh and the Yanbu coastal oil facility on Friday, in the most significant escalation against Saudi Arabia in the current phase of the conflict. Saudi air defences intercepted the missiles, with residents near Riyadh’s airport reporting a large plume of black smoke from an interception overhead. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree claimed the strikes were retaliation for a Saudi-led coalition attack on Sanaa and targeted “sensitive” sites including Aramco infrastructure.

No casualties or serious damage were reported, but the attack marks the first time Houthis have successfully launched against the Saudi capital in the current escalation cycle and represents a significant extension of their strike range and ambition. Brent crude spiked to $108 briefly on drone-strike fears before stabilising around $105 as Riyadh confirmed its defences held. The incident overshadows Saudi-Houthi talks that US diplomats were quietly facilitating via Oman, and will test whether Riyadh can maintain its restraint posture into UNGA week.

🇩🇪 World · Europe / Far Right

AfD Wins Mecklenburg-Vorpommern; Far-Left Takes Berlin — German Political Order Fractures

Sunday’s German state elections produced a fractured picture: the Alternative für Deutschland (AfD) came first in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern with 37.9% of the vote, barely ahead of the SPD at 35.7% — the first time the far-right has led a state poll in Germany’s north. In Berlin, the far-left Die Linke won the capital’s election, projected to head a coalition for the first time since reunification. The AfD polled 16% in Berlin. Chancellor Friedrich Merz called both results a “disaster” for his government, which is already under pressure from low approval ratings and its unpopular reform agenda.

The results confirm that the AfD surge is structural, not a one-off: the party has now finished first in Saxony-Anhalt (September 6, 43.8%), and Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, and maintains double-digit presence nationally. Yet a cross-party “firewall” of non-cooperation continues to prevent AfD from governing. The Berlin result pulls in the opposite direction — a signal that urban Germany is reacting against the right-wing wave. The NYT had flagged this Sunday vote as a potential turning point either way; the answer is: both happened simultaneously in different states.

Parliament Renews Confidence in Salam Government — 68 Votes, Hezbollah Boycotts

Lebanon’s parliament formally renewed its confidence in Prime Minister Nawaf Salam’s government by 68 votes to 12, with two abstentions, following two days of general policy debate on September 16-17. Hezbollah lawmakers left the chamber and did not participate — a tacit statement of opposition rather than a direct block. Amal Movement lawmakers from the Shiite political bloc sided with the government. PM Salam used his speech to address banking sector reform, the 2027 budget, southern Lebanon reconstruction, and the Israeli army withdrawal, signalling he intends to push forward on the reform agenda.

The confidence vote consolidates the Salam government’s parliamentary mandate in a period of intense international scrutiny: UNGA week brings Lebanon’s UNIFIL mandate (due for December expiry) back to the Security Council, where France — currently holding the rotating presidency — will be a key advocate for renewal. L’Orient Today notes that Salam’s ability to sustain reform momentum while managing the UNIFIL and Israeli-withdrawal dossiers simultaneously will define the political tone of Q4 2026.
