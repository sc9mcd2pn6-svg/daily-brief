🗳️ World · Europe (Update)

Update: AfD Wins Saxony-Anhalt with 44.5% — Germany’s Far-Right Claims Historic Victory, Government Formation Unclear

Sunday’s state election in Saxony-Anhalt delivered the AfD its strongest result in any German state since the war: 44.5% of the vote, more than double the CDU’s 18.5%, with the SPD at 8% and Greens at 9%. AfD co-leader Alice Weidel called it a “historic” mandate. The far-right party holds a clear plurality but no outright majority, so forming a government requires either a coalition partner or a collapse of the mainstream “firewall” that has kept all parties from governing alongside the AfD since 2021. The Economist ran a leader this weekend arguing Germany needs a new strategy for coping with AfD — the old taboo of refusing to engage is increasingly untenable when the party is polling at nearly half the vote in key states.

CDU chancellor Friedrich Merz now faces an acute dilemma: lead a minority government propped up by left-leaning parties, or watch the firewall fracture. BRICS tensions, the Iran war, and rising inflation mean this moment lands in a particularly fragile European context. Results from Germany’s most economically deprived east rarely stay contained there — this will reverberate in the 2028 federal election calculus.

🕊️ World · Ukraine (Update)

Update: Witkoff & Kushner Land in Kyiv After Moscow — Ukraine Peace Talks Revived After Eight-Month Freeze

President Trump’s envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner arrived in Kyiv on Sunday after a five-hour meeting with Putin in Moscow on Saturday, marking the most serious US-brokered diplomacy on Ukraine since January. Both Russia and Ukraine agreed to suspend aerial attacks on each other’s capitals for the duration of the visits, offering a brief respite but no broader ceasefire. Putin told the envoys he has “full trust” in US mediation; Zelenskyy’s position and what he offered in return is not yet public.

The NYT reports that no deal was reached after the lengthy Moscow session. The talks aim to revive negotiations that have been in a deep freeze since Russia rejected Trump’s January framework. Treasury sanctions official Gene Lange joined the mission, signalling that economic inducements — sanctions relief — may be part of the offer to Moscow. A deal remains far off but the shuttle itself signals a renewed US push before November’s midterms.

🌐 World · Geopolitics

BRICS Summit Opens in New Delhi This Saturday — Xi, Putin, Pezeshkian Attending; Iran-UAE Tensions Inside the Bloc

The 18th BRICS Summit convenes at Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi, on 12–13 September under the theme “Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability.” Xi Jinping, Vladimir Putin, and Iran’s Masoud Pezeshkian are all expected to attend, making this the first gathering of those three since the Iran war began in February. Iran and the UAE — both BRICS members — clashed at a foreign ministers’ session in May; their shared summit seating this week will test whether the bloc can paper over a live war inside its own ranks.

Running alongside is the inaugural iBRICS sovereign capital summit, bringing 500+ institutional investors managing a combined $1 trillion. India has framed this as its coming-out moment as a Global South convener, a role that gains weight as US alliances fracture. The Economist notes that Xi’s attendance caps a heavy travel month including Kyrgyzstan and Egypt — a sustained display of Chinese diplomatic reach aimed squarely at the US-led order.

🏦 World · Economics

ECB Decision Thursday: Another Rate Hike Expected as Euro-Zone Inflation and Bond Yields Rise Alarmingly

The European Central Bank meets Thursday September 10 with markets almost fully pricing in a second consecutive 25bp rate increase, following June’s hike — the first in three years. The Economist highlights that both euro-zone bond yields and inflation are rising “alarmingly” — the oil price spike driven by the Iran war is the key transmission mechanism. ECB minutes from July said the pause should “not be interpreted as the end of the tightening cycle,” and July’s data gave no reason to hold.

A Thursday hike would mark the second ECB increase of this cycle and signal at least one more by year-end. Tightening in Europe, tightening in the US, and elevated Brent above $96 create a triple pressure on emerging markets that borrow in dollars — including Lebanon’s reform creditors and Nigeria’s data-centre developers relying on foreign capital.

🤖 World · Technology

Economist: AI Has Already Created 1 Million US Jobs — Apocalypse Postponed, Boom Is Here

The Economist’s lead story this Sunday, pegged to Friday’s blockbuster US August jobs report (162,000 added, unemployment steady at 4.1%), argues that young workers — long cast as AI’s first victims — are holding up remarkably well. The magazine estimates AI has so far created around one million new jobs in America, with an “AI hiring boom” displacing some roles while generating many new ones. The technology is severely disrupting certain companies and specific workers, but the headline apocalypse has not arrived.

The NYT covers the same trend through a harsher lens: Kenyan essay-writing gig workers have seen their livelihoods wiped out as overseas students switch to AI, and 12.7 million Chinese graduates are entering a job market simultaneously squeezed by AI and slowing growth. The picture divides sharply by country and skill level. For the US, August’s jobs resilience makes a September Fed rate cut far less likely and strengthens Warsh’s hand against Trump’s pressure to ease.

⚔️ World · Iran War (Update)

Update: Saudi Arabia Joins US in Striking Iranian Militias in Iraq — War Widens as Oman Brokers Hormuz Deal Talks

The United States and Saudi Arabia jointly struck Iranian-backed militia positions in Iraq last week, killing at least 20 fighters and six Iranian military advisers — Saudi Arabia’s first active combat role after six months of absorbing Iranian missile and drone strikes on its territory and oil refineries. Riyadh had previously limited its role to expelling Iranian officials and signing a mutual-defence pact with Turkey and Pakistan. The strikes mark a qualitative escalation that draws the Gulf’s largest economy directly into the war’s kinetic phase.

On the diplomatic track, a US official says Iran and Oman are making “progress” on a framework to reopen the Strait of Hormuz; Washington has committed to lifting its Iranian-port blockade once commercial shipping is fully restored. Iran insists it won’t move first without sanctions relief and frozen-asset release. Brent at $96.82 reflects the market’s verdict: a deal remains far off.

🔍 Lebanon · Finance

Alvarez & Marsal Starts Forensic Audit of Lebanon’s Central Bank — Tracing $20 Billion in Missing Reserves

Lebanon’s central bank (BDL) officially engaged Alvarez & Marsal on September 4 for a new forensic audit covering all BDL financial transactions from October 1, 2019 to December 31, 2023 — the period spanning the country’s financial collapse. The scope includes foreign-asset movements, fuel-import letters of credit, government subsidies for basic goods, and transfers to the public sector. The audit aims to trace more than $20 billion in depleted foreign reserves and identify any misuse, embezzlement, or waste. This comes alongside an IMF-welcomed amended Bank Restructuring Law passed in August and Goldman Sachs’s warning that Lebanese Eurobonds at BBI ~29.7 look “overvalued.”

An independent audit of this depth is a prerequisite for any IMF programme and creditor restructuring talks. L’Orient Today notes it is the firmest accountability step taken since the 2023 BDL audit released preliminary findings. Whether the Salam government can sustain the political will to act on findings — especially if they implicate current power brokers — remains the critical question.

BLOMINVEST · L’Orient Today · Asharq Al-Awsat · Daily Guardian
Why it matters to you

A credible audit that advances the IMF reform track is the single most important catalyst for Lebanon’s banking re-normalisation — which in turn unlocks dollar-denominated project financing for Beirut real estate and major systems projects. This is a slow fuse, but if the audit holds, 2027 starts to look like a real recovery window for the Lebanese market.

Act on this: Watch for any A&M preliminary findings due within 3–6 months; if the audit proceeds cleanly, flag to your Beirut real-estate clients that dollar financing may begin to flow earlier than they expect.
📉 Lebanon · Economy

Lebanon PMI at 50.1 in August — Economy Stalls as Tourism Disappoints and Export Orders Contract

The BLOM Lebanon PMI fell to 50.1 in August, down from 50.7 in July and the third consecutive month of barely-positive expansion, according to BLOMINVEST. New export orders contracted for the first time in two months, and new domestic orders also fell — attributed to a poor summer tourism season and regional security concerns weighing on confidence. The reading confirms that Lebanon’s partial economic rebound of early 2026 is losing momentum as the Iran war elevates fuel costs and disrupts regional trade routes.

Lebanon’s M3 money supply registered $65.11 billion as of August 27, broadly stable. But with World Bank projecting GDP at -6.4% for 2026 and inflation at 17.5%, the PMI reading aligns with a picture of stagnation rather than recovery. The IMF’s technical team resumed meetings in Beirut last week, pressing for more ambitious 2027 budget targets.
