Trump signs order exempting Russia from Lindsey Graham’s “Sanctions from Hell” Act

On the very day mandated by law for the first round of sanctions designations, Trump issued an executive order exempting Russia from the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026 — the bill he signed in September bearing the name of the late South Carolina senator. The act authorised 100% tariffs on the five largest buyers of Russian energy and required Treasury to announce designations by October 18. Instead, the White House moved to shield Moscow, drawing furious bipartisan condemnation; a cross-party group of lawmakers had explicitly urged enforcement as recently as September 30.

The move lands ten days before Israel’s election and three weeks before US midterms, deepening the already severe US-Ukraine rift. Zelensky’s camp responded by saying Trump could yet lift further Russia sanctions, suggesting Kyiv sees this as an opening for fresh pressure rather than a final position.

Albania: student riots burn Education Ministry in Tirana — PM Rama faces resignation calls

Student protests in Albania’s capital escalated into full-scale riots this week, with rioters setting the Education Ministry on fire, attacking banks and government buildings, and clashing with police for more than eight hours across the city. Authorities reported 23 officers injured and 35 people detained. Demonstrations began over unaffordable university fees, poor campus conditions and bleak graduate job prospects; organizers are now demanding Prime Minister Edi Rama’s outright resignation. Rama blamed opposition politicians for hijacking the movement and signalled openness to talks with student leaders.

OCCRP reports the violence is the worst since the 1997 civil unrest, with multiple government buildings and bank branches set ablaze. The protests have drawn comparisons to the 2019 Albanian unrest over corruption, but analysts note they are more socially broad-based and student-led rather than politically orchestrated.

Source: Al Jazeera (15 Oct 2026); OCCRP; ABC News / AP

China dominates EV battery “black mass” recycling — rivals scramble to keep up

China has cornered the processing of “black mass” — the metallic powder produced by shredding spent electric-vehicle batteries, containing lithium, cobalt and nickel — and is now the world’s dominant recycler of EV battery materials. The Economist reports that as the first wave of EV batteries reaches end-of-life, China’s rivals in the EU and US are racing to build competing capacity but face a years-long lag. China’s advantage here extends its control of critical minerals from upstream mining into the recycling loop, meaning Western supply-chain independence remains out of reach for this decade.

The piece is part of a broader Economist pattern this month examining how China secures strategic control in adjacent industries — a dynamic that will shape the energy transition globally and defence procurement across NATO members.

Source: The Economist (17 Oct 2026)

Financial Gap Law: IMF mission over — parliament faces November ultimatum

The October IMF mission has concluded without a staff-level agreement, as the Financial Gap Law — passed by cabinet in December 2025 but still without a parliamentary vote date — remains the last major structural hurdle to any deal. Finance Minister Yassine Jaber used the Bangkok IMF-World Bank meetings to reaffirm Lebanon’s reform commitment and to lobby for reconstruction financing tied to south Lebanon, but the IMF made clear that without parliamentary action on the loss-allocation law, a programme cannot advance. A November mission has been confirmed; the window to schedule a Knesset vote and demonstrate credibility before it is narrow.

The bill’s contested loss-sharing formula — splitting the banking sector’s estimated $70 billion hole between the state, the central bank, commercial banks and depositors — continues to face opposition from Hezbollah, Amal, the Lebanese Forces and the Association of Banks, making parliamentary passage far from certain.

Beirut

RCPIE Beirut closes with $3.7B in YTD transactions — Gulf capital fills the domestic vacuum

The three-day RCPIE Beirut real estate conference (15–17 October) wrapped up yesterday with data confirming $3.7 billion in Lebanese property transactions year to date, a figure sustained almost entirely by Gulf buyers from Saudi Arabia and Kuwait as domestic buyers stay sidelined by capital controls and the ongoing economic crisis. Participants flagged that prime Beirut asking prices remain “out of sync with reality” (RAMCO) — elevated by seller expectations set in pre-crisis dollar terms — while effective transaction prices are meaningfully lower.

The picture is one of a two-speed market: a thin, Gulf-dominated top end holding value, and a stagnant middle-to-lower tier where liquidity is absent. International interest remains focused on ultra-prime locations around Achrafieh and the waterfront, with little activity elsewhere.

Source: RCPIE conference data; RAMCO Lebanon; L’Orient Today

Why it matters to you

The resilience of ultra-prime Beirut is good news for existing holdings in the right areas, but the two-speed structure means price appreciation is concentrated in a very narrow segment. If you are assessing the value of personal holdings, the RAMCO note on the gap between asking and transaction prices is a practical caution: effective value may be 15–25% below listed prices in the current market.
