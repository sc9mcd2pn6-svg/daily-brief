🛢️ Update: Oil Shock — Jazan & SPR

Update: Brent surges to $87.69 on new fronts — Houthis strike Saudi Arabia’s Jazan refinery; US Strategic Petroleum Reserve falls below 300 million barrels for first time since 1983

Oil extended a four-session gain streak to brush $88 a barrel as three fresh risk factors compounded the Hormuz closure. First, Houthi militants claimed a drone-and-missile strike on Saudi Arabia’s Jazan refinery — the first successful hit on Gulf refining infrastructure since the conflict escalated. Second, the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve dropped below 300 million barrels, its lowest level since 1983, as Washington depletes emergency stockpiles during the protracted blockade. Third, President Trump publicly branded Iran’s war-reparations demand “outrageous,” while Tehran reaffirmed no Hormuz reopening until the US ends its “naval blockade” of Iranian ports. The Oman-brokered corridor talks remain the only realistic near-term path.

🇫🇷 France
📵 France · Cold-Call Ban — Day 1

France bans all unsolicited commercial phone calls from today — Macron government’s anti-démarchage téléphonique law enters force on 11 August

Starting today, France’s new anti-télémarketing law prohibits unsolicited commercial calls to consumers who have not explicitly opted in — replacing the previous opt-out Bloctel registry with a full opt-in regime. Fines reach €375,000 per violation; a 30-day grace period runs before active enforcement. Marketing, insurance, and energy sectors face the biggest compliance overhaul. Consumer groups called it France’s most significant consumer-protection reform since 2014. France becomes the first G7 country to impose an opt-in requirement for commercial telephony at national scale, and the regulation is expected to be watched closely by EU partners as a template.

🇱🇧 Lebanon
🪖 Update: Lebanon–Israel — Withdrawals Begin

Update: Lebanese army enters first agreed pilot zones as IDF begins phased south Lebanon redeployment — ceasefire framework moves from paper to ground

In the first tangible implementation of the Lebanon–Israel Washington framework signed in late June, the Lebanese Armed Forces (LAF) began entering agreed pilot zones in south Lebanon previously held by the IDF. Zawtar al-Gharbiya was among the first sites. Under the framework, Israel withdraws from areas it has cleared of Hezbollah infrastructure, transferring them to the LAF on condition they remain free of armed groups; if the pilot programme holds, a broader sequential redeployment follows. Israeli sources have been careful to describe this as “phased” rather than a general pullback, and the IDF continues operations in the wider security zone outside agreed areas. Lebanese Forces leader Geagea estimated Lebanon has now completed 60–70% of dismantling Hezbollah’s illegitimate state influence.

Update: Solidere SOLA slips to $73.75 — mandate-uncertainty overhang deepens; stock now 33% below 52-week high and approaching annual low

Solidere’s ADR (SOLA) traded at $73.75 on August 11, down from $76.35 last week — a further 3.4% decline that puts the stock 33% below its 52-week high of $109.50 and within striking distance of the annual low of $67.10.
