🎖️ Update: Iran Security Reshuffle

Update: Iran appoints IRGC veteran Mohsen Rezaee to lead Supreme National Security Council as Oman-mediated Hormuz deal edges closer — but US rejects Tehran’s terms

Supreme Leader Khamenei has elevated Mohsen Rezaee — a former Revolutionary Guard commander who advocated advancing Iran’s nuclear programme — to head the Supreme National Security Council, the body that will sign off on any Hormuz deal. Foreign Minister Araghchi simultaneously described the Oman-mediated shipping corridor agreement as “very close,” saying coordinates for vessels’ routes through the strait have been agreed. The sticking point remains fundamental: Iran insists US and Israeli vessels be permanently excluded, while Vice President JD Vance says Washington must maximise oil and gas flow through the waterway — positions that are not yet reconciled.

Bloomberg · Business Standard · NPR · Al Jazeera
Why it matters to you

Rezaee’s appointment signals Iran is going into any final deal with its hardest negotiator, not a conciliator. Expect Brent to stay in the $85–92 band until a signed, implemented agreement lands — not just a “very close” announcement. Budget fuel and freight escalators accordingly.

🕊️ Update: Gaza — Disarmament Deadlock

Update: Netanyahu rejects Trump’s Gaza disarmament plan; Hamas conditionally accepts but demands IDF withdrawal first — all three parties at an impasse

Trump’s “Board of Peace” declared a breakthrough Hamas disarmament deal on July 30, but the framework collapsed within days. Hamas conditionally agreed to disarm but insisted Israeli forces withdraw first; the Board of Peace ruled withdrawal only follows complete disarmament; and Netanyahu then publicly rejected the entire US-backed plan. Chatham House analysts describe Hamas’s stance as “calling Israel’s bluff,” positioning it as the party accepting peace while Netanyahu blocks. The IDF continues operating in Gaza, and the civilian toll keeps rising.

🌑 World · Solar Eclipse Eve

First total solar eclipse over mainland Europe since 1999 sweeps Spain, Iceland and Portugal tomorrow — 15 million people in the path of totality

On August 12 at 15:34 GMT the moon’s shadow will begin crossing Greenland and Iceland before sweeping into northern Spain, where cities including León, Valladolid, Zaragoza, Teruel and Valencia sit on the centre line. Totality begins around 18:27 GMT in western Spain, lasting up to 110 seconds. The rest of Europe, including France and the UK, will see more than 90% of the sun obscured at sunset. ESA has dedicated observation platforms along the totality path; this is the continent’s first total solar eclipse since 1999.

ESA · NASA Science · BBC Sky at Night · Al Jazeera
📦 Update: US–China Trade

Update: Trump extends 90-day pause on the 125% China tariff hike — announced today; November cliff now the next pricing flashpoint for equipment supply chains

For the third consecutive cycle, President Trump announced today that the threatened escalation of tariffs on Chinese goods to 125% will be suspended for another 90 days. The tactical truce has prevented a full trade rupture but changes little structurally: Section 301 levies remain in place, semiconductor and AI chip export controls continue, and US importers are holding 60–90 days of safety stock for critical components. China’s own 2026 tariff schedule cuts rates on its priority tech, green-energy and healthcare sectors. The next decision point falls in mid-November.

Tax Foundation · China Briefing · Dimerco · FreightAmigo
Why it matters to you

The November expiry is eight weeks away. If Trump does not extend again, component pricing on Chinese-sourced fire-detection panels, CCTV hardware and smart-building sensors could jump 15–25% overnight.

Act on this: Lock in pricing on Q4 and Q1 equipment orders now while the truce holds — ask suppliers for a 90-day price freeze in writing before October.
🏦 World · Fed Rate Watch

US Federal Reserve September meeting is nearly a coin-flip — PCE inflation at 2.7%, FOMC divided; a hike would be the first since the pause cycle began

Markets are pricing roughly 55% probability of a 25bp rate hike at the FOMC’s September meeting, against 45% for a hold. PCE and core PCE inflation are running at 2.7% — above the 2% target — partly driven by Hormuz-related energy pass-through. Three FOMC members dissented at July’s meeting in favour of a hike, and Chair Kevin Warsh gave contradictory signals at the post-meeting press conference. A hike would strengthen the US dollar and tighten capital flows to emerging markets.

Yahoo Finance · Federal Reserve Board · Forbes Advisor

🇱🇧 Lebanon
🏦 Lebanon · Banking Reform

IMF flags objections to Lebanon’s bank resolution law amendments — central bank governor defends approach before parliament; $70–80bn financial gap still unresolved

Central bank governor Karim Saeed appeared before the parliamentary finance committee to address IMF objections to amendments in Lebanon’s bank resolution law. The IMF is demanding a more independent and transparent resolution process aligned with international standards, and has signalled it will not advance toward a programme agreement until the legislation is strengthened and a comprehensive medium-term fiscal framework is in place. Lebanon’s 2026 draft budget was also criticised as insufficiently ambitious. The financial gap to be resolved is estimated at $70–80 billion, and the banking restructuring remains the single biggest blocker between Beirut and any IMF deal.

Daily Beirut · BLOMINVEST · IMF · BusinessNews Lebanon
Why it matters to you

Until Beirut and the IMF close the gap on banking law, international financing stays locked and client credit conditions in Lebanon remain constrained. Any public statement of IMF approval would be the clearest green light yet for project financing to loosen — worth watching before any large new Lebanese contract is signed on deferred payment terms.

🏘️ Lebanon · Real Estate H1 Data

Beirut captures 30% of Lebanon’s H1 2026 real estate deals — cumulative value reaches $2.58bn; June monthly rebound of 37% hints at pent-up demand returning

BLOMINVEST data shows Lebanon’s cumulative real estate transaction value reached $2,577.8 million by end-June 2026, with June alone registering 4,999 deals worth $619 million — a 36.8% monthly jump from May, though still 2.1% below June 2025. The Q1 2026 figures were weaker (transactions down 29% YoY as the renewed conflict weighed on confidence), but the Q2 recovery, led by June, suggests buyers who deferred during peak uncertainty are returning — at discounted entry prices and predominantly in fresh dollars.
