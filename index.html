💉 World · Science

First mRNA cancer vaccine succeeds in Phase 3 — melanoma recurrence cut 49%, a historic milestone

Merck and Moderna confirmed their personalised mRNA cancer vaccine, intismeran (V940), met both primary and secondary endpoints in the Phase 3 INTerpath-001 trial for resected Stage IIB–IV melanoma. Patients who received intismeran plus pembrolizumab (Keytruda) had a 49% lower risk of recurrence or death versus pembrolizumab alone, validated on both recurrence-free survival and distant-metastasis-free survival metrics. It is the first randomised Phase 3 trial to definitively prove the benefit of “neoantigen” cancer vaccines — which are personalised to each patient’s specific tumour mutations and manufactured individually.

The Economist calls this “It now seems possible to vaccinate against cancer” — deliberately cautious, as the technology is patient-specific rather than universal, and results are not yet definitive for other cancer types. The companies are engaging regulatory authorities on filing submissions; approval would open a multi-billion-dollar market and, potentially, extend the approach to lung, bladder and other immunogenic tumours. Clinical oncologists call it the biggest conceptual shift in oncology since checkpoint inhibitors.

🗳️ World · Europe

Iceland votes Saturday on EU membership restart — polls knife-edge, Trump’s Arctic posture the deciding factor

On 29 August, Icelanders vote on whether to resume EU accession negotiations — suspended since 2013 when the previous government withdrew its application over fisheries sovereignty. Current polls show a narrow majority (just over 50%) favouring a restart, but society is deeply divided: sovereignty over fish, the króna and the welfare model on one side; Arctic security, access to EU markets in a tariff-torn world, and protection from an assertive United States on the other. Both the Economist and Iceland Review emphasise that Trump’s stated interest in Greenland — and by extension the High Arctic — has been the single biggest factor shifting Icelandic opinion toward European institutions since the debate re-opened.

A “yes” vote does not mean Iceland joins the EU: it would merely restart formal accession negotiations, a process that could span many years and would itself require a second referendum on actual membership. But the symbolism is significant — it would signal that Trump’s posture is pushing historically neutral states toward Brussels and could accelerate similar conversations in Norway and Switzerland.

Tanzania inaugurates Africa’s most powerful dam — $2.9bn, 2,115 MW, now supplying half the national grid

President Samia Suluhu Hassan officially launched the Julius Nyerere Hydropower Plant (JNHPP) on Saturday 22 August — a 2,115-megawatt facility on the Rufiji River that already supplies roughly half of Tanzania’s electricity needs. 🌹 France · Obituary

Yvette Roudy, France’s first minister for women’s rights, dies at 97 — the architect of abortion reimbursement

The New York Times reports the death of Yvette Roudy, France’s trailblazing first Minister Delegate for Women’s Rights under President Mitterrand, at 97. Roudy, a Socialist MP and fierce feminist, successfully championed state reimbursement of abortion costs in the 1980s, framing reproductive rights as a matter of economic equality rather than simply social freedom — an argument that distinguished French policy from both the American and British approaches. She served as minister from 1981 to 1986, shepherding landmark legislation through a deeply resistant political culture.

Her death comes at a moment when France’s 2027 presidential election may hinge partly on social issues: the National Rally’s softened public position on abortion access has been viewed with scepticism by many French women, while Glucksmann and the centre-left will now lean on Roudy’s legacy to mobilise voters. The NYT notes she was fond of saying “I couldn’t get elected today” — a line that reflects both how far France has come and how fragile those gains feel in 2026.
