⚠️ World · Iran & Hormuz — Update

Update — Trump threatens to ‘bomb the s— out of’ Oman as Iran ceasefire expires; Brent surges to $90.87

The 60-day US-Iran memorandum of understanding expired Monday August 17 with no extension agreed and no new framework in sight. The shock development of the day was not the expiry itself but President Trump’s threat — issued publicly as negotiations stalled — to strike Oman if the Gulf nation “gets in the way” of a Hormuz deal. The threat risks destroying the only remaining diplomatic channel: Oman has served as the go-between for the entire ceasefire process, and its potential removal from the equation leaves Washington and Tehran with no agreed back-channel. Brent crude responded immediately, rising 2.7% to $90.87 per barrel. Iran’s Foreign Ministry said talks with Oman are “continuing in all seriousness,” but the US threat has put Muscat in an impossible position. Shipping traffic in the Strait of Hormuz has sunk to a new low; the USS George Washington carrier group is still in transit from Da Nang and will not reach the Arabian Sea for approximately a week, leaving the US below its preferred force posture at the most critical moment of the crisis.

Brent at $90.87 has already crossed the threshold where generator fuel, freight on imported materials and dollar-denominated project costs in both Beirut and Lagos require repricing. An Oman breakdown — removing the last mediator — is the scenario that sends Brent past $95 within days. Any open project pricing from the last two months now needs review.

🇪🇺 World · Europe & Ukraine

France, Germany & UK build joint E3 format to secure Europe’s seat at Ukraine peace table — fear of exclusion from any Trump-Putin deal

Fearing that Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin could reach a bilateral settlement that tramples European security interests, France, Germany and the United Kingdom are now coordinating a joint negotiating position through an “E3” format specifically designed to give Europe a formal seat at any future Ukraine peace talks. European diplomats told the NYT that the three powers are preparing a common position to ensure that any deal struck between Washington and Moscow must address European security guarantees — including forward NATO deployments, Ukraine’s sovereignty and the status of occupied territories — rather than leaving Europe to accept a fait accompli. The effort gained urgency after the inconclusive Alaska summit of August 16, where Trump and Putin described talks as “constructive” but announced no specifics. Zelensky met with Trump at the White House afterwards and was given assurances, but European leaders remain sceptical that US commitments will hold. The E3 move is unprecedented in its formality: the three nations are essentially counter-organising against the US to protect their own security architecture.

📉 World · US Markets

US equities retreat as Brent breaks $90 — S&P and Dow fall; markets pricing higher inflation and potential Fed rate hike before year-end

American equity markets fell on Monday as Brent crude’s breach of $90 per barrel rekindled fears of a sustained energy-driven inflation cycle. The S&P 500 slipped from near-record levels and the Dow Jones Industrial Average declined as oil prices climbed on the expiry of the Iran ceasefire. The energy-inflation transmission is now well understood by markets: higher Brent pushes US core CPI higher through fuel and freight costs, limiting the Federal Reserve’s ability to ease; swap markets are now pricing a non-trivial probability of one or two rate hikes before year-end, a reversal of the cuts expected at the start of 2026. The Fed’s July meeting minutes are scheduled for release on Wednesday August 20, and any hawkish signal there will reinforce the tightening narrative. Reddit (RDDT) bucked the trend, jumping 15% after being added to the S&P 500 index ahead of Monday’s open.

🏛️ Africa · Nigeria & Data Centres

Nigeria’s CBN orders all banks and fintechs to localise payment data by January 2027 — one of West Africa’s largest data centre migrations now underway

The Central Bank of Nigeria has directed every bank, fintech, payment service provider and mobile money operator licensed in Nigeria to migrate all Nigerian payment transaction data to domestically-hosted infrastructure before January 2027 — triggering what analysts describe as one of the country’s largest cloud migrations to date. Major Tier-1 commercial banks are largely compliant, but digital banks and fintechs hosting workloads on AWS, Azure or Google Cloud are racing to establish or contract local capacity. The mandate is already showing up as a significant driver of data centre demand in Lagos, where 14 of Nigeria’s 17 operational data centres are concentrated — a single-city concentration that creates systemic resilience risk even as demand grows. The CBN has additionally signalled that the localisation requirement may be extended beyond banking to cover oil, manufacturing and healthcare data, which would further dramatically expand the required capacity build-out and create demand for physical security, BMS and access-control systems at each new facility.

🇱🇧 Lebanon
🛡️ Lebanon · Security — Update

Update — Germany formally proposes EU peacekeeping force to replace UNIFIL — the first concrete framework for Lebanon’s December 31 security vacuum

German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul has formally and repeatedly proposed that the European Union deploy a peacekeeping mission to replace the UN’s UNIFIL force when its mandate expires on December 31 — making Germany’s proposal the most specific alternative framework yet put forward for what is otherwise a looming security vacuum in south Lebanon. Wadephul argued that an EU-mandated mission could “create the conditions for the Israeli army to withdraw without Hezbollah returning with its terror” — addressing Israel’s key objection to the current UNIFIL mandate, which Tel Aviv views as insufficiently empowered to prevent Hezbollah rearmament. Germany has simultaneously extended its own final participation in UNIFIL for the remainder of the mission. The proposal requires EU member-state consensus — including potentially contentious contributions from countries sharing less urgency about Lebanon — and Lebanon’s agreement; Israel has not yet formally responded. The UN Security Council is separately weighing post-UNIFIL options, but Germany’s EU framing could appeal to France, Italy and Spain, all of which have significant UNIFIL troop contributions at stake.

Asharq Al-Awsat · Times of Israel · Al Jazeera · Middle East Online · SANA
Why it matters to you

A credible EU successor to UNIFIL is the single development most likely to break the Lebanon security-vacuum deadlock: if it gains traction, it reduces the tail risk of renewed full-scale conflict in January 2027, which is the scenario that would freeze Beirut real estate values and halt reconstruction contracts. Watch for French and Italian endorsements as the key signal that this has momentum — without them, it is a German idea, not a European plan.

📊 Lebanon · Economy

Lebanon’s war economic toll deepens: Bank Audi cuts GDP forecast to 0%, $3bn in losses so far, Beirut hotel occupancy collapses to below 25%

A convergence of data from multiple institutions paints a sharply deteriorating picture of Lebanon’s 2026 economy. Bank Audi, Lebanon’s largest private bank, has revised its 2026 GDP growth forecast to zero percent — abandoning the 4% conditional recovery the World Bank had projected at the start of the year — with the sustained Israel-Hezbollah conflict cited as the decisive constraint. Finance Minister Yassine Jaber stated in June that war-related economic losses in 2026 already total approximately $3 billion, with assessments ongoing. In the bellwether tourism sector, Beirut hotel occupancy has collapsed to below 25% in early 2026, against a pre-conflict baseline of 55-60%, with the summer recovery season failing to materialise. Now layered on top of the war damage, the Brent oil spike to $90.87 is a direct hit to Lebanon’s import bill: the country produces no oil, imports essentially all its energy needs in dollars, and higher global fuel prices translate immediately into higher generator costs, electricity tariffs and freight on imported goods. The IMF’s conditional programme discussions — dependent on the FSDR banking law passing un-diluted and relative stability persisting — face both conditions under strain simultaneously.

Al Jazeera · World Bank · Bank Audi · Washington Times · IMF
Why it matters to you

Zero GDP growth in 2026, $3bn in war losses and a Brent oil spike together mean Lebanese clients are under acute cash pressure and may delay or reduce system procurement budgets. For Quasar Lebanon, any project awarded in this environment must be priced to survive delayed payment and potentially discounted draws. The same conditions make dollar-fresh invoicing — not LBP — essential for any new contracts.
