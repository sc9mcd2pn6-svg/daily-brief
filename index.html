NASA’s Nancy Grace Roman Telescope Launches — 100× Hubble’s Field of View

The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope lifted off successfully at 06:26 CDT Sunday on a SpaceX Falcon Heavy from Kennedy Space Center, heading toward the L2 Lagrange point where it will join the James Webb. Roman will study dark energy and dark matter, conduct a statistical census of planetary systems, and — crucially — potentially photograph light from one billion galaxies in its operational lifetime. Named after NASA’s first chief astronomer, the telescope has a field of view at least 100 times larger than Hubble’s.

The launch was followed by a live press conference at which President Trump called in to congratulate the team — a rare bipartisan moment for a mission that survived multiple budget threats. It will take roughly three months to reach L2; first images are expected in early 2027. Trump and SpaceX CEO Elon Musk both posted congratulations, though their posts emphasised American space dominance rather than the science.
