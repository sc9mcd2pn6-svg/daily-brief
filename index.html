🚢 World · Iran War (Update)

Update: Iran-US Tanker War Erupts at Hormuz — Ballistic Missile Fired at US Warships, Both Sides Attacking Oil Tankers

The seven-month Iran-US conflict has entered a dangerous new phase: both sides are now directly attacking commercial oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. The United States targeted three Iranian oil tankers; Iran hit US-linked vessels and fired a ballistic missile toward US warships patrolling the strait. OPEC+ held production steady on Sunday to avoid adding to market anxiety, and Brent has eased slightly to $92.37 from last week’s peak of ~$96 as traders price in a prolonged stalemate rather than a full closure. The US Navy is escorting tankers through the strait, but insurance premiums have hit record highs and shipping operators are pulling back.

This shift from airstrikes on military installations to a tanker war over the world’s most critical oil corridor is a major escalation. Ceasefire talks via Oman’s mediation are stalled; Iran has calculated it can bleed the US economically through the shipping lane without triggering a full military response. The Strait handles roughly 21% of global oil and 17% of LNG.

🖥️ World · Tech & AI

China’s Inspur Used Renamed US Subsidiary to Buy $5.6B in Nvidia AI Chips Despite US Blacklist

Blacklisted Chinese AI giant Inspur Group renamed its California operations to “Aivres” after being placed on the US sanctions list in 2023 — and kept buying. From April 2024 to February 2026, Aivres purchased over $5.6 billion in advanced technology including more than $3 billion in AI servers built on Nvidia’s latest Blackwell chips, which then flowed to China’s leading AI firms. Inspur exploited a specific loophole: its 33% ownership stake in Aivres fell below the 50% threshold triggering ownership-disclosure rules, giving the subsidiary legal cover to operate as a nominally independent US company.

The investigation, based on trade records analysed by ImportGenius and reported by the NYT, is now clouding US-China AI summit talks. Washington is scrambling to close the ownership threshold loophole and is expected to tighten export controls further in Q4. Inspur supplied computing infrastructure to the Chinese military; the Blackwell chips it obtained are among the most powerful AI processors in the world.

NYT (anchor) · Asia Times · Free Press Journal
Why it matters to you

Tighter US AI chip export controls will raise the cost and complexity of sourcing next-generation data centre hardware globally — including for clients in Lagos building AI data centre infrastructure. Expect availability tightness and price pressure on high-end AI servers through 2027.

Ukraine Lost $1.2 Billion to Defense Procurement Fraud in 2024 — Contracts Still Going to Firms Under Criminal Investigation

Secret military audits seen by the NYT reveal that Ukraine’s arms procurement system lost $1.2 billion in 2024 to fraud, waste, and mismanagement — while 7 of its 10 largest arms manufacturers received new contracts despite ongoing criminal investigations. One plant boss was awarded a $280 million deal while on bail on corruption charges. Since January 2026, prosecutors have opened 29 defence corruption cases covering at least $77 million embezzled or lost. The audit findings were classified; their emergence now is politically significant.

The revelations are deeply uncomfortable for Western governments that have been the primary funders of Ukraine’s war effort. They also complicate the Witkoff-Kushner peace shuttle: Russia will use the findings to argue that Western aid is wasted; Ukraine’s government says new oversight mechanisms are being put in place. The EU is reviewing its own oversight frameworks for military assistance.

✈️ World · Aviation

Amazon Cargo Boeing 767 Overruns Miami Runway, Killing 5 — NTSB Full Investigation Underway

Amazon Air Flight 7598, a 32-year-old Boeing 767 converted from passenger to cargo use, overran runway 30 at Miami International Airport at 1:53 pm ET on Sunday, killing five people and injuring five others. The aircraft, operated by 21 Air LLC after arriving from San Juan, Puerto Rico, careened into a van with seven occupants inside the airport perimeter and an SUV outside the fence. NTSB Chair Jennifer Homendy is leading the investigation team on-site. The incident has grounded the specific aircraft type pending initial findings.

The crash puts renewed scrutiny on aging converted passenger-to-cargo jets in the booming e-commerce logistics sector. Amazon has rapidly expanded its cargo fleet to reduce reliance on FedEx and UPS; the 767s in its fleet average 25-30 years old. Boeing’s already-battered reputation faces further pressure; the company had nothing to do with the conversion but the type is in focus.

⛓️ World · Americas

Milei “Reloaded”: The Economist Finds Argentina’s Experiment Struggling — President Brandishes Gold Chainsaw, Stays Unyielding

In a sit-down interview at the Casa Rosada, Javier Milei plonked his visitors in front of his “favourite toy” — a glinting gold chainsaw — and promised a second-term “Milei reloaded” ahead of his 2027 re-election bid. The Economist found a more ambiguous reality: growth forecasts cut from 5% to 2.7% for 2026, annual inflation still at 33.8% with prices rising for the second consecutive month, and growth uneven with minimal job creation. On the plus side, poverty has fallen and disinflation from 211% annual inflation in late 2023 has been remarkable.

Argentina faces $20 billion in debt maturities in 2026, and political capital is eroding as ordinary Argentinians feel the squeeze of a structural adjustment that has benefited investors more than workers so far. The Economist’s assessment: Milei has had real successes, but the harder reforms — deregulation, privatisation, labour market liberalisation — remain undone, and his window before next year’s election is narrowing fast.

🏦 Africa · Nigeria

Nigeria’s Central Bank Orders All Banks and Fintechs to Store Data Locally by January 2027 — Data Centre Demand Surge Imminent

The Central Bank of Nigeria has directed every bank, payment service provider and fintech company to localise all customer and transaction data within Nigeria’s borders by January 2027, with penalties for non-compliance. The mandate covers the entire Nigerian financial sector — from Tier-1 banks to mobile money operators — and effectively forces institutions that currently rely on overseas cloud infrastructure to procure or contract local data centre capacity at speed. Nigeria’s installed data centre capacity is projected to grow from 56MW in 2025 to over 218MW by 2030, and this regulatory push compresses that timeline into a hard 16-month window.

The directive follows a broader push for African data sovereignty, with similar rules already enacted in South Africa and Ghana. For Nigeria’s emerging hyperscale data centre market — where West Africa’s first AI campus just opened in Lagos — the bank mandate adds a legally-mandated demand layer on top of commercial AI infrastructure growth.

🚨 Lebanon · Security

Israel Kills 12 in Kfar Reman Airstrike Including 2 Children — Aoun Accuses Israel of Destroying the US-Brokered Deal

Israeli fighter jets struck a residential building in the southern Lebanese village of Kfar Reman after midnight Sunday, killing 12 people — including two children — and wounding 13. A separate Israeli drone strike on a civilian vehicle in the same village killed two medics. Israel said the strikes followed two Hezbollah drone attacks against Israeli troops in the area and that the residential building was used to transport weapons. No evacuation warning had been issued. The UN reported a “marked deterioration” in security along the Blue Line and said Israeli forces are intensifying their military footprint in violation of UN Security Council Resolution 2790.

President Joseph Aoun condemned the strikes in the strongest terms yet, accusing Israel of “undermining the US-brokered framework agreement” signed on June 26, which calls for a gradual Israeli withdrawal from Lebanese territory in exchange for Hezbollah disarmament and Lebanese army deployment. The incident is the deadliest since the June framework and raises serious questions about whether the deal can survive. Washington has called for restraint but stopped short of censuring Israel.

Al Jazeera · Washington Post · Times of Israel · The National · UN OCHA
Why it matters to you

Continued Israeli strikes in southern Lebanon erode confidence in the ceasefire, slow reconstruction demand, and keep your southern-Lebanon-based clients cautious. The Aoun-Israel confrontation is politically significant — watch whether Washington steps in harder this week.

Hospital Workers Stage National Mobilisation Today; Firefighters on Month-Long Strike Notice — September Labour Unrest Hits France

Two major healthcare unions called a national mobilisation day for Tuesday September 8, with demonstrations planned outside hospitals and care facilities across France over chronic pay shortfalls, understaffing and deteriorating working conditions. No walk-outs are planned but the symbolic action signals mounting pressure on the Bayrou government as it opens the autumn legislative session. Separately, nine firefighting unions and associations have filed a strike notice covering the entirety of September, citing the same pay and staffing complaints.

The action follows a summer of rolling transport disruptions — easyJet cabin crew walked out in early September — and coincides with a gradual uptick in Covid-19 cases detected in French wastewater monitoring. September is shaping up as a difficult month for France’s public services as the government prepares its 2027 budget.
