⏳ World · Iran & Hormuz

Iran–US 60-day ceasefire expires today — no deal in sight as Brent hits $89 and Trump claims Hormuz is ‘US territory’

The June memorandum of understanding between Washington and Tehran included a 60-day ceasefire that expires this Monday, making today the next major test of whether diplomacy over the Strait of Hormuz can hold. Negotiations are at a standstill: Iran has reclosed the Strait after accusing the US of trying to establish an alternative shipping route, and Washington has reinstated a naval blockade of Iranian ports. President Trump went further on Friday, posting on Truth Social that “Pretty soon, I’ll be declaring the Hormuz Strait a territory of the United States” — a claim Iran immediately and furiously rejected. Brent crude rose to nearly $89 a barrel on Friday and opened the week higher, with traders watching whether the ceasefire’s expiry triggers a return to active strikes. The IEA had already warned that global oil stockpiles were dangerously low; any full resumption of hostilities could push Brent past $95 within days. The USS George Washington carrier group is still in transit from Da Nang and will not reach the Arabian Sea for roughly a week, leaving the US short of its preferred force posture at a critical moment.

🎯 World · Lebanon & Israel

IDF names two Hezbollah commanders killed in Saturday strikes — Badr Unit chief and Radwan Force battalion commander confirmed dead

Following Saturday’s strikes that killed at least eleven people in southern Lebanon, the Israeli military on Sunday formally named two senior Hezbollah commanders it says were killed. Abu Hassan Alaa (identified as Ali Muhammad Fakhr al-Din), a senior commander in Hezbollah’s Badr regional division, was killed in the Deir al-Zahrani area; the IDF said he was responsible for organising numerous drone attacks against Israeli forces in the south. A second commander, Ali Samir Al-Haj Hassan of Hezbollah’s elite Radwan Force, was killed in a strike on a headquarters building in the town of Ansar. Lebanese President Joseph Aoun accused Israel of sending “a clear message” ahead of the next round of US-sponsored negotiations and warned of grave consequences. Hezbollah has not yet responded militarily, but security analysts say named-commander kills at this level typically precede a retaliatory attack within days. Rome Round 7 talks ended inconclusively in early August, and no new round is scheduled before September, leaving the ceasefire framework under strain with no active diplomatic channel to de-escalate.

🕊️ World · Gaza

Hamas conditionally accepts Gaza disarmament roadmap — Board of Peace sets 14-day deadline; Israel has not agreed and implementation remains far from certain

In the most significant diplomatic development on Gaza in months, the US-led Board of Peace and its international stabilisation partners announced on July 30 that Hamas had agreed in principle to a staged disarmament roadmap. Under the framework, a National Committee for the Administration of Gaza would oversee a sequential inventory and storage of Hamas weapons, linked to the entry of an International Stabilisation Force into the Strip. Mediators from Egypt, Qatar, Türkiye and the US finalised the roadmap details, with a formal 14-day window set for the disarmament timetable to be produced. However, Hamas made clear it will not implement any part of the deal unless Israeli forces first fulfil their own obligations and withdraw from Gaza; Israel has not publicly endorsed the framework; and Netanyahu’s coalition partners are expected to resist any plan that does not guarantee Israel full veto over implementation. Germany described the plan as “an opportunity to make progress,” while Palestinian groups outside Hamas have urged caution, noting the precedent of previous agreements unravelling at implementation.

🌊 World · Japan — Update

Update — Japan flood recovery: Narita airport reopens, trains resume; death toll confirmed at 8; worst Chiba rainfall in recorded history

Japan’s immediate recovery from last week’s devastating flood event is proceeding. Narita International Airport resumed normal flight operations over the weekend after runways and approach roads were cleared, and rail links between Narita and central Tokyo have been partially restored, allowing thousands of stranded passengers to continue their journeys during one of Japan’s busiest summer travel weeks. The confirmed death toll stands at eight, with rescue and clean-up operations continuing in flooded residential areas around Chiba City. Japan’s Meteorological Agency confirmed that Chiba’s 367mm of rainfall in 24 hours was the highest ever recorded for the prefecture, exceeding the previous 2013 record by nearly 20 per cent. Authorities have lifted the highest-level Level 5 emergency warning for the area, though some evacuation orders remain in force for landslide-prone zones. Japan is simultaneously managing ongoing aftershock sequences from the earlier Kumamoto earthquake, stretching national emergency management resources. Insurance estimates for property damage in the Chiba and Tokyo metropolitan areas are expected to reach several billion dollars.

📈 World · US Economy & Fed

Fed holds rates at 3.5–3.75% as Iran energy shock pushes core PCE to 3.3% — market pricing has flipped from cuts to hikes

The Federal Open Market Committee held the federal funds rate unchanged at 3.50–3.75% at its July 29 meeting, but the context has shifted dramatically from the rate-cut expectations that dominated markets at the start of 2026. Core PCE inflation has accelerated from 3.0% in December 2025 to 3.3% in June 2026, with energy prices from the Iran-Hormuz conflict acting as the primary catalyst: West Texas Intermediate began 2026 near $57 per barrel and reached $113 in April before settling back. Firms have been passing higher energy costs through to selling prices at the fastest rate since August 2022, creating a broader inflation pulse. As a result, interest-rate swap markets have fully reversed their earlier pricing of 2026 cuts and are now implying one or two rate hikes before year-end — a sharp tightening of global financial conditions. Employment remains firm, giving the Fed little cover to ease. The August 18 expiry of the Iran ceasefire, if followed by resumed hostilities, could push energy prices higher still and force the Fed’s hand.

ECOWAS Development Bank approves $510m in new West Africa infrastructure packages — energy, data, healthcare and private sector growth in focus

The ECOWAS Bank for Investment and Development (EBID) approved five new financing packages totalling more than $510 million in mid-August 2026, targeting infrastructure, healthcare, energy and private-sector growth across West Africa. The announcement coincides with broader momentum: more than 30% of the Emerging Africa and Asia Infrastructure Fund’s roughly $1.7 billion portfolio is now invested in West Africa across renewable energy, toll roads, electric buses, airports, telecoms and data centres. Separately, in Côte d’Ivoire, the Council of Ministers approved financing of 101.7 billion CFA francs from the West African Development Bank plus a $300 million Millennium Challenge Corporation grant, allocated across security, water, agriculture and housing. Nigeria’s President Tinubu has also called for reactivation of the ECOWAS Regional Standby Force to address the documented sixfold rise in security threats across the region, creating additional demand for integrated physical security infrastructure. The pipeline of bankable projects in the region is at its highest in a decade, with data centres alone projected to reach 218 MW of new capacity in Nigeria by 2030 against a current base of roughly 80 MW.

🇫🇷 France
🔥 France · Wildfires & Politics

France’s 2026 wildfire season worst since 1949 — 115,000 hectares burned; political rupture deepens as Macron calls crisis summit and parties trade blame

France’s catastrophic 2026 wildfire season — with 115,000 hectares burned, making it the worst since 1949 — has now become a defining political crisis, not merely an environmental one. President Macron convened an emergency crisis meeting after declaring that France “does not have enough firefighters to tackle wildfires on this scale.” The fires destroyed significant areas of Bordeaux wine country, forced the evacuation of the entire Cap Ferret peninsula, and created what French Finance Minister Roland Lescure described as “a clap of thunder for an area which could have done without this.” The political consequences are growing: a Foreign Policy analysis this week characterises the wildfire crisis as “swallowing French politics,” intensifying friction between ideological extremes — the far-right claiming the government failed to invest in prevention, the far-left citing climate inaction — and threatening to consume the moderate parties in between ahead of the next presidential cycle. European firefighters from Britain and Croatia are now operating alongside French forces under a pooled response. An independent climate assessment said the 2026 season would have been virtually impossible without man-made warming.

🇺🇳 Lebanon · Security & Geopolitics

UNIFIL exits Lebanon permanently on December 31 — southern security vacuum looms as replacement force and framework remain completely undecided

The UN Security Council voted in August 2025 to renew UNIFIL’s mandate for one final year, meaning the 10,000-strong UN peacekeeping force that has patrolled Lebanon’s southern border since 1978 will cease operations on 31 December 2026. As of mid-August, the fundamental questions about what comes next remain entirely unanswered: who, if anyone, will replace UNIFIL; what legal framework and troop-contributing nations would underpin a successor mission; and how the fragile pilot-zone arrangement under which the Lebanese Army has begun assuming security control in three villages will scale to the whole south. Defense News published an extensive analysis on August 3 describing “uncertainty persisting in Lebanon over what comes next,” and Foreign Policy noted in July that UNIFIL is “heading for the exits” with no credible plan agreed between Israel, Lebanon, the US, France and the UN. The parallel collapse of Rome Round 7 talks and ongoing IDF strikes creates a situation where a multinational security buffer is expiring precisely as the bilateral security arrangement it was meant to support has broken down.

Defense News · Foreign Policy · House of Commons Library · Al Jazeera

A southern security vacuum by January 2027 — the most likely outcome if no replacement framework is agreed — dramatically raises the tail risk of renewed full-scale conflict in Lebanon. 🏦 Lebanon · Economy & IMF

IMF expresses satisfaction with Lebanon banking law amendments — FSDR law must now pass without further dilution to unlock programme talks

The International Monetary Fund has expressed satisfaction with Parliament’s approval of amendments to the Banking Restructuring Law and voiced hope that the Financial Stabilisation and Depositor Recovery (FSDR) law will be enacted soon without further dilution. Finance Minister Yassine Jaber met with the IMF’s Resident Representative Federico Lima in Beirut on August 14 and publicly affirmed that both the government and Parliament are determined to complete the reforms. The IMF’s concern is that a pattern of amendments — 11 of which were approved by the Finance and Budget Committee — has already weakened the law’s depositor-protection architecture; Washington, Paris and Riyadh have all separately pressed Beirut to resist pressure from bank-owners to dilute the law further. Lebanon’s banking crisis has left an estimated $70–80 billion in depositor savings inaccessible since 2019, and no IMF programme — and therefore no large-scale multilateral budget support — is possible until the FSDR is enacted and a credible loss-allocation plan is in place. The Salam government’s reform dialogue with the Fund remains the most sustained Lebanon has managed with the IMF in years.

Fana News · LBC Group · Beiruter · IMF.org · BLOMINVEST
Why it matters to you

IMF satisfaction is meaningful but conditional: the FSDR still has to pass un-diluted and a full programme still has to be agreed. When it does, the first tranche of external support typically precedes a broad depositor confidence recovery by 6–12 months — that recovery is the moment pent-up building and systems demand in Lebanon unlocks. Watch the law’s final passage vote as the key signal.

🏢 Real Estate
🔑 Real Estate · Beirut

Beirut Q1 real estate transactions fall 29% year-on-year — financing freeze locks out all but cash diaspora buyers; market cannot recover without banks

First-quarter 2026 data published by Lebanon’s Land Registry shows a 29.3 per cent year-on-year decline in the number of real estate transactions and an 18.2 per cent decline in their total value — a significant deterioration from the H1 2025 rebound that had been fuelled by returning diaspora confidence. The fundamental constraint, real estate experts told Executive Magazine, is financing: “the real estate market cannot return to normal without a healthy banking sector,” as developers cannot build without bank financing and buyers cannot purchase without housing loans. The only active buyers remain cash-holding diaspora purchasers — predominantly from Gulf states, France and West Africa — who can transact entirely in fresh dollars without touching the banking system. This dynamic has kept prime Achrafieh and Metn values nominally firm in fresh-dollar terms while driving volumes sharply lower. A parallel constraint is that an estimated 23% of Beirut dwellings built between 1996 and 2018 remain vacant, creating a large shadow inventory that will weigh on any recovery. The FSDR law’s passage is cited as the single most important policy trigger for normalising transaction volumes.

Executive Magazine · Libnanews · Beiruter · World Bank
Why it matters to you

Your Beirut holdings are benefiting from the cash-buyer floor that is keeping prime values firm in fresh-dollar terms — but the 29% transaction volume drop signals a thin market where liquidity is limited if you needed to exit. Hold strategy is sound; any new acquisition in this environment should be priced for the cash-buyer market only, not leveraged.
