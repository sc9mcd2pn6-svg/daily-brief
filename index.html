US Economy

US economy grows 3.4% in Q3 — fastest pace in a year, driven by AI and consumers

The Bureau of Economic Analysis reported Thursday that US GDP expanded at a 3.4% annualised rate in the third quarter of 2026, the fastest in a year. Businesses poured money into artificial intelligence infrastructure, and consumers continued to spend despite elevated inflation, with the household savings rate falling to just 4.1% of after-tax income — the lowest in nearly four years. The strong growth contrasts sharply with widespread economic discontent heading into next week’s midterm elections.

Christine Lagarde simultaneously announced she will step down as ECB president, ending a tenure that spanned multiple economic crises. The NYT Evening newsletter noted the robust growth could be contributing to inflation by allowing businesses to pass on price increases, complicating the Fed’s already difficult path to a December rate decision.

Source: NYT Evening (Oct 29); Bureau of Economic Analysis

Why it matters to you

A growing US economy sustains demand for data centres, AI infrastructure, and commercial construction globally — which benefits Quasar Nigeria’s Lagos pipeline and FGC’s project financing environment. However, the Fed’s likely December hike will push the dollar higher and squeeze Lebanon-dollar spreads further.

US Midterms – Rule of Law

DOJ issues criminal grand jury subpoenas to California election officials — 6 days before vote

The Trump Justice Department has issued criminal grand jury subpoenas to at least two employees of the Los Angeles County Registrar-Recorder’s office, as part of its widening campaign to prove noncitizens are on California voter rolls. The proceeding is scheduled for the morning after Election Day. Legal experts called it without precedent — a pre-election criminal investigation targeting the officials who run the count.

The move escalates a weeks-long campaign: the DOJ has already sued California to block counting of mail-in ballots it claims belong to noncitizens, and earlier arrested 11 and charged 28 noncitizens it says voted. A federal judge rejected a prior DOJ suit seeking California voter details, and California’s AG called the demands meritless. Studies find noncitizen voting fraud is extremely rare.

Source: NYT Evening (Oct 29); NYT Today’s Headlines (Oct 29)

AI – National Security

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang targeted by confirmed Chinese intelligence asset — Trump knew but did not act

American spy agencies identified that a businesswoman with a close personal relationship with Nvidia’s chief executive Jensen Huang was a Chinese intelligence asset funnelling information to Beijing. The White House was alarmed — Huang is among the most powerful figures in the AI industry and a close Trump ally — but President Trump, briefed on the intelligence roughly a year ago, chose not to take action. The NYT reported the story Thursday evening.

The revelation lands as Nvidia dominates global AI chip supply and as US chip export controls remain a central front in US-China rivalry. Nvidia has been at the centre of the AI arms race: Trump previously invited Huang to the China summit in May, and Senators had warned against Huang making that trip citing export-control risks.

Source: NYT Evening (Oct 29)

Why it matters to you

This story puts pressure on US chip policy and Nvidia’s regulatory standing. US Midterms – T−6 Days

Economist cover: Hispanic and Asian voters are America’s new swing bloc — and they have swung hard against Trump

The Economist’s cover this week argues that Hispanic and Asian Americans — who helped elect Trump in 2024 — have swung sharply away from him, primarily over mass deportations that devastated construction and agriculture in South Texas. Unlike tribal voters whose views on immigration predict their views on Iran, these voters are bread-and-butter pragmatists who neither party can take for granted, making them decisive in next week’s midterms. The Economist says Democrats have good chances of flipping the Senate — but not as good as betting markets believe, and a lame-duck Trump freed from Congress could be more dangerous internationally.

The NYT reported Thursday that Alaska’s governor has opened a criminal investigation into the Democratic Senate campaign of incumbent Mary Peltola, and a federal judge has rejected the DOJ’s bid to set aside California ballots in a pre-election ruling. The midterms are November 4.

Source: The Economist (Oct 29, cover); NYT Today’s Headlines (Oct 29)

Business – M&A

DoorDash agrees to buy Instacart for $17 billion — delivery giants merge

DoorDash, the dominant US restaurant-delivery platform, agreed on October 29 to acquire Instacart, the leading online grocery delivery service, at $65 per share — a 26% premium and an equity value of $17 billion. Instacart’s board unanimously approved the deal. DoorDash will fund the cash portion through existing cash and a JPMorgan bridge loan; Instacart shareholders can elect up to 25% in DoorDash stock.

The merger awaits US antitrust clearance and shareholder approval, with closing expected in late 2027 or early 2028. It unites the largest restaurant-delivery network with the largest grocery-delivery platform in a single super-app model. Earlier talks between Instacart and Uber are no longer active.

Source: NYT Evening (Oct 29); DoorDash SEC 8-K (Oct 29); Reuters

Israel – Government Formation

Update: Israel coalition wrangling begins — no clear path, Arab parties hold the key

With vote-counting complete, Israel’s October 27 election has left the country without a government. Netanyahu’s right-wing bloc and Eisenkot’s centrist opposition both fell short of the 61-seat majority required. Arab parties, with a record 17–18 seats, are the mathematical kingmakers. The NYT reported Thursday that “Israel’s parties gird for wrangling to form a government, with no clear path”: Eisenkot needs Arab support he has long avoided, and Netanyahu has floated unity talks Eisenkot has rejected.

The Economist, in an editorial, urged the Jewish and Arab opposition to work together quickly if they want to dislodge Netanyahu from power. Weeks of negotiations — or a fifth election — are both plausible outcomes. The coalition uncertainty is a direct drag on Lebanon ceasefire talks.

Source: NYT Today’s Headlines (Oct 29); The Economist (Oct 29)

Why it matters to you

The coalition vacuum means no clear signal on Israel’s south Lebanon policy. Hold any major commitments to south Lebanon project timelines until a government is in place and its ceasefire stance is known.

UK – Budget

Burnham’s first budget: “Talks of change, then does nothing” — Economist verdict

UK Chancellor John Healey presented Prime Minister Andy Burnham’s first Autumn Budget on Wednesday, October 28. The Economist’s blunt verdict: small handouts (winter heating supplements, VAT removed from domestic electricity) that spooked neither the bond market nor Labour backbenchers — but deferred the big, structural decisions Britain needs. Growth, housing and AI preparedness were all punted to a later date.

The Economist wrote that “treading water is not good enough” for Britain, which needs faster growth, more houses and serious reform for the AI era. The budget represents a missed window: with Labour’s first majority in over a decade, the moment to act big was now. Markets were calm; sterling held steady; bond yields did not spike.

Source: The Economist (Oct 29)

France – General Strike

Update: France general strike T−6 days — October 31 protests confirmed tomorrow, rail paralysis maps published

France’s general strike on November 5 is now six days away, with all five major unions — CGT, CFDT, FO, CFTC and CFE-CGC — confirmed. SNCF has published pre-strike service maps showing rail paralysis, and preliminary strike notices have been filed. But the protests are already starting earlier: Saturday October 31 marches are confirmed in Paris, Lyon and Marseille, adding a Halloween-weekend dimension to the rolling unrest.

Unrest has spread beyond France: solidarity protests in Italy and Belgium are also confirmed for the week. The French government met union leaders on October 21 and reached no deal. The Le Pen right is watching closely: a sufficiently disruptive strike season could reopen her 2027 presidential path.

Source: NYT Evening (Oct 29); The Guardian (search, Oct 29)

Lebanon – Security & Diplomacy

Aoun urges end to Israeli strikes; France pledges army donor conference; disarmament stalls

Lebanese President Joseph Aoun issued a public call Thursday for an end to Israeli attacks in southern Lebanon, warning against any military solution and appealing to European partners for increased support for the Lebanese army. The statement came as Israeli forces continued demolitions in the south and disarmament talks remained deadlocked: Hezbollah’s leader Naim Qassem rejected the US-brokered June framework linking ceasefire to disarmament, and Israeli forces have maintained strikes despite that agreement.

France has committed to hosting a donor conference for the Lebanese army after Israel’s election result is certified, signalling Paris remains engaged. On the economic front, US Treasury Secretary Bessent reaffirmed Washington’s expectation that Beirut advance banking and financial sector reforms as a condition for IMF progress, while BdL Governor continues to project a year-end breakthrough window.

Source: L’Orient Today (Oct 22, Oct 29); Middle East Monitor (Oct 29); NYT

Why it matters to you

The disarmament deadlock and continued Israeli strikes keep south Lebanon project pipelines on hold indefinitely. The France-army donor conference is positive for stability medium-term but will not unlock projects this quarter. The IMF banking-reform precondition means no major financial-sector reopening before Q1 2027 at the earliest — dollar-exposed Lebanese clients need repricing and careful credit assessment now.
